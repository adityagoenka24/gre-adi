# GRE Quant Pro — trust strategy without a named person

**Date:** 2026-10-24. Follows the de-identification pass of the same date. The site no longer makes
any personal claim, so trust now has to be earned by the artifact rather than the author. This
document is the plan for that.

---

## The reframe

You were trying to borrow authority from a credential. That route is closed — and it was always the
weaker route anyway, because for the person deciding whether to pay, **a score is a proxy and an
explanation is direct evidence.** A student cannot verify your 170. They can verify, in ninety
seconds, whether your explanation of a hard question is better than the one they just read on
Magoosh. The skeptic persona did exactly that: she couldn't check your score, dismissed "8,390+" on
sight, and then said your `STA-PRM-061` explanation was better than Magoosh's.

So the strategy is: **stop asking to be believed, and start being checkable.** Three pillars, in the
order they should be built.

---

## Pillar 1 — The explanations are the marketing

Right now your best asset is behind a paywall and your marketing is a number. Invert that.

**Trap of the Day.** One genuinely hard question, fully worked, published publicly and permanently —
stem, answer, the named trap, and why each distractor exists. Five a week. Never gated, never
abridged.

Each one does four jobs at once:

1. **Trust** — a stranger can judge the quality without buying anything or believing anything.
2. **SEO** — a permanent indexed page targeting a long-tail query. This is the format that beats you
   today: PrepAiro ranks for your exact keywords with a single blog post containing 18 worked
   questions, an FAQ block and schema tags, while your 43 `/learn/` pages rank nowhere because
   they're written as lecture scripts ("Class length: ~75 min", "Teaching note. Open by asking the
   room"). Question-led pages with worked solutions are what the search intent actually is.
3. **Forum content** — the same post goes to myprepclub's GRE forum and r/GRE, where it earns
   reputation instead of spending it.
4. **Reels** — a 20-second "here's the answer most people pick and why it's wrong" cut.

One piece of work, four channels. This is the whole acquisition engine and it costs nothing but
evenings.

**The rule that makes it work:** publish the *hard* ones. Your extreme-hard tier is genuinely good —
the audit showed 340 of them survive and the four worked by hand were real 165+ items. Publishing
easy questions proves nothing. Publishing a question that a 165-scorer gets wrong, with an
explanation that makes them say "oh", is the entire pitch.

**Also rewrite 8–10 `/learn/` pages out of teacher-script voice into student self-study voice.** A
21-year-old alone on a phone is currently reading stage directions for a class he isn't in.

## Pillar 2 — Radical transparency, which no competitor can copy

This is the pillar that actually replaces the credential, and it's the one you're uniquely positioned
for because you just did the work.

**Ship a public `/audit/` page.** The 806 deleted question IDs, the tag each one failed on
(`explanation_error`, `figure_contradicts_stem`, `no_correct_option`, `difficulty_mislabelled`…), and
the date. Plus the rubric itself — `ETS_RUBRIC.md` is already in the repo. The message is not "our
questions are perfect". It is:

> We hold every question to a written standard, we test against it, 806 failed, we deleted them, and
> here is the list.

Nobody in this market publishes their own defect log. It is falsifiable, it is checkable in one
click, and an anonymous team can make the claim just as credibly as a named one — which is precisely
why it fits you now. The skeptic's own words were that an audit line like this would be a stronger
trust signal than any question count.

**Add a "report this question" control to every question.** One tap, prefilled with the question ID.
Then publish what comes of it: a dated changelog — *"Week of 3 Nov: 2 questions reported, both pulled."*
Every report you act on publicly is a trust event, and it converts your biggest liability (a bank
with known defects) into visible diligence.

**Publish the method, not the person.** The new "How this was built" section on the homepage does
this: named traps, calibrated bands, a written rubric, 806 deletions, nothing AI-generated and
shipped unread. All checkable statements, no identity required.

**One caution.** The site now says "our coaching team" in about sixty places. That's a normal
corporate voice and it's fine — but don't ever elaborate it into specifics you can't support. No team
size, no "our instructors have…", no invented bios. "We" with no decoration is honest; "we" with
fabricated detail is the same problem you just removed, wearing a plural.

## Pillar 3 — Reverse the risk instead of asking for belief

Trust and risk are substitutes. When you can't supply proof, supply a guarantee — and make it so
generous that the student has nothing to lose by testing your claims.

- **Refund: 7 days → 30 days**, no forms, no questions. At your volume the refund cost is a rounding
  error and the conversion effect is not.
- **No card to start.** Already true. Say it louder.
- **Fix the handbook gate promise.** It currently says *"One email, no list, no spam. Just the
  handbook."* That sentence forbids you from ever emailing those leads about Pro. Change it before
  you collect another address — something like *"One email. The handbook, plus occasional GRE quant
  practice. Unsubscribe anytime."*

### The ₹3,999 tier survives — but the offer has to change

It was never really the score that made it sellable. The retaker persona would have paid ₹5,000 for
*diagnosis and a prescription*, and said so explicitly; the 170 was shorthand for "someone who can
tell me what's costing me points." You can demonstrate that directly.

**Don't sell "a 170/170 scorer reviews your mock." Sell the output:**

> **Retake Rescue — ₹3,999.** Send us your mock. You get back a written report: your estimated score,
> the two topics costing you the most points, where your pacing breaks, and a week-by-week drill plan
> built from it. Plus a rebuilt plan after each of your next two mocks.

Then prove it the only way that works without a credential:

1. **Do the first ten free.** Not a discount — free, in exchange for permission to publish the report
   with the name removed.
2. **Publish two of those reports as samples.** A sample report is itself a credential: it is
   checkable work product. Anyone reading a good diagnostic report can tell it was written by someone
   who knows the exam. That is the thing you were trying to prove with a scorecard.
3. **Pay-after option for the first cohort** — "if the report doesn't tell you something you didn't
   already know, don't pay." You will lose almost nothing and you'll have ten reports to point at.

This is also why the mock work from earlier today matters commercially: the estimated score, the
weakness report and the pacing numbers mean a good chunk of each Retake Rescue report is now
generated for you. Your manual work is the judgement layer on top.

### Students' results, without asking for testimonials

You're right that nobody writes testimonials. But you don't need prose. If any past student scored
well, the ask is *"can I post your score screenshot with your name blurred?"* — thirty seconds of
their time, not an essay. Five of those, with dates, beats any paragraph. And if you kept your own
records, *"14 students, median quant improvement +6 points"* is a claim from your own data that
troubles nobody. Neither of these requires a credential of your own.

## Sequence

**Weeks 1–2 — make the site checkable.** Build `/audit/` from the backup data (a few hours; I have
the IDs and failure tags). Add report-this-question. Extend the refund to 30 days. Fix the handbook
gate line. Publish the first five Trap of the Day pages.

**Weeks 3–6 — earn reputation where the students are.** Three myprepclub posts a week. Two weeks of
answering on r/GRE with no links at all, then one handbook post (check the self-promotion rule and DM
the mods first). Ten Quora answers. The handbook into Telegram groups as a gift. Kolkata campus
workshops — the one channel where you have an unfair advantage, and one that needs no public identity.

**Weeks 5–10 — paid, small and measured.** ₹6,000 on long-tail Google Search optimising to
`mock_started`, ₹5,000 on four nano creators at ₹1,000–1,500 a reel, ₹4,000 held back for whichever
produced the cheapest email. All of it still gated on the Worker being deployed, because lead capture
records nothing until then.

**Ongoing — the Retake Rescue cohort.** Ten free reports, two published as samples, then open it at
₹3,999 capped at five to eight a month.
