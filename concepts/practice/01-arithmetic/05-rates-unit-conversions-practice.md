**Lecture capsule:** [05-rates-unit-conversions.md](../../01-arithmetic/05-rates-unit-conversions.md)

> A rate is a ratio with units attached: add rates, never times, and average speed is total distance over total time - always.

## Part C — Worked Solutions

### Q1 · `ARI-RAE-004` — Answer: D

Step 1 — write the conversion as a fraction equal to 1, with the unit you want on top:
(1.6 km)/(1 mile).
Step 2 — multiply and cancel units diagonally:
5 miles x (1.6 km)/(1 mile) = 5 x 1.6 km = 8 km.
The word 'miles' cancels and 'km' survives, which is the check that the fraction was the right way up.
Answer: 8 km.

**Trap.** Dividing instead of multiplying: 5/1.6 = 3.125, choice A. That is the conversion for going km -> miles, and you can catch it instantly by units - dividing by (1.6 km/mile) leaves miles^2/km, which is meaningless. Choice B, 6.6, comes from adding 5 + 1.6 instead of multiplying. Choice C, 7.5, is 5 x 1.5, the factor rounded down to 1.5 before multiplying. Choice E, 16, comes from clearing the decimal - rewriting 1 mile = 1.6 km as 10 miles = 16 km - and then reporting 16 without scaling back down from 10 miles to 5.

### Q2 · `ARI-RAE-011` — Answer: C

Step 1 — chain the two conversions as fractions and cancel:
72 km/h x (1000 m)/(1 km) x (1 h)/(3600 s)
Step 2 — km cancels against km and h cancels against h, leaving m/s:
72 x 1000/3600 = 72000/3600 = 20 m/s.
Step 3 — the shortcut worth memorising: 1000/3600 = 5/18, so km/h to m/s is x5/18.
72 x 5/18 = 4 x 5 = 20.
Check: going back, 20 x 18/5 = 72 km/h. Correct.
Answer: 20 m/s.

**Trap.** Using the factor upside down: 72 x 18/5 = 259.2, choice D. 18/5 is the m/s -> km/h direction, and 259 m/s is faster than sound, which is the sanity check. Choice E is converting km to m but forgetting hours to seconds, and choice A is dividing by 3600 without converting km to m. Choice B, 1.2, is 72/60 - converting the hour to 60 minutes instead of 3600 seconds, so the answer is really km per minute with the wrong unit attached.

### Q3 · `ARI-RAE-012` — Answer: 45

Step 1 — find the unit rate, which is always 'amount per one unit of time':
120 units / 8 hours = 15 units per hour.
Step 2 — Rate x Time = Amount:
15 units/hour x 3 hours = 45 units. The word 'hour' cancels, leaving units.
Check: 8 hours would give 15 x 8 = 120. Correct.
Answer: 45.

**Trap.** Building the rate upside down as 8/120 = 1/15 and answering 0.2, which is hours per unit rather than units per hour. Name the units as you divide: 120 units / 8 hours can only be units per hour, so the units go on top.

### Q4 · `ARI-RAE-013` — Answer: A, B, D

Convert 30 m/s into each unit in turn.
A - m/s to km/h: 30 x 18/5 = 30 x 3.6 = 108 km/h. TRUE.
B - seconds to minutes: 30 m/s x 60 s/min = 1,800 m/min. TRUE.
C - metres to kilometres: 30 m = 30/1000 km = 0.03 km, so the speed is 0.03 km/s, not 0.3. FALSE.
D - metres to centimetres: 30 m x 100 cm/m = 3,000 cm/s. TRUE.
E - 1,800 is the m/min figure with the wrong units pasted on; the true km/h value is 108. FALSE.
Answer: A, B, D.

**Trap.** C is a one-decimal-place slip: dividing by 100 instead of 1000 when going metres -> kilometres. E is the more revealing error - a student computes 30 x 60 = 1,800 correctly but then labels it km/h instead of m/min. Always carry the units through the multiplication rather than attaching them at the end.

### Q5 · `ARI-RAE-018` — Answer: C

Step 1 — the answer is wanted in km per HOUR, so convert the time to hours first.
45 minutes x (1 hour)/(60 minutes) = 45/60 = 3/4 hour.
Step 2 — speed = distance / time:
60 / (3/4) = 60 x 4/3 = 80 km/h.
Check: at 80 km/h the car covers 80 x 3/4 = 60 km in three quarters of an hour. Correct.
Answer: 80 km/h.

**Trap.** Writing 45 minutes as 0.45 hours and computing 60/0.45 = 133.3, choice D. Minutes convert by dividing by 60, not by sliding a decimal point: 45 minutes is 0.75 hours. Choice B comes from treating the 45 minutes as a full hour, and choice E from multiplying 60 x 45 instead of dividing.

### Q6 · `ARI-RAE-039` — Answer: C

Chain every conversion as a fraction and let the units cancel.
Step 1 — build the chain:
5 mL/min x 60 min/h x 24 h/day x 7 day/week x (1 L)/(1000 mL)
min, h, day and mL all cancel, leaving L per week.
Step 2 — the time factor: 60 x 24 = 1,440 minutes per day, and 1,440 x 7 = 10,080 minutes per week.
Step 3 — volume: 5 x 10,080 = 50,400 mL.
Step 4 — convert: 50,400/1000 = 50.4 L.
Check by size: 5 mL/min is about 7.2 L a day, and 7 x 7.2 = 50.4. Correct.
Answer: 50.4 L.

**Trap.** Dropping one link in the chain. Choice A, 2.1 L, is 5 x 60 x 7 / 1000 - the 24 hours per day was left out, so a week became seven hours. Choice E is the full 50,400 mL never converted to litres, choice B divides by 10,000 instead of 1,000, and choice D, 504, divides the 50,400 mL by 100 instead of 1,000 - one decimal place short. Write the chain out and check that the surviving units are litres per week before you multiply.

### Q7 · `ARI-RAE-042` — Answer: 12

Step 1 — notice that pace and speed are reciprocals: pace is min/km, speed is km/h. Flipping the fraction flips the units.
Step 2 — invert the pace: 1 km per 5 minutes.
Step 3 — scale the time up to an hour: 60 minutes / 5 minutes per km = 12 km.
As a unit chain: (1 km)/(5 min) x (60 min)/(1 h) = 60/5 = 12 km/h.
Check: at 12 km/h each kilometre takes 60/12 = 5 minutes. Correct.
Answer: 12.

**Trap.** Answering 5, or 0.083, by treating pace as if it were already a speed. Minutes per kilometre and kilometres per hour are upside down from each other - a SMALLER pace means a FASTER runner - so the first move must be to invert, and only then to convert the time unit.

### Q8 · `ARI-RAE-071` — Answer: C

Step 1 — write the whole chain before computing anything:
250 L/min x 60 min/h x 2 h x (1 m^3)/(1000 L)
min, h and L all cancel, leaving m^3.
Step 2 — total minutes: 60 x 2 = 120 minutes.
Step 3 — total litres: 250 x 120 = 30,000 L.
Step 4 — convert to cubic metres: 30,000/1000 = 30 m^3.
Check: 250 L/min is 15,000 L per hour = 15 m^3 per hour, and 2 hours gives 30 m^3. Correct.
Answer: 30 m^3.

**Trap.** Choice E, 30,000, is the litre figure with the wrong unit attached - the L -> m^3 step was skipped. Choice B, 15, uses one hour instead of two. Choice D, 500, comes from multiplying 250 by the 2 hours directly, which silently treats the pump's rate as 250 litres per hour. Choice A converts 250 L to 0.25 m^3 and then forgets the time entirely.

### Q9 · `ARI-RAE-025` — Answer: B

The useful compound unit here is the worker-day: one worker working for one day.
Step 1 — size the job: 8 workers x 15 days = 120 worker-days.
Step 2 — the wall is the same wall, so it still needs 120 worker-days.
Step 3 — share them out: 120 worker-days / 12 workers = 10 days. 'Workers' cancels and 'days' survives.
Check: 12 x 10 = 120 worker-days, the same job. Correct.
Answer: 10 days.

**Trap.** Choice D, 22.5, scales in DIRECT proportion (15 x 12/8), as though more workers meant more days. Workers and days are inversely proportional at a fixed job size. Choice A halves the time because the crew grew by 50 percent - but a 50 percent bigger crew cuts the time by a factor of 1.5, not 2, so 15/1.5 = 10. Choice C subtracts the 4 extra workers as 4 days, and choice E multiplies 12 x 15 without ever dividing by the original 8.

### Q10 · `ARI-RAE-022` — Answer: B

Average speed is ALWAYS total distance divided by total time. Never average the speeds.
Step 1 — time for the first leg: 100/50 = 2 hours.
Step 2 — time for the second leg: 100/100 = 1 hour.
Step 3 — total time = 3 hours, total distance = 200 km.
Step 4 — average speed = 200/3 = 66.67 km/h.
66.67 < 75, so Column B is greater.
The harmonic-mean shortcut for two EQUAL distances: 2ab/(a+b) = 2(50)(100)/150 = 10000/150 = 66.67. Same answer.

**Trap.** Averaging the two speeds - (50 + 100)/2 = 75 - and choosing C. The legs cover the same distance but NOT the same time: the car spends 2 hours crawling and only 1 hour flying, so the slow speed gets double weight and the average must be pulled below the midpoint. For equal distances the answer is always strictly less than the plain average of the speeds.

### Q11 · `ARI-RAE-029` — Answer: A, C, D

Let each half of the journey last t hours, so the total time is T = 2t.
Total distance = 80t + 60t = 140t.
Average speed = 140t/(2t) = 70 km/h. The t cancels, so this holds for any t.
A - the average speed is exactly 70 km/h. TRUE.
B - 2(80)(60)/140 = 9600/140 = 68.57 km/h. That is the harmonic mean, which applies when the two DISTANCES are equal, not the two times. FALSE.
C - equal times at unequal speeds means the faster leg covers more ground: 80t > 60t. TRUE.
D - total distance = 140t = 70 x (2t) = 70T. TRUE.
E - the average is exactly 70, not less; the harmonic mean is the wrong tool here. FALSE.
Answer: A, C, D.

**Trap.** Reaching for 2ab/(a+b) the moment two speeds appear, which gives 68.57 and makes B and E look right. The formula is only for equal DISTANCES. When the TIMES are equal, the plain average (a+b)/2 is correct - the one case in which averaging the speeds is legitimate.

### Q12 · `ARI-RAE-023` — Answer: D

Step 1 — turn each time into a rate in tanks per hour.
A fills at 1/6 tank per hour. B drains at 1/9 tank per hour.
Step 2 — the pipes oppose each other, so SUBTRACT:
net rate = 1/6 - 1/9 = 3/18 - 2/18 = 1/18 tank per hour.
Step 3 — invert the rate to get the time for one whole tank:
1 / (1/18) = 18 hours.
Check: in 18 hours A puts in 18/6 = 3 tanks and B takes out 18/9 = 2 tanks; net 1 tank. Correct.
Answer: 18 hours.

**Trap.** Adding the rates instead of subtracting: 1/6 + 1/9 = 5/18, giving 3.6 hours, choice A. Rates add only when the agents push the same way; a drain works against the fill and its rate carries a minus sign. Choice C adds the two TIMES (6 + 9), which is never a legal move, choice B averages them, and choice E multiplies them (6 x 9 = 54) on the mistaken analogy with the ab/(a + b) formula, whose denominator has been dropped.

### Q13 · `ARI-RAE-028` — Answer: 60

Because the rates are already in the same units (litres per minute), they can be combined directly - no fractions of a tank needed.
Step 1 — inflow: 5 + 3 = 8 L/min.
Step 2 — outflow: 2 L/min, which opposes the fill, so subtract.
Net rate = 8 - 2 = 6 L/min.
Step 3 — time = amount / rate = 360 L / 6 L/min = 60 minutes. Litres cancel, minutes survive.
Check: in 60 minutes 480 L go in and 120 L come out, leaving 360 L. Correct.
Answer: 60.

**Trap.** Adding all three rates, 5 + 3 + 2 = 10, and answering 36 minutes. The outlet removes water, so its rate is negative in the combination. The second slip is dividing 360 by only one of the inlet rates.

### Q14 · `ARI-RAE-038` — Answer: 30

The two legs cover the same distance, so the average speed is the harmonic mean of the two speeds.
Step 1 — set up: 2(20)(v)/(20 + v) = 24.
Step 2 — clear the fraction: 40v = 24(20 + v) = 480 + 24v.
Step 3 — collect: 40v - 24v = 480, so 16v = 480 and v = 30.
Check with a convenient distance. Let each leg be 60 km.
Out: 60/20 = 3 hours. Back: 60/30 = 2 hours. Total 120 km in 5 hours = 24 km/h. Correct.
Answer: 30.

**Trap.** Treating 24 as the plain average of the two speeds and answering 28, because (20 + 28)/2 = 24. Test it: at 20 and 28 the true average would be 2(20)(28)/48 = 23.33, not 24. Since the slower leg always takes longer, the average is dragged toward 20, so v has to be pushed further out than 28 to compensate.

### Q15 · `ARI-RAE-046` — Answer: E

Step 1 — let the one-way distance be d. Time = distance / speed.
