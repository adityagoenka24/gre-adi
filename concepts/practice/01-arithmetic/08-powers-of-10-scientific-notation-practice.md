## Part C — Worked Solutions

### Q1 · `ARI-P10-003` — Answer: D

Step 1 — read the negative exponent as a reciprocal, not as a sign: 10^(-n) = 1/10^n.
Step 2 — 10^(-2) = 1/10^2 = 1/100 = 0.01.
Step 3 — sanity check the sign of the exponent. A negative exponent always produces a number between 0 and 1, never a negative number.
Answer: 0.01.

**Trap.** Choosing -100, which comes from carrying the minus sign into the value instead of into the exponent. Choice B, -0.01, is the half-corrected version: reciprocal taken but the sign kept. Choice E, 0.1, moves the decimal point only one place instead of two.

### Q2 · `ARI-P10-004` — Answer: -4

Step 1 — find the coefficient. The decimal point must land just after the first nonzero digit, so a = 5.6.
Step 2 — count the places moved. Going from 0.00056 to 5.6 moves the point 4 places to the RIGHT.
Step 3 — moving right makes the coefficient bigger, so the exponent must shrink to compensate: n = -4.
Check: 5.6 x 10^(-4) = 5.6/10000 = 0.00056. Correct.
Answer: -4.

**Trap.** Answering 4. The number is less than 1, so its exponent must be negative - a positive exponent would make 5.6 x 10^4 = 56,000. A second slip is answering -3 by counting only the zeros after the decimal point (three) instead of counting all the way to the first nonzero digit (four places).

### Q3 · `ARI-P10-005` — Answer: C

Step 1 — correct scientific notation is a x 10^k with the coefficient a satisfying 1 <= a < 10 and k an integer. Place the decimal point to meet that rule: a = 4.5.
Step 2 — count places from 4.5 to 45,000: 4.5 -> 45 -> 450 -> 4,500 -> 45,000, four places to the left, so the exponent is 4.
Step 3 — 45,000 = 4.5 x 10^4.
Check: 4.5 x 10,000 = 45,000. Correct. Note that A (45) and D (0.45) also equal 45,000 but violate 1 <= a < 10, so neither is correct scientific notation.

**Trap.** Choices A and D are both numerically equal to 45,000 but neither is scientific notation: 45 is not less than 10 and 0.45 is not at least 1. Answer choices are always in proper standard form, so a coefficient outside [1, 10) is automatically wrong. Choice B, 4.5 x 10^3 = 4,500, moves the point one place too few; choice E, 4.5 x 10^5 = 450,000, one place too many.

### Q4 · `ARI-P10-009` — Answer: B

Step 1 — a positive exponent moves the decimal point to the RIGHT, one place per unit of the exponent.
Step 2 — move the point in 2.4 six places right: 24 -> 240 -> 2,400 -> 24,000 -> 240,000 -> 2,400,000.
Step 3 — as a place-value check, 10^6 is one million, so 2.4 x 10^6 is 2.4 million = 2,400,000.
Answer: 2,400,000.

**Trap.** Choosing 240,000, which is 2.4 x 10^5 - the point was moved five places, not six. Choice C comes from appending six zeros to the digits '24' instead of moving the point six places from 2.4. Choice E moves the point in the wrong direction, which would require a negative exponent.

### Q5 · `ARI-P10-010` — Answer: A, B, C

First fix the target: 0.001 = 1/1000 = 10^(-3).
A - 10^(-3) is exactly the target. TRUE.
B - 1/10^3 = 1/1000 = 0.001. This is the definition of a negative exponent, so it is the same object as A. TRUE.
C - multiplying powers of the same base ADDS exponents: 10^(-1) x 10^(-2) = 10^(-1 + -2) = 10^(-3) = 0.001. TRUE.
D - this is addition, so exponents do not combine: 0.1 + 0.01 = 0.11. FALSE.
E - 1/10^(-3) = 10^3 = 1000, because dividing by 10^(-3) is multiplying by 10^3. FALSE.
Answer: A, B, C.

**Trap.** D is the live bait: students who have just learned that 10^(-1) x 10^(-2) = 10^(-3) apply the same move to a plus sign and get 0.001 again, when the true value is 0.11. E punishes the opposite carelessness - a reciprocal of a negative exponent flips it to positive, turning a thousandth into a thousand, a factor of a million away from the target.

### Q6 · `ARI-P10-012` — Answer: C

Step 1 — split the problem in two: coefficients with coefficients, powers with powers.
Step 2 — coefficients: 5 x 4 = 20.
Step 3 — powers: 10^3 x 10^6 = 10^(3+6) = 10^9. So far: 20 x 10^9.
Step 4 — renormalise. Correct scientific notation requires the coefficient a to satisfy 1 <= a < 10, and 20 does not, so write 20 = 2 x 10^1 and absorb the extra power:
20 x 10^9 = 2 x 10^1 x 10^9 = 2 x 10^10.
Check: 5,000 x 4,000,000 = 20,000,000,000 = 2 x 10^10. Correct.

**Trap.** Choice D, 20 x 10^9, is the right number in the wrong form - the arithmetic is finished but the renormalising step is skipped, and GRE options are always in proper standard form. Choice B is the mirror error: the coefficient is renormalised to 2 but the freed power of 10 is thrown away instead of being added to the exponent. Choice E multiplies the exponents (3 x 6 = 18) instead of adding them, and choice A adds the coefficients (5 + 4 = 9).

### Q7 · `ARI-P10-013` — Answer: 5

Step 1 — isolate the power: 10^n = 480,000/4.8 = 100,000.
Step 2 — 100,000 = 10^5, so n = 5.
Alternative route, by moving the point: to turn 4.8 into 480,000 the decimal point travels 4.8 -> 48 -> 480 -> 4,800 -> 48,000 -> 480,000, which is five places to the left, so n = 5.
Check: 4.8 x 100,000 = 480,000. Correct.
Answer: 5.

**Trap.** Answering 4 by counting the zeros in 480,000. Zeros are not the exponent unless the coefficient is a single digit followed only by zeros. Here the leading digit 4 sits in the hundred-thousands place, which is 10^5, so five places of movement are needed even though only four zeros are visible.

### Q8 · `ARI-P10-014` — Answer: C

Step 1 — the outer power hits BOTH factors: (2 x 10^3)^2 = 2^2 x (10^3)^2.
Step 2 — square the coefficient: 2^2 = 4.
Step 3 — square the power: (10^3)^2 = 10^(3x2) = 10^6. Note that a power of a power MULTIPLIES the exponents.
Step 4 — Column A = 4 x 10^6, which is exactly Column B.
Check in full: 2,000^2 = 4,000,000 = 4 x 10^6. Correct.
The two quantities are equal.

**Trap.** Squaring only the power of 10 and leaving the coefficient alone gives 2 x 10^6 = 2,000,000, which makes Column B look greater. The other slip is adding the exponents, (10^3)^2 = 10^5, which also points to Column B. Everything inside the bracket gets squared, and a power raised to a power multiplies.

### Q9 · `ARI-P10-016` — Answer: C

Column A: 10^(-3) = 1/10^3 = 1/1000 = 0.001.
Column B: rewrite the base as a power of 10 first. 0.1 = 10^(-1), so (0.1)^3 = (10^(-1))^3 = 10^(-1 x 3) = 10^(-3).
Both columns equal 0.001, so the two quantities are equal.
Check by direct multiplication: 0.1 x 0.1 x 0.1 = 0.01 x 0.1 = 0.001. Correct.

**Trap.** Computing (0.1)^3 as 0.1 x 3 = 0.3, which makes Column B look much greater. Cubing a number between 0 and 1 makes it SMALLER, not larger. The reliable move is to convert every decimal to a power of 10 before doing anything else - once 0.1 becomes 10^(-1), the comparison is a one-line exponent calculation.

### Q10 · `ARI-P10-019` — Answer: A, C, E

The test is one line: a number has a negative exponent exactly when it is strictly between 0 and 1.
A - 0.0083 = 8.3 x 10^(-3). Negative. TRUE.
B - 8,300 = 8.3 x 10^3. Positive. FALSE.
C - 0.83 = 8.3 x 10^(-1). It is less than 1, so the exponent is negative even though the number looks 'close to 1'. TRUE.
D - 1 = 1 x 10^0. Zero is not negative, so this fails the test. FALSE.
E - 0.00083 = 8.3 x 10^(-4). Negative. TRUE.
Answer: A, C, E.

**Trap.** Missing C because 0.83 does not look small. Any number below 1 - even 0.99 - has a negative exponent. D is the boundary case: 1 = 1 x 10^0, and 10^0 = 1, not 0, so its exponent is zero, which is neither positive nor negative.

### Q11 · `ARI-P10-021` — Answer: B

None of these is in standard form except Y, so renormalise everything to a common footing first.
Step 1 — X = 0.72 x 10^(-3). Move the point one place right and drop the exponent by 1: X = 7.2 x 10^(-4) = 0.00072.
Step 2 — Y = 9.1 x 10^(-4) = 0.00091.
Step 3 — Z = 830 x 10^(-6). Move the point two places left and raise the exponent by 2: Z = 8.3 x 10^(-4) = 0.00083.
Step 4 — all three now share the exponent -4, so compare coefficients: 7.2 < 8.3 < 9.1.
Therefore X < Z < Y.
Check: 0.00072 < 0.00083 < 0.00091. Correct.

**Trap.** Choice D comes from comparing the exponents exactly as printed, -6 < -4 < -3, which puts Z smallest and X largest - the reverse of the truth for X. That shortcut is only legal once every coefficient is in [1, 10). Choice A comes from ranking by coefficient alone (0.72 < 9.1 < 830), choice C from renormalising X and Y but leaving Z at 10^(-6) and assuming it must therefore be the smallest, and choice E is the correct ranking written greatest-to-least when least-to-greatest was asked.

### Q12 · `ARI-P10-022` — Answer: 395

Step 1 — 'how many times as far' means divide, so form (1.5 x 10^8)/(3.8 x 10^5).
Step 2 — split it: coefficients divide, exponents subtract.
coefficients: 1.5/3.8 = 0.3947...
exponents: 10^(8-5) = 10^3
Step 3 — 0.3947 x 10^3 = 394.7...
Step 4 — round: 395.
Order-of-magnitude sanity check: 1.5/3.8 is a bit under 0.4, and 0.4 x 1,000 = 400, so the answer must be a three-digit number just under 400. Correct.
Answer: 395.

**Trap.** Subtracting the exponents in the wrong order, 10^(5-8) = 10^(-3), which gives 0.395 - a Sun closer than the Moon, which the sanity check kills instantly. The dividend's exponent comes first. The other slip is reading 0.3947 x 10^3 as 3.947 or as 39.47 by mis-shifting the point.

### Q13 · `ARI-P10-025` — Answer: A, C, E

A - a power of a power multiplies exponents: (10^2)^3 = 10^(2x3) = 10^6. TRUE.
B - division subtracts exponents: 10^12/10^2 = 10^(12-2) = 10^10, not 10^6. FALSE.
C - multiplication adds exponents, and a negative exponent is just a negative addend: 10^8 x 10^(-2) = 10^(8 + (-2)) = 10^6. TRUE.
D - addition does not touch exponents at all: 10^3 + 10^3 = 2 x 10^3 = 2,000. FALSE.
E - multiply the exponents, keeping both signs: (10^(-2))^(-3) = 10^((-2)x(-3)) = 10^6. TRUE.
Answer: A, C, E.

**Trap.** B is the sharpest bait: 12/2 = 6, so dividing the exponents lands on the target by an illegal route - division of same-base powers subtracts, giving 10^10. D catches the belief that adding two equal powers combines their exponents; it only doubles the coefficient, leaving 2,000. E is dropped by students who see two minus signs and expect a small number, when two negatives multiply to a positive exponent.

### Q14 · `ARI-P10-027` — Answer: C

Step 1 — rewrite 2^40 so the given approximation can be used: 2^40 = (2^10)^4.
Step 2 — substitute: (2^10)^4 is approximately (10^3)^4 = 10^(3x4) = 10^12.
Step 3 — convert an order of magnitude into a digit count. 10^12 is a 1 followed by 12 zeros, which is 13 characters, so any number of size about 10^12 has 13 digits.
The general rule: a positive integer N with 10^(k) <= N < 10^(k+1) has k + 1 digits.
Check against the true value: 2^40 = 1,099,511,627,776, which indeed has 13 digits. Correct.

**Trap.** Choice B, 12, reads the exponent itself as the digit count; the count is always exponent + 1 for a number of size 10^k. Choice D applies that same 'exponent + 1' rule to the wrong exponent, the 40 in 2^40. Choice E comes from attaching the outer power to the approximation as (10^3)^40 = 10^120 and then adding 1, and choice A reads the outer exponent 4 as the answer.

### Q15 · `ARI-P10-028` — Answer: 11

The insight: never solve for x and y separately. An exponent sum becomes a product.
Step 1 — 10^(x+y) = 10^x x 10^y.
Step 2 — substitute the two given values:
10^(x+y) = (5 x 10^7)(2 x 10^3)
Step 3 — coefficients multiply, exponents add: (5 x 2) x 10^(7+3) = 10 x 10^10.
Step 4 — renormalise: 10 x 10^10 = 10^1 x 10^10 = 10^11.
Step 5 — since 10^(x+y) = 10^11 and the bases match, x + y = 11 exactly.
Answer: 11.

**Trap.** The setup is engineered so the coefficients multiply to exactly 10; a student who writes 10 x 10^10 and reads off the exponent answers 10. That extra factor of 10 must be folded in, pushing the exponent to 11. The other route - x = log(5) + 7 = 7.699 and y = log(2) + 3 = 3.301 - is slow and invites rounding error, and no logarithm is needed here.

### Q16 · `ARI-P10-029` — Answer: C

Adding or subtracting in scientific notation requires MATCHING exponents first - only then may the coefficients be combined.
Step 1 — rewrite the second term with exponent -4. Moving from 10^(-5) up to 10^(-4) raises the exponent by 1, so the coefficient must shrink by a factor of 10:
2 x 10^(-5) = 0.2 x 10^(-4)
Step 2 — subtract the coefficients: (3 - 0.2) x 10^(-4) = 2.8 x 10^(-4).
Step 3 — that is exactly Column B, so the two quantities are equal.
Check in decimals: 0.0003 - 0.00002 = 0.00028 = 2.8 x 10^(-4). Correct.

**Trap.** Subtracting the coefficients as they stand, 3 - 2 = 1, and reporting 1 x 10^(-4) or 1 x 10^(-5) - both make Column B look greater.
