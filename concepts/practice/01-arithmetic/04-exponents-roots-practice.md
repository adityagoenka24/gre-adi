## Part C — Worked Solutions

### Q1 · `ARI-EXP-006` — Answer: A

The whole question is where the brackets sit.
Step 1 - Quantity A. The brackets make -2 the base, so all four factors are -2: (-2)(-2)(-2)(-2). An even number of negative factors gives a positive product, so (-2)^4 = 16.
Step 2 - Quantity B. There are no brackets, so the exponent attaches only to the 2 and the minus sign is applied afterwards: -2^4 = -(2^4) = -16.
Step 3 - compare. 16 > -16, so Quantity A is greater.
Check: Quantity A is positive and Quantity B is negative, so no arithmetic beyond the sign is needed.

**Trap.** Reading both columns the same way and choosing C. Without brackets only the 2 is raised, so -2^4 = -16; with brackets the negative is part of the base, so (-2)^4 = +16. The two differ by a sign, never by nothing.

### Q2 · `ARI-EXP-008` — Answer: B

Step 1 - find the largest perfect square that divides 75. That is 25, since 75 = 25 x 3.
Step 2 - split the radical: sqrt(75) = sqrt(25) x sqrt(3).
Step 3 - take the root of the perfect square only: sqrt(25) = 5, so sqrt(75) = 5 sqrt(3).
Check: (5 sqrt(3))^2 = 25 x 3 = 75. Correct.

**Trap.** Choice A, 3 sqrt(5), comes from mis-factoring 75 as 9 x 5 and rooting the 9; but 9 x 5 = 45, so 3 sqrt(5) is sqrt(45), not sqrt(75). Choice C, 5 sqrt(15), splits 75 as 5 x 15 and moves the 5 outside the radical without taking its root - that is sqrt(375). Only a PERFECT SQUARE factor may come out, and only as its root. Choice D, 25 sqrt(3), uses the right factor 25 but again pulls it out unrooted. Choice E, 37.5, is 75 divided by 2, from reading the square root as 'half of'.

### Q3 · `ARI-EXP-011` — Answer: B

Everything is a power of 2, so work only with the exponents.
Step 1 - product rule on the top: 2^8 x 2^(-3) = 2^(8 + (-3)) = 2^5.
Step 2 - quotient rule: 2^5 / 2^2 = 2^(5 - 2) = 2^3.
Step 3 - 2^3 = 8.
Check: 256 x (1/8) = 32, and 32/4 = 8. Correct.

**Trap.** Choice E, 512 = 2^9, comes from subtracting the negative exponent (8 + 3 - 2) instead of adding it - a^(-n) means you add a negative number, which is subtraction, not the reverse. Choice C, 32, is 2^5, from stopping after the top and forgetting to divide by 2^2. Choice D, 128 = 2^7, adds the divisor's exponent instead of subtracting it. Choice A, -512, comes from reading 2^(-3) as -(2^3) = -8; a negative exponent gives a reciprocal, never a negative number.

### Q4 · `ARI-EXP-013` — Answer: A, B, C

Write every option as a power of 2 and compare with 32 = 2^5.
A - 2^5 = 32. TRUE.
B - 4^3 = 64, and 64/2 = 32. TRUE.
C - product rule: 2^3 x 2^2 = 2^(3+2) = 2^5 = 32. TRUE.
D - power of a power: (2^2)^3 = 2^(2x3) = 2^6 = 64, not 32. FALSE.
E - odd power of a negative base: (-2)^5 = -32, not +32. FALSE.
Answer: A, B, C.

**Trap.** D is the bait for the student who adds the exponents in a power of a power: 2 + 3 = 5 looks like 2^5 = 32, but the rule multiplies, giving 2^6 = 64. E is the sign bait: five negative factors leave the product negative, so (-2)^5 = -32. Only an EVEN power of a negative base comes back positive.

### Q5 · `ARI-EXP-015` — Answer: 9

Step 1 - a negative exponent means take the reciprocal of the base: (1/3)^(-2) = (3/1)^2.
Step 2 - (3)^2 = 9.
Longer route, same answer: (1/3)^(-2) = 1/((1/3)^2) = 1/(1/9) = 9.
Check: 9 x (1/3)^2 = 9 x 1/9 = 1, which is what a number times its reciprocal must give. Correct.
Answer: 9.

**Trap.** Answering -9 by moving the minus sign onto the value. A negative exponent flips the base; it never makes the result negative. The other slip is answering 1/9, which is (1/3)^2 - that ignores the minus sign in the exponent altogether.

### Q6 · `ARI-EXP-021` — Answer: D

Step 1 - split the exponent with the product rule: 2^(x+3) = 2^x x 2^3.
Step 2 - substitute what you were given: 2^x = 32, and 2^3 = 8.
Step 3 - 32 x 8 = 256.
Check by solving for x instead: 32 = 2^5, so x = 5, and 2^(5+3) = 2^8 = 256. Correct.
Answer: 256.

**Trap.** Choice A, 35, adds 3 to the value 32 - the +3 lives in the exponent, not in the number. Choice B, 96, multiplies 32 by 3 instead of by 2^3 = 8. Choice C, 160, multiplies 32 by x = 5. Choice E, 32,768, is 32^3, from cubing the value instead of multiplying it by 2^3.

### Q7 · `ARI-EXP-025` — Answer: C

Step 1 - combine the radicals first, then reduce inside: sqrt(8)/sqrt(6) = sqrt(8/6) = sqrt(4/3).
Step 2 - split again now that the fraction is reduced: sqrt(4/3) = sqrt(4)/sqrt(3) = 2/sqrt(3).
Step 3 - rationalise by multiplying top and bottom by sqrt(3): (2 x sqrt(3))/(sqrt(3) x sqrt(3)) = 2 sqrt(3)/3.
Check numerically: sqrt(8) = 2.828, sqrt(6) = 2.449, and 2.828/2.449 = 1.155. Also 2 x 1.732/3 = 1.155. Correct.

**Trap.** Choice D, 4/3, is 8/6 reduced with the square root never taken. Choice A, 2/3, comes from rationalising 2/sqrt(3) by simply erasing the radical in the denominator rather than multiplying by sqrt(3)/sqrt(3). Choice E, 2 sqrt(3), multiplies the numerator by sqrt(3) but forgets that the denominator becomes 3. Choice B, sqrt(3)/3, drops the factor 2 from sqrt(4).

### Q8 · `ARI-EXP-026` — Answer: B

Step 1 - relate the two columns instead of computing them: x^3 = x^2 x x.
Step 2 - x^2 is positive (x is not 0), and the extra factor x is strictly between 0 and 1.
Step 3 - multiplying a positive number by something less than 1 makes it SMALLER, so x^3 < x^2 for every x in the range.
Check with x = 1/2: x^2 = 1/4 = 0.25 and x^3 = 1/8 = 0.125. Check with x = 0.9: 0.81 versus 0.729. Quantity B wins both times.
Quantity B is greater.

**Trap.** Assuming a bigger exponent always means a bigger number and choosing A. That rule holds only for bases greater than 1. Between 0 and 1 the direction reverses, because each extra factor shrinks the product. Note the stem pins x strictly between 0 and 1, so the relationship is fixed and D is wrong.

### Q9 · `ARI-EXP-037` — Answer: A, C, E

A root is rational exactly when the radicand is a perfect power of a rational number.
A - sqrt(49) = 7, an integer. TRUE.
B - 0.4 = 2/5, and 2/5 is not the square of a rational number, so sqrt(0.4) = sqrt(10)/5 = 0.632... is irrational. FALSE.
C - the cube root of -8 is -2, because (-2)^3 = -8. Cube roots of negatives are real, and -2 is rational. TRUE.
D - sqrt(50) = sqrt(25 x 2) = 5 sqrt(2), and sqrt(2) is irrational. FALSE.
E - multiply under one radical first: sqrt(3) x sqrt(12) = sqrt(36) = 6. TRUE.
Answer: A, C, E.

**Trap.** E is the one most often left out: two irrational factors are assumed to give an irrational product, but sqrt(3) x sqrt(12) = sqrt(36) = 6. B is the mirror error - 0.04 does have a rational root (0.2), so students assume 0.4 does too; it does not, because 0.4 = 2/5 is not a perfect square. C tempts students to answer 'not defined', which is true of even roots of negatives, not odd ones.

### Q10 · `ARI-EXP-039` — Answer: 8

Step 1 - read the fractional exponent: a^(m/n) means take the n-th root, then raise to the m-th power. Here that is the 4th root of 16, cubed.
Step 2 - the cleaner route is to rewrite the base as a prime power: 16 = 2^4.
Step 3 - 16^(3/4) = (2^4)^(3/4) = 2^(4 x 3/4) = 2^3 = 8.
Check the other order: the 4th root of 16 is 2, and 2^3 = 8. Same answer. Correct.
Answer: 8.

**Trap.** Answering 12 by computing 16 x 3/4. A fractional exponent is a root-and-power instruction, not multiplication. The second error is answering 64, from cubing 16's square root (4^3) instead of its fourth root.

### Q11 · `ARI-EXP-043` — Answer: 5

Radicals combine only after each one is simplified to the same radicand.
Step 1 - sqrt(45) = sqrt(9 x 5) = 3 sqrt(5).
Step 2 - sqrt(80) = sqrt(16 x 5) = 4 sqrt(5).
Step 3 - sqrt(20) = sqrt(4 x 5) = 2 sqrt(5).
Step 4 - now they are like terms: 3 sqrt(5) + 4 sqrt(5) - 2 sqrt(5) = (3 + 4 - 2) sqrt(5) = 5 sqrt(5).
Check numerically: 6.708 + 8.944 - 4.472 = 11.180, and 5 x 2.236 = 11.180. Correct.
Answer: 5.

**Trap.** Combining under the radical: sqrt(45) + sqrt(80) - sqrt(20) is NOT sqrt(45 + 80 - 20) = sqrt(105) = 10.25, which is not even a multiple of sqrt(5). Roots do not distribute over addition or subtraction; only the coefficients of matching radicals may be added.

### Q12 · `ARI-EXP-045` — Answer: E

Step 1 - the power of a product distributes over every factor: (2x^3 y^2)^3 = 2^3 x (x^3)^3 x (y^2)^3.
Step 2 - the coefficient is raised too: 2^3 = 8.
Step 3 - power of a power multiplies the exponents: (x^3)^3 = x^9 and (y^2)^3 = y^6.
Step 4 - put it together: 8x^9 y^6.
Check with x = y = 1: (2 x 1 x 1)^3 = 8, and 8 x 1 x 1 = 8. Correct.

**Trap.** Choice C, 6x^9 y^6, multiplies the coefficient 2 by the exponent 3 instead of cubing it - the outer exponent applies to the 2 as well. Choice D, 8x^6 y^5, adds the exponents (3 + 3 and 2 + 3) instead of multiplying them; the product rule is for multiplying like bases, not for a power of a power. Choice A leaves the 2 untouched, and choice B commits both errors at once.

### Q13 · `ARI-EXP-049` — Answer: A

Both quantities are tiny positive numbers, so compare their reciprocals instead - the larger reciprocal belongs to the smaller number.
Step 1 - (1/2)^100 = 1/2^100 and (1/3)^70 = 1/3^70.
Step 2 - compare 2^100 with 3^70 by forcing a common exponent. Both 100 and 70 are multiples of 10:
2^100 = (2^10)^10 = 1024^10
3^70 = (3^7)^10 = 2187^10
Step 3 - 2187 > 1024, so 3^70 > 2^100.
Step 4 - the bigger denominator makes the smaller fraction, so 1/3^70 < 1/2^100.
Quantity A is greater.

**Trap.** Reasoning that the larger exponent (100) must make the smaller number and choosing B. With different bases the exponents alone decide nothing; rewrite both with a common exponent and compare the bases. The second error is forgetting the reciprocal flip - once 3^70 is shown to be the larger number, its reciprocal is the SMALLER quantity.

### Q14 · `ARI-EXP-042` — Answer: B

Do not compute any of these. Force a COMMON EXPONENT - every exponent here (25, 20, 15, 10, 5) is a multiple of 5, so rewrite all five as fifth powers.
Step 1 - A: 2^25 = (2^5)^5 = 32^5.
Step 2 - B: 3^20 = (3^4)^5 = 81^5.
Step 3 - C: 4^15 = (4^3)^5 = 64^5.
Step 4 - D: 6^10 = (6^2)^5 = 36^5.
Step 5 - E: 12^5 is already a fifth power, base 12.
Step 6 - with the exponent fixed at 5, the largest base wins: 81 > 64 > 36 > 32 > 12. So 3^20 is the greatest.
Check the two front-runners in full: 3^20 = 3,486,784,401 and 4^15 = 1,073,741,824. Correct.
Answer: 3^20.

**Trap.** Choice A is the pick of a student chasing the largest exponent (25) and choice E of one chasing the largest base (12); as fifth powers they are 32^5 and 12^5, the two smallest on the list, so neither quantity decides anything on its own. Choice C, 4^15, tempts a student who compares base and exponent separately - 4 beats 3, and 15 looks close enough to 20 - instead of rewriting: 64^5 loses to 81^5. Choice D attracts the reasoning that 6 = 2 x 3 must combine the strength of A and B, but 36^5 beats only A and E.

### Q15 · `ARI-EXP-052` — Answer: D

The stem allows x to be anything except 0, so test the three regions that behave differently.
Case 1 - x > 1, say x = 2: x^2 = 4 and x^3 = 8. Quantity B is greater.
Case 2 - 0 < x < 1, say x = 1/2: x^2 = 0.25 and x^3 = 0.125. Quantity A is greater.
Case 3 - x < 0, say x = -2: x^2 = 4 (positive) and x^3 = -8 (negative). Quantity A is greater.
Two different orderings occur, so the relationship cannot be determined.
Answer: D.

**Trap.** Testing only x = 2, seeing 8 > 4, and choosing B. An even power is never negative and a fractional base reverses the order, so both x < 0 and 0 < x < 1 break that conclusion. On any QC with a bare variable, test a number greater than 1, a fraction between 0 and 1, and a negative before committing.

### Q16 · `ARI-EXP-054` — Answer: C

To undo an exponent you raise both sides to its RECIPROCAL.
Step 1 - the exponent is 2/3, so raise both sides to the power 3/2: (x^(2/3))^(3/2) = x^(2/3 x 3/2) = x^1 = x.
Step 2 - the right side: 4^(3/2) = (sqrt(4))^3 = 2^3 = 8.
Step 3 - so x = 8.
Check: 8^(2/3) = (cube root of 8)^2 = 2^2 = 4. Correct.

**Trap.** Choice D, 16, comes from raising 4 to the power 2 instead of 3/2 - using the numerator of the exponent rather than its reciprocal. Choice E, 64, is 4^3, from taking the cube root instruction and forgetting the square. Choice B, 6, is 4 x 3/2, treating the fractional exponent as multiplication. Choice A, 2, is 4^(1/2), undoing only half the exponent.

### Q17 · `ARI-EXP-055` — Answer: B, C, D

The square-root symbol always returns the NON-NEGATIVE root, so sqrt(a^2) = |a|, not a. Note b^4 is already non-negative and sqrt(b^4) = b^2, which is non-negative on its own - no absolute value is needed there.
So sqrt(a^2 b^4) = |a| b^2.
A - a b^2 fails whenever a < 0. With a = -3, b = 2: sqrt(9 x 16) = 12, but a b^2 = -12. FALSE.
B - |a| b^2 is exactly the simplified form. TRUE.
C - |a b^2| = |a| x |b^2| = |a| b^2, since b^2 is never negative. TRUE.
D - splitting the radical over a product is legal: sqrt(a^2) x sqrt(b^4) = |a| x b^2. TRUE.
E - |a| |b|^4 = |a| b^4, which halves nothing. With a = -3, b = 2 it gives 3 x 16 = 48, not 12. FALSE.
Answer: B, C, D.

**Trap.** A is the headline trap: sqrt(x^2) = |x|, not x. It looks right because it is right whenever a happens to be positive, and the stem deliberately allows a to be negative. E is the exponent-arithmetic trap - taking a square root HALVES the exponent, so sqrt(b^4) = b^2, not b^4; with a = -3, b = 2 it produces 48 instead of 12.
