## Part C — Worked Solutions

### Q1 · `ARI-EST-003` — Answer: 0.0039

Step 1 — find the first significant figure. Leading zeros never count, so counting starts at the first non-zero digit: 3.
Step 2 — the second significant figure is the next digit, 8. So we are rounding at the 8.
Step 3 — look at the digit after it: 6. Since 6 >= 5, round the 8 up to 9.
Result: 0.0039.
Check: 0.0039 and 0.003862 differ by 0.000038, about 1% - the right size of error for 2 significant figures.
Answer: 0.0039.

**Trap.** Counting the two leading zeros as significant figures, which makes 0 and 0 the first two digits and gives 0.0038 (or even 0.00). Significant figures start at the first non-zero digit, so the leading zeros are placeholders only.

### Q2 · `ARI-EST-006` — Answer: C

Step 1 — notice the direction of each rounding. 197 rounds UP to 200 and 48 rounds UP to 50, so 200 x 50 = 10,000 inflates the product twice over and must be an OVERestimate.
Step 2 — let the two roundings cancel instead. Round 197 DOWN to 190 and 48 UP to 50, so the errors point in opposite directions: 190 x 50 = 9,500.
Step 3 — check how good that is. 200 x 48 = 9,600, and correcting for the 3 added to 197 gives 9,600 - 3 x 48 = 9,600 - 144 = 9,456.
Step 4 — match to the choices: 9,456 is 44 from 9,500 and 544 from 10,000. 9,500 is closest by a wide margin.
Check: 197 x 48 = 9,456. Correct.
Answer: 9,500.

**Trap.** Rounding both factors up to 200 x 50 = 10,000 and picking D: both errors push the same way, nothing cancels, and the estimate lands 544 high. Choice B, 9,000, is 200 x 45, rounding 48 down instead of up. Choice A, 7,880, is 197 x 40, truncating 48 to 40 rather than rounding. Choice E, 11,820, is 197 x 60, rounding 48 up to the wrong ten. The habit worth keeping: round one factor up and the other down so the two errors cancel.

### Q3 · `ARI-EST-008` — Answer: 100

Step 1 — do not touch the 49. It is already friendly: 49 x 100 = 4,900.
Step 2 — 4,892 is only 8 short of 4,900, so the quotient is only a hair under 100.
Step 3 — exactly: 4,892/49 = 99.84.
Rounded to the nearest whole number: 100.
Answer: 100.

**Trap.** Rounding 49 up to 50 and computing 4,892/50 = 97.84, then answering 98. Rounding the DENOMINATOR up drags the quotient down, and here it costs more than 2 whole units. The 49 needed no rounding at all - 49 x 100 was already a clean landmark.

### Q5 · `ARI-EST-010` — Answer: A, C, E

Rounding to the nearest tenth means looking at the hundredths digit only, and asking whether it is 5 or more.
A - 3.45: hundredths digit is 5, so round the tenths digit up from 4 to 5, giving 3.5. TRUE.
B - 3.449: hundredths digit is 4, so round down, giving 3.4. FALSE.
C - 3.54: hundredths digit is 4, so round down, giving 3.5. TRUE.
D - 3.55: hundredths digit is 5, so round up, giving 3.6. FALSE.
E - 3.501: hundredths digit is 0, so round down, giving 3.5. TRUE.
Answer: A, C, E.

**Trap.** Treating B (3.449) as rounding to 3.5 by rounding twice: 3.449 -> 3.45 -> 3.5. You may only look at the ONE digit to the right of the place you are keeping. The mirror error is reading C (3.54) as rounding up to 3.6 because 3.54 is above 3.5 - it is, but not by enough.

### Q6 · `ARI-EST-012` — Answer: B

Step 1 — spot the symmetry: 299 and 301 sit one below and one above 300.
Step 2 — difference of squares: (300 - 1)(300 + 1) = 300^2 - 1^2 = 90,000 - 1 = 89,999.
Step 3 — compare: 89,999 < 90,000, so Quantity B is greater.
The general fact worth carrying: (a - d)(a + d) is ALWAYS less than a^2, by exactly d^2. A product of two numbers straddling a is never as large as a^2.

**Trap.** Estimating 299 x 301 as 300 x 300 = 90,000 and choosing C. The two roundings look like they cancel, but they do not: moving one factor down by 1 costs 301, while moving the other up by 1 gains only 299. On a QC where your estimate lands exactly on Quantity B, the estimate has told you nothing - you must compute.

### Q7 · `ARI-EST-013` — Answer: B

Step 1 — bracket it. 5^2 = 25 and 6^2 = 36, so sqrt(26) is between 5 and 6, and very close to 5.
Step 2 — position it proportionally. 26 is 1 above 25 in a gap of 11, and near a square the shift is about (gap above)/(2 x root): 1/(2 x 5) = 0.1. So sqrt(26) is about 5.1.
Step 3 — confirm by squaring the candidates. 5.05^2 = 25.5025, which is below 26, so sqrt(26) > 5.05. And 5.1^2 = 26.01, just above 26, so sqrt(26) < 5.1.
So 5.05 < sqrt(26) < 5.1, and to the nearest tenth that is 5.1.
Answer: 5.1.

**Trap.** Stopping at 'just above 5' and answering 5.0. Since sqrt(26) must round to a tenth, you have to decide whether it clears 5.05, and squaring 5.05 settles it in one step. Choice C (5.2) comes from over-shifting - using 1/5 instead of 1/(2 x 5).

### Q8 · `ARI-EST-017` — Answer: A, B, C

'Within 0.05 of 0.7' means the value lies strictly between 0.65 and 0.75 - a distance of less than 0.05.
A - 5/7 = 0.7143. Distance 0.0143. Inside. TRUE.
B - 7/10 = 0.7000. Distance 0, which is certainly less than 0.05. Inside. TRUE.
C - 2/3 = 0.6667. Distance 0.0333. Inside. TRUE.
D - 3/4 = 0.7500. Distance is exactly 0.0500, which is not LESS than 0.05. It sits on the boundary, not inside it. FALSE.
E - 7/9 = 0.7778. Distance 0.0778. Outside. FALSE.
Answer: A, B, C.

**Trap.** Including D. 3/4 lands exactly 0.05 away, and 'within 0.05' excludes the boundary - read the inequality before you round. The opposite slip is dropping C: 2/3 = 0.6667 sits on the low side of 0.7, but its gap of 0.0333 is still inside the window, so it belongs. B is the free one - 7/10 IS 0.7, a distance of zero.

### Q9 · `ARI-EST-018` — Answer: A

Do not estimate here - the numerator is exact in one step.
Step 1 — 0.48 x 210 = 48 x 2.1 = 100.8.
Step 2 — Quantity A = 100.8/99. Since 100.8 > 99, the fraction is greater than 1.
Quantity A is greater.
For the record, 100.8/99 = 1.018.
Why the estimate is dangerous: 0.48 rounds up to 0.50 and 99 rounds up to 100, so BOTH roundings act on the answer, and one of them (raising 0.48) pushes the value up while the other (raising 99) pushes it down.

**Trap.** Rounding 0.48 to 0.5 AND 210 to 200 AND 99 to 100, which gives (0.5 x 200)/100 = 1 exactly, and choosing C. Three roundings of a quantity that sits only 1.8% from the comparison value is far too coarse. Whenever an estimate on a QC comes out equal to Quantity B, that is the signal to compute exactly, not to answer C.

### Q10 · `ARI-EST-020` — Answer: 25

Step 1 — round both parts the SAME way (both up): 247 -> 250 and 9.8 -> 10.
Step 2 — 250/10 = 25. Because numerator and denominator were both nudged up, the two errors partly cancel and this estimate is trustworthy.
Step 3 — confirm: 9.8 x 25 = 245, and the true distance is 247, only 2 miles more. So the true value is a shade above 25.
Exactly: 247/9.8 = 25.2, which rounds to 25.

**Trap.** Rounding 9.8 DOWN to 9 while leaving 247 alone: 247/9 = 27.4, an answer of 27. Shrinking a denominator inflates the quotient. The mirror error is rounding 247 down to 240 and 9.8 up to 10, giving 24 - numerator down and denominator up compounds the error the other way.

### Q11 · `ARI-EST-022` — Answer: C

The choices differ by factors of 10, so only the order of magnitude matters - do not compute the digits.
Step 1 — round: 7,823 is about 8 x 10^3, and 0.00512 is about 5 x 10^-3.
Step 2 — multiply the leading digits and add the exponents: 8 x 5 = 40, and 10^3 x 10^-3 = 10^0 = 1.
So the product is about 40 x 1 = 40.
Check: 7,823 x 0.00512 = 40.05. Correct.
Answer: 40.

**Trap.** Miscounting the zeros in 0.00512 and reading it as 0.0512, which gives 8,000 x 0.05 = 400 (choice D). When choices are a factor of 10 apart, the whole question is place value - write each number in the form (digit) x 10^k before multiplying and the exponent bookkeeping becomes automatic.

### Q13 · `ARI-EST-025` — Answer: 1.6

Step 1 — do not reach for decimals. Factor the repeated number out of the numerator: 3.14 x 48 + 3.14 x 32 = 3.14 x (48 + 32) = 3.14 x 80.
Step 2 — read the denominator in the same currency: 6.28 = 2 x 3.14, so 6.28 x 25 = 3.14 x 2 x 25 = 3.14 x 50.
Step 3 — now 3.14 cancels top and bottom: (3.14 x 80)/(3.14 x 50) = 80/50.
Step 4 — 80/50 = 1.6, exactly. No approximation is involved anywhere.
Check the long way: 3.14 x 48 = 150.72 and 3.14 x 32 = 100.48, so the numerator is 251.2; the denominator is 6.28 x 25 = 157; and 251.2/157 = 1.6. Correct.
Answer: 1.6.

**Trap.** Cancelling 6.28 as though it were a single 3.14 - missing that 6.28 = 2 x 3.14 - which leaves 80/25 = 3.2, exactly twice the true value. The other slip is multiplying out 3.14 x 48 and 3.14 x 32 by hand before noticing they share a factor: it burns a minute and invites a decimal error. Factor and cancel first; round only what refuses to cancel.

### Q15 · `ARI-EST-028` — Answer: B

Method 1 - benchmarks. sqrt(2) = 1.41, sqrt(3) = 1.73, sqrt(5) = 2.24.
Quantity A = 1.41 + 1.73 = 3.14.
Quantity B = 2.24 + 1 = 3.24.
Quantity B is greater. The gap, 0.10, is three times the total rounding slack in the two benchmarks, so the estimate is safe.
Method 2 - square both, which is legal since both columns are positive.
A^2 = 2 + 3 + 2 sqrt(6) = 5 + 2 sqrt(6)
B^2 = 5 + 1 + 2 sqrt(5) = 6 + 2 sqrt(5)
Subtract: B^2 - A^2 = 1 + 2 sqrt(5) - 2 sqrt(6) = 1 - 2(sqrt(6) - sqrt(5)).
Now sqrt(6) = 2.449 and sqrt(5) = 2.236, so 2(sqrt(6) - sqrt(5)) = 0.43, which is less than 1.
So B^2 - A^2 > 0, and Quantity B is greater.

**Trap.** Adding under the radical: sqrt(2) + sqrt(3) is NOT sqrt(5), so Quantity A is not sqrt(5) and the columns are not sqrt(5) versus sqrt(5) + 1. A second error is squaring term by term - (sqrt(2) + sqrt(3))^2 is 5 + 2 sqrt(6), not 5 - dropping the cross term makes Quantity A look far too small.

### Q16 · `ARI-EST-029` — Answer: D

A fraction gets bigger when the numerator gets bigger or the denominator gets smaller. So an estimate is GUARANTEED to be too large only when the numerator is rounded up and the denominator is rounded down.
A - numerator down (4,800,000 < 4,847,000) and denominator up (210 > 206). Both moves shrink the fraction, so this is guaranteed too SMALL. No.
B - numerator down, denominator down. The two moves fight each other, so nothing is guaranteed without computing. No.
C - numerator up, denominator up. Again the moves fight each other. No.
D - numerator up (4,900,000 > 4,847,000) and denominator down (200 < 206). Both moves enlarge the fraction, so the result is certainly greater. YES.
E - numerator down, denominator unchanged, so this is guaranteed too small. No.
Answer: D.
