## Part C — Worked Solutions

### Q1 · `STA-DDI-001` — Answer: C

Step 1 - a normal curve is perfectly symmetric about its center and has a single peak.
Step 2 - symmetry puts the balance point (the mean) at the center, and it puts half the area on each side of the center, so the median is at the center too.
Step 3 - the single peak sits at the center as well, so the mode is there.
All three measures land on the same value, so C is the only statement forced by normality.
Check: a normal distribution is fully described by its mean and standard deviation, and the mean is exactly where the curve is highest. Correct.

**Trap.** A, B, D and E are all statements about SKEWED data smuggled into a normal-curve question. A and B describe right skew (mean above median, mode lowest), E describes left skew. A normal distribution has no skew at all, so any statement putting one of the three measures strictly above another is false.

### Q2 · `STA-DDI-007` — Answer: B

Step 1 - skew is named for the direction the TAIL points, not for where the bulk of the data sits.
Step 2 - the tail here stretches toward the low values, on the left, so the distribution is left-skewed (negatively skewed).
Step 3 - the consequence: those few very low values drag the mean down, so mean < median for this shape.
Answer: left-skewed.

**Trap.** Reading 'most values on the right' as right-skewed and choosing A. The bulk of the data always sits OPPOSITE the tail, so the crowded side is never the side that names the skew. Remember the hook: the tail pulls the mean, and the tail is what gets named.

### Q3 · `STA-DDI-008` — Answer: C

Step 1 - a normal curve is symmetric about its mean m.
Step 2 - symmetry means the area to the right of m equals the area to the left of m, and the two areas make up the whole curve.
Step 3 - so each area is 0.50: Quantity A = 0.50 and Quantity B = 0.50.
The two quantities are equal.
Note that s never enters the calculation - a wide curve and a narrow curve both split 50/50 at the mean.

**Trap.** Choosing D because s is unknown. The size of s stretches the curve sideways but cannot move the axis of symmetry off the mean, so the split at m is always 50/50. This 'half the area is below the mean' fact is what makes the 68-95-99.7 bands split evenly on each side.

### Q4 · `STA-DDI-009` — Answer: A

Step 1 - the original six values: sum = 3 + 5 + 7 + 9 + 11 + 13 = 48, so mean = 48/6 = 8. With six values the median is the average of the 3rd and 4th: (7 + 9)/2 = 8.
Step 2 - with 100 appended: sum = 48 + 100 = 148, so mean = 148/7 = 21.14 (about). With seven values the median is the 4th value, which is 9.
Step 3 - compare. The mean jumped from 8 to about 21.1, a rise of more than 13. The median moved from 8 to 9, a rise of 1.
Answer: the mean rises sharply, the median barely moves.

**Trap.** Choosing C on the assumption that an outlier drags both measures equally. The mean uses the actual SIZE of every value, so one huge value moves it a long way; the median only cares about POSITION in the ordered list, so an extreme value shifts it by at most one place. That resistance is exactly why the median is reported for skewed data such as incomes.

### Q5 · `STA-DDI-KMF2-003` — Answer: 3

A depth that is 2.5 standard deviations above the average exceeds the average by 2.5 times the standard deviation.
The excess is
31.1 − 23.6 = 7.5 centimeters,
and that equals 2.5 standard deviations. So the standard deviation is
7.5/2.5 = 3 centimeters.
Checking: 23.6 + 2.5(3) = 23.6 + 7.5 = 31.1.

**Trap.** Multiplying instead of dividing gives 18.75, which would answer how far 2.5 standard deviations reaches if the deviation were 7.5. The excess of 7.5 centimeters is itself 2.5 standard deviations, so the deviation is recovered by dividing. Subtracting the 2.5 from the excess, as in 7.5 − 2.5, treats a count of deviations as a length.

### Q6 · `STA-DDI-011` — Answer: C

Step 1 - locate the peak. The mode, 55, is the highest point of a single-peaked histogram, so the bulk of the data sits near 55.
Step 2 - locate the mean. The mean, 40, sits 15 BELOW the peak. The mean is the balance point, so something far out on the low side must be pulling it down.
Step 3 - that something is a long tail of low values. A tail pointing toward the low end is left skew (negative skew).
Step 4 - the ordering confirms it: mean 40 < median 50 < mode 55, which is the standard signature of left skew.
Answer: left-skewed with the tail toward lower values.

**Trap.** Choosing B by pairing the words 'mode is highest' with 'skewed right'. The direction of skew is set by where the TAIL is, and the tail is on the side the mean is dragged toward - here the low side. Choices A and E are also live: they both require mean = median, which 40 and 50 rule out immediately.

### Q7 · `STA-DDI-013` — Answer: 105

Step 1 - the original sum: 12 + 15 + 18 + 21 + 24 + 27 + 30 = 147, and 147/7 = 21, confirming the given mean.
Step 2 - the new mean: 21 + 10.5 = 31.5.
Step 3 - the new sum: 8 values with mean 31.5 gives 8 x 31.5 = 252.
Step 4 - the added value: 252 - 147 = 105.
Check: 147 + 105 = 252, and 252/8 = 31.5, which is 10.5 above 21. Correct.
Answer: 105.

**Trap.** Answering 31.5 (the new mean) or 10.5 (the shift in the mean) instead of the value that was added. The single new value has to carry its own share of the mean PLUS the extra 10.5 needed by all eight entries: 31.5 + 7 x 10.5 = 105 is the same computation seen a second way.

### Q8 · `STA-DDI-017` — Answer: C

Step 1 - write down the midpoints: 65, 75, 85, 95.
Step 2 - the mean of a frequency distribution is a WEIGHTED average: multiply each value by its frequency, add, then divide by the total frequency.
5 x 65 = 325
10 x 75 = 750
20 x 85 = 1700
15 x 95 = 1425
Step 3 - total of the products: 325 + 750 + 1700 + 1425 = 4200.
Step 4 - total frequency: 5 + 10 + 20 + 15 = 50.
Step 5 - mean = 4200/50 = 84.
Check: 84 lies between the two heaviest intervals (80-90 with 20 scores and 90-100 with 15), pulled slightly below 85 by the lighter low intervals. Correct.
Answer: 84.

**Trap.** Choice B, 80, is the average of the four midpoints (65 + 75 + 85 + 95)/4 - it ignores the frequencies entirely and so treats 5 scores as though they counted as much as 20. Choice D, 85, is the midpoint of the modal interval, the interval with the most scores. Choice A, 79, comes from using the LOWER endpoint of each interval instead of the midpoint, and choice E, 89, from using the upper endpoint.

### Q9 · `STA-DDI-019` — Answer: A, B, C

A - 'skewed to the right' means precisely that the long tail runs toward the high values, away from the peak. True.
B - by the definition of the median, at least half of the values are at or below it, and the median is 44. True.
C - the largest value is always at least the mean, and it is strictly greater unless every value is identical - which is ruled out here, since identical values would force mean = median. Removing a value that sits above the mean must pull the mean down: the nine-value list 10, 10, 10, 10, 10, 10, 10, 10, 370 has mean 50, and dropping the 370 leaves a mean of 10. True.
D - mode < median < mean is a rule of thumb for skewed data, not a theorem, and nothing given pins the mode below 44. False.
E - the mean is a balance point, not an entry in the list. The two values 44 and 56 have mean 50 even though neither of them is 50. False.
Answer: A, B, C.

**Trap.** D is the tempting one: the chain mode < median < mean describes typical skewed data, so it can never be asserted as something that must be true. E catches the belief that the mean has to be an actual data value - it is only the balance point of the list, and it frequently sits between the values rather than on one.

### Q10 · `STA-DDI-022` — Answer: 400

Step 1 - the 68-95-99.7 rule: about 68% of the data lies within 1 SD of the mean, so about 100% - 68% = 32% lies outside that band.
Step 2 - the curve is symmetric, so halve the 32%: about 16% lies below mean - 1 SD, and about 16% above mean + 1 SD.
Step 3 - so mean - 1 SD is the point with about 16% of the data below it, which is the 16th percentile to the accuracy of the rule.
Step 4 - mean - 1 SD = 500 - 100 = 400.
Check: the marks read 300, 400, 500, 600, 700 with cumulative percentages 2.5, 16, 50, 84, 97.5. The 16th percentile is 400. Correct.
Answer: 400.

**Trap.** Forgetting to halve the 32% and treating the 32nd percentile as the 1-SD mark, or taking 16% of 500 and answering 80. The percentile scale on a normal curve is built from the halved tails: 2.5 / 16 / 50 / 84 / 97.5 at -2, -1, 0, +1 and +2 SDs.

### Q11 · `STA-DDI-023` — Answer: A

Step 1 - the standard deviation measures typical distance from the mean, so it describes SPREAD, not center.
Step 2 - Class A has SD 5, Class B has SD 15. Class A's scores sit on average three times closer to the mean, so they are more tightly clustered. A is true.
Step 3 - check the others. C is false because 5 is not 15. D is false because the two means are both 70. E sounds plausible but is not forced: the standard deviation is computed from every value, while the highest score is a single value, and a larger SD does not guarantee a larger maximum in any particular pair of data sets.
Answer: A.

**Trap.** Reading a bigger standard deviation as a bigger or better score and choosing D or E. Standard deviation says nothing about the center and nothing about any single value - it reports only how far from the mean a typical value lies. Note also that no normal-curve percentages may be used here: the question never says the scores are normally distributed.

### Q12 · `STA-DDI-024` — Answer: D

Step 1 - with 11 values sorted, the median is the 6th value, so the 6th value is 45.
Step 2 - 200 is larger than 45, so it is inserted somewhere after position 6. The first six values are therefore unchanged and the new 6th value is still 45, while the new 7th value is whichever is smaller, the old 7th value or 200.
Step 3 - with 12 values the median is the average of the 6th and 7th: (45 + w)/2, where w is the smaller of the old 7th value and 200, so w >= 45.
Step 4 - test two legal data sets.
If the data set is 41, 42, 43, 44, 44, 45, 45, 45, 45, 46, 47, then the old 7th value is 45, so w = 45 and the new median is (45 + 45)/2 = 45, equal to Quantity B.
If the data set is 41, 42, 43, 44, 44, 45, 55, 56, 57, 58, 59, then the old 7th value is 55, so w = 55 and the new median is (45 + 55)/2 = 50, greater than Quantity B.
Two legal data sets give two different comparisons, so the relationship cannot be determined.

**Trap.** Choosing C on the slogan 'the median ignores outliers'. The median is resistant to an outlier's SIZE, but adding a twelfth value still changes which positions are averaged, so the median can move - here it can only move up, but whether it actually moves depends on the 7th value. Choosing A is the mirror error: assuming a large addition must raise the median.

### Q13 · `STA-DDI-025` — Answer: A, E

Step 1 - IQR = Q3 - Q1 = 60 - 40 = 20.
Step 2 - 1.5 x IQR = 30.
Step 3 - lower fence = Q1 - 30 = 40 - 30 = 10. Upper fence = Q3 + 30 = 60 + 30 = 90.
Step 4 - test each value against the interval from 10 to 90.
A - 8 < 10, below the lower fence. OUTLIER.
B - 35 is between 10 and 90. Not an outlier.
C - 55 is between 10 and 90 (it is inside the box itself). Not an outlier.
D - 85 is between 10 and 90, so it is unusual but not an outlier by this rule. Not an outlier.
E - 92 > 90, above the upper fence. OUTLIER.
Answer: A and E.

**Trap.** Choice D, 85, is the bait: it lies outside the box (above Q3 = 60) and looks extreme, but the fence is 90, not 60. The other common slip is applying 1.5 x IQR to the wrong ends - subtracting 30 from Q3 and adding 30 to Q1 gives the reversed interval 30 to 70, which flags 85 as well and produces the wrong answer A, D and E.

### Q14 · `STA-DDI-030` — Answer: B, C, D

1.5 standard deviations is 1.5 * 8 = 12.
Values within 1.5 standard deviations of the mean lie from 60 - 12 = 48 to 60 + 12 = 72.
A: 46 is below 48. No.
B: 50 is between 48 and 72. Yes.
C: 60 is the mean. Yes.

**Trap.** Using 1.5 itself, or 8 + 1.5 = 9.5, as the allowed distance, which excludes 71 or 50. The allowed distance is 1.5 times the standard deviation, 12.
