# Percentage Word Problems — Practice Set

**Lecture capsule:** [08-percentage-word-problems.md](../../04-word-problems/08-percentage-word-problems.md)

> Find the base before you find the percent: "percent of the remainder" is not "percent of the original", and "percent of the subgroup" is not "percent of the total".

> **Quantitative Comparison — the four choices are always the same, so they are not reprinted:**
> **(A)** Quantity A is greater · **(B)** Quantity B is greater · **(C)** The two quantities are equal · **(D)** The relationship cannot be determined from the information given

## Part C — Worked Solutions

### Q1 · `WRD-PCT-006` — Answer: A

Step 1 — Find the base for the second percentage. "55 percent of those who voted" is measured against the voters, not against all 5,000 registered voters.
Step 2 — Voters = 60% of 5,000 = 0.60 * 5000 = 3,000.
Step 3 — Candidate A = 55% of 3,000 = 0.55 * 3000 = 1,650.
Step 4 — One line: 0.55 * 0.60 * 5000 = 0.33 * 5000 = 1,650, so Candidate A took 33% of all registered voters.
Check: the other candidates got 45% of 3,000 = 1,350, and 1,650 + 1,350 = 3,000, the full turnout. Correct.
Answer: A.

**Trap.** Applying 55% to all 5,000 registered voters gives 0.55 * 5000 = 2,750, which is choice D. The stem attaches 55% to the people who actually voted. Choice C, 2,250, is 45% of 5,000: the same wrong base and the losing share. Choice B, 1,800, reuses the FIRST rate on the turnout, 0.60 * 3000, instead of the second. Choice E, 3,000, is the turnout itself — the second stage never applied.

### Q2 · `WRD-PCT-003` — Answer: B

Step 1 — Identify what the 5 percent is a percentage OF. The commission is 5% of sales, not 5% of salary and not 5% of the two added together.
Step 2 — Commission = 0.05 * 6000 = 300.
Step 3 — Total income = 3200 + 300 = 3,500.
Check: 300 is 5% of 6,000 because 10% of 6,000 is 600 and half of that is 300. Adding it to the fixed 3,200 gives 3,500. Correct.
Answer: B.

**Trap.** Taking 5% of the salary instead of the sales gives 3200 + 160 = 3,360, choice A. Taking 5% of salary and sales combined gives 3200 + 0.05 * 9200 = 3,660, choice C. The commission rate has exactly one base: the sales figure.

### Q3 · `WRD-PCT-008` — Answer: B

Step 1 — The 20 percent is taken off the list price, so the base is $50.
Step 2 — Discount = 0.20 * 50 = 10, so Store X charges 50 - 10 = 40.
Step 3 — Store Y charges 42. Since 40 < 42, Quantity B is greater.
Faster: 20% off means paying 80% of list, and 0.80 * 50 = 40 directly.
Check: $42 off a $50 list is a discount of 8/50 = 16%, a smaller cut than 20%, so Store Y must indeed be the dearer shop. Correct.
Answer: B.

**Trap.** Reading "20 percent off" as "pay 20 percent" gives 0.20 * 50 = $10 and the wrong conclusion that Quantity A is far smaller for the wrong reason; reading it as 20 percent off Store Y's price gives 0.80 * 42 = 33.60 and again mislocates the base. The discount base is the $50 list price.

### Q4 · `WRD-PCT-002` — Answer: 33600

Step 1 — The 40 percent is a percentage of the under-18 group, not of the whole city.
Step 2 — Under 18 = 0.35 * 240000 = 84,000.
Step 3 — Under 10 = 0.40 * 84000 = 33,600.
Step 4 — As a single multiplier chain: 0.40 * 0.35 = 0.14, and 0.14 * 240000 = 33,600, so the under-10 group is 14 percent of the city.
Check: 14% of 240,000 is 10% (24,000) plus 4% (9,600), which is 33,600. Correct.
Answer: 33600.

**Trap.** Applying 40 percent to the whole city gives 0.40 * 240000 = 96,000, a number more than half the entire under-18 group. Adding the two percentages, 35 + 40 = 75 percent, gives 180,000; percentages measured against different bases never add.

### Q5 · `WRD-PCT-051` — Answer: A, B, C

Step 1 — Turn the percentage into a count and keep the count as a count. Paperbacks = 0.40 * 250 = 100. Textbooks sold = 30, all of them paperbacks.
A — TRUE. 0.40 * 250 = 100.
B — TRUE. The 30 textbooks are measured against the paperbacks, their own group: 30/100 = 30 percent.
C — TRUE. Measured against all books sold, the same 30 give 30/250 = 0.12 = 12 percent. Same 30 books, two different bases, two different percentages.
D — FALSE. 30 is a count, not a percent. Since no hardback textbooks were sold, textbooks are 30/250 = 12 percent of the books sold, not 30 percent.
E — FALSE. 150 is 0.60 * 250, the books that were NOT paperbacks.
Check: 100 paperbacks + 150 non-paperbacks = 250, and 30 of the 100 paperbacks is 12 percent of 250. Correct.
Answer: A, B and C.

**Trap.** Choice D reads the bare number 30 as "30 percent" because the 40 percent beside it was a percentage. It is a headcount: 30 out of 250 books is 12 percent. Choice E is the complement, 0.60 * 250 = 150, the non-paperbacks reported as the paperbacks.

### Q6 · `WRD-PCT-021` — Answer: D

Step 1 — Two percentages, two different bases. The 6 percent sits on her own sales; the 2 percent sits on the team's total.
Step 2 — Check the condition: her own sales are $120,000, which exceeds $100,000, so the bonus is earned.
Step 3 — Commission = 0.06 * 120000 = 7,200.
Step 4 — Bonus = 0.02 * 450000 = 9,000.
Step 5 — Total = 7200 + 9000 = 16,200.
Check: 1% of 120,000 is 1,200 so 6% is 7,200; 1% of 450,000 is 4,500 so 2% is 9,000. The two add to 16,200. Correct.
Answer: D.

**Trap.** Putting the 2 percent bonus on her own sales as well gives 7200 + 0.02 * 120000 = $9,600, choice C — the wrong-base error, and the one the stem is built to punish. Choice A stops at the commission and forgets the bonus; choice B reports the bonus alone; choice E charges both rates against the team total, 0.08 * 450000 = $36,000.

### Q7 · `WRD-PCT-026` — Answer: C

Step 1 — Find the quantity that does not move. Only girls join, so the number of boys is fixed throughout.
Step 2 — Boys = 0.55 * 800 = 440.
Step 3 — Afterwards girls are 50 percent of the school, so boys are the other 50 percent. If T is the new total, 0.50 * T = 440, so T = 880.
Step 4 — Girls who enrolled = 880 - 800 = 80.
Check: girls were 800 - 440 = 360; after 80 join there are 440 girls and 440 boys out of 880, which is exactly 50 percent each. Correct.
Answer: C.

**Trap.** Treating the shift from 45 percent girls to 50 percent girls as "5 percent of 800" gives 40, choice B: percentage points measured against a base that is itself changing. The total grows as the girls arrive, so the 5-point move is not 5 percent of the old total. Choice A is 5 percent of the 440 boys; choice D is the original number of girls. Choice E, 400, is 50 percent of the original 800 — the NEW share applied to the OLD total, which is exactly the shifting base this question is built on.

### Q8 · `WRD-PCT-032` — Answer: A

Step 1 — "25 percent more than Sarah's" attaches the 25 percent to Sarah's salary, so Tom = 1.25 * Sarah.
Step 2 — Sarah / Tom = 1 / 1.25 = 0.80, so Sarah earns 80 percent of Tom's salary.
Step 3 — 80 > 75, so Quantity A is greater.
Step 4 — Concretely, let Sarah earn 100. Then Tom earns 125, and 100/125 = 0.80 = 80 percent.
Check: if Sarah earned 75 percent of Tom, then with Tom at 100 Sarah would be 75, and Tom would be (100 - 75)/75 = 33.3 percent more than Sarah, not 25 percent. Correct.
Answer: A.

**Trap.** Subtracting the 25 straight off 100 gives 75 percent and the answer C. The two comparisons use different bases: Tom is 25 percent above SARAH's salary, while "Sarah as a percent of Tom" is measured against TOM's salary, which is the larger number, so the gap of 25 shrinks to 20 points.

### Q9 · `WRD-PCT-052` — Answer: D

Step 1 — The stem gives two shares, not two headcounts. The number of remote workers is (share) * (total employees), and the total is never stated for either year.
Step 2 — Suppose the company had 1,000 employees in both years. Remote workers go from 150 to 240, an increase of 90/150 = 60 percent, so the columns are equal.
Step 3 — Now suppose the company had 1,000 employees in 2020 and 2,000 in 2021. Remote workers go from 150 to 480, an increase of 330/150 = 220 percent, so Quantity A is greater.
Step 4 — Now suppose the company shrank from 1,000 employees to 500. Remote workers go from 150 to 120, a DECREASE, so Quantity B is greater.
Step 5 — Three legal scenarios give three different orderings, so the relationship cannot be determined.
Check: each scenario respects the stem exactly — 15 percent remote in 2020, 24 percent in 2021 — and nothing in the stem fixes the headcount. Correct.
Answer: D.

**Trap.** Computing (24 - 15)/15 = 60 percent and keying C. That calculation is right only if the workforce is the same size in both years, which the stem never says. A separate error reads the move from 15 percent to 24 percent as "a 9 percent increase" — it is 9 percentage points, and 9 percent would put Quantity B ahead and give B.

### Q10 · `WRD-PCT-031` — Answer: 200

Step 1 — The 75 percent sits on the smartphone owners, not on all respondents. Chain the multipliers onto the survey total N.
Step 2 — Daily social media users = 0.75 * 0.80 * N = 0.60 * N.
Step 3 — 0.60 * N = 120, so N = 120 / 0.60 = 200.
Check: 200 respondents give 0.80 * 200 = 160 smartphone owners, and 0.75 * 160 = 120 daily users. Correct.
Answer: 200.

**Trap.** Dividing by only one rate. 120 / 0.75 = 160 stops at the smartphone owners and reports them as the survey total; 120 / 0.80 = 150 uses the wrong rate entirely. Adding the rates to 155 percent, or averaging them to 77.5 percent, also misses: the two percentages multiply because the second is measured inside the first.

### Q11 · `WRD-PCT-027` — Answer: 90000

Step 1 — The count you are given belongs to the loser, so convert the winner's share into the loser's share first.
Step 2 — Loser's share = 100% - 54% = 46%.
Step 3 — 0.46 * T = 41,400, so T = 41400 / 0.46 = 90,000.
Step 4 — Arithmetic without a calculator: 41,400 / 46 = 900, so 41,400 / 0.46 = 90,000.
Check: 54% of 90,000 = 48,600 for the winner, and 48,600 + 41,400 = 90,000. The winner's margin is 8 percent of the total, or 7,200 votes. Correct.
Answer: 90000.

**Trap.** Dividing by 0.54 instead of 0.46 gives 41400 / 0.54 = 76,666.7, which attaches the loser's vote count to the winner's percentage. A second slip is answering 48,600 — the winner's votes — instead of the total.

### Q12 · `WRD-PCT-053` — Answer: B, C, E

Step 1 — Let the income be 100, the standard move when a problem is entirely percentages.
Rent = 40. What remains = 100 - 40 = 60.
Food = 30% of the REMAINDER = 0.30 * 60 = 18.
Left over = 100 - 40 - 18 = 42.
A — FALSE. 40 + 18 = 58 percent, not 70 percent. The 30 percent is measured against the 60 that remains, not against the income.
B — TRUE. 0.30 * 0.60 = 0.18, so food is 18 percent of income.
C — TRUE. Twice the food spend is 2 * 18 = 36, and rent is 40, which is more than 36.
D — FALSE. Every line is a fixed fraction of income, so the left-over amount is 0.42 * income and rises by the same 10 percent.
E — TRUE. Left over = 0.60 * 0.70 = 0.42, or 42 percent.
Check: 40 + 18 + 42 = 100, so the three pieces account for the whole income. Correct.
Answer: B, C and E.
