**Lecture capsule:** [01-translating-word-problems.md](../../04-word-problems/01-translating-word-problems.md)

## Part C — Worked Solutions

### Q1 · `ALG-WPM-005` — Answer: A

Step 1 — name the unknown. k = the number of marbles Kevin has. Lisa's count, 11, is already known, so it is a constant, not a variable.
Step 2 — translate one phrase at a time. 'Kevin has 4 more marbles than Lisa' means Kevin's count is Lisa's count increased by 4, so k = 11 + 4.
Step 3 — check the direction. Kevin is the one with more, so the 4 must be ADDED to Lisa's 11, and the sum must sit on Kevin's side of the equals sign.
Check: A gives k = 15, and 15 is indeed 4 more than 11. Correct.
Answer: A.

**Trap.** Choice B, k + 4 = 11, adds the 4 to Kevin instead of to Lisa; it gives k = 7, which makes Kevin the one with FEWER marbles. Choice C reverses the subtraction order into 4 - 11 = -7, the classic 'less than' order error applied to the wrong operation. Choice D reads '4 more' as '4 times', giving 44, and choice E makes that same substitution and then reverses who has more, giving k = 11/4.

### Q2 · `ALG-WPM-012` — Answer: B

Step 1 — name the unknown. Let n = the smaller integer. Consecutive EVEN integers differ by 2, so the two integers are n and n + 2.
Step 2 — translate 'the sum is 54'.
n + (n + 2) = 54
2n + 2 = 54
Step 3 — solve.
2n = 52
n = 26
Step 4 — answer what was asked. The question wants the SMALLER integer, which is n = 26.
Check: the two integers are 26 and 28, both even, consecutive even, and 26 + 28 = 54. Correct.
Answer: B.

**Trap.** Writing the pair as n and n + 1 gives 2n + 1 = 54 and n = 26.5, and a student who rounds it reports 27, choice C - but 27 is not even. Choice D, 28, is the larger integer rather than the smaller one, and choice E, 52, is the value of 2n reported before the final division by 2. Choice A comes from taking the average 54/2 = 27 and then subtracting the whole gap of 2 instead of half of it.

### Q3 · `ALG-WPM-011` — Answer: 4

Step 1 — translate each purchase into a cost. The apples cost 2x dollars and the oranges cost 3(x + 1) dollars.
Step 2 — translate 'spending $23 in total' as part + part = whole.
2x + 3(x + 1) = 23
Step 3 — expand and solve. The parentheses matter: the 3 multiplies the whole count x + 1.
2x + 3x + 3 = 23
5x + 3 = 23
5x = 20
x = 4
Check: 4 apples cost $8 and 5 oranges cost $15, and 8 + 15 = $23. Correct.
Answer: 4.

**Trap.** Dropping the parentheses and writing 2x + 3x + 1 = 23 gives 5x = 22 and x = 4.4, a non-integer that should immediately signal the slip. The 3 is a price per orange, so it must multiply the entire orange count x + 1, not just the x.

### Q4 · `ALG-WPM-008` — Answer: C

Step 1 — translate 'is 30 years older than'. Father's age now = son's age now + 30 = 10 + 30 = 40.
Step 2 — translate 'in 5 years'. Five years from now the father will be 40 + 5 = 45.
Step 3 — compare. Column A = 45 and Column B = 45, so the two quantities are equal.
Check: in 5 years the son is 15 and the father is 45, and 45 - 15 = 30, the stated gap. Correct.
Answer: C.

**Trap.** Reading '30 years older' backwards gives a father of 10 - 30 = -20 now, or 30 - 10 = 20 now and 25 in five years, both pointing to choice B. A second route to B is adding the 5 years to the son instead of the father, getting a father of 40 against Column B's 45.

### Q5 · `WRD-MST-051` — Answer: A, C, E

A - '5 less than n' takes 5 away from n, so it is n - 5. The words come in the reverse of the algebra: the 5 is spoken first but subtracted second. TRUE.
B - 'n less than 5' takes n away from 5, so it is 5 - n, not n - 5. FALSE.
C - 'n reduced by 5' starts at n and decreases it by 5, so it is n - 5. TRUE.
D - 'the quotient of n and 5' is a division, n/5, not a subtraction. FALSE.
E - if n exceeds 5, the amount of the excess is n - 5. TRUE.
Check with n = 12: A gives 7, B gives -7, C gives 7, D gives 2.4, E gives 7. Exactly A, C and E return 12 - 5 = 7. Correct.
Answer: A, C and E.

**Trap.** Selecting B because the words 'n' and '5' appear in that order. 'Less than' always reverses the order of the two quantities, so B is 5 - n. Choice D fails for a different reason entirely: 'quotient' is the division keyword, so nothing about D is a subtraction.

### Q6 · `ALG-WPM-016` — Answer: C

Step 1 — name the unknown. Let h = the number of hours of work.
Step 2 — translate each charge. The flat fee is paid once, not per hour.
Plumber: 50 + 40h
Electrician: 60h
Step 3 — translate 'charge the same total amount' as one equation.
50 + 40h = 60h
50 = 20h
h = 2.5
Check: plumber 50 + 40(2.5) = 50 + 100 = $150; electrician 60(2.5) = $150. Equal. Correct.
Answer: C.

**Trap.** Choice A, 0.5, comes from dividing 50 by the SUM of the two hourly rates, 40 + 60 = 100, instead of by their difference. Choice B, 1.25, comes from solving 40h = 50, ignoring the electrician entirely. Choice E, 5, comes from dividing the flat fee by 60 - 50 = 10, differencing a fee and a rate instead of the two rates. Choice D, 3, is where a student lands who insists the answer must be a whole number of hours and rounds 2.5 up; hours are a continuous quantity here, so 2.5 is a legitimate answer.

### Q7 · `ALG-WPM-020` — Answer: A

Step 1 — name the unknowns. Let q = the number of quarters and d = the number of dimes.
Step 2 — translate the two sentences into two equations, working in cents so the units match.
q + d = 20 (a count of coins)
25q + 10d = 380 (a value in cents)
Step 3 — substitute d = 20 - q.
25q + 10(20 - q) = 380
25q + 200 - 10q = 380
15q = 180
q = 12
Step 4 — answer what was asked. The question wants DIMES: d = 20 - 12 = 8.
Check: 12 quarters = 300 cents, 8 dimes = 80 cents, total 380 cents = $3.80, and 12 + 8 = 20 coins. Correct.
Answer: A.

**Trap.** Choice C, 12, is the number of quarters - the value you solve for first, not the one the question asks for. Swapping the two coin values into 10q + 25d = 380 also produces 12 and lands on C. Choice B, 10, assumes the 20 coins split evenly between the two kinds, using neither the $3.80 nor any equation; choice D, 15, comes from treating the whole $3.80 as quarters, 380/25 = 15.2, and truncating to 15 coins; and choice E is the total number of coins reported by mistake.

### Q8 · `ALG-WPM-022` — Answer: C

Step 1 — name the unknown. Let n = the smallest integer. Consecutive ODD integers differ by 2, so the four are n, n + 2, n + 4 and n + 6.
Step 2 — translate 'the sum is 80'.
n + (n + 2) + (n + 4) + (n + 6) = 80
4n + 12 = 80
Step 3 — solve.
4n = 68
n = 17
Step 4 — list and answer what was asked. The integers are 17, 19, 21, 23. The second-largest is 21.
Check: 17 + 19 + 21 + 23 = 80, and all four are odd and consecutive odd. Correct.
Answer: C.

**Trap.** Choice B, 19, is where the n, n + 1, n + 2, n + 3 setup leads: it gives 4n + 6 = 80, so n = 18.5, and a student who rounds to 19 reports the second term of the wrong list. Choice A, 17, is n itself, choice D, 23, is the largest rather than the second-largest, and choice E, 25, is one term further along the list than the largest.

### Q9 · `ALG-WPM-015` — Answer: 63

Step 1 — name the SMALLER part as the variable, so that no fractions appear. Let x = the smaller part; then the larger part is 3x.
Step 2 — translate 'the sum of the two parts is 84' as part + part = whole.
x + 3x = 84
4x = 84
x = 21
Step 3 — answer what was asked. The question wants the LARGER part: 3x = 3 * 21 = 63.
Check: 21 + 63 = 84, and 63 = 3 * 21. Correct.
Answer: 63.

**Trap.** Reporting 21, the value of x. The variable was deliberately attached to the smaller part to keep the algebra clean, so x is not the answer here. Naming the larger part x instead gives x + x/3 = 84 and the same 63, but with a fraction to clear - which is why the capsule says to let x be the simplest quantity.

### Q10 · `ALG-WPM-024` — Answer: 480

Step 1 — name the unknowns. Let a = the larger integer and b = the smaller, so a > b.
Step 2 — translate both sentences.
a - b = 14
a + b = 46
Step 3 — add the two equations; the b terms cancel.
2a = 60, so a = 30
Then b = 46 - 30 = 16.
Step 4 — answer what was asked: the PRODUCT, not either integer.
30 * 16 = 480
Check: 30 - 16 = 14 and 30 + 16 = 46, and 30 * 16 = 480. Correct.
Answer: 480.

**Trap.** Reporting 30 or 16, the integers themselves, instead of their product; or writing b - a = 14, which gives a = 16 and b = 30 and contradicts 'a is the larger'. The sum-and-difference pair is fastest solved by adding the equations: the larger is always (sum + difference)/2 = (46 + 14)/2 = 30.

### Q11 · `ALG-WPM-023` — Answer: C

Step 1 — name the unknown around the MIDDLE term, which is the efficient choice here. Let the three integers be m - 1, m and m + 1.
Step 2 — translate 'have a sum of 3k'.
(m - 1) + m + (m + 1) = 3m = 3k
Step 3 — solve. The -1 and +1 cancel, so 3m = 3k gives m = k exactly, for every allowed k.
Column A = m = k = Column B, so the two quantities are equal.
Check with k = 5: the sum is 15, and the three integers are 4, 5, 6, whose middle term is 5 = k. Try k = 100: the sum is 300 and the integers are 99, 100, 101, middle 100 = k. Correct.
Answer: C.

**Trap.** Choosing D on the grounds that the actual integers are never named. They do not need to be: the sum of three consecutive integers is always 3 times the middle one, so the middle term is pinned to k no matter which k is used. Setting the integers up as n, n + 1, n + 2 instead gives 3n + 3 = 3k, so n = k - 1; a student who then compares n rather than the middle term n + 1 wrongly concludes that Column B is greater.

### Q12 · `WRD-MST-052` — Answer: D

Step 1 — translate '5 less than 3 times m'. Three times m is 3m, and 5 less than that is 3m - 5. So n = 3m - 5.
Step 2 — compare the two columns as expressions in m.
Column A - Column B = (3m - 5) - (2m + 1) = m - 6
Step 3 — test the sign of m - 6 against the only condition given, m > 4.
If m = 5: Column A = 10 and Column B = 11, so Column B is greater.
If m = 7: Column A = 16 and Column B = 15, so Column A is greater.
Step 4 — the comparison changes direction inside the allowed range, so it cannot be determined.
Check: the two columns are equal exactly at m = 6, which m > 4 permits, and m may lie on either side of 6. Correct.
Answer: D.

**Trap.** Reading '5 less than 3 times m' as 5 - 3m makes Column A negative while Column B is at least 9, which points confidently at B. The phrase 'less than' reverses the order: the quantity being reduced, 3m, comes first. Students who translate correctly but then test only m = 5 also stop at B without checking whether m - 6 can turn positive.

### Q13 · `ALG-WPM-021` — Answer: B, C, D

Step 1 — translate the revenue sentence. With a adult tickets and s student tickets, 12a + 8s = 1560.
Step 2 — solve for s so each candidate can be tested. s = (1560 - 12a)/8 = 195 - 1.5a.
Step 3 — state the two hidden conditions. Tickets are counted, so s must be a whole number, and 'at least one of each type' forces s >= 1 and a >= 1.
A - a = 25: s = 195 - 37.5 = 157.5, not a whole number of tickets. FALSE.
B - a = 30: s = 195 - 45 = 150, a whole number and at least 1. TRUE.
C - a = 60: s = 195 - 90 = 105. TRUE.
D - a = 100: s = 195 - 150 = 45. TRUE.
E - a = 130: s = 195 - 195 = 0, but at least one student ticket was sold. FALSE.
Check: 12(30) + 8(150) = 360 + 1200 = 1560; 12(60) + 8(105) = 720 + 840 = 1560; 12(100) + 8(45) = 1200 + 360 = 1560. Correct.
Answer: B, C and D.

**Trap.** Choice A survives only if the whole-number condition is ignored: 25 adult tickets leaves $1,260, which is not divisible by 8, so half a student ticket would have to be sold. Because s = 195 - 1.5a, a must be even. Choice E fails for a different reason - it is even and gives a whole number, but it uses up the entire $1,560 on adults and leaves no student ticket at all, which the stem forbids.

### Q14 · `ALG-WPM-004` — Answer: E

Step 1 — build the distance model first. 60 is miles per HOUR, so it multiplies the time t; 45 is a distance already covered, so it is added once and never multiplied by t. After t hours the car is d = 60t + 45 miles from the town.
Step 2 — translate the target distance. 'When the timer started' means t = 0, where d = 45. 5 times as far is 5 * 45 = 225 miles.
Step 3 — set the model equal to the target distance, not to a rescaled rate.
