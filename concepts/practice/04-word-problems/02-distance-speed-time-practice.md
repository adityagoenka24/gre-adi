## Part C — Worked Solutions

### Q1 · `WRD-DST-006` — Answer: C

Step 1 — use T = D/S. T = 135/45 = 3 hours.
Step 2 — add the travel time to the departure time. 9:00 AM + 3 hours = 12:00 PM.
Check: in 3 hours at 45 mph the bus covers 45 * 3 = 135 miles, exactly the distance given. Correct.
Answer: C.

**Trap.** Dividing 135 by 45 correctly but then adding only 2 hours (from a sloppy 135/45 = 2 something) gives 11:00 AM, choice A. Choices B and D, 11:30 AM and 12:30 PM, are half-hour slips in the same division, 135/45 read as 2.5 or as 3.5 hours. The other live error is reading 45 mph as 45 miles per trip and guessing 1:00 PM, choice E.

### Q2 · `WRD-DST-014` — Answer: C

Step 1 — the answer is wanted in miles per HOUR, so convert the time first. 30 minutes = 30/60 = 0.5 hour.
Step 2 — S = D/T = 1.5/0.5 = 3 miles per hour.
Check: at 3 mph, half an hour covers 3 * 0.5 = 1.5 miles, exactly the distance to school. Correct.
Answer: C.

**Trap.** Dividing by 30 instead of by 0.5, giving 1.5/30 = 0.05, choice A. Choice B, 0.75, comes from MULTIPLYING by the half hour instead of dividing: 1.5 * 0.5. Choice D, 4.5, comes from multiplying 1.5 by 3 (mistaking 30 minutes for one third of an hour and then multiplying rather than dividing). Choice E, 45, drops the conversion entirely and multiplies the 1.5 miles by the 30 minutes.

### Q3 · `WRD-DST-015` — Answer: C

Step 1 — time for Train P: T = D/S = 300/75 = 4 hours.
Step 2 — time for Train Q: T = D/S = 240/60 = 4 hours.
Step 3 — 4 = 4, so the two times are equal.
Check: 75 * 4 = 300 and 60 * 4 = 240, both matching the stated distances. Correct.
Answer: C.

**Trap.** Comparing the distances instead of the times: 300 > 240, so Column A looks greater, choice A. Train P is also faster, which reinforces the wrong instinct; the larger distance and the larger speed cancel exactly.

### Q4 · `WRD-DST-013` — Answer: 18

Step 1 — downstream the current pushes the boat, so the two rates add: downstream speed = still-water speed + current.
Step 2 — 15 + 3 = 18 miles per hour.
Check: going back upstream the boat would manage 15 - 3 = 12 mph, slower than in still water, which is what fighting a current should do. The downstream figure 18 is correspondingly faster. Correct.
Answer: 18.

**Trap.** Subtracting instead of adding and answering 12, which is the upstream speed. The other live error is answering 15, treating the still-water speed as the actual speed and ignoring the current entirely.

### Q5 · `WRD-DST-010` — Answer: A, D, E

Step 1 — leg 1: T = 60/30 = 2 hours. Leg 2: T = 60/60 = 1 hour.
Step 2 — total distance = 60 + 60 = 120 miles. Total time = 2 + 1 = 3 hours.
Step 3 — average speed = total distance / total time = 120/3 = 40 mph.
A. Total distance 120 miles. TRUE.
B. Total time is 3 hours, not 2.5. FALSE - 2.5 comes from reading the first leg as 1.5 hours.
C. Average speed 45 mph. FALSE - 45 is (30 + 60)/2, the average of the speeds, which is not the average speed here.
D. Average speed 40 mph. TRUE.
E. The second leg takes 1 hour out of the 3, which is one third. TRUE.
Check: the driver spends 2 of the 3 hours at the slower speed, so the average must land below the midpoint 45 - and 40 does. Correct.
Answer: A, D and E.

**Trap.** Selecting C because (30 + 60)/2 = 45. The two legs are equal in DISTANCE, not in time, so averaging the speeds overstates the result; the true figure 40 is option D, and C and D cannot both be selected. Selecting B comes from not dividing at all and splitting some rounded total; the legs take 2 hours and 1 hour, so the clock reads 3 hours, and that same split is what makes E true.

### Q6 · `WRD-DST-018` — Answer: B

Step 1 — they approach each other, so the gap closes at the sum of the speeds: 15 + 20 = 35 mph.
Step 2 — time to meet = gap / closing speed = 70/35 = 2 hours.
Step 3 — distance from A's start = A's own speed * time = 15 * 2 = 30 miles.
Check: B rides 20 * 2 = 40 miles, and 30 + 40 = 70 miles, the whole trail. Correct.
Answer: B.

**Trap.** Answering 40, choice D, by computing B's distance instead of A's - the faster rider covers more, so the meeting point is nearer A. Choice C, 35, comes from halving the trail as if the two speeds were equal. Choices A and E, 28 and 42, split the 70 miles in the ratio 2:3 - the speeds 15 and 20 mis-reduced to 2:3 instead of the correct 3:4 (28 takes that wrong split one way round, 42 the other).

### Q7 · `WRD-DST-021` — Answer: C

Step 1 — passing a pole means the train covers its own length. S = 200/10 = 20 meters per second.
Step 2 — passing a platform means the train covers platform length plus train length: 300 + 200 = 500 meters.
Step 3 — T = 500/20 = 25 seconds.
Check: 20 m/s for 25 s is 500 m, which is exactly the platform plus the train. Correct.
Answer: C.

**Trap.** Using only the platform length: 300/20 = 15 seconds, choice A. The train is not past the platform until its rear end clears it, so its own 200 m must be added. Choice B, 20, adds a second 10 seconds for the platform without recomputing, 10 + 10. Choice D, 30, counts the platform twice instead of adding the train: (300 + 300)/20. Choice E, 35, adds the train's length twice: (500 + 200)/20.

### Q8 · `WRD-DST-025` — Answer: D

Step 1 — both animals move the same way, so the gap closes at the DIFFERENCE of the speeds: 30 - 20 = 10 meters per second.
Step 2 — time to close the gap = 200/10 = 20 seconds.
Check: in 20 s the cheetah runs 30 * 20 = 600 m; the deer runs 20 * 20 = 400 m from a point 200 m ahead, so it is at 400 + 200 = 600 m. Same point. Correct.
Answer: D.

**Trap.** Adding the speeds, 30 + 20 = 50, and answering 200/50 = 4 seconds, choice A - that closing rate belongs to animals running toward each other. Choice B, 6.7, comes from 200/30, dividing by the cheetah's own speed and ignoring that the deer keeps running; choice C, 10, from 200/20, dividing by the deer's speed. Choice E, 40, comes from getting 20 correctly and then doubling it to 'allow for the deer also moving', which double-counts the deer.

### Q9 · `WRD-DST-023` — Answer: C

Step 1 — find the head start. In 2 hours Car A covers 50 * 2 = 100 miles, so the gap when Car B starts is 100 miles.
Step 2 — same direction, so the gap closes at 70 - 50 = 20 mph.
Step 3 — time to catch = 100/20 = 5 hours. Column A = 5.
Step 4 — 5 = 5, so the quantities are equal.
Check: in those 5 hours Car B covers 70 * 5 = 350 miles; Car A has by then been driving 7 hours, covering 50 * 7 = 350 miles. Same point. Correct.
Answer: C.

**Trap.** Forgetting the head start and dividing nothing by 20, or using the head start in HOURS rather than miles: 2/20 = 0.1 hour, which makes Column B look far greater, choice B. The head start must be converted to a distance before it can be closed.

### Q10 · `WRD-DST-033` — Answer: C

Step 1 — the distance is the same for both runners, so speed and time are inversely proportional: S = D/T.
Step 2 — the second runner's time is 0.80 of the first runner's time, so the second runner's speed is 1/0.80 = 1.25 times the first runner's speed.
Step 3 — second runner's speed = 8 * 1.25 = 10 mph. Column A = 10.
Check: pick a distance, say 40 miles. First runner: 40/8 = 5 hours. Second runner: 20 percent less is 4 hours, and 40/4 = 10 mph. Correct.
Answer: C.

**Trap.** Reading '20 percent less time' as '20 percent more speed' and computing 8 * 1.20 = 9.6 mph, which makes Column B greater, choice B. Time down by a factor 0.8 means speed up by a factor 1/0.8 = 1.25, not 1.20.

### Q11 · `WRD-DST-019` — Answer: 2

Step 1 — upstream speed = 48/4 = 12 mph. This equals b - c, where b is the still-water speed and c the current.
Step 2 — downstream speed = 48/3 = 16 mph. This equals b + c.
Step 3 — subtract: (b + c) - (b - c) = 2c, so 16 - 12 = 2c, giving c = 2 mph.
Check: b = (16 + 12)/2 = 14. Upstream 14 - 2 = 12 mph over 48 miles is 4 hours; downstream 14 + 2 = 16 mph over 48 miles is 3 hours. Both match. Correct.
Answer: 2.

**Trap.** Answering 4, the full difference 16 - 12, instead of half of it. The difference between the downstream and upstream speeds is TWO currents, because the current is subtracted once and added once.

### Q12 · `WRD-DST-032` — Answer: 72

Step 1 — the schedule allows 300/60 = 5 hours, so the train is due at B at 2:00 PM.
Step 2 — in its first 2 hours it covers 60 * 2 = 120 km, leaving 300 - 120 = 180 km.
Step 3 — the 30-minute hold burns elapsed time but no distance, so the time left for those 180 km is 5 - 2 - 0.5 = 2.5 hours.
Step 4 — S = D/T = 180/2.5 = 72 km/h.
Check: 120 km in 2 hours, then half an hour standing still, then 180 km in 2.5 hours - that is 300 km in 5 hours, exactly on schedule. Correct.
Answer: 72.

**Trap.** Answering 90, which is 180/2 - the halt is left out of the remaining time even though it is the whole reason a new speed is needed. Answering 60 is the scheduled speed, which can no longer work once half an hour has been lost. Answering 120, from 300/2.5, uses the full route distance against the time left for only the last part of it.

### Q13 · `WRD-DST-024` — Answer: A, B, E

Step 3 — 9:00 AM + 2 hours = 11:00 AM, and the car is then 50 + 50 * 2 = 150 km from X, the bus 75 * 2 = 150 km from Y.

B. 150 km from each town is the halfway point. TRUE.
C. The car left at 8:00 AM, so at 11:00 AM it has been driving 3 hours, not 2. FALSE - 2 hours is the BUS's driving time.

E. With both leaving at 8:00 AM they meet after 300/125 = 2.4 hours, with the car 50 * 2.4 = 120 km along - and 150 - 120 = 30 km nearer to X. TRUE.
Check: 150 + 150 = 300 km, the whole road, and the head start pushes the meeting point away from X, which is why removing it moves the point back toward X. Correct.

Selecting D by comparing only the running times 6 hours and 4 hours without shifting the bus's start by an hour. The other live error is dividing the full 300 km by 125 from the start, which ignores the 50 km the car has already covered and puts the meeting at 10:24 AM.

### Q14 · `WRD-DST-039` — Answer: B
