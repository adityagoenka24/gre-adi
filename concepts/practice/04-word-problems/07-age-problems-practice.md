# Age Problems — Practice Set

**Bank code:** `WRD-AGE` · **Section:** Word Problems · **30 questions** — Easy 5 · Medium 8 · Hard 12 · Extreme 5

**Lecture capsule:** [07-age-problems.md](../../04-word-problems/07-age-problems.md)

> Everyone ages at the same rate, so the difference between two ages never changes while the ratio does, and every age problem is built on exactly that contrast.

Every question below is live in the question bank under the ID shown — the sheet and the portal are the same questions. Attempt a level with the clock running, then check Part C.

## Part C — Worked Solutions

### Q1 · `WRD-AGE-001` — Answer: C

Step 1 — let Rohan be r. Then Maya is r + 8.
Step 2 — the sum condition gives r + (r + 8) = 34, so 2r + 8 = 34 and 2r = 26, r = 13.
Step 3 — Maya is r + 8 = 13 + 8 = 21.
Faster route from the capsule: with sum S = 34 and difference D = 8, the two ages are (S + D)/2 = (34 + 8)/2 = 21 and (S - D)/2 = (34 - 8)/2 = 13. The older age is the one with the plus sign.
Check: 21 + 13 = 34, and 21 - 13 = 8. Both stated sentences hold.
Answer: C.

**Trap.** Solving correctly and then reporting the younger sibling's age, 13, is choice A: the question names Maya, the older one. Choice B, 17, is half the sum with the 8-year difference ignored entirely. Choice D, 26, is 34 - 8 with the halving step forgotten, and choice E, 42, is 34 + 8 with the same omission.

### Q2 · `WRD-AGE-002` — Answer: B

Step 1 — let the answer be t years. In t years BOTH ages increase by t: Carlos is 24 + t and Sofia is 3 + t.
Step 2 — the condition is 24 + t = 4*(3 + t).
Step 3 — expand: 24 + t = 12 + 4t, so 12 = 3t and t = 4.
Faster route: the age difference is 24 - 3 = 21 and it never changes. When Carlos is 4 times Sofia, the difference is 3 times Sofia's age then, so Sofia is 21/3 = 7 and that is 7 - 3 = 4 years from now.
Check: in 4 years Carlos is 28 and Sofia is 7, and 4*7 = 28. The difference is still 28 - 7 = 21. Correct.
Answer: B.

**Trap.** Adding the t years to Sofia only and leaving Carlos at 24 gives 24 = 4*(3 + t), so 3 + t = 6 and t = 3, which is choice A. Everyone ages together. Choice C, 7, is Sofia's age at the moment the condition holds rather than the number of years until it holds, and choice E, 21, is the constant age difference. Choice D, 18, solves 24 + t = 2*(3 + t), the 'twice as old' condition instead of the stated 'four times'.

### Q3 · `WRD-AGE-004` — Answer: A

Step 1 — let Dev be d. Then Priya is 3d.
Step 2 — the difference is 3d - d = 2d, and that equals 18.
Step 3 — 2d = 18, so d = 9.
Check: Dev is 9 and Priya is 3*9 = 27. Their difference is 27 - 9 = 18, exactly as stated, and 27 is 3 times 9. Both sentences hold.
Answer: A.

**Trap.** Choice C, 27, is Priya's age: the correct work reported for the wrong person. Choice B, 18, hands back the stated difference as if it were Dev's age. Choice E, 54, makes that same mistake and then multiplies: treating 18 as Dev's age gives 3*18 = 54 for Priya. Choice D, 36, treats 18 as Dev's age but applies 'older by 18' instead of '3 times', giving 18 + 18.

### Q4 · `WRD-AGE-007` — Answer: 30

Step 1 — move from the past to the present. Five years ago Elena was 17, so today she is 17 + 5 = 22.
Step 2 — move from the present to the future: 22 + 8 = 30.
Faster route: the two stated moments are 5 + 8 = 13 years apart, so the answer is 17 + 13 = 30.
Check: if Elena is 30 in 8 years, she is 22 today, and 5 years before that she was 17, matching the stem. Correct.
Answer: 30.

**Trap.** Adding 8 to the 17 without first returning to the present gives 25, treating 'five years ago' as if it were today. Subtracting instead of adding at the first step gives 12 today and 20 later. The two stated times are 13 years apart, not 8 and not 3.

### Q5 · `WRD-AGE-009` — Answer: C

Step 1 — let Marcus be m today, so Nina is 2m. Today's difference is 2m - m = m.
Step 2 — in 10 years Marcus is m + 10 and Nina is 2m + 10.
Step 3 — that difference is (2m + 10) - (m + 10) = m. The two 10s cancel.
Step 4 — both columns equal m, whatever m is, so they are equal.
Check: take m = 7. Today 14 and 7, difference 7. In 10 years 24 and 17, difference 7. Take m = 30: today 60 and 30, difference 30; in 10 years 70 and 40, difference 30. Equal either way.
Answer: C.

**Trap.** Choice A comes from thinking the gap grows because the older person is older: Nina gains 10 years, so the gap must gain something. It does not, because Marcus gains the same 10. Choice D comes from noticing that Marcus's age m is unknown and concluding nothing can be compared, but the unknown m appears identically in both columns and cancels. The difference between two ages is invariant in time; only the ratio changes.

### Q6 · `WRD-AGE-011` — Answer: A

Step 1 — let Sasha be s today, so Priya is 3s today.
Step 2 — six years ago BOTH were 6 years younger: Sasha was s - 6 and Priya was 3s - 6.
Step 3 — the past condition gives 3s - 6 = 5*(s - 6).
Step 4 — expand: 3s - 6 = 5s - 30, so 24 = 2s and s = 12.
Check: Sasha is 12 and Priya is 36, which is 3 times 12. Six years ago they were 6 and 30, and 30 = 5*6. Both sentences hold.
Answer: A.

**Trap.** Subtracting the 6 from Priya's side only, 3s - 6 = 5s, is impossible, but the mirror slip of subtracting it from Sasha's side only gives 3s = 5*(s - 6), so 3s = 5s - 30 and s = 15, which is choice B. Choice E, 36, is Priya's present age: right work, wrong person. Choice D, 30, is Priya's age six years ago, answering for the past year the condition was stated in. Choice C, 24, is the constant age difference 36 - 12.

### Q7 · `WRD-AGE-013` — Answer: 18

Step 1 — let Jordan be j today, so Mei is 44 - j.
Step 2 — in five years Jordan is j + 5 and Mei is 49 - j.
Step 3 — the condition is 49 - j = 2*(j + 5), so 49 - j = 2j + 10.
Step 4 — 39 = 3j, so j = 13 and Mei is 44 - 13 = 31.
Step 5 — the present difference is 31 - 13 = 18. (The difference is invariant, so this is also the difference five years from now.)
Check: 31 + 13 = 44. In five years they are 36 and 18, and 36 = 2*18. Both sentences hold, and 36 - 18 = 18 confirms the invariance.
Answer: 18.

**Trap.** Applying the 5-year shift to Mei only gives 49 - j = 2j and j = 49/3, a non-integer age that should signal the error. Answering 31 reports Mei's age instead of the difference the question asked for, and answering 13 reports Jordan's. A student who adds 5 to the difference because the question is about five years from now gets 23; the difference between two ages never changes.

### Q8 · `WRD-AGE-015` — Answer: A, B, D

Step 1 — let Ben be b. Then Ana is 2b and Carlos is b + 10.
Step 2 — the sum gives 2b + b + (b + 10) = 90, so 4b + 10 = 90 and 4b = 80, b = 20.
Step 3 — Ben is 20, Ana is 2*20 = 40, Carlos is 20 + 10 = 30.
A. TRUE. 20 is Ben's age.
B. TRUE. 30 is Carlos's age.
C. FALSE. 32 is what you get by reversing 'Ana is twice as old as Ben' into Ben = 2*Ana: then with Ana = a the sum is a + 2a + (2a + 10) = 90, so 5a = 80, a = 16 and Ben = 32. Nobody here is 32.
D. TRUE. 40 is Ana's age.
E. FALSE. 50 comes from attaching 'twice' to Carlos, Ana = 2*Carlos: then 2*(b + 10) + b + (b + 10) = 90, so 4b + 30 = 90, b = 15 and Ana = 50. The same 50 comes from reading 'Carlos is 10 years YOUNGER than Ben': 2b + b + (b - 10) = 90 gives b = 25 and Ana = 50.
Check: 40 + 20 + 30 = 90, Ana's 40 is twice Ben's 20, and Carlos's 30 is 10 more than Ben's 20. All three sentences hold.
Answer: A, B and D.

**Trap.** Both false options are the outputs of specific mis-translations. Flipping the direction of 'Ana is twice as old as Ben' into Ben = 2*Ana makes Ben 32, which is option C. Attaching the 'twice' to Carlos instead, Ana = 2*Carlos, makes Ana 50, which is option E, and so does turning 'Carlos is 10 years older than Ben' into 'younger'. Name the youngest person as the variable and attach every relationship to the exact person the stem names.

### Q9 · `WRD-AGE-020` — Answer: B, C, D

Step 1 — the ratio is k/(k - 12). Require 2 <= k/(k - 12) <= 3, and note k - 12 >= 1 so the denominator is positive and the inequalities can be cross-multiplied without flipping.
Step 2 — the left inequality: k >= 2*(k - 12) = 2k - 24, so 24 >= k, that is k <= 24.
Step 3 — the right inequality: k <= 3*(k - 12) = 3k - 36, so 36 <= 2k, that is k >= 18.
Step 4 — the condition is 18 <= k <= 24.
A. FALSE. k = 16 gives 16/4 = 4, which is above 3.
B. TRUE. k = 18 gives 18/6 = 3 exactly, and 'inclusive' admits it.
C. TRUE. k = 21 gives 21/9 = 7/3, about 2.33.
D. TRUE. k = 24 gives 24/12 = 2 exactly, admitted by 'inclusive'.
E. FALSE. k = 26 gives 26/14 = 13/7, about 1.86, below 2.
Check: the ratio falls as k grows, from 4 at k = 16 down to 1.86 at k = 26, so the admissible values form one unbroken block, 18 through 24, and the two endpoints are exactly the values that hit 3 and 2. Correct.
Answer: B, C and D.

**Trap.** Dropping the endpoints because the word 'between' feels strict rejects B and D and leaves only C; the stem says 'inclusive', so both boundary values count. The other live error is expecting the ratio to rise with k: the gap of 12 years is fixed, so as both ages grow the ratio falls toward 1, which is why the small value k = 16 fails at the top end and the large value k = 26 fails at the bottom.

### Q10 · `WRD-AGE-023` — Answer: C

Step 1 — let Marcus be m, so Sasha is m + 4.
Step 2 — the product condition gives m*(m + 4) = 192, that is m^2 + 4m - 192 = 0.
Step 3 — factor: 192 = 16*12 and 16 - 12 = 4, so (m + 16)*(m - 12) = 0 and m = 12 (the root m = -16 is rejected as an age).
Step 4 — Sasha is m + 4 = 16.
Faster route: look for two factors of 192 that differ by 4. 192 = 12*16, and 16 - 12 = 4. The older age is 16.
Check: 16*12 = 192 and 16 - 12 = 4. Both sentences hold.
Answer: C.

**Trap.** Choice A, 12, is Marcus's age: the factor pair is found and then the younger member reported. Choice B, 14, assumes the ages are close to equal and takes sqrt(192), about 13.9, rounded to 14; 14*14 = 196, not 192, and 14 has no partner 4 away. Choice D, 20, adds the 4 to the correct answer a second time, as if 16 were Marcus. Choice E, 48, divides the product by the difference, 192/4, an operation with no meaning here.

### Q11 · `WRD-AGE-024` — Answer: 3

Step 1 — let it be t years ago. Both ages were t years smaller: Priya was 35 - t and Dev was 11 - t.
Step 2 — the condition is 35 - t = 4*(11 - t).
Step 3 — expand: 35 - t = 44 - 4t, so 3t = 9 and t = 3.
Faster route: the difference 35 - 11 = 24 is fixed. When Priya is 4 times Dev, the difference is 3 times Dev's age then, so Dev was 24/3 = 8, which was 11 - 8 = 3 years ago.
Check: three years ago Priya was 32 and Dev was 8, and 4*8 = 32. The difference is still 32 - 8 = 24. Correct.
Answer: 3.

**Trap.** Subtracting the t from Dev only, 35 = 4*(11 - t), gives 11 - t = 8.75 and t = 2.25, a fractional answer produced purely by ageing one person. Answering 8 reports Dev's age at that past moment rather than how many years ago it was, and answering 24 reports the invariant age difference.

### Q12 · `WRD-AGE-025` — Answer: A

Step 1 — let Jordan be j, so Mei is 56 - j.
Step 2 — 'Mei is strictly older' means 56 - j > j, so 56 > 2j and j < 28.
Step 3 — then Mei = 56 - j > 56 - 28 = 28.
Step 4 — Quantity A exceeds 28 for every admissible pair, so Quantity A is greater.
Check: try Jordan 27, Mei 29: sum 56 and Mei older, and 29 > 28. Try Jordan 6, Mei 50: sum 56 and Mei older, and 50 > 28. The only way Mei could be 28 is Jordan 28 as well, which the word 'strictly' forbids.
Answer: A.

**Trap.** Choice C is the trap: 28 is half of 56, and the equal split is exactly the one case the word 'strictly' rules out. Choice D comes from noting that the individual ages are not determined, but the comparison is determined even though the ages are not: in the larger half of any unequal split of 56, the larger part is always more than 28.

### Q13 · `WRD-AGE-026` — Answer: A, C, D

Step 1 — name the youngest as the variable. Let Carla be c. Then Beto is c + 3 and Ana is c + 7.
Step 2 — the sum gives c + (c + 3) + (c + 7) = 40, so 3c + 10 = 40 and c = 10.
Step 3 — Carla 10, Beto 13, Ana 17.
A. TRUE. Carla is 10.
B. FALSE. The average of Ana's 17 and Carla's 10 is 27/2 = 13.5, not Beto's 13. The middle sibling is the average of the outer two only when the two gaps are equal, and here they are 4 and 3.
C. TRUE. Seven years ago each of the three was 7 years younger, so the sum was 40 - 3*7 = 40 - 21 = 19.
D. TRUE. 17/10 = 1.7, so Ana is 70 percent older than Carla, and 70 > 40.
E. FALSE. Ana is 17 - 10 = 7 years older than Carla and always will be. In ten years they are 27 and 20, still 7 apart. The 17 comes from adding the ten years to the seven-year gap.
Check: 10 + 13 + 17 = 40, Ana's 17 is 4 more than Beto's 13, and Beto's 13 is 3 more than Carla's 10. All three sentences hold.
Answer: A, C and D.

**Trap.** Option E is the signature error of the topic dressed as a statement: the gap between two ages is invariant, so adding the ten years to it is simply wrong, yet 17 is exactly the number a student who does that produces.
