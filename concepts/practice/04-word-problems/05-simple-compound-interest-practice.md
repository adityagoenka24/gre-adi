# Simple & Compound Interest — Practice Set

**Bank code:** `WRD-INT` · **Section:** Word Problems · **30 questions** — Easy 5 · Medium 13 · Hard 12

**Lecture capsule:** [05-simple-compound-interest.md](../../04-word-problems/05-simple-compound-interest.md)

> Simple interest grows on the principal while compound interest grows on the balance, so every question turns on which base the rate is applied to and how often.

Every question below is live in the question bank under the ID shown — the sheet and the portal are the same questions. Attempt a level with the clock running, then check Part C.

## Part C — Worked Solutions

### Q1 · `WRD-INT-001` — Answer: A

Step 1 — simple interest is computed on the original principal every year, so SI = P*r*t.
Step 2 — write the rate as a decimal: 6 percent = 0.06.
Step 3 — SI = 500 * 0.06 * 3 = 30 * 3 = 90.
Check: one year of interest is 6 percent of 500 = 30, and three identical years give 30 + 30 + 30 = 90. The balance would be 500 + 90 = 590, so 90 is the interest and 590 is the amount. Correct.
Answer: A.

**Trap.** Choice D, $590, reports the balance after 3 years instead of the interest earned; the question asks for I = A - P. Choice C, $530, is the balance after one year only, making the same mistake and stopping early. Choice B, $95.51, compounds a stem that never says 'compounded': 500*(1.06)^3 - 500 = 95.51. Choice E, $9,000, leaves the rate as the whole number 6 and computes 500 * 6 * 3.

### Q2 · `WRD-INT-003` — Answer: 80

Step 1 — simple interest ignores the running balance entirely: SI = P*r*t.
Step 2 — P = 800, r = 0.04, t = 2.5.
Step 3 — one year of interest is 800 * 0.04 = 32.
Step 4 — SI = 32 * 2.5 = 80.
Check: two full years give 64 and the half year gives 16, and 64 + 16 = 80. Correct.
Answer: 80.

**Trap.** Rounding 2.5 years down to 2 gives 64, and rounding up to 3 gives 96; simple interest is exactly proportional to time, so a half year earns exactly half a year's interest. Entering 880 reports the amount owed rather than the interest.

### Q3 · `WRD-INT-004` — Answer: D

Step 1 — compounded annually means each year multiplies the balance by 1 + r = 1.10.
Step 2 — A = 1200 * (1.10)^3 = 1200 * 1.331 = 1597.20.
Step 3 — year by year: 1200 -> 1200 + 120 = 1320 -> 1320 + 132 = 1452 -> 1452 + 145.20 = 1597.20. Each year's interest is larger than the last because it is computed on the new balance.
Check: the interest earned is 1597.20 - 1200 = 397.20, which is 37.20 more than the 3*120 = 360 of simple interest, and every dollar of that 37.20 is interest earned on earlier interest. Correct.
Answer: D.

**Trap.** Choice B, $397.20, is the interest earned rather than the total amount; confusing I with A is the single most common way a correct computation fails here. Choice C, $1,560, uses simple interest and charges all three years of 10 percent on the original 1,200. Choice A, $360, makes both errors at once. Choice E, $1,756.92, is 1200*(1.10)^4, one compounding period too many.

### Q4 · `WRD-INT-011` — Answer: C

Step 1 — Quantity A: simple interest gives A = P(1 + r*t) = 1000 * (1 + 0.10*1) = 1100.
Step 2 — Quantity B: annual compounding gives A = P(1 + r)^t = 1000 * (1.10)^1 = 1100.
Step 3 — the structural reason: compounding only helps once interest has been added to the balance, and after exactly one compounding period no interest has yet been re-invested. Simple and compound agree at t = 1 and diverge only afterwards.
Check: both accounts pay 10 percent of 1,000 = 100 for the single year, so both hold 1,100. Correct.
Answer: C.

**Trap.** Assuming compound always beats simple and answering A or B without checking the number of periods. Compound strictly exceeds simple only for t greater than one compounding period; at exactly one period they are equal. Answering D on the grounds that 'compounding frequency is not stated' also fails: the stem says compounded annually, and one year is one period.

### Q5 · `WRD-INT-012` — Answer: 1000

Step 1 — SI = P*r*t, so P = SI/(r*t).
Step 2 — r*t = 0.06 * 4 = 0.24, so the account earns 24 percent of its principal over the four years.
Step 3 — P = 240/0.24 = 1000.
Check: 1000 * 0.06 = 60 per year, and 60 * 4 = 240. Correct.
Answer: 1000.

**Trap.** Dividing by the rate alone, 240/0.06 = 4,000, forgets the four years. Dividing by 4 alone, 240/4 = 60, returns one year's interest rather than the principal. Entering 1,240 reports the final amount instead of the principal.

### Q6 · `WRD-INT-014` — Answer: E

Step 1 — semi-annual compounding means the rate per period is r/n = 0.05/2 = 0.025 and the number of periods is n*t = 2*2 = 4.
Step 2 — A = 4000 * (1.025)^4.
Step 3 — (1.025)^2 = 1.050625, so (1.025)^4 = (1.050625)^2 = 1.103812890625.
Step 4 — A = 4000 * 1.103812890625 = 4415.25 to the nearest cent.
Check: the interest is 4415.25 - 4000 = 415.25, slightly more than the 410.00 that annual compounding would give and more than the 400 of simple interest, which is what extra compounding periods should do. Correct.
Answer: E.

**Trap.** Choice B, $415.25, is the interest earned, not the amount; the stem asks for the total in the account. Choice D, $4,410.00, compounds annually, using (1.05)^2 and ignoring the word semi-annually. Choice C, $4,400.00, uses simple interest. Choice A, $410.00, is the interest under annual compounding, making both mistakes at once.

### Q7 · `WRD-INT-015` — Answer: B

Step 1 — Quantity A: A = 2000 * (1 + 0.08*2) = 2000 * 1.16 = 2320.
Step 2 — Quantity B: A = 2000 * (1.08)^2 = 2000 * 1.1664 = 2332.80.
Step 3 — the structural route needs no multiplication of the whole amount: over exactly 2 years the compound account beats the simple account by P*r^2 = 2000 * (0.08)^2 = 2000 * 0.0064 = 12.80, and 12.80 is positive, so Quantity B is greater.
Check: 2320 + 12.80 = 2332.80, matching Step 2. Correct.
Answer: B.

**Trap.** Answering C by recalling that simple and compound agree for one period and failing to notice that t = 2 here; the accounts are equal only after the first period. Answering D because 'the compounding frequency of the simple account is unstated' misreads the setup: simple interest has no compounding at all, so both columns are fully determined.

### Q8 · `WRD-INT-019` — Answer: 6

Step 1 — the interest earned is I = A - P = 1040 - 800 = 240. Subtract the principal first; the 1,040 is an amount, not an interest.
Step 2 — one year of simple interest is 800 * 0.05 = 40, the same every year.
Step 3 — t = 240/40 = 6.
Check: 800 + 6*40 = 800 + 240 = 1040, exactly the stated balance. Correct.
Answer: 6.

**Trap.** Using the amount instead of the interest, 1040/40 = 26, is the amount-versus-interest error in its purest form. Dividing the amount by the principal and reading the quotient as years, 1040/800 = 1.3, gives 1.3 years. Both skip Step 1.

### Q9 · `WRD-INT-024` — Answer: A

Step 1 — Quantity A: monthly means rate per period 0.12/12 = 0.01 and 12 periods, so A = 10000 * (1.01)^12 = 10000 * 1.126825 = 11268.25.
Step 2 — Quantity B: quarterly means rate per period 0.12/4 = 0.03 and 4 periods, so A = 10000 * (1.03)^4 = 10000 * 1.12550881 = 11255.09.
Step 3 — the structural route: for a fixed nominal rate, every extra split of the year puts interest to work sooner, so the amount strictly increases with the number of compounding periods. Monthly has 12 periods against quarterly's 4, so Quantity A must be larger without computing either power.
Check: 11268.25 is greater than 11255.09, and both exceed the 11,200 that simple interest would give. Correct.
Answer: A.

**Trap.** Answering C because 'the nominal rate is 12 percent in both columns' treats the stated annual rate as the effective rate; the nominal rate is the same but the effective rates, 12.68 percent and 12.55 percent, are not. Multiplying the rate by the number of periods instead of dividing, 12 * 12 = 144 percent for Quantity A, produces an absurd balance and should be caught by inspection.

### Q10 · `WRD-INT-026` — Answer: B, D, E

A. False. CI = P[(1 + r)^t - 1] is not proportional to r. With P = 100, t = 2 and r = 0.10, CI = 100*(1.21 - 1) = 21; at r = 0.30, CI = 100*(1.69 - 1) = 69, which is more than 3*21 = 63.
B. True. SI = P*r*t is directly proportional to t, so replacing t by 2t replaces SI by 2*SI, for every P and r.
C. False. After exactly one compounding period no interest has yet been added to the balance, so CI = P*r = SI. They are equal, not unequal.
D. True. CI = P[(1 + r)^2 - 1] = P(2r + r^2) and SI = 2*P*r, so CI - SI = P*r^2 exactly.
E. True. Semi-annual gives P(1 + r/2)^2 = P(1 + r + r^2/4), which exceeds the annual P(1 + r) by P*r^2/4 > 0 since r > 0.
Check with P = 1000 and r = 0.10: SI over 2 years is 200 and over 4 years is 400, confirming B; CI over 2 years is 210, and 210 - 200 = 10 = 1000*(0.10)^2, confirming D; semi-annual for one year gives 1000*(1.05)^2 = 1102.50 against 1100, confirming E.
Answer: B, D and E.

**Trap.** Option A tempts students who remember that simple interest is linear in the rate and assume compound interest is too; the extra r^2 term makes the growth faster than proportional. Option C is the mirror image of the real rule: compound never beats simple within the first compounding period, only after it.

### Q11 · `WRD-INT-029` — Answer: A

Step 1 — over exactly 2 years the gap between compound and simple interest is CI - SI = P*r^2, with r as a decimal.
Step 2 — 4000 * r^2 = 10, so r^2 = 10/4000 = 0.0025.
Step 3 — r = sqrt(0.0025) = 0.05, that is 5 percent.
Check by building both interests: SI = 4000 * 0.05 * 2 = 400. CI = 4000*(1.05)^2 - 4000 = 4410 - 4000 = 410. The gap is 410 - 400 = 10, exactly as stated. Correct.
Answer: A.

**Trap.** Choice B, 10%, comes from misreading the principal as $1,000: 1000*r^2 = 10 gives r^2 = 0.01 and r = 0.10. Choice C, 20%, comes from reading 'exceeds the simple interest by $10' as 'exceeds it by 10 percent', so that 4000*r^2 = 0.10*(4000*r*2) cancels one factor of r and leaves r = 0.20. Choice D, 25%, is the decimal slip in the square root: sqrt(0.0025) is read as 0.25 instead of 0.05. Choice E, 50%, drops two zeros from the principal: r^2 = 10/40 = 0.25, so r = 0.50.

### Q12 · `WRD-INT-031` — Answer: A, D, E

A. True. The rate per period is the nominal rate divided by the number of periods per year: 0.08/4 = 0.02, that is 2 percent per quarter.
B. False. At 16 percent quarterly the rate per quarter is 4 percent, so one year gives (1.04)^4 - 1 = 0.16985856, about 16.99 percent, while double the 8 percent result is 2 * 0.08243216 = 0.16486432, about 16.49 percent. Compound growth is not proportional to the rate.
C. False. After 2 years there are 8 quarters, so the balance is 1000*(1.02)^8 = 1171.66 and the interest is 171.66, not 160. The 160 is what simple interest at 8 percent would give.
D. True. 1000*(1.02)^4 = 1082.43, which exceeds 1,080; splitting the year into quarters must beat the single annual 8 percent.
E. True. The effective annual rate is (1.02)^4 - 1 = 0.08243216, that is 8.243 percent, which lies between 8.2 and 8.3 percent.
Check: 1082.43 - 1000 = 82.43 on a $1,000 deposit is an effective 8.243 percent for the year, which confirms D and E together and is more than the 80 of a single annual 8 percent. Correct.
Answer: A, D and E.

**Trap.** Option C is the signature frequency error in reverse: it takes 8 percent per year times 2 years on the original 1,000 and ignores both compounding and the quarterly split, giving 160 instead of 171.66. Option B catches students who treat compound interest as proportional to the rate the way simple interest is.

### Q13 · `WRD-INT-032` — Answer: D

Step 1 — under simple interest every year adds the same fixed amount, so the growth from year 2 to year 5 is three equal years of interest.
