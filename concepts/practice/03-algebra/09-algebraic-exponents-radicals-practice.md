## Part C — Worked Solutions

### Q1 · `ALG-EXR-007` — Answer: D

Step 1 — the exponent outside the bracket applies to every factor inside: (2x^3)^2 = 2^2 * (x^3)^2.
Step 2 — square the coefficient: 2^2 = 4.
Step 3 — a power raised to a power multiplies the exponents: (x^3)^2 = x^(3*2) = x^6.
Step 4 — put the two pieces back together: 4x^6.
Check: at x = 2 the original is (2 * 2^3)^2 = (2 * 8)^2 = 16^2 = 256, and 4 * 2^6 = 4 * 64 = 256. Correct.
Answer: D.

**Trap.** Leaving the coefficient alone gives 2x^6, choice B; at x = 2 that is 128 rather than 256. Adding the exponents instead of multiplying them gives x^(3+2) = x^5, which is choice C when the coefficient is squared and choice A when it is not. Choice E reads (x^3)^2 as x^(3^2) = x^9, stacking the powers instead of multiplying them.

### Q2 · `ALG-EXR-013` — Answer: E

Step 1 — the rule is x^(-a) = 1/x^a. Read it from right to left: 1/x^5 = x^(-5).
Step 2 — check what the minus sign does. It inverts the base, it does not negate the value, so x^(-5) is positive whenever x is positive.
Check: at x = 2, 1/x^5 = 1/32, and 2^(-5) = 1/2^5 = 1/32. Correct. By contrast -2^5 = -32 and 2^(1/5) is about 1.15, so neither of those matches 1/32.
Answer: E.

**Trap.** Reading the minus sign as a sign on the value gives -x^5, choice B, which at x = 2 is -32 instead of 1/32. Choice D both negates and inverts, giving -1/32. Choice A confuses a negative exponent with a fractional one: x^(1/5) is the fifth root of x, not its reciprocal. Choice C simply drops the reciprocal.

### Q3 · `ALG-EXR-011` — Answer: C

Step 1 — Quantity A multiplies two powers of the same base x, so the product rule applies: keep the base and add the exponents.
Step 2 — add 2/3 and 3/4 over the common denominator 12: 2/3 = 8/12 and 3/4 = 9/12, so 8/12 + 9/12 = 17/12.
Step 3 — Quantity A is therefore x^(17/12), which is exactly Quantity B. Because x > 0, every fractional power here is defined and positive, so the two columns agree for every allowed x.
Check: at x = 4096, Quantity A = 4096^(2/3) * 4096^(3/4) = 256 * 512 = 131072 and Quantity B = 4096^(17/12) = 2^17 = 131072. At x = 1/4096 both columns equal 1/131072. Equal both times. Correct.
Answer: C.

**Trap.** Multiplying the exponents instead of adding them gives (2/3)*(3/4) = 1/2, so Quantity A would read x^(1/2), which is smaller than x^(17/12) for x > 1 and larger for x < 1; that split invites D. Adding the fractions by adding numerators and denominators gives 5/7, again not 17/12. Exponents multiply only for a power of a power, while a product of powers of one base adds them, which is the case here.

### Q4 · `ALG-EXR-008` — Answer: 8

Step 1 — x^(1/3) is the cube root of x, so the equation says the cube root of x is 2.
Step 2 — undo a cube root by cubing both sides: (x^(1/3))^3 = 2^3.
Step 3 — on the left the exponents multiply, (1/3)*3 = 1, leaving x. On the right, 2^3 = 8. So x = 8.
Check: 8^(1/3) = 2 because 2 * 2 * 2 = 8. Correct.
Answer: 8.

**Trap.** Treating the exponent 1/3 as division gives x = 2/3, and multiplying instead gives x = 2 * 3 = 6; neither has a cube root of 2. The denominator of a fractional exponent is a root, and a root is undone by raising to that power, not by multiplying or dividing the other side by it.

### Q5 · `ALG-EXR-021` — Answer: A, C

Step 1 — test each statement against the exponent rules, then confirm with a number.
A — TRUE. A negative exponent means reciprocal: x^(-2) = 1/x^2. At x = 2 both sides are 1/4.
B — FALSE. At x = 2 the left side is 1/4 and the right side is -4. A negative exponent inverts the base; it never changes the sign of the value.
C — TRUE. A power of a power multiplies the exponents: 2 * 3 = 6. At x = 2 the left side is 4^3 = 64 and the right side is 2^6 = 64.
D — FALSE. Multiplying like bases ADDS the exponents, so x^2 * x^3 = x^5, not x^6. At x = 2 the left side is 4 * 8 = 32 while x^6 = 64.
E — FALSE. sqrt(x^2) = |x|. At x = -3 the left side is sqrt(9) = 3 and the right side is -3; a square root is never negative.
Check: every statement was tested at an actual value, and only A and C survived.
Answer: A and C.

**Trap.** Selecting B comes from reading the minus sign in x^(-2) as a sign on the answer rather than an instruction to invert, so 2^(-2) is reported as -4 instead of 1/4. Selecting D comes from adding exponents when a power is raised to a power and multiplying them when two powers are multiplied, which is the rule pair reversed. Selecting E ignores that x may be negative, the error that makes sqrt(x^2) = |x| rather than x.

### Q6 · `ALG-EXR-017` — Answer: B

Step 1 — split the radicand into a perfect-square part and a leftover part: 12 = 4 * 3 and x^5 = x^4 * x.
Step 2 — sqrt(12x^5) = sqrt(4 * x^4) * sqrt(3 * x).
Step 3 — take the square root of the perfect square: sqrt(4) = 2 and sqrt(x^4) = x^2. Since the stem says x > 0, no absolute value is needed.
Step 4 — the leftover stays under the radical: the result is 2x^2*sqrt(3x).
Check: at x = 3, 12x^5 = 12 * 243 = 2916 and sqrt(2916) = 54. The answer gives 2 * 9 * sqrt(9) = 18 * 3 = 54. Correct.
Answer: B.

**Trap.** Pulling only one x out of x^5, as if sqrt(x^4) were x, leaves 2x*sqrt(12x), choice A, which at x = 3 gives 2 * 3 * 6 = 36 instead of 54 and still hides the square factor 4 inside the radical. Writing sqrt(12) = 3*sqrt(2) gives choice C, and writing sqrt(12) = 6 by halving the 12 gives choice E; at x = 3 those report 27*sqrt(6) and 54*sqrt(3), neither of which is 54. Choice D doubles sqrt(4) = 2 to 4.

### Q7 · `ALG-EXR-020` — Answer: A

Step 1 — the outside exponent applies to both factors inside: (16x^8)^(1/4) = 16^(1/4) * (x^8)^(1/4).
Step 2 — 16^(1/4) is the fourth root of 16. Since 2^4 = 16, this is 2.
Step 3 — (x^8)^(1/4) = x^(8 * 1/4) = x^2.
Step 4 — multiply the pieces: 2x^2.
Check: at x = 2, 16x^8 = 16 * 256 = 4096, and 4096^(1/4) = 8 because 8^4 = 4096. The answer gives 2 * 2^2 = 8. Correct.
Answer: A.

**Trap.** Computing 16^(1/4) as 4 by reflex, because 16^(1/2) = 4, gives 4x^2, choice C, which at x = 2 reports 16 instead of 8. Dividing the exponent 8 by 2 instead of by 4 gives x^4 and leads to choice B or, with both errors, choice D. Choice E leaves the coefficient untouched, applying the 1/4 only to the variable.

### Q8 · `ALG-EXR-022` — Answer: E

Step 1 — handle each base on its own with the quotient rule: subtract the bottom exponent from the top exponent.
Step 2 — for x: (-2) - 2 = -4, so the x part is x^(-4) = 1/x^4.
Step 3 — for y: 3 - (-1) = 3 + 1 = 4, so the y part is y^4.
Step 4 — combine: y^4/x^4.
Check: at x = 2 and y = 3 the original is ((1/4) * 27) / (4 * (1/3)) = 6.75 / 1.3333 = 5.0625, and y^4/x^4 = 81/16 = 5.0625. Correct.
Answer: E.

**Trap.** Subtracting the -1 as though it were +1 gives y^(3-1) = y^2 and choice C, which at x = 2, y = 3 reports 9/16 = 0.5625 instead of 5.0625. Subtracting top from bottom instead of bottom from top for both bases gives x^4/y^4, choice B. Adding the x exponents, -2 + 2 = 0, cancels the x entirely and gives y^4, choice D. Choice A makes both mistakes at once.

### Q9 · `ALG-EXR-019` — Answer: C

Step 1 — multiplying like bases adds the exponents: x^(1/2) * x^(1/3) = x^(1/2 + 1/3).
Step 2 — add the fractions over the common denominator 6: 1/2 = 3/6 and 1/3 = 2/6, so 3/6 + 2/6 = 5/6.
Step 3 — Quantity A is therefore x^(5/6), which is Quantity B, for every x > 0.
Check: at x = 64, Quantity A = 64^(1/2) * 64^(1/3) = 8 * 4 = 32, and Quantity B = 64^(5/6) = (64^(1/6))^5 = 2^5 = 32. Equal. Correct.
Answer: C.

**Trap.** Multiplying the exponents instead of adding them gives x^(1/6), which at x = 64 is 2 rather than 32 and points to answer B. Adding the fractions by adding numerators and denominators gives x^(2/5), which at x = 64 is about 5.3, again pointing to B. There is no rule that multiplies exponents when powers are multiplied; that rule belongs to a power of a power.

### Q10 · `ALG-EXR-028` — Answer: A

Step 1 — rewrite Quantity A with the negative-exponent rule: x^(-2) = 1/x^2. Since x is not 0, x^2 > 0, so Quantity A is positive for every allowed x.
Step 2 — read Quantity B carefully. The minus sign sits outside the power, so -x^2 means -(x^2), and x^2 > 0 makes Quantity B negative for every allowed x.
Step 3 — a positive quantity beats a negative one, whatever x is.
Check: at x = 3, Quantity A = 1/9 and Quantity B = -9. At x = -1/2, Quantity A = 4 and Quantity B = -1/4. Quantity A is greater both times, and the negative test value shows the sign of x does not matter. Correct.
Answer: A.

**Trap.** Reading x^(-2) as -x^2 makes the two columns identical and produces answer C; at x = 3 that claims 1/9 = -9. Suspecting that a negative x might flip the comparison and answering D is the other error: both columns square x first, so the sign of x is erased before anything else happens.

### Q11 · `ALG-EXR-016` — Answer: 27

Step 1 — x^(2/3) means the cube root of x squared. Undo a fractional exponent by raising both sides to its reciprocal, 3/2.
Step 2 — (x^(2/3))^(3/2) = x^((2/3)*(3/2)) = x^1 = x, so x = 9^(3/2).
Step 3 — evaluate 9^(3/2) = (9^(1/2))^3 = 3^3 = 27.
Check: 27^(2/3) = (27^(1/3))^2 = 3^2 = 9, which is the equation we started from. Correct.
Answer: 27.

**Trap.** Raising both sides to 2/3 instead of 3/2 gives 9^(2/3) = (9^(1/3))^2, about 4.33, and multiplying 9 by 3/2 gives 13.5; neither has a two-thirds power of 9. An exponent is undone by its reciprocal, not by itself.

### Q12 · `ALG-EXR-023` — Answer: 4

Step 1 — both sides are non-negative wherever they are defined, so squaring is safe provided the result is checked at the end.
Step 2 — square both sides. The left gives 3x + 4. The right gives (2*sqrt(x))^2 = 2^2 * (sqrt(x))^2 = 4x, because the coefficient 2 is inside the squaring.
Step 3 — solve 3x + 4 = 4x: subtract 3x from both sides to get 4 = x.
Check: sqrt(3*4 + 4) = sqrt(16) = 4, and 2*sqrt(4) = 2 * 2 = 4. The two sides agree, so x = 4 is genuine and not extraneous. Correct.
Answer: 4.

**Trap.** Squaring only the radical on the right and leaving the coefficient alone gives 3x + 4 = 2x and x = -4, at which point neither radical is even defined, since 3(-4) + 4 = -8. The factor outside a radical is squared along with it: (2*sqrt(x))^2 = 4x, not 2x.

### Q13 · `ALG-EXR-027` — Answer: B, C

Step 1 — multiplying like bases adds the exponents: 2^m * 2^n = 2^(m+n). Since 32 = 2^5, this forces m + n = 5.
Step 2 — list the ways to write 5 as a sum of two POSITIVE integers: 1 + 4, 2 + 3, 3 + 2, 4 + 1. The split 0 + 5 is barred because both m and n must be positive.
Step 3 — evaluate 2^m + 2^n for each split: 2^1 + 2^4 = 2 + 16 = 18, and 2^2 + 2^3 = 4 + 8 = 12. The reversed splits repeat these two values.
Step 4 — test the options. A (10) FALSE, no split gives it. B (12) TRUE, from m = 2 and n = 3. C (18) TRUE, from m = 1 and n = 4. D (32) FALSE. E (33) FALSE.
Check: m = 2, n = 3 gives 2^2 * 2^3 = 4 * 8 = 32 as required and 4 + 8 = 12; m = 1, n = 4 gives 2 * 16 = 32 and 2 + 16 = 18. Correct.
Answer: B and C.

**Trap.** Writing 2^m + 2^n = 2^(m+n) = 32 turns the sum into the product and selects D; there is no rule for adding powers, only for multiplying them. Allowing m = 0 ignores the word positive and produces 2^0 + 2^5 = 1 + 32 = 33, choice E. Reading 2^m + 2^n as 2m + 2n = 2(m + n) = 10 gives choice A.

### Q14 · `ALG-EXR-034` — Answer: D

Step 1 — every base is a power of 2, so rewrite them all before touching the exponents: 4 = 2^2, 8 = 2^3, 16 = 2^4.
Step 2 — left side: (2^2)^x * (2^3)^(x+1) = 2^(2x) * 2^(3x+3) = 2^(2x + 3x + 3) = 2^(5x + 3).
Step 3 — right side: (2^4)^(x+2) = 2^(4x + 8).
Step 4 — the base 2 is not 0, 1 or -1, so the exponents must be equal: 5x + 3 = 4x + 8.
Step 5 — subtract 4x and 3 from both sides: x = 5.
Check: at x = 5 the left side is 4^5 * 8^6 = 2^10 * 2^18 = 2^28, and the right side is 16^7 = 2^28. Both are 268435456. Correct.
Answer: D.

**Trap.** Multiplying the two exponents on the left instead of adding them gives (2x)(3x + 3) = 4x + 8, a quadratic whose positive root is x = 1, choice B. Dropping the +1 inside 8^(x+1) gives 5x = 4x + 8 and x = 8, choice E. Writing 16 as 2^3 gives 5x + 3 = 3x + 6 and x = 3/2, choice C. Merging the left side into 32^(2x+1) by multiplying the bases 4 and 8 gives 10x + 5 = 4x + 8 and x = 1/2, choice A.
