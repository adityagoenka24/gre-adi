**Lecture capsule:** [04-systems-of-equations.md](../../03-algebra/04-systems-of-equations.md)

> Ask what is actually wanted before you solve: a sum or difference often falls straight out of adding or subtracting the raw equations, and when the coefficient ratios match, the system has either no solution or infinitely many rather than one.

## Part C — Worked Solutions

### Q1 · `ALG-SIM-002` — Answer: A

Step 1 — the second equation already gives one variable, so substitute x = 3 into the first equation.
Step 2 — 2(3) + y = 11, so 6 + y = 11.
Step 3 — subtract 6 from both sides: y = 5.
Check: substitute the pair (x, y) = (3, 5) back into BOTH original equations. First: 2(3) + 5 = 6 + 5 = 11. Correct. Second: x = 3. Correct.
Answer: A.

**Trap.** Dropping the coefficient 2 and computing 11 - 3 = 8, which is choice C. Choice B, 6, is the value of 2x, reported in place of y. Choice D, 9, comes from subtracting the coefficient 2 instead of the product 2x = 6. Choice E, 11, is the right-hand side copied out unchanged.

### Q2 · `ALG-SIM-007` — Answer: E

Step 1 — use the coefficient-ratio test. For a1x + b1y = c1 and a2x + b2y = c2: one solution when a1/a2 is not equal to b1/b2; no solution when a1/a2 = b1/b2 but that ratio is not equal to c1/c2; infinitely many when all three ratios agree.
Step 2 — A: 1/1 = 1 and 1/(-1) = -1. Different, so exactly one solution.
Step 3 — B: 2/1 = 2 and 1/2. Different, so exactly one solution.
Step 4 — C: 1/2 and (-1)/(-1) = 1. Different, so exactly one solution.
Step 5 — D: 1/2 = 1/2 and 5/10 = 1/2. All three agree, so the second equation is the first one doubled: the same line, infinitely many solutions.
Step 6 — E: 1/2 = 1/2 but 5/12 is not 1/2. Same slope, different intercept: parallel and distinct, so no solution.
Check: any pair satisfying x + y = 5 makes 2x + 2y = 10, never 12, so the two equations of E cannot both hold. Confirmed.
Answer: E.

**Trap.** Choosing D, where the second equation is exactly twice the first. That is the infinitely-many case, not the no-solution case. The two are separated only by the constants: 2(5) = 10 matches D and contradicts E's 12.

### Q3 · `ALG-SIM-011` — Answer: B

Step 1 — the question compares y with x, so find y - x rather than each variable separately.
Step 2 — subtract the first equation from the second. Every term of the first changes sign: (x - 3x) + (3y - y) = 10 - 14, so -2x + 2y = -4.
Step 3 — divide by 2: y - x = -2, which is negative, so y is less than x and Quantity B is greater.
Step 4 — for confirmation, add the equations: 4x + 4y = 24, so x + y = 6. With x - y = 2 this gives x = 4 and y = 2.
Check: substitute (x, y) = (4, 2) into BOTH original equations. First: 3(4) + 2 = 12 + 2 = 14. Correct. Second: 4 + 3(2) = 4 + 6 = 10. Correct. Quantity A is 2 and Quantity B is 4.
Answer: B.

**Trap.** Subtracting in the wrong order, writing (3x + y) - (x + 3y) = 14 - 10 and reading 2x - 2y = 4 as y - x = 2; that sign slip makes Quantity A look larger and produces choice A. Choosing D because neither variable is given numerically is also wrong: the system pins both values exactly.

### Q5 · `ALG-LN2-011` — Answer: 18

### Q6 · `ALG-LN2-016` — Answer: C

Step 1 — no coefficient pair matches and none is 1, so nothing cancels as the equations stand. Elimination needs scaling first.
Step 2 — to clear y, scale each equation by the other's y-coefficient: multiply the first by 5 to get 35x + 15y = 185, and the second by 3 to get 6x + 15y = 69.
Step 3 — the y-terms now match, so subtract: (35x - 6x) + (15y - 15y) = 185 - 69, giving 29x = 116.
Step 4 — divide by 29: x = 4.
Step 5 — back-substitute into the equation not used for the final step, 2x + 5y = 23: 8 + 5y = 23, so 5y = 15 and y = 3.
Check: substitute (x, y) = (4, 3) into BOTH original equations. First: 7(4) + 3(3) = 28 + 9 = 37. Correct. Second: 2(4) + 5(3) = 8 + 15 = 23. Correct.
Answer: C.

**Trap.** Adding or subtracting the equations unscaled, which leaves both variables in play and invites guessing. Choice B, 3, is y, reported after solving correctly but answering the wrong variable. Choice D, 7, is x + y. Choice A, 1, is x - y. Choice E, 12, is the product xy.

### Q7 · `ALG-SIM-018` — Answer: D

Step 1 — let p be the number of pens and n the number of notebooks. The item count gives p + n = 14 and the money gives 2p + 5n = 43.
Step 2 — from the first equation, n = 14 - p. Substitute into the second: 2p + 5(14 - p) = 43.
Step 3 — expand: 2p + 70 - 5p = 43, so -3p = -27.
Step 4 — divide by -3: p = 9, and then n = 14 - 9 = 5.
Faster route: 14 pens would cost 2(14) = $28. The bill is $43, which is $15 more, and each swap of a pen for a notebook adds $5 - $2 = $3. So 15/3 = 5 notebooks and 14 - 5 = 9 pens.
Check: substitute (p, n) = (9, 5) into BOTH original equations. First: 9 + 5 = 14 items. Correct. Second: 2(9) + 5(5) = 18 + 25 = $43. Correct.
Answer: D.

**Trap.** Solving correctly and reporting the notebooks, 5, which is choice B; the question asks for pens. Choice A, 3, comes from dividing the $15 surplus by the notebook price $5 instead of by the $3 price gap, and choice E, 11, is 14 - 3, the complement of that same error. Choice C, 7, is the guess that the 14 items split evenly.

### Q10 · `ALG-SIM-017` — Answer: A

Step 1 — do not solve, and do not trust the eye: 6 and 10 look unrelated to 15 and 25. Compute the three coefficient ratios: 6/10 = 3/5, (-15)/(-25) = 3/5, and 9/15 = 3/5.
Step 2 — all three ratios agree at 3/5, so the first equation is exactly 3/5 of the second. The two equations describe the same line.
Step 3 — a single line contains infinitely many points, so the number of ordered pairs satisfying both is infinite, which is greater than 1.
Check: pick a point on the first line, say (4, 1). Substitute into BOTH equations: 6(4) - 15(1) = 24 - 15 = 9. Correct. 10(4) - 25(1) = 40 - 25 = 15. Correct. Now pick (9, 3): 6(9) - 15(3) = 54 - 45 = 9. Correct. 10(9) - 25(3) = 90 - 75 = 15. Correct. Two different solutions already exist, so Quantity A exceeds 1.

**Trap.** Assuming that two equations in two unknowns always determine exactly one pair and choosing C. Choosing B treats the matching slopes as parallel lines with no solution, which would require the constant ratio to differ from 3/5.

Step 2 — replace x in xy = 21: (2y + 1)y = 21, so 2y^2 + y - 21 = 0.

### Q12 · `ALG-SIM-033` — Answer: A, C, D

Step 1 — model the fleet. Let b be the number of buses and v the number of vans: b + v = 12 and 4b + 3v = 41.
Step 2 — the count equation is already one step from isolating a variable, so substitute v = 12 - b into the capacity equation: 4b + 3(12 - b) = 41, so 4b + 36 - 3b = 41 and b = 5. Then v = 12 - 5 = 7.
A. TRUE - b = 5.
B. FALSE - 7 is the number of vans, not buses; the two are swapped.
C. TRUE - the buses carry 5(4) = 20 students and the vans carry 7(3) = 21, so the van total is larger. This needs the second computation, not just the counts.
D. TRUE - remodel with b + v = 13: 4b + 3(13 - b) = 41 gives b + 39 = 41, so b = 2 and v = 11.
E. FALSE - swapping a 3-seat van for a 4-seat bus raises the total by 1 student, to 42.
Check: substitute (b, v) = (5, 7) into BOTH original equations. First: 5 + 7 = 12. Correct. Second: 4(5) + 3(7) = 20 + 21 = 41. Correct.
Answer: A, C and D.

**Trap.** Selecting B by solving correctly and then attaching the count to the wrong vehicle; 7 is the van count. Rejecting C because there are fewer vehicles of the larger type is backwards - here the smaller vans are more numerous, and 21 against 20 is decided only by the arithmetic. Selecting E treats the two vehicle types as interchangeable, ignoring the one-seat capacity gap that the whole system turns on.

### Q13 · `ALG-LN2-034` — Answer: B, D

Step 1 — no solution requires the two x-and-y ratios to agree while the constant ratio differs: 2/4 = k/8 and 2/4 is not equal to 6/c.
Step 2 — 2/4 = 1/2, so k/8 = 1/2 forces k = 4. And 6/c must not equal 1/2, so c must not equal 12.
Step 3 — test each pair against k = 4 and c not equal to 12.
A. FALSE - k = 2 gives k/8 = 1/4, which is not 1/2. The slopes differ, so there is exactly one solution.
B. TRUE - k = 4 and c = 10. The ratio 6/10 = 3/5 is not 1/2, so the lines are parallel and distinct: no solution.
