**Lecture capsule:** [08-sequences-series.md](../../03-algebra/08-sequences-series.md)

> Constant differences mean arithmetic, constant ratios mean geometric - and every count is (last - first)/step + 1, because you count the terms, not the gaps.

## Part C — Worked Solutions

### Q1 · `ARI-SEQ-001` — Answer: C

Step 1 — identify a and d. The first term is a = 3, and each step adds d = 7 - 3 = 4.
Step 2 — use a_n = a + (n - 1)d. Getting from term 1 to term 10 takes 9 steps, not 10.
a_10 = 3 + 9 x 4 = 3 + 36 = 39.
Check by listing: 3, 7, 11, 15, 19, 23, 27, 31, 35, 39. The tenth number is 39. Correct.
Answer: 39.

**Trap.** Using a + nd instead of a + (n - 1)d: 3 + 10 x 4 = 43, choice E. The first term has had zero steps of d applied to it, so the 10th term has had 9. Choice A, 35, is the mirror slip of counting only 8 steps; choices B and D drop the first term and report just the accumulated difference, 9 x 4 = 36 and 10 x 4 = 40.

### Q2 · `ARI-SEQ-003` — Answer: C

Step 1 — check ratios, not differences: 6/2 = 3, 18/6 = 3, 54/18 = 3. So r = 3 and a = 2.
Step 2 — use a_n = a x r^(n-1). The exponent is 4, not 5.
a_5 = 2 x 3^4 = 2 x 81 = 162.
Check by extending the list: 2, 6, 18, 54, 162. Correct.
Answer: 162.

**Trap.** Writing 2 x 3^5 = 486, choice E - the same off-by-one as in arithmetic sequences, now living in the exponent. Choice A, 90, comes from treating the sequence as arithmetic and adding the last difference (54 + 36). Choice B, 108, doubles the 4th term instead of tripling it, and choice D, 324, multiplies 54 by the second term 6 rather than by the ratio.

### Q3 · `ARI-SEQ-007` — Answer: C

Step 1 — count the terms. The list is 2, 4, 6, ..., 20 with step 2, so the count is (20 - 2)/2 + 1 = 9 + 1 = 10 terms. Say the '+1' out loud.
Step 2 — sum = count x average, and for an evenly spaced list the average is (first + last)/2 = (2 + 20)/2 = 11.
Step 3 — sum = 10 x 11 = 110.
Check: 2 + 20 = 22, 4 + 18 = 22, 6 + 16 = 22, 8 + 14 = 22, 10 + 12 = 22. Five pairs of 22 = 110. Correct.
Answer: 110.

**Trap.** Stopping the count at (20 - 2)/2 = 9 terms, which gives 9 x 11 = 99 (choice A); overshooting to 11 terms gives 121 (choice D). Choice E, 210, is the sum of all integers from 1 to 20, and choice B, 100, is 10^2, the sum of the first 10 ODD numbers, not the even ones.

### Q4 · `ARI-SEQ-010` — Answer: B, D, E

A sequence is arithmetic when the DIFFERENCES between consecutive terms are constant.
A - differences are 2, 4, 8: not constant. The ratios are constant (r = 2), so this one is geometric. False.
B - differences are 4, 4, 4. Constant, so arithmetic with d = 4. True.
C - differences are 3, 5, 7: not constant. These are the perfect squares. False.
D - differences are -3, -3, -3. Constant, so arithmetic with d = -3. A negative common difference is still a common difference. True.
E - differences are 0, 0, 0. Constant, so arithmetic with d = 0. True.
Answer: B, D, E.

**Trap.** Two errors sit on opposite sides. Including A, because 2, 4, 8, 16 'has a pattern' - it does, but a multiplicative one. Excluding D or E, because the differences are not positive: d = -3 and d = 0 are perfectly legal common differences.

### Q5 · `ARI-SEQ-015` — Answer: 18

Step 1 — the step size is d = 8 - 5 = 3, first term 5, last term 56.
Step 2 — count = (last - first)/step + 1 = (56 - 5)/3 + 1 = 51/3 + 1 = 17 + 1 = 18.
Step 3 — why the +1: 51/3 = 17 counts the GAPS between terms, and a row of 17 gaps has 18 terms at its ends.
Check with the nth-term formula: 56 = 5 + (n - 1)3 gives (n - 1)3 = 51, n - 1 = 17, n = 18. Correct.
Answer: 18.

**Trap.** Answering 17, the number of steps of 3 needed to walk from 5 up to 56. Fence posts, not fence panels: from 5 to 56 there are 17 jumps but 18 numbers. This is the single most common error in the whole topic.

### Q6 · `ARI-SEQ-014` — Answer: D

Step 1 — one block has length 3 and sums to 4 + 9 + 2 = 15.
Step 2 — divide the position count by the cycle length: 50 = 3 x 16 + 2. So there are 16 complete blocks (48 terms) with 2 terms left over.
Step 3 — the complete blocks contribute 16 x 15 = 240.
Step 4 — the remainder is 2, so the leftover terms are the FIRST two of a fresh block: term 49 = 4 and term 50 = 9, contributing 4 + 9 = 13.
Step 5 — total = 240 + 13 = 253.
Check: term 48 is the last of block 16, so term 49 restarts at 4. Correct.
Answer: 253.

**Trap.** Stopping at 240 (choice A), the 16 complete blocks, and forgetting the two terms hanging off the end. Choice E, 255, is the opposite error of rounding 50/3 up to 17 whole blocks, which counts 51 terms. Choice C, 251, takes the leftover terms from the wrong end of the block (9 + 2 instead of 4 + 9), and choice B, 244, counts only one leftover term instead of two. In a cycle question the remainder tells you how far INTO the next block you land, counting from its start.

### Q7 · `ARI-SEQ-017` — Answer: 59

Step 1 — count the steps between the known terms: from position 3 to position 7 is 7 - 3 = 4 steps, not 5 and not 7.
Step 2 — those 4 steps produce a rise of 27 - 11 = 16, so d = 16/4 = 4.
Step 3 — jump straight from the 7th term to the 15th: that is 15 - 7 = 8 more steps.
a_15 = 27 + 8 x 4 = 27 + 32 = 59.
Check by going back to the first term instead: a_3 = 11 means a_1 = 11 - 2 x 4 = 3, and a_15 = 3 + 14 x 4 = 3 + 56 = 59. Both routes agree. Correct.
Answer: 59.

**Trap.** Dividing 16 by 5 (positions counted instead of gaps) or by 7, which corrupts d before the work even starts. The other slip is treating a_3 = 11 as 'a_1 plus 3d' when it is a_1 plus 2d - working forward from a_7 avoids that step entirely.

### Q8 · `ARI-SEQ-019` — Answer: C

Step 1 — Quantity A, arithmetic: a_5 = 4 + (5 - 1) x 15 = 4 + 60 = 64.
Step 2 — Quantity B, geometric: b_5 = 4 x 2^(5-1) = 4 x 2^4 = 4 x 16 = 64.
Step 3 — 64 = 64, so the two quantities are equal.
Check by listing. A: 4, 19, 34, 49, 64. B: 4, 8, 16, 32, 64. Both fifth entries are 64. Correct.
Note the shape of the two formulas: the arithmetic term adds d exactly (n - 1) times and the geometric term multiplies by r exactly (n - 1) times. Here 4 additions of 15 and 4 doublings happen to land on the same number; one term later the doubling has already run away, 128 against 79.
Answer: The two quantities are equal.

**Trap.** Using the exponent n instead of n - 1 in Quantity B: 4 x 2^5 = 128, which makes Quantity B look greater and gives choice B. The mirror slip, reading the geometric term as first term x n x r = 4 x 5 x 2 = 40, makes Quantity A look greater and gives choice A. The exponent is n - 1 = 4, exactly parallel to the (n - 1)d = 4 x 15 in Quantity A, and with the exponent right the columns tie at 64.

### Q9 · `ARI-SEQ-021` — Answer: 41

Do not look for a closed form - four terms is faster to list.
Step 1 — a_1 = 2.
Step 2 — a_2 = 3 x 2 - 1 = 5.
Step 3 — a_3 = 3 x 5 - 1 = 14.
Step 4 — a_4 = 3 x 14 - 1 = 41.
Check by running the rule backwards from 41: (41 + 1)/3 = 14, (14 + 1)/3 = 5, (5 + 1)/3 = 2 = a_1. Correct.
Answer: 41.

**Trap.** Feeding a_1 into the rule for the wrong term, e.g. a_4 = 3 x 2 - 1 = 5, or treating the rule as geometric and computing 2 x 3^3 - 1 = 53. Each term must be built from the one immediately before it; the '-1' is applied at every single step, not once at the end.

### Q10 · `ARI-SEQ-023` — Answer: A, B, C

The sequence has a = 6 and d = 4, so its terms are 6 + 4k for whole numbers k. A number x is a term exactly when x - 6 is a non-negative multiple of 4.
A - 50 - 6 = 44, and 44/4 = 11. True (it is the 12th term).
B - 62 - 6 = 56, and 56/4 = 14. True (the 15th term).
C - 78 - 6 = 72, and 72/4 = 18. True (the 19th term).
D - 100 - 6 = 94, and 94/4 = 23.5, not a whole number. False.
E - 200 - 6 = 194, and 194/4 = 48.5, not a whole number. False.
Answer: A, B, C.

**Trap.** Testing only whether the candidate is even. Every term here is even, but the terms are exactly the numbers that leave remainder 2 on division by 4 - and 100 and 200 are multiples of 4, so they are skipped over. The test is (x - a) divisible by d, not (x divisible by d).

### Q11 · `ARI-SEQ-029` — Answer: B

Step 1 — count the steps between the two given positions: from term 2 to term 5 is 5 - 2 = 3 multiplications by r.
Step 2 — so a_5 = a_2 x r^3, giving 162 = 6 x r^3.
Step 3 — r^3 = 162/6 = 27, so r = 3.
Check by building the sequence: a_2 = 6, a_3 = 18, a_4 = 54, a_5 = 162. Correct.
Answer: 3.

**Trap.** Reporting 27 (choice E), the value of r^3, without taking the cube root. Choice D, 9, is the half-measure of dividing 27 by 3 instead of cube-rooting it. The positions 2 and 5 are three steps apart, not one - the same gap-counting that trips up arithmetic sequences decides the exponent here.

### Q12 · `ARI-SEQ-034` — Answer: B

Step 1 — see what is left. Removing every even number from 1 through 100 leaves exactly the odd numbers 1, 3, 5, ..., 99. So the expression equals the sum of the odd numbers below 100.
Step 2 — count them: (99 - 1)/2 + 1 = 49 + 1 = 50 odd numbers.
Step 3 — sum of the first n odd numbers = n^2, so the answer is 50^2 = 2500.
Check the long way: 1 + 2 + ... + 100 = 100 x 101/2 = 5050, and 2 + 4 + ... + 100 = 50 x (2 + 100)/2 = 50 x 51 = 2550. Then 5050 - 2550 = 2500. Correct.
Answer: 2500.

**Trap.** Choice A, 2401, is 49^2 - counting only 49 odd numbers by taking (99 - 1)/2 and forgetting the +1. Choice D, 2550, is the even sum on its own, and choice E, 5050, is the total; both come from answering before subtracting. Choice C, 2525, is 5050/2, from assuming the evens must be exactly half the total (they are more than half, since each even exceeds the odd below it).

### Q13 · `ARI-SEQ-039` — Answer: B

From the 4th term to the 10th term there are 6 steps, and the terms rise by 29 - 11 = 18.
Each step adds 18/6 = 3.
From the 10th term to the 20th term there are 10 steps: 29 + 10 * 3 = 59.
Answer: B.

**Trap.** Dividing 18 by 7, the number of terms from the 4th through the 10th, instead of by the 6 steps between them. Counting steps, not terms, gives a common difference of 3.

### Q14 · `ARI-SEQ-042` — Answer: C

Step 1 — name the terms symmetrically as a - d, a, a + d. The d's cancel in the sum, which is the whole point of this labeling.
Step 2 — sum: (a - d) + a + (a + d) = 3a = 18, so a = 6. In any evenly spaced set the mean equals the middle term.
Step 3 — product: (6 - d)(6)(6 + d) = 6(36 - d^2) = 162, so 36 - d^2 = 27, giving d^2 = 9 and d = 3.
Step 4 — the numbers are 3, 6, 9.
Check: 3 + 6 + 9 = 18 and 3 x 6 x 9 = 162. Correct.
Every option already sums to 18, so only the product separates them: A gives 66, B gives 120, D gives 192, E gives 216.
Answer: 3, 6, 9.

**Trap.** All five choices are arithmetic and all five sum to 18, so a student who checks only the sum picks at random. The second trap is choice E: the mean is 6, but 'mean = middle term' does not force all three terms to equal the mean. Only the product 162 pins down d.

### Q15 · `ARI-SEQ-043` — Answer: 6

Work backward. If a term is t, the next term is 3t + 2.
Third term: 3 * (second term) + 2 = 62, so the second term is 60/3 = 20.
Second term: 3 * (first term) + 2 = 20, so the first term is 18/3 = 6.
Check: 6, then 3 * 6 + 2 = 20, then 3 * 20 + 2 = 62.

**Trap.** Undoing the steps in the wrong order: dividing 62 by 3 first and then subtracting 2. To reverse 'multiply by 3, then add 2', subtract 2 first and then divide by 3.

### Q16 · `ARI-SEQ-053` — Answer: B

Step 1 — write both stated terms in a and d.
a_6 = a + 5d and a_3 = a + 2d.
Step 2 — impose the condition a_6 = 2a_3:
a + 5d = 2(a + 2d) = 2a + 4d
Subtract a + 4d from both sides: d = a.
Step 3 — use the second fact: a_2 = a + d = 4. Since d = a, this is 2a = 4, so a = 2.
Check: with a = 2 and d = 2 the sequence is 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12. The 6th term is 12, the 3rd is 6, and 12 = 2 x 6. The 2nd term is 4. Correct.
Answer: 2.

**Trap.** Expanding 2(a + 2d) as 2a + 2d gives a = 3d, then a_2 = 4d = 4 and a first term of 3 (choice C). Choice D, 4, is the given 2nd term reported as the first. Choice E, 6, comes from stepping the wrong way: knowing d = 2 and adding it to a_2 instead of subtracting. Choice A, 0, follows from reading a_2 = 4 as the common difference.

### Q17 · `ARI-SEQ-062` — Answer: 24

The nth term is 7 + 4(n - 1).
7 + 4(n - 1) < 100 gives 4(n - 1) < 93, so n - 1 < 23.25.
n - 1 is at most 23, so n is at most 24.
Check: the 24th term is 7 + 92 = 99 and the 25th is 103.

**Trap.** Entering 23, the largest value of n - 1, and forgetting to add 1 for the first term.

### Q18 · `ARI-SEQ-KMF-006` — Answer: 4

1. Services are 4 days apart, so service number j falls 4(j - 1) days after the first one.
2. Weekdays repeat every 7 days, so service j is on a Friday exactly when 4(j - 1) is a multiple of 7.
3. Because 4 and 7 have no common factor, this requires (j - 1) to be a multiple of 7: j - 1 = 0, 7, 14, 21, so j = 1, 8, 15, 22.
4. The next such service would be j = 29, which is past 24, so exactly 4 services fall on a Friday.
