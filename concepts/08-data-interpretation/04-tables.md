# Tables & Data Sets

**Section:** Data Interpretation  |  **Bank code:** DAT-TBL  |  **GRE frequency:** Medium-High

---

## 1. Core Idea

A table gives you **exact values** rather than a picture, so there is no estimation error — but also no visual shortcut. Everything must be read carefully.

The efficiency skill is **selective reading**: never read the whole table. Locate the row and column the question needs, extract those numbers, and stop.

---

## 2. Must-Know Rules

**Before the questions, spend 30 seconds on:**

1. **Row labels** — what does each row represent?
2. **Column headers** — what does each column measure?
3. **Units** — check every column separately; they often differ.
4. **Totals row/column** — is one provided, or must you compute it?
5. **Footnotes** — often define a term or flag an exception.

**The recurring calculations:**

| Question | Method |
|---|---|
| Row total | sum across the row |
| Column total | sum down the column |
| Percent of total | cell / relevant total |
| Percent change across columns | (later - earlier)/earlier |
| Ratio between two cells | divide |
| Average of a row | row total / number of entries |
| Which is greatest | scan, shortlist, then compute |

**Cross-referencing.** Many table questions need two cells from different rows and columns. Point to both with two fingers (or note them down) before computing — this prevents the classic "read the wrong row" error.

## 3. Worked Examples

### Example 1 — Percent of a row total

*A table shows Q1 sales by region: North 120, South 180, East 90, West 210 (in thousands). What percent of total sales came from the South?*

Total = 120 + 180 + 90 + 210 = 600.

South share = 180/600 = **30%**.

### Example 2 — Percent change across columns

*A table shows a product's units sold: 2022 = 4,500; 2023 = 5,400. What is the percent change?*

(5400 - 4500)/4500 = 900/4500 = 0.20 = **20% increase**.

### Example 3 — Ratio across rows

*Employees: Department A has 45 staff with a total payroll of $2,700,000. Department B has 30 staff with a total payroll of $2,100,000. Which has the higher average salary, and by how much?*

A: 2,700,000/45 = $60,000.

B: 2,100,000/30 = $70,000.

**B is higher, by $10,000.**

## 4. GRE Traps

- **Reading the adjacent row or column.** The most common careless error under time pressure.
- **Mixed units across columns.** One column in thousands, another in units — always check each header.
- **Using the grand total when a row total is needed** (or vice versa).
- **Assuming the columns sum to the total row.** Sometimes a table includes an "Other" category excluded from the listed rows; footnotes will say.
- **Ignoring a footnote.** They are there for a reason and usually matter for at least one question.
- **Computing more than needed.** If the question asks only about two rows, don't total the table.
- **Percent change base.** Always the earlier value.

---

## 5. Speed Tricks

- **Locate before you calculate.** Find both cells, write them down, then compute.
- **Compute a total once** if several questions in the set need it, and reuse.
- **Estimate to shortlist** on "which is greatest" questions — compute only the top two.
- **For per-unit comparisons (average salary, cost per item), divide the totals** rather than working with individual entries.
- **Use the calculator only for genuinely awkward division** — 180/600 is a mental step.
- **Rewrite awkward numbers as fractions:** 180/600 = 3/10 = 30%, faster than long division.

## 6. Self-Check

**Q1.** A table shows costs: 240, 360, 200, 400. What percent of the total is the second value?

**Q2.** A value goes from 250 in 2023 to 200 in 2024. What is the percent change?

**Q3.** Team A: 8 members, 640 total points. Team B: 5 members, 425 total points. Which has the higher average?

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

**A1.** Total = 1,200. 360/1200 = **30%**.

**A2.** (200 - 250)/250 = -0.20 = **a 20% decrease**.

**A3.** A: 80. B: 85. **Team B.**

## 7. One-Line Summary for the Board

> Read the headers and units first. Locate the exact cells, then compute — never total the whole table.
