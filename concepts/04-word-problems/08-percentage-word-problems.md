# Percentage Word Problems

**Section:** Word Problems  |  **Bank code:** WRD-PCT  |  **GRE frequency:** High

---

## 1. Core Idea

This capsule is about applying percentages inside a story. (For the mechanics, see *Arithmetic > Percentages*.)

The only genuinely hard part of a percent word problem is identifying **the base** — the "of what" that the percentage refers to. Circle the base before you compute anything.

---

## 2. Must-Know Rules

| Phrase | Base |
|---|---|
| x% of the total | the total |
| x% increase | the original value |
| x% discount | the marked price |
| x% more than B | B |
| x% of the remainder | what is left, not the original |
| x% of the students who passed | the passers, not all students |

**Multiple-stage problems.** Chain the multipliers, applying each to the correct base:

*A class of 200 students: 60% are girls; 25% of the girls play a sport.*

Girls = 0.6 x 200 = 120. Sport-playing girls = 0.25 x 120 = 30 (which is 15% of the class, not 25%).

**Percent of a remainder.** "She spends 40% on rent, then 25% of the remainder on food." The food spend is 0.25 x 0.60 = **15% of the original**, and she is left with 0.60 x 0.75 = 45%.

**Population growth.** Final = Initial x (1 + r)^n — the same structure as compound interest.

---

## 3. Worked Examples

### Example 1 — Percent of a remainder

*A man spends 30% of his salary on rent and 20% of the remainder on transport. If he saves the rest and saves $2,800, what is his salary?*

After rent: 70% left.

Transport: 20% of 70% = 14% of salary. Remaining = 70% - 14% = 56%.

0.56 x S = 2800 -> S = **$5,000**.

Check: rent 1500, remainder 3500, transport 700, saved 2800. Correct.

### Example 2 — Two-group weighted percent

*In a company, 40% of employees are engineers and 60% are not. 25% of engineers and 10% of non-engineers have a PhD. What percent of all employees have a PhD?*

Take 100 employees: 40 engineers, 60 non-engineers.

PhDs: 0.25(40) + 0.10(60) = 10 + 6 = 16.

**16%.**

### Example 3 — Percent change in a story

*A shop's sales fell from 480 units to 384 units. By what percent did sales fall, and by what percent must they now rise to return to 480?*

Fall: (480 - 384)/480 = 96/480 = **20%**.

Rise needed: 96/384 = **25%**.

The two percentages differ because the bases differ. This asymmetry is the point of the question.

---

## 4. GRE Traps

- **Percent of the remainder read as percent of the original.** The single biggest trap in this topic.
- **A 20% fall needs a 20% rise to recover.** It needs 25%.
- **Averaging two group percentages.** 25% and 10% do not average to 17.5% unless the groups are equal in size.
- **Percentage points vs percent.** A rise from 8% to 12% is 4 percentage points but a 50% increase.
- **Applying a percent to the wrong subgroup.** "30% of the women" is not "30% of the people".
- **Rounding too early** in multi-stage percentage chains.

---

## 5. Speed Tricks

- **Set the total to 100** whenever the problem is entirely percentages. Every answer then reads directly as a percent.
- **Convert everything to multipliers of the original** before combining. "70% left, then 80% of that" -> 0.56 of the original.
- **Draw a two-by-two table** for problems with two overlapping categories (engineers/non-engineers x PhD/no PhD).
- **To reverse a percent change, divide** by the multiplier — never subtract the same percent.
- **Check that all the pieces sum to 100%** at the end.

## 6. Self-Check

**Q1.** A student scores 45 out of 60. What percent is that?

**Q2.** After spending 25% of her money and then 40% of what remained, Priya has $360. How much did she start with?

**Q3.** A price falls by 25%. By what percent must it rise to return to the original?

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

**A1.** 45/60 x 100 = **75%**.

**A2.** Remaining fraction = 0.75 x 0.60 = 0.45. 0.45S = 360 -> S = **$800**.

**A3.** New price is 0.75 of the original; to get back, multiply by 1/0.75 = 4/3, a **33.33% rise**.

</details>

## 7. One-Line Summary for the Board

> Find the base before you find the percent. "Percent of the remainder" is not "percent of the original".
