# Age Problems

**Section:** Word Problems  |  **Bank code:** WRD-AGE  |  **GRE frequency:** Low-Medium

---

## 1. Core Idea

Age problems are pure translation exercises with one structural feature:

> **Everyone ages at the same rate.** In t years, every person's age increases by exactly t.

That means the **difference between two ages never changes**, while the **ratio does**. Questions are built on exactly that contrast.

---

## 2. Must-Know Rules

| Phrase | Algebra (present age x) |
|---|---|
| t years ago | x - t |
| in t years / t years hence | x + t |
| twice as old as | 2 x (the other) |
| older than by k | x = y + k |
| age difference | constant over time |

**The table method.** Always build:

| Person | t years ago | Now | In t years |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | a - t | a | a + t |
| B | b - t | b | b + t |

Fill in what is known, and write one equation per condition.

**Sum and difference.** If the sum of ages is S and the difference is D, then the ages are (S+D)/2 and (S-D)/2.

---

## 3. Worked Examples

### Example 1 — Two conditions

*A father is 3 times as old as his son. In 12 years, he will be twice as old. Find their present ages.*

Let the son be s; the father is 3s.

In 12 years: 3s + 12 = 2(s + 12)

3s + 12 = 2s + 24 -> s = 12.

**Son 12, father 36.** Check: in 12 years, 48 and 24 — exactly twice. Correct.

### Example 2 — Past condition

*Meera is 5 years older than Nina. Four years ago, Meera was twice Nina's age. How old is Nina now?*

Let Nina = n; Meera = n + 5.

Four years ago: (n + 5 - 4) = 2(n - 4)

n + 1 = 2n - 8 -> n = **9**.

Check: Nina 9, Meera 14. Four years ago: 5 and 10. Twice. Correct.

### Example 3 — Ratio that changes

*The ratio of A's age to B's age is 4 : 3. In 6 years it will be 6 : 5. Find A's present age.*

Let A = 4k, B = 3k.

(4k + 6)/(3k + 6) = 6/5

5(4k + 6) = 6(3k + 6) -> 20k + 30 = 18k + 36 -> 2k = 6 -> k = 3.

A = 4(3) = **12**. (B = 9. In 6 years: 18 and 15 = 6 : 5. Correct.)

## 4. GRE Traps

- **Adding t to only one person.** Everyone ages together.
- **"Was twice as old" applied to present ages.** The condition belongs to the past column.
- **Assuming the age ratio is constant.** Only the difference is.
- **Answering the wrong person's age**, or answering the past age when the present is wanted.
- **Negative or absurd answers.** If an age comes out negative or a child is older than the parent, re-check the setup.
- **"Ages ago" vs "years ago".** Read the exact time offset.

---

## 5. Speed Tricks

- **Draw the three-column table.** It costs 10 seconds and removes almost all setup errors.
- **Name the younger person as the variable** to avoid fractions.
- **The age difference is an invariant** — write it down first; it often solves the problem alone.
- **Back-solve from the answer choices.** Age problems are ideal for this: plug each choice into the story.
- **Check your answer against every sentence,** not just the one you used to solve.

## 6. Self-Check

**Q1.** Ravi is 4 times as old as his daughter. In 20 years he will be twice as old. Find their present ages.

**Q2.** The sum of two ages is 44 and the difference is 8. Find the ages.

**Q3.** Ten years ago, A was 3 times as old as B. Now A is twice as old as B. How old is B now?

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

**A1.** 4d + 20 = 2(d + 20) -> 4d + 20 = 2d + 40 -> d = 10. **Daughter 10, Ravi 40.**

**A2.** (44+8)/2 = 26 and (44-8)/2 = 18. **26 and 18.**

**A3.** a = 2b now. Ten years ago: 2b - 10 = 3(b - 10) -> 2b - 10 = 3b - 30 -> b = **20**. (A is 40; ten years ago 30 and 10. Correct.)

</details>
