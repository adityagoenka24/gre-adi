# Translating Word Problems into Algebra

**Section:** Word Problems  |  **Bank codes:** ALG-WPM, WRD-MST  |  **GRE frequency:** Very High

---

## 1. Core Idea

Almost every word problem failure is a **translation** failure, not a maths failure. Students who set up correctly nearly always finish correctly.

The reliable four-step routine:

1. **Name the unknown** — write "Let x = ..." in words, with units.
2. **Translate each sentence** into an equation, one sentence at a time.
3. **Solve.**
4. **Answer the question actually asked** — often not x itself.

---

## 2. Must-Know Rules

**Translation dictionary:**

| English | Algebra |
|---|---|
| is, are, was, will be, gives | = |
| sum, more than, increased by, together | + |
| difference, less than, decreased by, fewer | - |
| of, product, times, twice, triple | x |
| per, quotient, ratio, out of, divided by | / |
| what, a number, how many | the variable |
| consecutive integers | n, n+1, n+2 |
| percent | /100 |

**The two order traps:**

- "5 less than x" -> `x - 5` (not 5 - x)
- "the quotient of a and b" -> `a/b` (order given)

**Choosing the variable well.** Name the *smallest* or *simplest* quantity as x. If Ram has 3 times as much as Sita, let Sita = x (not Ram), so Ram = 3x and you avoid fractions.

**The universal setup templates:**

| Situation | Equation |
|---|---|
| Total | part + part = whole |
| Comparison | bigger = smaller + difference |
| Rate | rate x time = amount |
| Before/After | new = old +/- change |
| Two conditions | write two equations |

## 3. Worked Examples

### Example 1 — Naming the right variable

*Anil has three times as many books as Bina. Together they have 48. How many does Anil have?*

Let Bina = x. Then Anil = 3x.

x + 3x = 48 -> 4x = 48 -> x = 12.

Anil = 3(12) = **36**.

Note: the question asks for Anil, not x. Answering 12 is the classic trap.

### Example 2 — Two conditions, two equations

*A cinema sells adult tickets at $12 and child tickets at $7. It sold 200 tickets for $1,950. How many child tickets?*

Let a = adult tickets, c = child tickets.

- a + c = 200
- 12a + 7c = 1950

From the first, a = 200 - c. Substitute:

12(200 - c) + 7c = 1950 -> 2400 - 12c + 7c = 1950 -> -5c = -450 -> **c = 90**.

Check: 110 adults x 12 = 1320; 90 children x 7 = 630; total 1950. Correct.

### Example 3 — Before and after

*After spending 30% of her money and then $40 more, Meera has $205 left. How much did she start with?*

Let the start be x.

After spending 30% she has 0.7x. Then $40 more goes: 0.7x - 40 = 205.

0.7x = 245 -> x = **$350**.

Check: 30% of 350 is 105, leaving 245; minus 40 leaves 205. Correct.

*Teaching note:* model the sentence in the order it is written. "Spends 30%, then $40 more" is multiply first, subtract second — reversing the order gives a different (wrong) answer.

## 4. GRE Traps

- **Solving for x but answering the wrong quantity.** Circle what is asked before you start.
- **"Less than" reversed.** "7 less than twice a number" is 2n - 7.
- **Ignoring units.** Mixing minutes and hours, or rupees and paise, silently ruins the equation.
- **Assuming the answer must be a whole number.** People and tickets must be; money and time need not be.
- **Setting up with too many variables.** Two unknowns need two equations; if you only have one relationship, express everything in terms of a single variable.
- **Not using all the information.** If a number in the problem never appears in your equations, you have probably misread something.

---

## 5. Speed Tricks

- **Write "Let x = " in full words with units.** It takes four seconds and prevents the wrong-quantity error.
- **Translate sentence by sentence.** Do not try to build the whole equation in your head.
- **Back-solve from answer choices** when the setup is messy — plug choices in and see which satisfies the story. Start with the middle choice.
- **Pick smart numbers** when the problem is entirely in percents or fractions with no actual amounts: use 100 or the LCM of the denominators.
- **Sanity-check the size of your answer.** If a discount problem gives an answer larger than the original price, you have a sign error.

## 6. Self-Check

**Q1.** The sum of two numbers is 45 and their difference is 9. What is the larger number?

**Q2.** A number decreased by 40% of itself equals 54. What is the number?

**Q3.** Ravi is 4 years older than twice Sam's age. Their ages sum to 34. How old is Sam?

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

**A1.** x + y = 45, x - y = 9. Adding: 2x = 54 -> **x = 27**.

**A2.** 0.6n = 54 -> **n = 90**.

**A3.** Let Sam = s; Ravi = 2s + 4. s + 2s + 4 = 34 -> 3s = 30 -> **s = 10**.

</details>
