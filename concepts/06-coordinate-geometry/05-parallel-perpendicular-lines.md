# Parallel & Perpendicular Lines

**Section:** Coordinate Geometry  |  **Bank code:** COG-PPL  |  **GRE frequency:** Medium-High

---

## 1. Core Idea

Two rules, and they are the whole topic:

- **Parallel lines have equal slopes.** m1 = m2
- **Perpendicular lines have slopes whose product is -1.** m1 x m2 = -1

The second is more usefully remembered as: **flip the fraction and change the sign** (the "negative reciprocal").

---

## 2. Must-Know Rules

| Relationship | Slope condition |
|---|---|
| Parallel | m1 = m2 (and different y-intercepts) |
| Perpendicular | m2 = -1/m1 |
| Same line | same slope and same intercept |
| Horizontal and vertical | perpendicular to each other (special case) |

**Negative reciprocal examples:**

| Slope | Perpendicular slope |
|---|---|
| 2 | -1/2 |
| -3/4 | 4/3 |
| 1 | -1 |
| 5/2 | -2/5 |
| 0 (horizontal) | undefined (vertical) |

**Why the product is -1.** Rotating a line 90 degrees swaps the rise and the run and reverses one sign — hence flip and negate. The horizontal/vertical pair is the exception where the product is undefined rather than -1.

**Distance between parallel lines.** Rarely tested on the GRE, but if needed: take any point on one line and find its perpendicular distance to the other.

---

## 3. Worked Examples

### Example 1 — Parallel

*Find the equation of the line parallel to y = 4x - 7 passing through (1, 3).*

Parallel means the same slope, m = 4.

y - 3 = 4(x - 1) -> **y = 4x - 1**.

### Example 2 — Perpendicular

*Find the equation of the line perpendicular to y = (2/3)x + 5 passing through (4, -1).*

Perpendicular slope = -3/2.

y + 1 = (-3/2)(x - 4) -> y + 1 = -3x/2 + 6

**y = -3x/2 + 5.**

### Example 3 — Testing perpendicularity

*Are the lines 2x + 3y = 6 and 3x - 2y = 12 perpendicular?*

First: slope = -A/B = -2/3.

Second: slope = -3/(-2) = 3/2.

Product = (-2/3)(3/2) = -1. **Yes, perpendicular.**

---

## 4. GRE Traps

- **Reciprocal without the negative.** The perpendicular to slope 2 is -1/2, not 1/2.
- **Negative without the reciprocal.** It is not -2 either.
- **Assuming equal slopes means the same line.** Equal slopes with different intercepts means parallel and distinct.
- **The horizontal/vertical case.** Their slopes are 0 and undefined; the product rule does not apply, but they *are* perpendicular.
- **Forgetting to convert to slope form** before comparing. Ax + By = C has slope -A/B, not A/B.
- **Perpendicular bisector questions:** these need *both* the perpendicular slope **and** the midpoint. Students often supply only one.

---

## 5. Speed Tricks

- **"Flip and negate."** Say it every time; it is faster than the product rule.
- **For Ax + By = C, slope = -A/B.** Compare two lines without rearranging either.
- **Perpendicular check by inspection:** swap the coefficients of x and y and change one sign. 2x + 3y = k is perpendicular to 3x - 2y = k.
- **Perpendicular bisector recipe:** midpoint + negative reciprocal slope + point-slope form.
- **Sketch it.** If your "perpendicular" line looks parallel on a rough sketch, you dropped the negative.

## 6. Self-Check

**Q1.** What is the slope of a line perpendicular to one with slope -5/2?

**Q2.** Find the line parallel to 3x - y = 8 through the point (2, 1).

**Q3.** Are y = 3x + 1 and y = -3x + 4 perpendicular?

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

**A1.** Flip and negate: **2/5**.

**A2.** Slope of the given line = 3. y - 1 = 3(x - 2) -> **y = 3x - 5**.

**A3.** Product = 3 x (-3) = -9, not -1. **No** — they are reflections of each other, not perpendicular.

</details>
