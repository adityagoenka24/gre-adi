# GRE Quant Question Formats

**Section:** Exam Strategy  |  **Applies to:** every Quant question you will see

---

## 1. Core Idea

The GRE Quant section uses **four formats**, and each has its own scoring rule and its own risks. Students lose points not because they cannot do the maths, but because they answer in the wrong form — selecting one option when the question wanted all that apply, or rounding when the question wanted an exact fraction.

---

## 2. Must-Know Rules

| Format | What it looks like | Key rule |
|---|---|---|
| **Multiple choice, one answer** | 5 options, oval buttons | Exactly one is correct |
| **Multiple choice, one or more** | 3-8 options, **square** boxes | Select **every** correct one; no partial credit |
| **Numeric entry** | a box (or two, for fractions) | No options to guide you; type the answer |
| **Quantitative comparison** | the standard four choices | Compare, don't compute |

**How to tell the two multiple-choice types apart:** **oval buttons = pick one; square boxes = pick all that apply.** Teach students to check the shape before answering.

**Multiple-answer questions:**

- There is **no partial credit**. Two of three correct scores zero.
- The number correct is usually not stated — check every option independently.
- Treat each option as its own true/false question.

**Numeric entry:**

- No answer choices means **no back-solving and no elimination**. Your work must be right.
- Enter fractions in the two-box form when offered; they need **not** be reduced.
- Follow any rounding instruction exactly ("round to the nearest 0.1").
- Negative answers: use the minus sign in the box.
- Do not type units, commas or symbols unless the format shows them.

**Section structure (current GRE):** two Quant sections — the first has **12 questions in 21 minutes**, the second **15 questions in 26 minutes**. That is roughly **1 minute 45 seconds per question** — but DI sets and QC questions should run faster to leave room for the hard ones.

**The on-screen calculator** is available throughout the Quant section. It is basic (four functions plus a square root) and slower than mental arithmetic for simple work.

## 3. Worked Examples

### Example 1 — Multiple answer

*Which of the following are factors of 60? Select all that apply. (A) 4 (B) 7 (C) 12 (D) 15 (E) 18*

Test each independently:

- 4: 60/4 = 15. Yes.
- 7: not a factor. No.
- 12: 60/12 = 5. Yes.
- 15: 60/15 = 4. Yes.
- 18: 60/18 is not an integer. No.

**Answer: A, C and D.** Selecting only A and C scores zero.

### Example 2 — Numeric entry with a fraction

*If 3x + 5 = 14, what is x/3? Enter your answer as a fraction.*

3x = 9 -> x = 3. So x/3 = 1.

Enter **1/1** or **1** — but check the format shown. If two boxes are given, 1 and 1 is safe; if one box, enter 1.

### Example 3 — Rounding instruction

*A circle has radius 5. What is its area, rounded to the nearest integer?*

Area = 25 pi = 78.539...

**79.**

Answering 25 pi, or 78, both score zero.

## 4. GRE Traps

- **Answering a multiple-answer question with one selection.** Check the box shape.
- **Stopping after finding two correct options.** There may be a third.
- **Rounding when no rounding was asked for**, or failing to round when it was.
- **Entering a decimal when a fraction was requested** (or vice versa).
- **Typing units** ("5 cm") in a numeric-entry box.
- **Choosing D on a QC question with no variables.**
- **Over-relying on the calculator.** It cannot handle order of operations well and is slower than paper for most GRE arithmetic.

---

## 5. Speed Tricks

- **Check the button shape first.** One second, and it prevents a full-mark loss.
- **For multiple-answer questions, go down the list once, marking yes/no.** Do not try to be clever about how many are correct.
- **Numeric entry: work carefully, then sanity-check.** With no answer choices, there is no safety net — budget an extra 15 seconds to verify.
- **Back-solve on single-answer multiple choice** when the algebra is ugly. Start with the middle value.
- **Plug in numbers** when the question is entirely variables and the choices are expressions.
- **Flag and move on.** Both Quant sections allow review within the section; a question you cannot start in 30 seconds should be flagged and revisited.

## 6. Self-Check

**Q1.** A question shows square selection boxes. How many answers should you select?

**Q2.** A numeric entry question asks for an answer "to the nearest tenth" and your value is 4.267. What do you enter?

**Q3.** In a QC question, both quantities are 12 x 17 and 13 x 16. Can the answer be D?

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

**A1.** **All** that are correct — could be one, could be all of them. There is no partial credit.

**A2.** **4.3**.

**A3.** **No** — both are specific numbers, so a definite relationship exists. (204 vs 208, so B is greater.)

</details>

## 7. One-Line Summary for the Board

> Oval = pick one, square = pick all. Numeric entry has no safety net — verify. Follow rounding instructions exactly.
