# Time Management & Test-Day Tactics

**Section:** Exam Strategy  |  **Applies to:** both Quant sections

---

## 1. Core Idea

On the GRE, **every question is worth the same**. A 20-second percentage question counts exactly as much as a four-minute geometry monster.

That single fact dictates the whole strategy: **collect the easy points first, and never let one hard question consume the time of three easy ones.**

---

## 2. Must-Know Rules

**The time budget:**

| Section | Questions | Time | Average per question |
|---|---|---|---|
| Quant 1 | 12 | 21 min | about 1 min 45 s |
| Quant 2 | 15 | 26 min | about 1 min 44 s |

But the average is misleading. A better working target:

| Question type | Target |
|---|---|
| Quantitative comparison | 60-75 seconds |
| Straightforward multiple choice | 60-90 seconds |
| Data interpretation (per question) | 60-90 seconds, plus 40 s to read the set |
| Hard multi-step problem | up to 2 minutes, then move on |

The maths: banking 30 seconds on each of six easy questions buys you three extra minutes for the hard ones. That trade is the whole game.

**The 30-second rule.** If you cannot see a route into the problem within 30 seconds, flag it and move on. Come back with whatever time remains.

**The adaptive structure.** The GRE is adaptive **between sections**, not within them. Your performance on the first Quant section determines the difficulty of the second. This means: the first section matters, and within any section you can move freely between questions.

**Review within a section is allowed.** Use the flag/mark tool, finish the section, then return.

## 3. Worked Examples

### Example 1 — The cost of stubbornness

*A student spends 5 minutes on one hard geometry question and gets it right, then runs out of time and guesses on the last three.*

Points gained: 1. Points likely lost: 2 or 3 (guessing gives about 20% on five-option questions).

Had they flagged and moved on, they would likely have banked 3 correct answers and still had a minute to attempt the hard one.

**Net: skipping was worth about two extra points.**

### Example 2 — Two-pass technique

*Recommended approach for the 15-question, 26-minute section:*

- **Pass 1 (about 17 minutes):** answer every question you can see a route into. Flag the rest. Aim to have 11-12 answered.
- **Pass 2 (about 8 minutes):** work the flagged questions, hardest last.
- **Final 60 seconds:** ensure **every** question has an answer selected.

### Example 3 — Guessing arithmetic

*With 40 seconds left and three unanswered questions, what should you do?*

Answer all three, even randomly. There is **no penalty for a wrong answer** on the GRE. Blank answers score zero; random answers score, on average, about 20% on five-option questions.

**Never leave a question blank.**

---

## 4. Common Mistakes

- **Leaving questions blank.** There is no negative marking. Always fill something in.
- **Refusing to abandon a question** out of sunk-cost pride.
- **Not reading the last line of the question.** "What is the value of 2x?" after solving for x is a standard trap.
- **Doing arithmetic the calculator should do — or using the calculator for arithmetic you should do mentally.** Know which is which.
- **Skipping the sanity check.** A negative length, a probability above 1, or a percentage over 100 signals an error.
- **Panicking after a hard question.** Difficulty within a section says nothing about how you are performing.
- **Neglecting scratch work.** Mental algebra under time pressure produces sign errors.

---

## 5. Speed Tricks

- **Two-pass every section.** Easy points first, always.
- **Underline what is asked** before solving.
- **Estimate before computing** — check the answer spread; if it is wide, estimation is enough.
- **Learn to recognise the shortcut families:** Pythagorean triples, percent-fraction equivalents, special right triangles, sum = mean x count, LCM units for work problems.
- **Write down every intermediate value.** Re-deriving a number you already had is pure waste.
- **Practise with a timer from day one.** Untimed accuracy does not transfer automatically to timed accuracy.

## 6. Self-Check

**Q1.** You have 4 minutes left and 3 questions remaining, one of which looks very hard. What is your plan?

**Q2.** Is there a penalty for guessing on the GRE?

**Q3.** You solved for x = 7, and the question asks for x^2 - 1. What is the answer, and what is the lesson?

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

**A1.** Do the two manageable ones first (about 3 minutes), then put *any* answer on the hard one. Never leave it blank.

**A2.** **No.** Wrong answers cost nothing beyond the missed point, so always answer.

**A3.** 48. The lesson: **read the last line of the question** — solving for x is not the same as answering it.

</details>

## 7. One-Line Summary for the Board

> Every question is worth the same. Two passes, 30-second triage, never leave a blank, and always read the last line.
