Q1 · ALG-EXP-001 — Answer: C

Step 1 — the distributive law says the 3 multiplies every term inside the bracket. 3(x + 4) = 3x + 34 Step 2 — evaluate each product: 3x = 3x and 34 = 12. So 3(x + 4) = 3x + 12. Check: put x = 2. The original is 3(2 + 4) = 36 = 18. The answer is 32 + 12 = 6 + 12 = 18. They agree. Answer: C.

Trap. Multiplying only the first term and copying the 4 across, giving 3x + 4, which is choice A. Adding 3 + 4 instead of multiplying gives 3x + 7, choice B. Dividing by the 3 instead of multiplying by it on the first term only - turning 3x into x while copying the 12 across - gives x + 12, choice D. Adding the coefficient 3 to the 4 to make a single like term gives 7x, choice E, but 3x and 12 are unlike terms and cannot be combined at all.

Q2 · ALG-EXP-005 — Answer: B

Step 1 — you may cancel a common FACTOR, never a single term out of a sum, so split the fraction first. (x^2 + x)/x = x^2/x + x/x Step 2 — simplify each piece: x^2/x = x and x/x = 1. So the expression is x + 1. Alternative route: factor the numerator as x(x + 1), then the x is a genuine common factor of numerator and denominator and cancels, leaving x + 1. Check: put x = 3. The original is (9 + 3)/3 = 12/3 = 4, and x + 1 = 4. They agree. Answer: B.

Trap. Cancelling the lone +x in the numerator against the denominator, which leaves x^2 + 1 - choice A - even though x is not a factor of the whole numerator. Doing that same illegal cancellation and then discarding the leftover 1 gives x^2, choice C. Cancelling the denominator against the x^2 only and keeping nothing else gives x, choice D. Choice E, 1 + 1/x, comes from dividing both numerator terms by x^2 instead of by x - taking the highest power in the numerator as the divisor - since x^2/x^2 = 1 and x/x^2 = 1/x. Cancelling is legal only against a factor of the WHOLE numerator, and the divisor is the denominator you were actually given.

Q3 · ALG-EXP-008 — Answer: A

Step 1 — expand Quantity A in full. (x + 2)^2 = (x + 2)(x + 2) = x^2 + 2x + 2x + 4 = x^2 + 4x + 4. Step 2 — subtract Quantity B from Quantity A. (x^2 + 4x + 4) - (x^2 + 4) = 4x Step 3 — x is a positive integer, so 4x > 0, which means Quantity A is always the larger quantity. Check: at x = 1, Quantity A = 9 and Quantity B = 5. At x = 5, Quantity A = 49 and Quantity B = 29. Quantity A wins both times. Answer: A.

Trap. Believing (x + 2)^2 = x^2 + 2^2 and calling the columns equal, which is choice C. The square of a sum always carries the middle term 2ab; here that missing term is 4x, and it is exactly the gap between the two columns.

Q4 · ALG-EXP-010 — Answer: 35

Step 1 — distribute the 4: 4(2x^2 - 1) = 8x^2 - 4. Step 2 — distribute the -3 across both terms: -3(x^2 + 2) = -3x^2 - 6. Step 3 — collect like terms. 8x^2 - 4 - 3x^2 - 6 = (8x^2 - 3x^2) + (-4 - 6) = 5x^2 - 10 Step 4 — substitute x = 3, respecting order of operations: square first, then multiply. 5x^2 = 5 * 3^2 = 5 * 9 = 45, so 5x^2 - 10 = 45 - 10 = 35. Check: substitute x = 3 into the original instead. 4(2*9 - 1) - 3(9 + 2) = 4(17) - 3(11) = 68 - 33 = 35. Same value. Answer: 35.

Trap. Evaluating 5x^2 at x = 3 as (5 * 3)^2 = 225, giving 215. The exponent binds to x alone, so you square before you multiply by the coefficient: 5 * 3^2 = 45, not 225. The other common slip is distributing the minus sign to the first term only, writing -3x^2 + 6 instead of -3x^2 - 6; that gives 5x^2 + 2, which at x = 3 is 47.

Q5 · ALG-EXP-012 — Answer: A, C

Expand each option and compare with x^2 - 16. A - (x - 4)(x + 4) = x^2 + 4x - 4x - 16 = x^2 - 16. This is the difference-of-squares form read straight off the target. True. B - (x - 4)^2 = x^2 - 8x + 16. A middle term -8x has appeared and the constant has the wrong sign. False. C - (x - 2)(x + 2) - 12 = (x^2 - 4) - 12 = x^2 - 16. A DIFFERENT difference of squares, x^2 - 2^2, with the constant then corrected by 12. Nothing is factored out here and no bracket is shared with A, so this option has to be expanded and the constants combined on its own. True. D - (x - 8)(x + 2) = x^2 + 2x - 8x - 16 = x^2 - 6x - 16. The constant is right but a -6x survives. False. E - x^2 - 4^2 - 8x = x^2 - 16 - 8x. The -16 is correct, but the -8x from the (x - 4)^2 expansion has been carried along by mistake. False. Check at x = 5: the target is 25 - 16 = 9. A gives 1 * 9 = 9 and C gives 3 * 7 - 12 = 21 - 12 = 9. B gives 1, D gives -21 and E gives 25 - 16 - 40 = -31, so all three are correctly rejected. Answer: A and C.

Trap. Selecting B by confusing the two identities: (a - b)^2 is a^2 - 2ab + b^2, while a^2 - b^2 is (a + b)(a - b). Selecting E is the same confusion half-corrected - the constant has been fixed to -16 but the -8x is still there. Selecting D because the -16 looks right, without checking that the x-terms cancel; they do not, -8x + 2x = -6x. Rejecting C because it is not a single product and the -12 looks arbitrary; the only test that settles an option is expanding it, and x^2 - 4 - 12 is x^2 - 16.

Q6 · ALG-EXP-014 — Answer: B

Step 1 — multiply each term of the first bracket by each term of the second. 2x * 3x = 6x^2 2x * (-4) = -8x 3 * 3x = 9x 3 * (-4) = -12 Step 2 — collect the two middle terms: -8x + 9x = +x. So the product is 6x^2 + x - 12. Check: put x = 1. The original is (2 + 3)(3 - 4) = 5 * (-1) = -5, and 6 + 1 - 12 = -5. They agree. Answer: B.

The middle term is where the sign slips happen: -8x - 9x = -17x gives choice E, +8x + 9x = +17x gives choice D, and subtracting in the wrong order, 8x - 9x = -x, gives choice C. Q7 · ALG-EXP-020 — Answer: A

Step 1 — clear the inner denominators by multiplying the top and the bottom of the big fraction by xy, the least common multiple of x, y and xy. Numerator: xy(1/x + 1/y) = y + x Denominator: xy * 1/(xy) = 1 Step 2 — the whole expression is therefore (x + y)/1 = x + y. Alternative route: dividing by 1/(xy) is the same as multiplying by xy, so (1/x + 1/y) * xy = y + x. Check: put x = 2 and y = 3. The original is (1/2 + 1/3) / (1/6) = (5/6) / (1/6) = 5, and x + y = 5. They agree. Answer: A.

Trap. Stopping after simplifying only the numerator and answering (x + y)/(xy), which is choice D - that is the numerator alone, not the quotient. Flipping the wrong fraction and multiplying by 1/(xy) instead of xy lands on choice C. Multiplying by xy twice, once for each of the two inner denominators, gives xy(x + y), choice B, and cancelling the numerator's sum against the denominator entirely leaves xy, choice E.

Q8 · ALG-EXP-022 — Answer: 2320

Step 1 — x^4 - y^4 is a difference of squares of squares: x^4 - y^4 = (x^2 - y^2)(x^2 + y^2). Step 2 — the first factor is another difference of squares: x^2 - y^2 = (x - y)(x + y) = 4 * 10 = 40. Step 3 — get x^2 + y^2 from the two derived forms. (x + y)^2 = x^2 + 2xy + y^2 = 100 and (x - y)^2 = x^2 - 2xy + y^2 = 16. Adding them kills the 2xy term: 2(x^2 + y^2) = 100 + 16 = 116, so x^2 + y^2 = 58. Step 4 — multiply: x^4 - y^4 = 40 * 58 = 2320. Check: x - y = 4 and x + y = 10 give x = 7 and y = 3. Then 7^4 - 3^4 = 2401 - 81 = 2320. Correct. Answer: 2320.

Trap. Answering 40, which is only x^2 - y^2, the first of the two factors. The other error is writing x^4 - y^4 = (x - y)^4 * something, or treating x^2 + y^2 as (x + y)^2 = 100, which gives 40 * 100 = 4000.

Q9 · ALG-EXP-025 — Answer: A, E

An expression is undefined exactly where its denominator equals 0. The numerator is irrelevant to that question. Step 1 — set the denominator to 0: x^2 - 9 = 0, so x^2 = 9 and x = 3 or x = -3. Step 2 — test each option in the denominator. A - x = -3: 9 - 9 = 0. Undefined. True. B - x = -2: 4 - 9 = -5, nonzero. The value is (4 + 2 - 6)/(-5) = 0, which is defined. False. C - x = 0: 0 - 9 = -9, nonzero. The value is -6/-9 = 2/3. False. D - x = 2: 4 - 9 = -5, nonzero. The value is (4 - 2 - 6)/(-5) = 4/5. False. E - x = 3: 9 - 9 = 0. Undefined. True. Note on x = 3: the numerator is also 0 there, so the expression reads 0/0. That is still undefined - a numerator of 0 does not rescue a denominator of 0. Answer: A and E.

Trap. Selecting B and D because they are the roots of the NUMERATOR, x^2 - x - 6 = (x - 3)(x + 2). Numerator roots make the expression equal to zero, not undefined. The other trap is excusing x = 3 on the grounds that the (x - 3) factor cancels; the cancellation is only valid for x not equal to 3, so the original expression is still undefined there.

Q10 · ALG-EXP-026 — Answer: C

Step 3 — the two expansions are identical term for term, so the columns are equal for every p and q, positive or not. This is the standard derived form (a + b)^2 - (a - b)^2 = 4ab, rearranged. Check: put p = 5 and q = 2. Quantity A = 49. Quantity B = 9 + 40 = 49. Equal. Answer: C.

Trap. Reasoning that because p > q the bigger-looking Quantity A must win, and choosing A. The extra +4pq in Quantity B is exactly the 4pq gap between the two squares, so it closes the difference precisely. The other error is expanding (p - q)^2 as p^2 - q^2, which gives Quantity B = p^2 - q^2 + 4pq and makes the columns look unequal.

Q12 · ALG-EXP-029 — Answer: B

Step 1 — treat it as a two-term square by grouping: let u = x + y, so the expression is (u + z)^2 = u^2 + 2uz + z^2. Step 2 — expand u^2 = (x + y)^2 = x^2 + 2xy + y^2, and 2uz = 2(x + y)z = 2xz + 2yz. Step 3 — put the pieces together. x^2 + 2xy + y^2 + 2xz + 2yz + z^2 = x^2 + y^2 + z^2 + 2xy + 2yz + 2xz Every pair of distinct letters contributes a cross term with coefficient 2, because each product appears twice in the multiplication grid. Check: put x = 1, y = 2, z = 3. The original is (1 + 2 + 3)^2 = 36. The answer gives 1 + 4 + 9 + 2(2) + 2(6) + 2(3) = 14 + 4 + 12 + 6 = 36. They agree. Answer: B.

Trap. Squaring term by term and answering x^2 + y^2 + z^2, which is choice A - the three-variable version of the (a + b)^2 = a^2 + b^2 error. Choice C is the same expansion with the coefficient 2 forgotten: each cross term such as xy appears twice in the grid, once as xy and once as yx. Choice D replaces the three separate cross terms with a single 2xyz, a term of degree 3 that cannot appear in a square of degree-1 terms. Choice E is the result of squaring x + y correctly but forgetting z's cross terms.

Q29 · ALG-EXP-049 — Answer: A, D, E

A: x - y = (2k + 1) - (2k - 1) = 2. No k remains. Same value always. B: x + y = 4k. Depends on k. C: x^2 - y^2 = (x - y)(x + y) = 2 * 4k = 8k. Depends on k. D: (x - y)^2 = 2^2 = 4. Same value always. E: xy = (2k + 1)(2k - 1) = 4k^2 - 1, so xy - 4k^2 = -1. Same value always. Check with k = 0 and k = 1: A gives 2 and 2; B gives 0 and 4; C gives 0 and 8; D gives 4 and 4; E gives -1 and -1. Answer: A, D, E.

Trap. Selecting C because x - y is constant and assuming x^2 - y^2 must be too. x^2 - y^2 = (x - y)(x + y) = 8k, which changes with k. Skipping E is the other common miss: the product (2k + 1)(2k - 1) is 4k^2 - 1, so subtracting 4k^2 leaves -1.

Q30 · ALG-EXP-050 — Answer: A

Combine the fractions: 1/x + 1/y = (x + y)/(xy). So (x + y)/20 = 3/5, which gives x + y = 12. x^2 + y^2 = (x + y)^2 - 2xy = 144 - 40 = 104. Check: x = 10 and y = 2 give 1/10 + 1/2 = 3/5, xy = 20, and 100 + 4 = 104. Answer: A.

Trap. Stopping at (x + y)^2 = 144 (choice C), or subtracting xy only once to get 124 (choice B). The identity is x^2 + y^2 = (x + y)^2 - 2xy.
