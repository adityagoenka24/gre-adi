2. Must-Know Rules

Concept Rule
Pythagoras leg^2 + leg^2 = hypotenuse^2
Converse if a^2 + b^2 = c^2, the angle opposite c is 90
Acute test a^2 + b^2 > c^2 (c the longest side)
Obtuse test a^2 + b^2 < c^2
Distance in the plane sqrt[(x2-x1)^2 + (y2-y1)^2] — Pythagoras in disguise
Diagonal of a rectangle sqrt(l^2 + w^2)
Space diagonal of a box sqrt(l^2 + w^2 + h^2)

Special right triangles (see the next capsule) give exact answers with radicals: 45-45-90 has sides 1 : 1 : sqrt(2); 30-60-90 has sides 1 : sqrt(3) : 2.

3. Worked Examples

Example 1 — Finding a leg

A right triangle has hypotenuse 26 and one leg 10. Find the other leg.

Recognise 10-24-26 (twice 5-12-13). Other leg = 24.

By formula: b^2 = 26^2 - 10^2 = 676 - 100 = 576, so b = 24. Same answer, more work.

Example 2 — Ladder problem

A 15 m ladder leans against a wall with its foot 9 m from the wall. How high up the wall does it reach?

The ladder is the hypotenuse.

h^2 = 15^2 - 9^2 = 225 - 81 = 144 -> h = 12 m. (A 9-12-15 triangle.)

Example 3 — Classifying a triangle

Is a triangle with sides 8, 11, 14 acute, right, or obtuse?

Longest side is 14. Compare 8^2 + 11^2 = 64 + 121 = 185 with 14^2 = 196.

185 < 196, so obtuse.

4. GRE Traps

  • Treating a leg as the hypotenuse. If you get an imaginary or negative result, you have swapped them.
  • Using the theorem on a non-right triangle. It only applies when there is a 90-degree angle.
  • Assuming a right angle from the picture without a marked square or stated fact.
  • Adding the sides instead of the squares. 3 + 4 = 7, not 5.
  • Forgetting to take the square root at the end.
  • c^2 = a^2 + b^2 read as c = a + b. For 3-4-5, that would give 7.
  • Not spotting a triple and grinding through the arithmetic — costly on a timed test.

5. Speed Tricks

  • Memorise the triples. 3-4-5, 5-12-13, 8-15-17, 7-24-25 and their multiples cover most GRE right triangles.
  • Check for a scaled triple before computing. If the sides share a factor, divide it out and see if a familiar triple appears.
  • The hypotenuse is always the biggest number. Use that to sanity-check.
  • The distance formula IS Pythagoras. Teach them as one idea, not two.
  • For a right triangle, area = (1/2) x leg x leg — the two legs are perpendicular, so they serve as base and height.
  • Estimate: sqrt(a^2 + b^2) is always less than a + b and more than the larger of a, b.

6. Self-Check

Q1. A right triangle has legs 9 and 12. Find the hypotenuse.

Q2. A rectangle is 8 by 15. Find its diagonal.

Q3. Is a triangle with sides 6, 8, 9 acute, right or obtuse?

Answers

A1. 9-12-15 triple -> 15.

A2. sqrt(64 + 225) = sqrt(289) = 17 (an 8-15-17 triple).

A3. 36 + 64 = 100 > 81, so acute.

7. One-Line Summary for the Board

Hypotenuse is opposite the right angle and is longest. Learn the triples — they turn calculation into recognition.
