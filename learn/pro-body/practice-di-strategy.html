Q1 · DAT-RCD-001 — Answer: D

Q2 · DAT-RCD-004 — Answer: B

Maximum value = City C = 520. Minimum value = City B = 380. Range = 520 - 380 = 140. Quantity A = 140. Quantity B = 150. 140 < 150, so Quantity B is greater.

Trap. Students may misidentify the minimum as City D (410) instead of City B (380), computing range = 520 - 410 = 110, and still conclude B is greater. The key step is correctly reading the minimum from the table.

Q8 · DAT-RCD-011 — Answer: E

Calculate total revenue (units × price) for each product: Product A: 200 × $15 = $3,000 Product B: 350 × $8 = $2,800 Product C: 120 × $25 = $3,000 Product D: 480 × $6 = $2,880 Revenues: A = $3,000, B = $2,800, C = $3,000, D = $2,880. Q17 · DAT-RCD-038 — Answer: B

Step 1 — Calculate annual totals: North: 180+220+195+240 = $835K South: 150+160+210+280 = $800K East: 200+175+230+195 = $800K Step 2 — Median of {835, 800, 800}: Sorted: 800, 800, 835. Median = 800. Quantity A = $800K. Step 3 — Mean of {835, 800, 800}: Sum = 835 + 800 + 800 = 2,435. Mean = 2,435 / 3 ≈ $811.67K. Quantity B ≈ $811.67K. $800K < $811.67K. Quantity B is greater.

Trap. Students may assume the median and mean are equal because two of the three locations share the same total ($800K). However, the presence of North ($835K) — a value above the median — pulls the mean above $800K. Whenever the distribution is not symmetric, mean ≠ median.

Q18 · DAT-RCD-KMF2-002 — Answer: C

Of the 1,000 employees surveyed, 45 percent indicated training programs, which is 450 employees, and 72 percent indicated flexible hours, which is 720 employees. The question asks what percent 450 is of 720, so divide 450 by 720: 450/720 = 0.625, which is 62.5 percent. Of the choices given, 63 percent is closest. Because both counts come from the same total of 1,000, the ratio 45/72 gives the same result without finding the counts at all.

Trap. 27% comes from subtracting the two percents, 72 − 45, which answers how many percentage points apart they are, not what percent one is of the other. 160% is 720/450, the reciprocal, which answers the reverse question. 45% and 72% are the table entries themselves.

Q19 · DAT-RCD-KMF2-003 — Answer: 16%

Work in percents of the employees surveyed. Commute time was indicated by 64 percent and health benefits by 58 percent, and 38 percent indicated both. Adding 64 and 58 counts everyone who indicated both of them twice, so subtract the overlap once to get the percent who indicated at least one of the two: 64 + 58 − 38 = 84 percent. Everyone else indicated neither, so the percent who indicated neither is 100 − 84 = 16 percent.

Trap. Adding the two percents to get 122 and stopping there, or subtracting 64 + 58 from 100 without adding the overlap back, which gives a negative figure and signals the overlap was missed. Subtracting only one factor, as in 100 − 64 = 36 or 100 − 58 = 42, ignores the other.

Q20 · DAT-RCD-KMF2-004 — Answer: C

Owning more than 2 printers is the same as owning at least 3 printers, and the table gives that count directly as 180 offices. The probability is therefore 180/750 = 18/75 = 6/25.

Trap. 16/25 is 480/750, the probability of owning at least 2 printers; that answers "more than 1" rather than "more than 2" and is the off-by-one row error. 2/5 is 300/750, the probability of owning exactly 2. 11/150 and 2/125 use the 55 and 12 rows.

Q21 · DAT-RCD-KMF2-008 — Answer: C

The range of a set of values is its greatest value minus its least value, so only the highest and lowest bar for each food matter. Cheese runs from 5 to 24, so a = 24 − 5 = 19. Poultry runs from 18 to 60, so b = 60 − 18 = 42. Ice cream runs from 12 to 20, so c = 20 − 12 = 8. Since 42 > 19 > 8, the order is b > a > c.

Trap. Ranking the foods by their 1990 values, or by how high they sit overall, puts poultry first and cheese second and ice cream last, which happens to give the same order here only by coincidence of the endpoints; the reliable route is the high-minus-low for each. The real pitfall is ice cream, which rises and then falls: its range is set by its 1960 peak of 20 and its 1940 value of 12, neither of which is an endpoint of the time span.

Q22 · DAT-RCD-KMF2-009 — Answer: D

A value with as many above it as below it is the middle value once the list is in order, and with seven lakes that is the fourth. Reading the maximum depths off the vertical axis and sorting them: Arrow 62, Cobalt 68, Birchmere 70, Elkhorn 74, Grayling 76, Dunlin 78, Fernwater 84. The fourth is 74, which belongs to Elkhorn. Three lakes are deeper (76, 78 and 84) and three are shallower (62, 68 and 70), as required.

Trap. Cobalt sits in the middle of the seven by minimum depth, which is the horizontal axis and the one the eye runs along first. Picking the point nearest the center of the cloud is also wrong: only the ordering of the maximum depths matters, not where a point sits in the plane.

Q27 · DAT-RCD-034 — Answer: B, D, E

GPA-points (enrollment × average GPA): CS 1,280; Economics 1,020; Biology 600; History 380. Total = 3,280 for 1,000 students, so the overall GPA is 3.28. A: 3.28 does not exceed 3.50. False. B: Economics 1,020 > History 380. True. C: Without Biology, (1,280 + 1,020 + 380)/800 = 3.35, which does not exceed 3.55. False. D: History has the highest average GPA (3.8) and the fewest points (380). True. E: CS and Economics together: 2,300/700 ≈ 3.29, below 3.55. True. Answer: B, D, E.

Q28 · DAT-RCD-KMF2-001 — Answer: A, C, E

The table gives percents of all 1,000 employees surveyed, so each percent is also a count out of 1,000: flexible hours 720, commute time 640, health benefits 580, training programs 450, on-site parking 300. A: there were 420 part-time employees out of 1,000, which is 42 percent, and 42 is less than 45. True. B: 720 employees indicated flexible hours and 580 indicated health benefits, but the table says nothing about how either group splits between part-time and full-time. All 580 full-time employees could have indicated health benefits while only 300 of them indicated flexible hours, with the other 420 who indicated flexible hours being part-time. That is consistent with every number given, so this need not be true. C: 720 employees indicated flexible hours, and at most 580 of them could be full-time, since there are only 580 full-time employees. So at least 720 − 580 = 140 of them are part-time. True. D: 580 employees indicated health benefits and there are exactly 580 full-time employees, so it is possible that all of them are the full-time employees, making the figure 100 percent of the full-time employees. This need not be true. E: 640 of the 1,000 employees surveyed indicated commute time, which is 64 percent, and that is more than half. True.

Trap. B and D look checkable but are not. The table reports each factor only for the 1,000 employees as a whole, never split by part-time and full-time, so any claim about what the full-time employees alone did is undetermined unless a count forces it. C is true only because 720 exceeds the 580 full-time employees; that overflow is the one piece of subgroup information the table does force.

Q29 · DAT-RCD-KMF2-011 — Answer: 3

A pair qualifies when the two lakes are ranked in opposite orders by the two depths, so list the lakes in order of minimum depth and write each one's maximum beside it: Arrow 62, Birchmere 70, Elkhorn 74, Cobalt 68, Dunlin 78, Grayling 76, Fernwater 84. Now look for a lake later in this list whose maximum is smaller than one earlier in it. There are three such pairs: Birchmere and Cobalt, 70 against 68; Elkhorn and Cobalt, 74 against 68; Dunlin and Grayling, 78 against 76. Every other pair is ranked the same way by both depths, so the answer is 3.

Trap. Counting the lakes that sit below the general upward drift rather than the pairs they form. Cobalt and Grayling are the two points below the drift, but Cobalt is involved in two qualifying pairs and Grayling in one, so the pair count is three, not two.

Q30 · DAT-RCD-KMF2-015 — Answer: D

First count the schools with more support staff than teachers. Those are the points above the line where the two counts would be equal: (36, 40), (38, 44), (42, 46), (46, 52), (50, 54), (54, 58), (56, 62) and (62, 72) — eight of them. The other twelve have more teachers. "At least one" is easier through its opposite. The probability that neither selected school qualifies is (12/20)(11/19) = 132/380, about 0.347. So the probability that at least one does is about 1 − 0.347 = 0.653, which is closest to 0.65.

Trap. 0.35 is the probability of the opposite event, the figure this method produces on the way and the easiest place to stop. 0.80 doubles 8/20, as though the two draws were independent and their chances could simply be added; they are made without replacement, and adding also double-counts the case where both qualify.
