Q1 · WRD-AGE-001 — Answer: C

Step 1 — let Rohan be r. Then Maya is r + 8. Step 2 — the sum condition gives r + (r + 8) = 34, so 2r + 8 = 34 and 2r = 26, r = 13. Step 3 — Maya is r + 8 = 13 + 8 = 21. Faster route from the capsule: with sum S = 34 and difference D = 8, the two ages are (S + D)/2 = (34 + 8)/2 = 21 and (S - D)/2 = (34 - 8)/2 = 13. The older age is the one with the plus sign. Check: 21 + 13 = 34, and 21 - 13 = 8. Both stated sentences hold. Answer: C.

Trap. Solving correctly and then reporting the younger sibling's age, 13, is choice A: the question names Maya, the older one. Choice B, 17, is half the sum with the 8-year difference ignored entirely. Choice D, 26, is 34 - 8 with the halving step forgotten, and choice E, 42, is 34 + 8 with the same omission.

Q2 · WRD-AGE-002 — Answer: B

Step 1 — let the answer be t years. In t years both ages increase by t: Carlos is 24 + t and Sofia is 3 + t. Step 2 — the condition is 24 + t = 4(3 + t). Step 3 — expand: 24 + t = 12 + 4t, so 12 = 3t and t = 4. Faster route: the age difference is 24 - 3 = 21 and it never changes. When Carlos is 4 times Sofia, the difference is 3 times Sofia's age then, so Sofia is 21/3 = 7 and that is 7 - 3 = 4 years from now. Check: in 4 years Carlos is 28 and Sofia is 7, and 47 = 28. The difference is still 28 - 7 = 21. Correct. Answer: B.

Trap. Adding the t years to Sofia only and leaving Carlos at 24 gives 24 = 4(3 + t), so 3 + t = 6 and t = 3, which is choice A. Everyone ages together. Choice C, 7, is Sofia's age at the moment the condition holds rather than the number of years until it holds, and choice E, 21, is the constant age difference. Choice D, 18, solves 24 + t = 2(3 + t), the 'twice as old' condition instead of the stated 'four times'.

Q3 · WRD-AGE-004 — Answer: A

Choice E, 54, makes that same mistake and then multiplies: treating 18 as Dev's age gives 3*18 = 54 for Priya. Choice D, 36, treats 18 as Dev's age but applies 'older by 18' instead of '3 times', giving 18 + 18.

Q4 · WRD-AGE-007 — Answer: 30

Q5 · WRD-AGE-009 — Answer: C

Step 1 — let Marcus be m today, so Nina is 2m. Today's difference is 2m - m = m. Step 2 — in 10 years Marcus is m + 10 and Nina is 2m + 10. Step 3 — that difference is (2m + 10) - (m + 10) = m. The two 10s cancel. Step 4 — both columns equal m, whatever m is, so they are equal. Check: take m = 7. Today 14 and 7, difference 7. In 10 years 24 and 17, difference 7. Take m = 30: today 60 and 30, difference 30; in 10 years 70 and 40, difference 30. Equal either way. Answer: C.

Trap. Choice A comes from thinking the gap grows because the older person is older: Nina gains 10 years, so the gap must gain something. It does not, because Marcus gains the same 10. Choice D comes from noticing that Marcus's age m is unknown and concluding nothing can be compared, but the unknown m appears identically in both columns and cancels. The difference between two ages is invariant in time; only the ratio changes.

Q9 · WRD-AGE-020 — Answer: B, C, D

Step 1 — the ratio is k/(k - 12). Require 2 <= k/(k - 12) <= 3, and note k - 12 >= 1 so the denominator is positive and the inequalities can be cross-multiplied without flipping. Step 2 — the left inequality: k >= 2(k - 12) = 2k - 24, so 24 >= k, that is k <= 24. Step 3 — the right inequality: k <= 3(k - 12) = 3k - 36, so 36 <= 2k, that is k >= 18. Step 4 — the condition is 18 <= k <= 24. A. False. k = 16 gives 16/4 = 4, which is above 3. B. True. k = 18 gives 18/6 = 3 exactly, and 'inclusive' admits it. C. True. k = 21 gives 21/9 = 7/3, about 2.33. D. True. k = 24 gives 24/12 = 2 exactly, admitted by 'inclusive'. E. False. k = 26 gives 26/14 = 13/7, about 1.86, below 2. Check: the ratio falls as k grows, from 4 at k = 16 down to 1.86 at k = 26, so the admissible values form one unbroken block, 18 through 24, and the two endpoints are exactly the values that hit 3 and 2. Correct. Answer: B, C and D.

Trap. Dropping the endpoints because the word 'between' feels strict rejects B and D and leaves only C; the stem says 'inclusive', so both boundary values count. The other live error is expecting the ratio to rise with k: the gap of 12 years is fixed, so as both ages grow the ratio falls toward 1, which is why the small value k = 16 fails at the top end and the large value k = 26 fails at the bottom.
