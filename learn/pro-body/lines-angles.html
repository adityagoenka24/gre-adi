2. Must-Know Rules

Concept Rule
Straight line angles sum to 180
Around a point angles sum to 360
Vertically opposite angles equal
Complementary angles sum to 90
Supplementary angles sum to 180
Perpendicular lines meet at 90

With parallel lines and a transversal:

Pair Relationship
Corresponding angles (F shape) equal
Alternate interior angles (Z shape) equal
Alternate exterior angles equal
Co-interior / same-side interior (C or U shape) supplementary (sum 180)

The practical shortcut. Mark one angle as x. Every other angle in the figure is either x or 180 - x. Decide which by asking: "does this angle look acute or obtuse compared to the one I marked?" On GRE figures (which are drawn to scale unless stated otherwise) this visual check is reliable.

Angle types: acute (< 90), right (= 90), obtuse (between 90 and 180), straight (= 180), reflex (between 180 and 360).

3. Worked Examples

Example 1 — Straight line chain

Three angles on a straight line measure (2x + 10), (3x), and (x - 10) degrees. Find x.

Sum = 180:

(2x + 10) + 3x + (x - 10) = 180

6x = 180 -> x = 30.

The angles are 70, 90 and 20. Sum 180. Correct.

Example 2 — Parallel lines

Two parallel lines are cut by a transversal. One of the co-interior angles is 3 times the other. Find both.

Co-interior angles are supplementary: x + 3x = 180

4x = 180 -> x = 45.

The angles are 45 and 135 degrees.

Example 3 — Angles around a point

Four angles around a point are in the ratio 2 : 3 : 4 : 6. Find the largest.

Total parts = 15, and the angles sum to 360.

One part = 360/15 = 24.

Largest = 6 x 24 = 144 degrees.

4. GRE Traps

  • Assuming lines are parallel because they look parallel. They must be marked or stated.
  • Assuming an angle is a right angle because it looks like one. GRE figures are drawn to scale but a right angle must be marked or deducible.
  • Alternate vs co-interior confusion. Alternate angles are equal; co-interior angles are supplementary. The Z shape means equal, the C shape means 180.
  • Forgetting reflex angles. "The angles around a point sum to 360" includes the reflex one if the figure shows it.
  • Using the figure's apparent measurements when the problem says "figure not drawn to scale".
  • Two angles adding to 90 called supplementary. Complementary is 90; supplementary is 180.

5. Speed Tricks

  • Label one angle x and propagate. In a parallel-lines figure, every angle falls out in seconds.
  • Look for the Z and the F. Z = alternate (equal). F = corresponding (equal). C or U = co-interior (supplementary).
  • Extend lines in your sketch to reveal a transversal that is not drawn.
  • Draw an auxiliary parallel line through any "bent" vertex between two parallels — this converts a zig-zag into two simple transversal setups.
  • Sum-check at the end. Straight lines to 180, points to 360. It catches arithmetic slips.

6. Self-Check

Q1. Two supplementary angles are in the ratio 4 : 5. Find them.

Q2. Two parallel lines are cut by a transversal; one angle is 63 degrees. What are the possible measures of the other seven angles?

Q3. An angle is 20 degrees more than its complement. Find the angle.

Answers

A1. 9 parts = 180, one part = 20. Angles are 80 and 100 degrees.

A2. Only two values exist: 63 and 117 degrees (four of each).

A3. x + (x - 20) = 90 -> 2x = 110 -> x = 55 degrees.

7. One-Line Summary for the Board

With parallel lines there are only two angles on the page: x and 180 - x. Z = equal, C = supplementary.
