Part C — Worked Solutions

Q1 · STA-PRM-001 — Answer: D

Step 1 — decide whether order matters. The books are distinguishable and a different left-to-right order counts as a different placement, so this is a permutation of all 5 books. Step 2 — draw one slot per shelf position and count the choices. No book may be reused, so the counts drop by one each time: 5 x 4 x 3 x 2 x 1 Step 3 — multiply: 5 x 4 = 20, 20 x 3 = 60, 60 x 2 = 120, 120 x 1 = 120. That is exactly 5! = 120. Answer: 120.

Trap. Adding the slot counts instead of multiplying gives 5 + 4 + 3 + 2 + 1 = 15 (choice A); sequential choices multiply. Stopping part-way through the slots gives 5 x 4 = 20 (choice B) or 5 x 4 x 3 = 60 (choice C). Choice E, 5^5 = 3125, lets a book be reused in every position, but each book is placed exactly once.

Q2 · STA-PRM-003 — Answer: 10

Step 1 — 0! = 1 by definition. It is not zero. Step 2 — 1! = 1. Step 3 — 2! = 2 x 1 = 2. Step 4 — 3! = 3 x 2 x 1 = 6. Step 5 — add: 1 + 1 + 2 + 6 = 10. Answer: 10.

Trap. Treating 0! as 0 and answering 9. The convention 0! = 1 is exactly what makes P(n, n) = n!/0! = n! come out right, so it is worth memorising rather than deriving under time pressure.

Q3 · STA-PRM-004 — Answer: C

Step 1 — does order matter? Yes. Making Ann president and Bob vice president is a different outcome from making Bob president and Ann vice president, because the three offices are distinct positions. Step 2 — repetition is not allowed, since no member holds two offices. Draw three slots: president: 8 choices vice president: 7 members left treasurer: 6 members left Step 3 — multiply: 8 x 7 = 56, and 56 x 6 = 336. This is P(8, 3) = 8!/5! = 336. Answer: 336.

Trap. Choice B, 56 = C(8, 3), counts unordered groups of three and so treats the three offices as interchangeable; the offices are named, so order matters. Choice D, 8^3 = 512, allows one member to hold more than one office. Choice E, 8! = 40320, arranges all eight members instead of filling only three posts.

Q4 · STA-PRM-010 — Answer: A

Column A - order matters, so this is a permutation of all 3 books: 3! = 3 x 2 x 1 = 6. The six orders are ABC, ACB, BAC, BCA, CAB, CBA. Column B - order does not matter, and there is exactly one way to select all 3 books out of 3: C(3, 3) = 1. The single selection is the set {A, B, C}. 6 > 1, so Column A is greater.

Trap. Reading 'arrange' and 'select' as the same operation and choosing C. For the same r objects the ordered count is always r! times the unordered count, so here Column A is 3! = 6 times Column B.

Q5 · STA-PRM-013 — Answer: D

Step 1 — the two characters are chosen one after the other and independently, so the fundamental counting principle applies: multiply the number of choices at each stage. Step 2 — first character: 3 choices. Second character: 2 choices. Step 3 — 3 x 2 = 6. Check by listing: 14, 15, 24, 25, 34, 35. That is 6 codes. Correct. Answer: 6.

Trap. Adding instead of multiplying: 3 + 2 = 5 (choice C). Addition is for mutually exclusive alternatives ('this or that'); sequential independent choices ('this and then that') multiply. Choice E, 9 = 3^2, uses the first set for both positions and ignores that the second character comes from a different, smaller set.

Q6 · STA-PRM-025 — Answer: B

Step 1 — glue Anna and Ben into one block. The table now holds 5 units: the block plus the 4 other people. Step 2 — arrange 5 units around a circle. Rotations are the same seating, so one degree of freedom is lost: (5 - 1)! = 4! = 24. Step 3 — inside the block Anna and Ben can sit in either order: 2! = 2. Step 4 — multiply: 24 x 2 = 48. Check by a different route: fix Anna's seat to kill rotations. Ben must take one of her 2 neighbouring seats, and the remaining 4 people fill the remaining 4 seats in 4! = 24 ways, giving 2 x 24 = 48. Correct. Answer: 48.

Trap. Choice C, 120 = (6 - 1)!, is the unrestricted circular count and ignores the adjacency requirement. Choice A, 24, forgets that the glued block has 2 internal orders. Choice D, 240 = 5! x 2, uses 5! instead of (5 - 1)! for the circle of 5 units, that is, forgets that rotations are the same seating.

Q7 · STA-PRM-027 — Answer: B

Step 1 — write out the multiset: 6 letters, with P three times, E twice and R once. Because identical letters are indistinguishable, swapping two P's does not create a new ordering. Step 2 — start as if all 6 letters were distinct: 6! = 720. Step 3 — divide by the factorial of each repeat: 3! = 6 for the three P's and 2! = 2 for the two E's. 720/(6 x 2) = 720/12 = 60. Check by a second route: choose the 3 positions for the P's, C(6, 3) = 20, then order E, E, R in the remaining 3 positions, 3!/2! = 3. 20 x 3 = 60. Correct. Answer: 60.

Trap. Choice E, 720 = 6!, skips the division entirely. Choice C, 120, divides only by 3! for the P's and choice D, 360, divides only by 2! for the E's - every repeated letter needs its own factorial in the denominator. Choice A, 20 = C(6, 3), places the three P's and then forgets to order E, E and R in the remaining slots.

Q8 · STA-PRM-028 — Answer: 60

Method 1 - direct. Step 1 — choose which slot A occupies: 3 choices (first, second or third). Step 2 — fill the other two slots from the remaining 5 letters, in order and without repetition: P(5, 2) = 5 x 4 = 20. Step 3 — multiply: 3 x 20 = 60. Method 2 - complement. All 3-letter arrangements from 6 letters: P(6, 3) = 6 x 5 x 4 = 120. Arrangements that avoid A entirely: P(5, 3) = 5 x 4 x 3 = 60. Arrangements containing A: 120 - 60 = 60. Both methods agree. Answer: 60.

Trap. Using C(5, 2) = 10 for the two non-A slots and answering 3 x 10 = 30. Those two slots are distinct positions, so BC and CB are different arrangements and the count is P(5, 2) = 20. Answering 120 ignores the requirement that A appear at all.

Q9 · STA-PRM-026 — Answer: B

Step 1 — expand Column A: P(n, 3) = n!/(n - 3)! = n(n - 1)(n - 2). Step 2 — factor Column B: n^3 - n = n(n^2 - 1) = n(n - 1)(n + 1). Step 3 — the two columns share the factor n(n - 1), which is positive for n >= 3. They differ only in the last factor: n - 2 against n + 1. Since n + 1 > n - 2 always, Column B is greater for every n >= 3. Check n = 3: P(3, 3) = 6 while 27 - 3 = 24. Check n = 10: P(10, 3) = 720 while 1000 - 10 = 990. Column B wins both times. Column B is greater.

Trap. Remembering the true identity P(n, 2) = n(n - 1) = n^2 - n and generalising it to P(n, 3) = n^3 - n, which gives C. The pattern does not continue: the third factor of P(n, 3) is n - 2, while n^3 - n factors as n(n - 1)(n + 1). Because the answer holds for every n >= 3, D is wrong too.

Q10 · STA-PRM-030 — Answer: B

Column A - NOON has 4 letters with N twice and O twice, so two repeats must be divided out: 4!/(2! x 2!) = 24/4 = 6. The six are NNOO, NONO, NOON, ONNO, ONON, OONN. Column B - MOON has 4 letters with only O repeated: 4!/2! = 24/2 = 12. 12 > 6, so Column B is greater.

Trap. Seeing two 4-letter words and choosing C. Equal length does not mean equal count: NOON carries two repeated pairs and needs a denominator of 2! x 2! = 4, while MOON needs only 2!. Every extra repeat cuts the number of distinct orderings.

Q13 · STA-PRM-035 — Answer: C

In any arrangement red is either left of blue or right of blue, never both and never neither. Swapping just the red and blue flags turns one kind into the other, one to one, so the two kinds are equally numerous. Step 3 — each kind is half the total: 120/2 = 60. Answer: 60.

Q14 · STA-PRM-039 — Answer: D

Use the gap method: seat the unrestricted people first, then drop the restricted ones into the gaps they create. Step 1 — arrange the 3 women in the row. They are distinguishable: 3! = 6 ways. Step 2 — three women in a row create 4 gaps, one before each woman and one after the last: _ W _ W _ W _ If every man sits in a gap of his own, no two men can touch. Step 3 — the 3 distinguishable men occupy 3 different gaps out of 4, and which man goes in which gap matters: P(4, 3) = 4 x 3 x 2 = 24. Step 4 — multiply the two stages: 6 x 24 = 144. Check: the unrestricted total is 6! = 720, so the answer must be a proper fraction of it; 144 is exactly one fifth. A direct enumeration of all 720 seatings also gives 144. Answer: 144.

Trap. Choice C, 72 = 2 x 3! x 3!, counts only the two perfectly alternating patterns MWMWMW and WMWMWM. Alternation is stricter than the condition asked: MWMWWM also keeps the men apart. Choice B, 36 = 3! x 3!, is a single alternating pattern. Choice A, 24 = P(4, 3), places the men in the gaps but forgets to arrange the women. Choice E, 720 = 6!, ignores the restriction.

Q17 · STA-PRM-042 — Answer: C

Step 1 — write both sides with factorials. P(A, B) = A!/(A - B)! P(A - 1, B - 1) = (A - 1)!/((A - 1) - (B - 1))! = (A - 1)!/(A - B)! Step 2 — multiply out Column B: A x (A - 1)!/(A - B)! = A!/(A - B)!, because A x (A - 1)! = A!. Step 3 — the two expressions are the same, so the columns are equal for every A > B > 1. Why it makes sense: filling the first of the B ordered slots (A ways) and then arranging B - 1 of the remaining A - 1 objects is the same job as arranging B of A objects. Check A = 5, B = 3: P(5, 3) = 60 and 5 x P(4, 2) = 5 x 12 = 60. Check A = 7, B = 4: P(7, 4) = 840 and 7 x P(6, 3) = 7 x 120 = 840. Correct. The two quantities are equal.

Trap. Testing one pair of values, seeing equality, then choosing D because A and B are unknown; the factorials cancel identically, so equality is forced for every admissible pair. The opposite slip is expanding P(A - 1, B - 1) as (A - 1)!/(A - B - 1)!, which mishandles the double shift, inflates Column B by a factor of A - B and points at B.

Q18 · STA-PRM-043 — Answer: C

Q19 · STA-PRM-044 — Answer: 5712

An integer is divisible by 5 exactly when its units digit is 0 or 5, and those two cases are mutually exclusive, so count each and add. Case 1 - units digit is 0. Leading digit: any of 1 through 9, so 9 choices. Middle three digits: taken in order, without repetition, from the 8 digits not yet used: P(8, 3) = 8 x 7 x 6 = 336. Subtotal = 9 x 336 = 3024. Case 2 - units digit is 5. Leading digit: cannot be 0 and cannot be the 5 already used, so 8 choices. Middle three digits: from the 8 digits not yet used: P(8, 3) = 336. Subtotal = 8 x 336 = 2688. Step 3 — add the cases: 3024 + 2688 = 5712. Answer: 5712.

Trap. Giving the leading digit 9 choices in Case 2 as well and answering 3024 + 3024 = 6048. When the units digit is 5, both 0 and 5 are barred from the front, leaving 8. Ignoring the leading-zero rule altogether gives the same wrong 6048 = 2 x P(9, 4), so 6048 is the value two different mistakes converge on.

Q28 · STA-PRM-056 — Answer: C

Step 4 — so each distinct necklace corresponds to 10 x 2 = 20 of the linear orders, and because all ten beads are different no necklace is left unchanged by any of those 20 motions, so no necklace is over- or under-counted.
