Part C — Worked Solutions

Q1 · STA-PRM-001 — Answer: D

Step 1 — decide whether order matters. The books are distinguishable and a different left-to-right order counts as a different placement, so this is a permutation of all 5 books. Step 2 — draw one slot per shelf position and count the choices. No book may be reused, so the counts drop by one each time: 5 x 4 x 3 x 2 x 1 Step 3 — multiply: 5 x 4 = 20, 20 x 3 = 60, 60 x 2 = 120, 120 x 1 = 120. That is exactly 5! = 120. Answer: 120.

Trap. Adding the slot counts instead of multiplying gives 5 + 4 + 3 + 2 + 1 = 15 (choice A); sequential choices multiply. Stopping part-way through the slots gives 5 x 4 = 20 (choice B) or 5 x 4 x 3 = 60 (choice C). Choice E, 5^5 = 3125, lets a book be reused in every position, but each book is placed exactly once.

Q2 · STA-PRM-004 — Answer: C

Step 1 — does order matter? Yes. Making Ann president and Bob vice president is a different outcome from making Bob president and Ann vice president, because the three offices are distinct positions. Step 2 — repetition is not allowed, since no member holds two offices. Draw three slots: president: 8 choices vice president: 7 members left treasurer: 6 members left Step 3 — multiply: 8 x 7 = 56, and 56 x 6 = 336. This is P(8, 3) = 8!/5! = 336. Answer: 336.

Trap. Choice B, 56 = C(8, 3), counts unordered groups of three and so treats the three offices as interchangeable; the offices are named, so order matters. Choice D, 8^3 = 512, allows one member to hold more than one office. Choice E, 8! = 40320, arranges all eight members instead of filling only three posts.

Q3 · STA-PRM-010 — Answer: A

Quantity A - order matters, so this is a permutation of all 3 books: 3! = 3 x 2 x 1 = 6. The six orders are ABC, ACB, BAC, BCA, CAB, CBA. Quantity B - order does not matter, and there is exactly one way to select all 3 books out of 3: C(3, 3) = 1. The single selection is the set {A, B, C}. 6 > 1, so Quantity A is greater.

Trap. Reading 'arrange' and 'select' as the same operation and choosing C. For the same r objects the ordered count is always r! times the unordered count, so here Quantity A is 3! = 6 times Quantity B.

Q4 · STA-PRM-013 — Answer: D

Step 1 — the two characters are chosen one after the other and independently, so the fundamental counting principle applies: multiply the number of choices at each stage. Step 2 — first character: 3 choices. Second character: 2 choices. Step 3 — 3 x 2 = 6. Check by listing: 14, 15, 24, 25, 34, 35. That is 6 codes. Correct. Answer: 6.

Trap. Adding instead of multiplying: 3 + 2 = 5 (choice C). Addition is for mutually exclusive alternatives ('this or that'); sequential independent choices ('this and then that') multiply. Choice E, 9 = 3^2, uses the first set for both positions and ignores that the second character comes from a different, smaller set.

Q5 · STA-PRM-032 — Answer: 24

Person A is fixed in seat 3. The remaining 4 people fill the remaining 4 seats in 4! = 24 ways.

Trap. Students compute 5! = 120, ignoring that A's position is fixed. Once A is placed, only 4 people remain to fill 4 positions: 4! = 24.

Q6 · STA-PRM-025 — Answer: B

Step 1 — glue Anna and Ben into one block. The table now holds 5 units: the block plus the 4 other people. Step 2 — arrange 5 units around a circle. Rotations are the same seating, so one degree of freedom is lost: (5 - 1)! = 4! = 24. Step 3 — inside the block Anna and Ben can sit in either order: 2! = 2. Step 4 — multiply: 24 x 2 = 48. Check by a different route: fix Anna's seat to kill rotations. Ben must take one of her 2 neighboring seats, and the remaining 4 people fill the remaining 4 seats in 4! = 24 ways, giving 2 x 24 = 48. Correct. Answer: 48.

Trap. Choice C, 120 = (6 - 1)!, is the unrestricted circular count and ignores the adjacency requirement. Choice A, 24, forgets that the glued block has 2 internal orders. Choice D, 240 = 5! x 2, uses 5! instead of (5 - 1)! for the circle of 5 units, that is, forgets that rotations are the same seating.

Q7 · STA-PRM-026 — Answer: B

Step 1 — expand Quantity A: P(n, 3) = n!/(n - 3)! = n(n - 1)(n - 2). Step 2 — factor Quantity B: n^3 - n = n(n^2 - 1) = n(n - 1)(n + 1). Step 3 — the two columns share the factor n(n - 1), which is positive for n >= 3. They differ only in the last factor: n - 2 against n + 1. Since n + 1 > n - 2 always, Quantity B is greater for every n >= 3. Check n = 3: P(3, 3) = 6 while 27 - 3 = 24. Check n = 10: P(10, 3) = 720 while 1000 - 10 = 990. Quantity B wins both times. Quantity B is greater.

Trap. Remembering the true identity P(n, 2) = n(n - 1) = n^2 - n and generalising it to P(n, 3) = n^3 - n, which gives C. The pattern does not continue: the third factor of P(n, 3) is n - 2, while n^3 - n factors as n(n - 1)(n + 1). Because the answer holds for every n >= 3, D is wrong too.

Q8 · STA-PRM-027 — Answer: B

Step 1 — write out the multiset: 6 letters, with P three times, E twice and R once. Because identical letters are indistinguishable, swapping two P's does not create a new ordering. Step 2 — start as if all 6 letters were distinct: 6! = 720. Step 3 — divide by the factorial of each repeat: 3! = 6 for the three P's and 2! = 2 for the two E's. 720/(6 x 2) = 720/12 = 60. Check by a second route: choose the 3 positions for the P's, C(6, 3) = 20, then order E, E, R in the remaining 3 positions, 3!/2! = 3. 20 x 3 = 60. Correct. Answer: 60.

Trap. Choice E, 720 = 6!, skips the division entirely. Choice C, 120, divides only by 3! for the P's and choice D, 360, divides only by 2! for the E's - every repeated letter needs its own factorial in the denominator. Choice A, 20 = C(6, 3), places the three P's and then forgets to order E, E and R in the remaining slots.

Q9 · STA-PRM-028 — Answer: 60

Method 1 - direct. Step 1 — choose which slot A occupies: 3 choices (first, second or third). Step 2 — fill the other two slots from the remaining 5 letters, in order and without repetition: P(5, 2) = 5 x 4 = 20. Step 3 — multiply: 3 x 20 = 60. Method 2 - complement. All 3-letter arrangements from 6 letters: P(6, 3) = 6 x 5 x 4 = 120. Arrangements that avoid A entirely: P(5, 3) = 5 x 4 x 3 = 60. Arrangements containing A: 120 - 60 = 60. Both methods agree. Answer: 60.

Trap. Using C(5, 2) = 10 for the two non-A slots and answering 3 x 10 = 30. Those two slots are distinct positions, so BC and CB are different arrangements and the count is P(5, 2) = 20. Answering 120 ignores the requirement that A appear at all.

Q10 · STA-PRM-029 — Answer: A, B, D

Fix A in the first position. The other 4 distinct letters fill positions 2 through 5 in 4! = 24 ways, so the count to match is 24. A - 24 is that count. True. B - P(4, 4) = 4!/0! = 24/1 = 24, using 0! = 1. True. C - P(5, 4) = 5 x 4 x 3 x 2 = 120. That counts 4-letter arrangements drawn from 5 letters, a different question, and it is not 24. False. D - 5!/5 = 120/5 = 24. By symmetry each of the 5 letters starts exactly one fifth of the 120 arrangements. True. E - 5! - 4! = 120 - 24 = 96. False. Answer: A, B, D.

Trap. E looks like complementary counting but removes the wrong set: the arrangements that do NOT begin with A number 4 x 4! = 96, so the correct complement is 120 - 96 = 24, not 120 - 24 = 96. C catches anyone who reads 'the remaining four letters are arranged' as P(5, 4) rather than P(4, 4).

Q11 · STA-PRM-030 — Answer: B

Quantity A - NOON has 4 letters with N twice and O twice, so two repeats must be divided out: 4!/(2! x 2!) = 24/4 = 6. The six are NNOO, NONO, NOON, ONNO, ONON, OONN. Quantity B - MOON has 4 letters with only O repeated: 4!/2! = 24/2 = 12. 12 > 6, so Quantity B is greater.

Trap. Seeing two 4-letter words and choosing C. Equal length does not mean equal count: NOON carries two repeated pairs and needs a denominator of 2! x 2! = 4, while MOON needs only 2!. Every extra repeat cuts the number of distinct orderings.

Q12 · STA-PRM-035 — Answer: C

Step 1 — count all arrangements. The 5 flags are distinct and each is used once: 5! = 120. Step 2 — use symmetry. In any arrangement red is either left of blue or right of blue, never both and never neither. Swapping just the red and blue flags turns one kind into the other, one to one, so the two kinds are equally numerous. Step 3 — each kind is half the total: 120/2 = 60. Answer: 60.

Trap. Choice E, 120, ignores the condition altogether. Choice A, 24 = 4!, glues red immediately in front of blue, but the stem allows them to be far apart. Choice B, 48 = 4! x 2, counts the arrangements in which they are adjacent in either order, and choice D, 72 = 120 - 48, counts the arrangements in which they are NOT adjacent - each answers a different question.

Q13 · STA-PRM-036 — Answer: A, C, E

Every count is 5! = 120 divided by the factorial of each repeat. A - TIGER has no repeats: 120/1 = 120. 120 > 30. True. B - TEETH has two repeated pairs: 120/(2! x 2!) = 120/4 = 30. Exactly 30, and the question asks for strictly greater. False. C - ELITE has one repeated pair, E twice: 120/2! = 60. 60 > 30. True. D - AABBB has a pair and a triple: 120/(2! x 3!) = 120/12 = 10. False. E - SEVEN has one repeated pair, E twice: 120/2! = 60. 60 > 30. True. Answer: A, C, E.

Trap. Including B: TEETH gives exactly 30, and 'strictly greater than 30' excludes it. The other slip is on D, where students divide by a single 3! for the B's and get 120/6 = 20, forgetting the two A's; the answer stays false, but for the wrong reason.

Q14 · STA-PRM-050 — Answer: B

The two tallest friends take the two end places in 2 ways (either one can be on the left). The other four friends fill the four middle places in 4 * 3 * 2 * 1 = 24 ways. 2 * 24 = 48. Answer: B.

Trap. Arranging only the four middle friends and answering 24 (choice A). The two tallest friends can exchange ends, which doubles the count.

Q15 · STA-PRM-039 — Answer: D

Use the gap method: seat the unrestricted people first, then drop the restricted ones into the gaps they create. Step 1 — arrange the 3 women in the row. They are distinguishable: 3! = 6 ways. Step 2 — three women in a row create 4 gaps, one before each woman and one after the last: _ W _ W _ W _ If every man sits in a gap of his own, no two men can touch. Step 3 — the 3 distinguishable men occupy 3 different gaps out of 4, and which man goes in which gap matters: P(4, 3) = 4 x 3 x 2 = 24. Step 4 — multiply the two stages: 6 x 24 = 144. Check: the unrestricted total is 6! = 720, so the answer must be a proper fraction of it; 144 is exactly one fifth. A direct enumeration of all 720 seatings also gives 144. Answer: 144.

Trap. Choice C, 72 = 2 x 3! x 3!, counts only the two perfectly alternating patterns MWMWMW and WMWMWM. Alternation is stricter than the condition asked: MWMWWM also keeps the men apart. Choice B, 36 = 3! x 3!, is a single alternating pattern. Choice A, 24 = P(4, 3), places the men in the gaps but forgets to arrange the women. Choice E, 720 = 6!, ignores the restriction.

Q16 · STA-PRM-040 — Answer: 36

The two end positions must be filled by non-dictionaries. There are 3 non-dictionaries. Left end: 3 choices. Right end: 2 remaining choices. That is 3 * 2 = 6 ways. The remaining 3 books (the two dictionaries and one other) fill the 3 middle positions in 3 * 2 * 1 = 6 ways. 6 * 6 = 36.

Trap. Placing the dictionaries in the middle three positions (3 * 2 = 6 ways) and forgetting to arrange the other three books, or subtracting only the arrangements with a dictionary at the left end. Fill the restricted end positions first, then arrange everything else.

Q17 · STA-PRM-041 — Answer: C

Ask 'does order matter?' separately at each stage - the answer is different at the two stages. Step 1 — the committee. Its three members have equal standing, so swapping two of them changes nothing: this is a selection, C(8, 3) = (8 x 7 x 6)/(3 x 2 x 1) = 336/6 = 56. Step 2 — the two titles. Floor Warden and Deputy Warden are different jobs, so swapping the two people does change the outcome: this is an arrangement, P(5, 2) = 5 x 4 = 20. Step 3 — the two stages happen one after the other, so the counting principle says multiply: 56 x 20 = 1120. Answer: 1120.

Trap. Choice B, 560 = C(8, 3) x C(5, 2), treats the two named titles as interchangeable. Choice D, 3360 = P(8, 3) x C(5, 2), gets both stages exactly backwards. Choice E, 6720 = P(8, 3) x P(5, 2), orders the committee as well. Choice A, 76 = 56 + 20, adds the stages instead of multiplying; sequential stages joined by 'and' always multiply.

Q18 · STA-PRM-042 — Answer: D

The number of pairs is n(n - 1)/2. n = 2: 1 pair, and 1 < 2. Quantity B is greater. n = 3: 3 pairs, and 3 = 3. The quantities are equal. n = 4: 6 pairs, and 6 > 4. Quantity A is greater. Answer: D.

Trap. Testing a larger value such as n = 10 (45 pairs) and choosing A. For n = 2 there is a single pair, and for n = 3 the number of pairs equals n.

Q19 · STA-PRM-043 — Answer: C

'Apart' is counted by complement: total minus together. Step 1 — total orderings. Seven letters with A twice and R twice: 7!/(2! x 2!) = 5040/4 = 1260. Step 2 — orderings with the two A's together, by the glue method. Treat AA as a single block, leaving 6 units: [AA], R, R, N, G, E. The R's are still repeated, so the count is 6!/2! = 720/2 = 360. There is no internal factor for the block, because the two A's are identical. Step 3 — subtract: 1260 - 360 = 900. Answer: 900.

Trap. Choice A, 360, is the 'together' count - the complement of what was asked. Choice D, 1260, is the unrestricted total. Choice E, 2520 = 7!/2!, divides out only one of the two repeated pairs. Choice B, 630 = 1260/2, assumes the A's are adjacent in half the orderings; adjacency requires two specific positions to be consecutive, which is far rarer than half.

Q20 · STA-PRM-044 — Answer: 5712

An integer is divisible by 5 exactly when its units digit is 0 or 5, and those two cases are mutually exclusive, so count each and add. Case 1 - units digit is 0. Leading digit: any of 1 through 9, so 9 choices. Middle three digits: taken in order, without repetition, from the 8 digits not yet used: P(8, 3) = 8 x 7 x 6 = 336. Subtotal = 9 x 336 = 3024. Case 2 - units digit is 5. Leading digit: cannot be 0 and cannot be the 5 already used, so 8 choices. Middle three digits: from the 8 digits not yet used: P(8, 3) = 336. Subtotal = 8 x 336 = 2688. Step 3 — add the cases: 3024 + 2688 = 5712. Answer: 5712.

Trap. Giving the leading digit 9 choices in Case 2 as well and answering 3024 + 3024 = 6048. When the units digit is 5, both 0 and 5 are barred from the front, leaving 8. Ignoring the leading-zero rule altogether gives the same wrong 6048 = 2 x P(9, 4), so 6048 is the value two different mistakes converge on.

Q21 · STA-PRM-045 — Answer: A, B

Without conditions there are 5 * 4 * 3 * 2 * 1 = 120 arrangements. A: Treat Ann and Bo as one unit: 4 units in 24 orders, times 2 for the order inside the unit. 48. Yes. B: Ann has 2 choices of end chair, and the other four people fill the rest in 24 ways. 48. Yes. C: Ann is to the left of Bo in exactly half of all arrangements. 60. No. D: Ann has 4 choices of chair, and the others fill the rest in 24 ways. 96. No. E: 120 - 48 = 72. No. Answer: A, B.

Trap. Forgetting the factor of 2 in condition A (24 instead of 48) and so rejecting it, or counting condition B as 24 by fixing Ann in one particular end chair. Ann and Bo can sit in either order, and there are two end chairs.

Q22 · STA-PRM-046 — Answer: B

Step 1 — glue the three L's into a single block. What is left to arrange is 6 units: [LLL], P, A, A, R, E. Step 2 — those 6 units still contain a repeat, the two A's, so the number of arrangements is 6!/2! = 720/2 = 360. Step 3 — do NOT multiply by 3! for the inside of the block. The three L's are identical, so reordering them inside the block produces nothing new; that internal factor only applies to blocks of distinguishable items. Check for size: the unrestricted total is 8!/(2! x 3!) = 40320/12 = 3360, and 360 is a bit over a tenth of it, which is the right order of magnitude for a three-letter block requirement. Answer: 360.

Trap. Choice C, 720 = 6!, forgets that the two A's are still repeated after the L's are merged. Choice D, 2160 = (6!/2!) x 3!, multiplies by 3! for the inside of the block - correct for a block of distinct items, wrong for three identical L's. Choice E, 3360, is the unrestricted total. Choice A, 60 = 6!/(2! x 3!), divides by 3! for the L's a second time after they have already been collapsed into one unit.

Q23 · STA-PRM-047 — Answer: D

The count depends entirely on how the letters repeat, and the stem does not fix that. Test the extremes. Case 1 - all 6 letters different, for example ABCDEF: 6! = 720. Quantity A > Quantity B. Case 2 - exactly one letter used twice, the rest different, for example AABCDE: 6!/2! = 720/2 = 360. Quantity A = Quantity B. Case 3 - three of one letter and three of another, AAABBB: 6!/(3! x 3!) = 720/36 = 20. Quantity A < Quantity B. All three relationships are possible, so the comparison cannot be settled. Answer: D.

Trap. Assuming that '6 letters' means 6 different letters, computing 6! = 720 and choosing A. Repetition is allowed unless the stem forbids it, and every repeat divides the count down. One repeated pair lands exactly on 360, which is what makes C tempting as well - but a single case that gives equality does not settle a comparison.

Q24 · STA-PRM-048 — Answer: 168

The three positions are distinct, so order matters throughout. Step 1 — place Thunder. He can be 1st, 2nd or 3rd: 3 choices. Step 2 — the other two top-three places must go to horses that are neither Thunder nor Lightning, and there are 10 - 2 = 8 of those. They fill two ordered places: P(8, 2) = 8 x 7 = 56. Step 3 — multiply: 3 x 56 = 168. Check by complement. Top-three outcomes containing Thunder: P(10, 3) - P(9, 3) = 720 - 504 = 216. Of those, the ones that also contain Lightning: pick ordered positions for the two of them, P(3, 2) = 6, then any of the remaining 8 horses for the last place, 8 ways, giving 48. So 216 - 48 = 168. Correct. Answer: 168.

Trap. Answering 216, which counts every top three containing Thunder and quietly lets Lightning in as well. Answering 56 = P(8, 2) forgets that Thunder himself has 3 possible finishing positions. Using C(8, 2) = 28 for the other two places treats 2nd and 3rd as interchangeable and gives 84.

Q25 · STA-PRM-054 — Answer: 30

The first digit must be 3, 4 or 5, and the last digit must be 2 or 4. First digit 3: the last digit is 2 or 4 (2 ways), and the other three digits fill the middle in 3 * 2 * 1 = 6 ways. 2 * 6 = 12. First digit 5: the same count, 12. First digit 4: the last digit can only be 2 (1 way), and the middle is filled in 6 ways. 1 * 6 = 6. Total = 12 + 12 + 6 = 30.

Trap. Multiplying 3 choices for the first digit by 2 for the last and 6 for the middle to get 36. When the first digit is 4, the 4 is no longer available for the last place, so that case has only 6 arrangements, not 12.

Q26 · STA-PRM-055 — Answer: 240

There are 6 * 5 * 4 * 3 * 2 * 1 = 720 possible orders of finish. Look only at Ana, Ben and Carl. Whichever three positions they occupy, each of the three is equally likely to be the first of the three to finish. So Ana is ahead of both in 1/3 of all orders. 720 / 3 = 240. Check by counting directly: choose the 3 positions for the three runners in 20 ways; Ana takes the earliest, Ben and Carl take the other two in 2 ways; the other three runners fill the rest in 6 ways. 20 * 2 * 6 = 240.

Trap. Halving twice, once for Ben and once for Carl, to get 720 / 4 = 180. The two conditions are not separate halvings: among the three runners, Ana is first in 2 of the 6 possible relative orders, which is 1/3.

Q27 · STA-PRM-056 — Answer: B

The hundreds digit can be any of 1 through 9: 9 choices (it cannot be 0). The tens digit can be any digit other than the hundreds digit, including 0: 9 choices. The units digit can be any of the remaining 8 digits. 9 * 9 * 8 = 648. 648 < 720. Answer: B.

Trap. Computing 10 * 9 * 8 = 720 and choosing C. That count includes strings such as 012 that begin with 0 and are not three-digit integers.

Q28 · STA-PRM-058 — Answer: A, C, D

A - seven letters with A repeated twice: 7!/2! = 5040/2 = 2520. True. B - put an A at each end. The A's are identical, so that is one single configuration, not two. The other 5 letters, all distinct, fill positions 2 through 6 in 5! = 120 ways. The count is 120, not 240. False. C - glue the two A's into one block, leaving 6 units: [AA], L, G, E, B, R, all different. That gives 6! = 720, with no internal factor of 2 because the A's are identical. True. D - 'not adjacent' is the complement of 'adjacent': 2520 - 720 = 1800. True. E - fix B in position 4. The other 6 positions take A, A, L, G, E, R, which still contains the repeated A: 6!/2! = 720/2 = 360, not 720. False. Answer: A, C, D.

Trap. B reads as 240 only if the two A's are treated as distinguishable and 5! is doubled for 'which A is on the left'; identical letters collapse that to one configuration, so the true value is 120. E reads as 720 only if you forget that the two A's are still repeated among the six letters left after B is placed - the division by 2! Never disappears. Notice the deliberate near-miss: 720 is the correct value for C and the wrong value for E.

Q29 · STA-PRM-061 — Answer: B, C, D

A - the two digits that appear form an unordered pair: C(10, 2) = 45 ways. The pattern is a multiset like x, x, y, y, which orders in 4!/(2! x 2!) = 6 ways. 45 x 6 = 270, not 540. False. B - repetition allowed means 10 choices in each of the 4 positions: 10 x 10 x 10 x 10 = 10^4 = 10000. True. C - repetition banned means the choices fall by one each time: 10 x 9 x 8 x 7 = 5040, which is P(10, 4). True. D - 'some digit used more than once' is the exact complement of 'all four digits different', so it is 10000 - 5040 = 4960. True. E - all four positions must carry the same digit, and that digit can be any of the 10, so there are exactly 10 such codes, not 40. False. Answer: B, C, D.

Trap. A doubles the true 270 by using P(10, 2) = 90 for the pair of digits instead of C(10, 2) = 45; which of the two digits is 'first' is already decided by the arrangement step, so ordering the pair counts every code twice. E multiplies 10 digits by 4 positions, but the positions are not a free choice here - all four are forced to match. D is the statement to reach for by complement: counting 'at least one repeat' directly needs messy cases, while total minus none is a single subtraction.

Q30 · STA-PRM-060 — Answer: D

Do the unordered selection first and the ordered assignment second; mixing the two stages is what wrecks this question. Step 1 — count the committees. A committee is an unordered group of 4 people out of 9, and 'at least 2 women' splits into three mutually exclusive cases: 2 women and 2 men: C(4, 2) x C(5, 2) = 6 x 10 = 60 3 women and 1 man: C(4, 3) x C(5, 1) = 4 x 5 = 20 4 women and 0 men: C(4, 4) x C(5, 0) = 1 x 1 = 1 Total = 60 + 20 + 1 = 81. Step 2 — check by complement: all committees C(9, 4) = 126, minus 0-woman committees C(5, 4) = 5, minus 1-woman committees C(4, 1) x C(5, 3) = 4 x 10 = 40. 126 - 5 - 40 = 81. Correct. Step 3 — now assign the offices. The four titles are distinct, so the four members can take them in 4! = 24 ways. Step 4 — multiply: 81 x 24 = 1944. Answer: 1944.

Trap. Choice C, 1440 = 60 x 24, reads 'at least 2 women' as 'exactly 2 women' and drops the 3-woman and 4-woman committees. Choice A, 81, stops at the committees and never assigns the offices. Choice B, 486 = 81 x 3!, uses 3! instead of 4!, the count for seating the four round a table rather than giving them four named titles. Choice E, 3024 = P(9, 4), fills four ordered offices from all nine people and ignores the requirement about women entirely.
