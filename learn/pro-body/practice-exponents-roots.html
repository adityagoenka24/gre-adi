Part C — Worked Solutions

Q1 · ARI-EXP-006 — Answer: A

The whole question is where the brackets sit. Step 1 - Column A. The brackets make -2 the base, so all four factors are -2: (-2)(-2)(-2)(-2). An even number of negative factors gives a positive product, so (-2)^4 = 16. Step 2 - Column B. There are no brackets, so the exponent attaches only to the 2 and the minus sign is applied afterwards: -2^4 = -(2^4) = -16. Step 3 - compare. 16 > -16, so Column A is greater. Check: Column A is positive and Column B is negative, so no arithmetic beyond the sign is needed.

Trap. Reading both columns the same way and choosing C. Without brackets only the 2 is raised, so -2^4 = -16; with brackets the negative is part of the base, so (-2)^4 = +16. The two differ by a sign, never by nothing.

Q2 · ARI-EXP-008 — Answer: B

Step 1 - find the largest perfect square that divides 75. That is 25, since 75 = 25 x 3. Step 2 - split the radical: sqrt(75) = sqrt(25) x sqrt(3). Step 3 - take the root of the perfect square only: sqrt(25) = 5, so sqrt(75) = 5 sqrt(3). Check: (5 sqrt(3))^2 = 25 x 3 = 75. Correct.

Trap. Choice A, 3 sqrt(5), comes from mis-factoring 75 as 9 x 5 and rooting the 9; but 9 x 5 = 45, so 3 sqrt(5) is sqrt(45), not sqrt(75). Choice C, 5 sqrt(15), splits 75 as 5 x 15 and moves the 5 outside the radical without taking its root - that is sqrt(375). Only a PERFECT SQUARE factor may come out, and only as its root. Choice D, 25 sqrt(3), uses the right factor 25 but again pulls it out unrooted. Choice E, 37.5, is 75 divided by 2, from reading the square root as 'half of'.

Q3 · ARI-EXP-011 — Answer: B

Everything is a power of 2, so work only with the exponents. Step 1 - product rule on the top: 2^8 x 2^(-3) = 2^(8 + (-3)) = 2^5. Step 2 - quotient rule: 2^5 / 2^2 = 2^(5 - 2) = 2^3. Step 3 - 2^3 = 8. Check: 256 x (1/8) = 32, and 32/4 = 8. Correct.

Trap. Choice E, 512 = 2^9, comes from subtracting the negative exponent (8 + 3 - 2) instead of adding it - a^(-n) means you add a negative number, which is subtraction, not the reverse. Choice C, 32, is 2^5, from stopping after the top and forgetting to divide by 2^2. Choice D, 128 = 2^7, adds the divisor's exponent instead of subtracting it. Choice A, -512, comes from reading 2^(-3) as -(2^3) = -8; a negative exponent gives a reciprocal, never a negative number.

Q4 · ARI-EXP-013 — Answer: A, B, C

Write every option as a power of 2 and compare with 32 = 2^5. A - 2^5 = 32. TRUE. B - 4^3 = 64, and 64/2 = 32. TRUE. C - product rule: 2^3 x 2^2 = 2^(3+2) = 2^5 = 32. TRUE. D - power of a power: (2^2)^3 = 2^(2x3) = 2^6 = 64, not 32. FALSE. E - odd power of a negative base: (-2)^5 = -32, not +32. FALSE. Answer: A, B, C.

Trap. D is the bait for the student who adds the exponents in a power of a power: 2 + 3 = 5 looks like 2^5 = 32, but the rule multiplies, giving 2^6 = 64. E is the sign bait: five negative factors leave the product negative, so (-2)^5 = -32. Only an EVEN power of a negative base comes back positive.

Q5 · ARI-EXP-015 — Answer: 9

Step 1 - a negative exponent means take the reciprocal of the base: (1/3)^(-2) = (3/1)^2. Step 2 - (3)^2 = 9. Longer route, same answer: (1/3)^(-2) = 1/((1/3)^2) = 1/(1/9) = 9. Check: 9 x (1/3)^2 = 9 x 1/9 = 1, which is what a number times its reciprocal must give. Correct. Answer: 9.

Trap. Answering -9 by moving the minus sign onto the value. A negative exponent flips the base; it never makes the result negative. The other slip is answering 1/9, which is (1/3)^2 - that ignores the minus sign in the exponent altogether.

Q6 · ARI-EXP-021 — Answer: D

Step 1 - split the exponent with the product rule: 2^(x+3) = 2^x x 2^3. Step 2 - substitute what you were given: 2^x = 32, and 2^3 = 8. Step 3 - 32 x 8 = 256. Check by solving for x instead: 32 = 2^5, so x = 5, and 2^(5+3) = 2^8 = 256. Correct. Answer: 256.

Trap. Choice A, 35, adds 3 to the value 32 - the +3 lives in the exponent, not in the number. Choice B, 96, multiplies 32 by 3 instead of by 2^3 = 8. Choice C, 160, multiplies 32 by x = 5. Choice E, 32,768, is 32^3, from cubing the value instead of multiplying it by 2^3.

Q7 · ARI-EXP-025 — Answer: C

Step 1 - combine the radicals first, then reduce inside: sqrt(8)/sqrt(6) = sqrt(8/6) = sqrt(4/3). Step 2 - split again now that the fraction is reduced: sqrt(4/3) = sqrt(4)/sqrt(3) = 2/sqrt(3). Step 3 - rationalise by multiplying top and bottom by sqrt(3): (2 x sqrt(3))/(sqrt(3) x sqrt(3)) = 2 sqrt(3)/3. Check numerically: sqrt(8) = 2.828, sqrt(6) = 2.449, and 2.828/2.449 = 1.155. Also 2 x 1.732/3 = 1.155. Correct.

Trap. Choice D, 4/3, is 8/6 reduced with the square root never taken. Choice A, 2/3, comes from rationalising 2/sqrt(3) by simply erasing the radical in the denominator rather than multiplying by sqrt(3)/sqrt(3). Choice E, 2 sqrt(3), multiplies the numerator by sqrt(3) but forgets that the denominator becomes 3. Choice B, sqrt(3)/3, drops the factor 2 from sqrt(4).

Q8 · ARI-EXP-026 — Answer: B

Step 1 - relate the two columns instead of computing them: x^3 = x^2 x x. Step 2 - x^2 is positive (x is not 0), and the extra factor x is strictly between 0 and 1. Step 3 - multiplying a positive number by something less than 1 makes it SMALLER, so x^3 < x^2 for every x in the range. Check with x = 1/2: x^2 = 1/4 = 0.25 and x^3 = 1/8 = 0.125. Check with x = 0.9: 0.81 versus 0.729. Column B wins both times. Column B is greater.

Trap. Assuming a bigger exponent always means a bigger number and choosing A. That rule holds only for bases greater than 1. Between 0 and 1 the direction reverses, because each extra factor shrinks the product. Note the stem pins x strictly between 0 and 1, so the relationship is fixed and D is wrong.

Q9 · ARI-EXP-037 — Answer: A, C, E

A root is rational exactly when the radicand is a perfect power of a rational number. A - sqrt(49) = 7, an integer. TRUE. B - 0.4 = 2/5, and 2/5 is not the square of a rational number, so sqrt(0.4) = sqrt(10)/5 = 0.632... is irrational. FALSE. C - the cube root of -8 is -2, because (-2)^3 = -8. Cube roots of negatives are real, and -2 is rational. TRUE. D - sqrt(50) = sqrt(25 x 2) = 5 sqrt(2), and sqrt(2) is irrational. FALSE. E - multiply under one radical first: sqrt(3) x sqrt(12) = sqrt(36) = 6. TRUE. Answer: A, C, E.

Trap. E is the one most often left out: two irrational factors are assumed to give an irrational product, but sqrt(3) x sqrt(12) = sqrt(36) = 6. B is the mirror error - 0.04 does have a rational root (0.2), so students assume 0.4 does too; it does not, because 0.4 = 2/5 is not a perfect square. C tempts students to answer 'not defined', which is true of even roots of negatives, not odd ones.

Q10 · ARI-EXP-039 — Answer: 8

Step 1 - read the fractional exponent: a^(m/n) means take the n-th root, then raise to the m-th power. Here that is the 4th root of 16, cubed. Step 2 - the cleaner route is to rewrite the base as a prime power: 16 = 2^4. Step 3 - 16^(3/4) = (2^4)^(3/4) = 2^(4 x 3/4) = 2^3 = 8. Check the other order: the 4th root of 16 is 2, and 2^3 = 8. Same answer. Correct. Answer: 8.

Trap. Answering 12 by computing 16 x 3/4. A fractional exponent is a root-and-power instruction, not multiplication. The second error is answering 64, from cubing 16's square root (4^3) instead of its fourth root.

Q11 · ARI-EXP-043 — Answer: 5

Radicals combine only after each one is simplified to the same radicand. Step 1 - sqrt(45) = sqrt(9 x 5) = 3 sqrt(5). Step 2 - sqrt(80) = sqrt(16 x 5) = 4 sqrt(5). Step 3 - sqrt(20) = sqrt(4 x 5) = 2 sqrt(5). Step 4 - now they are like terms: 3 sqrt(5) + 4 sqrt(5) - 2 sqrt(5) = (3 + 4 - 2) sqrt(5) = 5 sqrt(5). Check numerically: 6.708 + 8.944 - 4.472 = 11.180, and 5 x 2.236 = 11.180. Correct. Answer: 5.

Trap. Combining under the radical: sqrt(45) + sqrt(80) - sqrt(20) is NOT sqrt(45 + 80 - 20) = sqrt(105) = 10.25, which is not even a multiple of sqrt(5). Roots do not distribute over addition or subtraction; only the coefficients of matching radicals may be added.

Q12 · ARI-EXP-045 — Answer: E

Step 1 - the power of a product distributes over every factor: (2x^3 y^2)^3 = 2^3 x (x^3)^3 x (y^2)^3. Step 2 - the coefficient is raised too: 2^3 = 8. Step 3 - power of a power multiplies the exponents: (x^3)^3 = x^9 and (y^2)^3 = y^6. Step 4 - put it together: 8x^9 y^6. Check with x = y = 1: (2 x 1 x 1)^3 = 8, and 8 x 1 x 1 = 8. Correct.

Trap. Choice C, 6x^9 y^6, multiplies the coefficient 2 by the exponent 3 instead of cubing it - the outer exponent applies to the 2 as well. Choice D, 8x^6 y^5, adds the exponents (3 + 3 and 2 + 3) instead of multiplying them; the product rule is for multiplying like bases, not for a power of a power. Choice A leaves the 2 untouched, and choice B commits both errors at once.

Q13 · ARI-EXP-049 — Answer: A

Both quantities are tiny positive numbers, so compare their reciprocals instead - the larger reciprocal belongs to the smaller number. Step 1 - (1/2)^100 = 1/2^100 and (1/3)^70 = 1/3^70. Step 2 - compare 2^100 with 3^70 by forcing a common exponent. Both 100 and 70 are multiples of 10: 2^100 = (2^10)^10 = 1024^10 3^70 = (3^7)^10 = 2187^10 Step 3 - 2187 > 1024, so 3^70 > 2^100. Step 4 - the bigger denominator makes the smaller fraction, so 1/3^70 < 1/2^100. Column A is greater.

Trap. Reasoning that the larger exponent (100) must make the smaller number and choosing B. With different bases the exponents alone decide nothing; rewrite both with a common exponent and compare the bases. The second error is forgetting the reciprocal flip - once 3^70 is shown to be the larger number, its reciprocal is the SMALLER quantity.

Q14 · ARI-EXP-042 — Answer: B

Step 2 - B: 3^20 = (3^4)^5 = 81^5. Step 3 - C: 4^15 = (4^3)^5 = 64^5. Step 4 - D: 6^10 = (6^2)^5 = 36^5. Step 5 - E: 12^5 is already a fifth power, base 12. Step 6 - with the exponent fixed at 5, the largest base wins: 81 > 64 > 36 > 32 > 12. So 3^20 is the greatest. Check the two front-runners in full: 3^20 = 3,486,784,401 and 4^15 = 1,073,741,824. Correct. Answer: 3^20.

Trap. Choice A is the pick of a student chasing the largest exponent (25) and choice E of one chasing the largest base (12); as fifth powers they are 32^5 and 12^5, the two smallest on the list, so neither quantity decides anything on its own. Choice C, 4^15, tempts a student who compares base and exponent separately - 4 beats 3, and 15 looks close enough to 20 - instead of rewriting: 64^5 loses to 81^5. Q15 · ARI-EXP-052 — Answer: D

The stem allows x to be anything except 0, so test the three regions that behave differently. Case 1 - x > 1, say x = 2: x^2 = 4 and x^3 = 8. Column B is greater. Case 2 - 0 < x < 1, say x = 1/2: x^2 = 0.25 and x^3 = 0.125. Column A is greater. Case 3 - x < 0, say x = -2: x^2 = 4 (positive) and x^3 = -8 (negative). Column A is greater. Q16 · ARI-EXP-054 — Answer: C

To undo an exponent you raise both sides to its RECIPROCAL. Step 1 - the exponent is 2/3, so raise both sides to the power 3/2: (x^(2/3))^(3/2) = x^(2/3 x 3/2) = x^1 = x. Step 2 - the right side: 4^(3/2) = (sqrt(4))^3 = 2^3 = 8. Step 3 - so x = 8. Check: 8^(2/3) = (cube root of 8)^2 = 2^2 = 4. Correct.

Trap. Choice D, 16, comes from raising 4 to the power 2 instead of 3/2 - using the numerator of the exponent rather than its reciprocal. Choice E, 64, is 4^3, from taking the cube root instruction and forgetting the square. Choice B, 6, is 4 x 3/2, treating the fractional exponent as multiplication. Choice A, 2, is 4^(1/2), undoing only half the exponent.

Q17 · ARI-EXP-055 — Answer: B, C, D

The square-root symbol always returns the NON-NEGATIVE root, so sqrt(a^2) = |a|, not a. Note b^4 is already non-negative and sqrt(b^4) = b^2, which is non-negative on its own - no absolute value is needed there. So sqrt(a^2 b^4) = |a| b^2. A - a b^2 fails whenever a < 0. With a = -3, b = 2: sqrt(9 x 16) = 12, but a b^2 = -12. FALSE. B - |a| b^2 is exactly the simplified form. TRUE. C - |a b^2| = |a| x |b^2| = |a| b^2, since b^2 is never negative. TRUE. D - splitting the radical over a product is legal: sqrt(a^2) x sqrt(b^4) = |a| x b^2. TRUE. E - |a| |b|^4 = |a| b^4, which halves nothing. With a = -3, b = 2 it gives 3 x 16 = 48, not 12. FALSE. Answer: B, C, D.

Trap. A is the headline trap: sqrt(x^2) = |x|, not x. It looks right because it is right whenever a happens to be positive, and the stem deliberately allows a to be negative. E is the exponent-arithmetic trap - taking a square root HALVES the exponent, so sqrt(b^4) = b^2, not b^4; with a = -3, b = 2 it produces 48 instead of 12.

Q18 · ARI-EXP-057 — Answer: C

Factor rather than test cases. Step 1 - split the second term: (-1)^(n+1) = (-1)^n x (-1)^1 = -(-1)^n. Step 2 - substitute: (-1)^n + (-1)^(n+1) = (-1)^n - (-1)^n = 0. Step 3 - nothing in that cancellation depended on n, so it holds for every integer n. Check both parities: n = 4 gives 1 + (-1) = 0; n = 7 gives -1 + 1 = 0. Correct. Answer: all integers n.

Trap. Testing one even value, getting 0, and choosing A - then testing one odd value, getting 0, and switching to B. Consecutive powers of -1 always have opposite signs and equal magnitude, so the pair cancels regardless of parity. The factoring step makes that visible in one line and rules out A, B, D and E at once.

Q20 · ARI-EXP-060 — Answer: D

x^(1/2) is the square root of x and x^(1/3) is the cube root of x. Which root is larger depends entirely on which side of 1 the base sits. Case 1 - x > 1, say x = 64: x^(1/2) = 8 and x^(1/3) = 4. Column A is greater. Case 2 - 0 < x < 1, say x = 1/64: x^(1/2) = 1/8 = 0.125 and x^(1/3) = 1/4 = 0.25. Column B is greater. Case 3 - x = 1: both equal 1. Trap. Reasoning that a bigger exponent always gives a bigger value - true for x > 1, false for 0 < x < 1, and an equality at x = 1. The stem says only x > 0, which covers all three regions, so the answer must be D. Had the stem said x > 1 the answer would be A, and had it said 0 < x < 1 the answer would be B; that is exactly what the missing constraint is worth.

Q21 · ARI-EXP-061 — Answer: D

This is a binomial SQUARE, not a difference of squares: (p - q)^2 = p^2 - 2pq + q^2. Step 1 - p^2 = (sqrt(5))^2 = 5. Step 2 - q^2 = (sqrt(3))^2 = 3. Step 3 - the cross term: 2pq = 2 x sqrt(5) x sqrt(3) = 2 sqrt(15). Step 4 - assemble: 5 - 2 sqrt(15) + 3 = 8 - 2 sqrt(15). Check numerically: sqrt(5) - sqrt(3) = 2.236 - 1.732 = 0.504, and 0.504^2 = 0.254. Also 8 - 2(3.873) = 8 - 7.746 = 0.254. Correct.

Trap. Choice A, 2, is the classic (a - b)^2 = a^2 - b^2 error, giving 5 - 3 = 2. That formula belongs to (a + b)(a - b), not to a square. Choice B, 8, keeps both squares but drops the middle term entirely. Choice C multiplies the radicands by adding them, writing sqrt(5) x sqrt(3) as sqrt(8) instead of sqrt(15). Q22 · ARI-EXP-062 — Answer: 225

Never solve for x. Rewrite 9 in terms of 3 and let the given value do the work. Step 1 - split the exponent: 9^(x+1) = 9^x x 9^1. Step 2 - rewrite the base: 9^x = (3^2)^x = 3^(2x) = (3^x)^2. Step 3 - substitute the given value: (3^x)^2 = 5^2 = 25. Step 4 - multiply back in the 9: 25 x 9 = 225. Check: 3^x = 5 means x = 1.465, and 9^2.465 = 225. Correct. Answer: 225.

Trap. Answering 25 by computing 9^x and forgetting the +1 in the exponent. Answering 45 by treating 9^(x+1) as 9 x 5, that is by replacing 9^x with 3^x. And answering 10 by reading 9^x as 9 x x or by doubling 5. The move that makes this fast is (3^2)^x = (3^x)^2 - swapping the order of the two exponents so the given value can be substituted whole.

Q23 · ARI-EXP-063 — Answer: A, D, E

The exponent rules apply to products and quotients. They say nothing about sums. A - the product rule: same base, add exponents. TRUE. B - (x + y)^2 = x^2 + 2xy + y^2. Since x and y are positive, 2xy > 0, so the two sides never agree. Test x = y = 1: 4 versus 2. FALSE. C - test x = 3, y = 4: sqrt(9 + 16) = sqrt(25) = 5, while x + y = 7. A root does not distribute over addition. FALSE. D - the quotient rule: same base, subtract exponents. TRUE. E - a root DOES distribute over a product for non-negative factors: sqrt(xy) = sqrt(x) sqrt(y). Test x = 3, y = 12: sqrt(36) = 6 = 1.732 x 3.464. TRUE. Answer: A, D, E.

Trap. B and C are the same mistake wearing two costumes: pushing an operation through a PLUS sign. Squaring and square-rooting distribute over multiplication and division (A, D, E) and never over addition or subtraction. The 3-4-5 test in C is the fastest disproof to carry into the exam: sqrt(3^2 + 4^2) = 5, not 7.

Q24 · ARI-EXP-065 — Answer: D

Trap. Choice C, 11, comes from reading 8^(2/3) as just the cube root of 8 (that is 2) and forgetting to square it. Choice B, 7, does the mirror thing to the second term, taking the 4th root of 16 as 2 without cubing it. Choice A, 5, makes both of those omissions. Q25 · ARI-EXP-066 — Answer: 22

Step 1 - the left side is four copies of the SAME term, so factor rather than combine exponents: 2^x + 2^x + 2^x + 2^x = 4 x 2^x. Step 2 - write the 4 as a power of 2 so the left side is a single power: 4 x 2^x = 2^2 x 2^x = 2^(x+2). Step 3 - match the right side to the same base: 4^12 = (2^2)^12 = 2^24. Step 4 - equal bases means equal exponents: x + 2 = 24, so x = 22. Check: 4 x 2^22 = 2^24 = 16,777,216, and 4^12 = 16,777,216. Correct. Answer: 22.

Trap. Adding the exponents across the plus signs: 2^x + 2^x + 2^x + 2^x is read as 2^(4x). Matched against 4^12 = 2^24 that gives 4x = 24 and x = 6; matched against 4^12 while it is still written in base 4, it gives 4x = 12 and x = 3. There is no exponent rule for addition - four copies of a number is 4 times it, so you FACTOR. The other slip is stopping at x + 2 = 12 by leaving 4^12 in base 4 instead of converting it to 2^24, which returns x = 10.

Q26 · ARI-EXP-073 — Answer: 5

The insight: xyz in the exponent means three nested power-of-a-power steps, and each one hands you the next given equation. Step 1 - 2^(xyz) = ((2^x)^y)^z, because (a^m)^n = a^(mn) applied twice. Step 2 - the innermost bracket is given: 2^x = 3, so ((2^x)^y)^z = (3^y)^z. Step 3 - the next bracket is given: 3^y = 4, so (3^y)^z = 4^z. Step 4 - the last is given: 4^z = 5. So 2^(xyz) = 5. Check numerically: x = 1.585, y = 1.262, z = 1.161, so xyz = 2.322, and 2^2.322 = 5.00. Correct. Answer: 5.

Trap. Solving for x, y and z separately with logarithms and then multiplying - it works but takes several minutes and invites rounding error. The other error is grouping the exponent wrongly, as 2^x x 2^y x 2^z or as 2^(x+y+z); a product in the exponent means nested powers, not a product of powers.

Q27 · ARI-EXP-076 — Answer: C, D, E

Test each expression symbolically, then check the smallest awkward value of n. A - n^(1/n) is 1 at n = 1, but at n = 2 it is sqrt(2), which is irrational. FALSE. B - factor the sum first: 2^n + 2^n = 2 x 2^n = 2^(n+1). So the expression is (2^(n+1))^(1/n) = 2^((n+1)/n). That is an integer only when n divides n+1, which happens only at n = 1. At n = 2 it is 2^(3/2) = 2 sqrt(2). FALSE. C - (n^n)^(1/n) = n^(n/n) = n^1 = n, an integer for every positive integer n. TRUE. D - (n^2)^(1/2) = |n| = n, since n is positive. TRUE. E - (9^n)^(1/(2n)) = 9^(n/(2n)) = 9^(1/2) = 3, a constant. TRUE. Answer: C, D, E.

Trap. B is the trap that catches most people: 2^n + 2^n looks like it should be 2^(2n) or 4^n, and either misreading makes the whole expression collapse to an integer. It is actually 2^(n+1), and (n+1)/n is never a whole number for n at least 2. E looks like it depends on n, but the n cancels inside the exponent and leaves the constant 3 - a student who assumes any expression containing n must vary will wrongly reject it.

Q28 · ARI-EXP-077 — Answer: 0

Units digits of powers repeat in a cycle of length at most 4, so only the exponent's remainder on division by 4 matters. Step 1 - powers of 3 end in 3, 9, 7, 1 and then repeat (3^1 = 3, 3^2 = 9, 3^3 = 27, 3^4 = 81). Step 2 - 47 = 4 x 11 + 3, so 3^47 sits at position 3 in the cycle: units digit 7. Step 3 - powers of 7 end in 7, 9, 3, 1 and then repeat (7^1 = 7, 7^2 = 49, 7^3 = 343, 7^4 = 2401). Step 4 - 35 = 4 x 8 + 3, so 7^35 sits at position 3 in the cycle: units digit 3. Step 5 - add the two units digits: 7 + 3 = 10, whose units digit is 0. Check: 3^47 ends in 7 and 7^35 ends in 3, and any number ending in 7 plus any number ending in 3 ends in 0. Correct. Answer: 0.

Trap. Two errors dominate. First, using remainder 0 as position 0 rather than position 4 - a remainder of 3 means the THIRD entry of the cycle, and an exact multiple of 4 means the FOURTH (which is 1 for both 3 and 7). Second, stopping at 7 + 3 = 10 and answering 10 instead of its units digit, 0.

Q30 · ARI-EXP-079 — Answer: C

An infinite nested radical repeats itself, so name the whole thing and use that repetition. Step 1 - let x be the value of Column A. Everything under the outermost radical is 6 plus the SAME infinite expression, so x = sqrt(6 + x). Step 2 - square both sides: x^2 = 6 + x. Step 3 - solve: x^2 - x - 6 = 0, which factors as (x - 3)(x + 2) = 0, giving x = 3 or x = -2. Step 4 - a square root is never negative, so reject x = -2. Hence x = 3. Check by building up: sqrt(6) = 2.449, sqrt(6 + 2.449) = 2.907, sqrt(6 + 2.907) = 2.984, sqrt(6 + 2.984) = 2.997 - closing in on 3. The two quantities are equal.

Trap. Assuming an endlessly repeated addition must grow without bound and choosing A. It does not: each new square root shrinks the increment, and the sequence converges. The other error is keeping x = -2 from the quadratic; the radical symbol denotes the non-negative root, so the negative solution is discarded - the same rule that makes sqrt(x^2) = |x|.
