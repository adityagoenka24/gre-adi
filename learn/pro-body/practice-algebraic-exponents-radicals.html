Q1 · ALG-EXR-007 — Answer: D

Step 1 — the exponent outside the bracket applies to every factor inside: (2x^3)^2 = 2^2 * (x^3)^2. Step 2 — square the coefficient: 2^2 = 4. Step 3 — a power raised to a power multiplies the exponents: (x^3)^2 = x^(3*2) = x^6. Step 4 — put the two pieces back together: 4x^6. Check: at x = 2 the original is (2 * 2^3)^2 = (2 * 8)^2 = 16^2 = 256, and 4 * 2^6 = 4 * 64 = 256. Correct. Answer: D.

Trap. Leaving the coefficient alone gives 2x^6, choice B; at x = 2 that is 128 rather than 256. Adding the exponents instead of multiplying them gives x^(3+2) = x^5, which is choice C when the coefficient is squared and choice A when it is not. Choice E reads (x^3)^2 as x^(3^2) = x^9, stacking the powers instead of multiplying them.

Q2 · ALG-EXR-008 — Answer: 8

Step 1 — x^(1/3) is the cube root of x, so the equation says the cube root of x is 2. Step 2 — undo a cube root by cubing both sides: (x^(1/3))^3 = 2^3. Step 3 — on the left the exponents multiply, (1/3)*3 = 1, leaving x. On the right, 2^3 = 8. So x = 8. Check: 8^(1/3) = 2 because 2 * 2 * 2 = 8. Correct. Answer: 8.

Trap. Treating the exponent 1/3 as division gives x = 2/3, and multiplying instead gives x = 2 * 3 = 6; neither has a cube root of 2. The denominator of a fractional exponent is a root, and a root is undone by raising to that power, not by multiplying or dividing the other side by it.

Q3 · ALG-EXR-011 — Answer: C

Step 2 — add 2/3 and 3/4 over the common denominator 12: 2/3 = 8/12 and 3/4 = 9/12, so 8/12 + 9/12 = 17/12. Step 3 — Quantity A is therefore x^(17/12), which is exactly Quantity B. Because x > 0, every fractional power here is defined and positive, so the two columns agree for every allowed x. Check: at x = 4096, Quantity A = 4096^(2/3) * 4096^(3/4) = 256 * 512 = 131072 and Quantity B = 4096^(17/12) = 2^17 = 131072. At x = 1/4096 both columns equal 1/131072. Equal both times. Correct. Trap. Multiplying the exponents instead of adding them gives (2/3)*(3/4) = 1/2, so Quantity A would read x^(1/2), which is smaller than x^(17/12) for x > 1 and larger for x < 1; that split invites D. Exponents multiply only for a power of a power, while a product of powers of one base adds them, which is the case here.

Q5 · ALG-EXR-021 — Answer: A, C

Trap. Selecting B comes from reading the minus sign in x^(-2) as a sign on the answer rather than an instruction to invert, so 2^(-2) is reported as -4 instead of 1/4.
