Part C — Worked Solutions

Q1 · NUM-CON-001 — Answer: D

Step 1 — the count of integers from a to b inclusive is b - a + 1, not b - a. The +1 puts the starting integer back in, and the formula works unchanged when a is negative. Step 2 — apply it: 12 - (-8) + 1 = 12 + 8 + 1 = 21. Subtracting a negative adds, so the run is longer than it looks, not shorter. Step 3 — sanity-check by splitting at zero: -8 to -1 is 8 integers, 0 is 1 integer, 1 to 12 is 12 integers, and 8 + 1 + 12 = 21. Check by shifting the whole run up by 8: -8 to 12 has the same length as 0 to 20, and 0 to 20 holds 21 integers. Correct. Answer: D, 21.

Trap. Subtracting only, 12 - (-8) = 20, is the off-by-one that produces choice C. Choice B, 19, comes from counting the integers strictly between the endpoints (-7 through 11), choice E, 22, from over-correcting and adding the +1 twice, 12 - (-8) + 2. Choice A, 4, comes from mishandling the negative sign and adding the endpoints instead of subtracting them, -8 + 12 = 4 - the single most common slip once one endpoint is negative.

Q2 · NUM-CON-002 — Answer: B

Step 1 — work back from the term you were given. If the largest is 17, the three integers are 15, 16, 17. Step 2 — for a run with an odd number of terms the sum is count x middle, so the sum is 3 x 16 = 48. Note the middle is 16, not 17: the given term sits at the top of the run, not at its center. Check by adding directly: 15 + 16 + 17 = 48. Correct. Answer: B, 48.

Trap. Choice C, 51, is 3 x 17: it applies sum = count x middle but treats the given endpoint as the middle. Choice D, 54, builds the run upward from 17 instead of downward, summing 17 + 18 + 19 and reading 'largest' as 'smallest'. Choice A, 45, uses a spacing of 2 (13, 15, 17), the habit carried over from consecutive even or odd integers. Choice E, 57, is that same spacing-of-2 slip applied upward, 17 + 19 + 21.

Q3 · NUM-CON-007 — Answer: 34

Step 1 — the run is symmetric up to a point: -6 through 6 pairs off as (-6 + 6) + (-5 + 5) + ... + (-1 + 1) + 0 = 0. Step 2 — what is left is 7 + 8 + 9 + 10 = 34. Check with sum = count x average. Count = 10 - (-6) + 1 = 17. Average of an evenly spaced set = (first + last)/2 = (-6 + 10)/2 = 2. Sum = 17 x 2 = 34. Correct. Answer: 34.

Trap. Ignoring the negative half and answering 55, the sum of 1 through 10. The other slip is cancelling -6 through 6 but then counting the leftovers as 6 + 7 + 8 + 9 + 10 = 40, which double-counts 6 - it was already cancelled against -6.

Q4 · NUM-CON-008 — Answer: C

Step 1 — the count is odd, so the sum is 5 times the middle integer: middle = 85/5 = 17. Step 2 — consecutive ODD integers are spaced 2 apart, so the five are 13, 15, 17, 19, 21. Step 3 — the largest is 17 + 2 + 2 = 21. Check: 13 + 15 + 17 + 19 + 21 = 85. Correct. Answer: C, 21.

Trap. Answering 17, the middle integer, is choice A. Choice B, 19, comes from spacing the integers 1 apart (15, 16, 17, 18, 19) instead of 2, the single most common error in this topic. Choice D, 23, comes from stepping up three times from the middle instead of twice, 17 + 6, as if the middle sat second from the top. Choice E, 25, comes from treating 17 as the smallest of the five and adding four steps of 2.

Q5 · NUM-CON-009 — Answer: C

Step 1 — add Quantity A term by term: n + (n+1) + (n+2) = 3n + 3. Step 2 — that is exactly Quantity B, for every n. The quantities are identical expressions, so no value of n can separate them. Check with n = 4: Quantity A = 4 + 5 + 6 = 15; Quantity B = 3(4) + 3 = 15. With n = 100: Quantity A = 100 + 101 + 102 = 303; Quantity B = 303. Answer: C.

Trap. Adding the offsets as 1 + 2 = 3 but then also carrying a spare unit gives 3n + 6 and the wrong pick of A. The other failure is choosing D on the grounds that n is unknown: the comparison is between two expressions that simplify to the same thing, so the unknown is irrelevant.

Q6 · NUM-CON-014 — Answer: E

Step 2 — for any evenly spaced set, average = (first + last)/2. So (first + last)/2 = 21.5 and first + last = 43. Step 3 — that is the answer without ever finding the integers. Check by listing them: 19, 20, 21, 22, 23, 24 sum to 129, and 19 + 24 = 43. Correct. Answer: E, 43.

Q7 · NUM-CON-017 — Answer: A

Correct pair. Step 3 — their sum is 12 + 13 = 25. Check: the two consecutive integers around 12.5 are 12 and 13, their product is 156 and their sum is 25. Correct. Answer: A, 25.

Choice D, 32, comes from grabbing the factor pair 6 x 26, which is a factorization of 156 but not a consecutive one. Q8 · NUM-CON-018 — Answer: A, C, D

Write the five as n, n+1, n+2, n+3, n+4. Their sum is 5n + 10 = 5(n + 2), i.e. 5 times the middle term. A - 5(n + 2) is a multiple of 5 for every integer n. This is the odd-count rule: the sum of an odd number of consecutive integers is the count times the middle term. True. B - divisibility by 10 would also need the sum to be even, and 5(n + 2) is odd whenever n is odd. Take 1, 2, 3, 4, 5: the sum is 15. False. C - the product of k consecutive integers is divisible by k!, and 5! = 120. True. (Test 1 x 2 x 3 x 4 x 5 = 120 and 2 x 3 x 4 x 5 x 6 = 720 = 120 x 6.) D - multiples of 5 occur every fifth integer, so a window of exactly five consecutive integers catches exactly one - never zero, never two. True. E - a multiple of 6 needs a multiple of 3 that happens to be even. Five consecutive integers must contain a multiple of 3, but not necessarily an even one: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 contains 3 only. False. (Six consecutive integers would be needed to force a multiple of 6.) Check B and E on the same run 1, 2, 3, 4, 5: sum 15 is not a multiple of 10, and no term is a multiple of 6. Answer: A, C, D.

Trap. Selecting B by assuming that a sum divisible by the count is automatically divisible by twice the count; the sum is 5 x (middle term), and that middle term is odd half the time, so 1 + 2 + 3 + 4 + 5 = 15 kills it. Selecting E by stretching the guarantee 'among any 3 consecutive integers one is divisible by 3' into a guarantee for 6 - the window must be six long to force a multiple of 6, and 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 is the counterexample. The reverse slip is rejecting D on the fear that a run like 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10 holds two multiples of 5; that run is six integers long, not five.

Q9 · NUM-CON-019 — Answer: 8

The mean of the 8 integers is 92/8 = 11.5. For an even number of consecutive integers, the mean lies midway between the two middle integers, so the 4th and 5th integers are 11 and 12. The integers are 8, 9, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14, 15. The least is 8. Check: (8 + 15) * 8 / 2 = 92.

Q10 · NUM-CON-023 — Answer: D

The comparison is decided entirely by the sign of p, which the stem never fixes. Step 4 — test the three cases. p = 3, q = 4: A = 13, B = 19, Quantity B is greater. p = 0, q = 1: A = 1, B = 1, equal. p = -3, q = -2: A = 7, B = 1, Quantity A is greater. Trap. Testing only small positive values such as p = 1, 2, 3 - every one of them makes Quantity B larger - and settling on B. Q16 · NUM-CON-032 — Answer: 5

Q17 · NUM-CON-034 — Answer: C

Step 1 — the fast route is an identity. With the run n, n+1, n+2, n+3, pair the outer two and the inner two: n(n+3) = n^2 + 3n and (n+1)(n+2) = n^2 + 3n + 2. Putting t = n^2 + 3n, the product is t(t + 2) = (t + 1)^2 - 1. Step 2 — so the product plus 1 is a perfect square: 116280 + 1 = 116281. Since 340^2 = 115600 and 342^2 = 116964, the only candidate is 341, and 341^2 = 116281. So t + 1 = 341 and t = 340. Step 3 — solve n^2 + 3n = 340, i.e. n^2 + 3n - 340 = 0. The discriminant is 9 + 1360 = 1369 = 37^2, so n = (-3 + 37)/2 = 17. The run is 17, 18, 19, 20. Step 4 — sum = 4n + 6 = 4 x 17 + 6 = 74. Trap. Estimating the fourth root of 116280 as about 18.4 and settling on a run without multiplying it out. Q18 · NUM-CON-035 — Answer: B

Q20 · NUM-CON-038 — Answer: A, B, C, E

Write the five as n-2, n-1, n, n+1, n+2. A - the sum collapses to 5n, a multiple of 5. The sum of an odd count of consecutive integers is always divisible by that count. True. B - among any 5 consecutive integers exactly one is a multiple of 5, so the product carries a factor of 5. True. C - the product of k consecutive integers is divisible by k!, and 5! = 120. True. (Test 3 x 4 x 5 x 6 x 7 = 2520 = 120 x 21.) D - test 1, 2, 3, 4, 5: squares sum to 1 + 4 + 9 + 16 + 25 = 55, odd. Now test 2, 3, 4, 5, 6: 4 + 9 + 16 + 25 + 36 = 90, even. Not always. False. Trap. Selecting D after testing a single run such as 1 through 5 and getting the odd total 55. Q21 · NUM-CON-039 — Answer: A

Q24 · NUM-CON-045 — Answer: A, C, E

Q28 · NUM-CON-050 — Answer: A

Step 1 — split P into its even and its odd factors: P = (2k)! = [2 x 4 x ... x 2k] x [1 x 3 x ... x (2k-1)] = Q x [1 x 3 x ... x (2k-1)]. Step 2 — so Quantity A = P/Q = 1 x 3 x 5 x ... x (2k-1), the product of the first k odd integers. It is NOT k!; k! is what you get by factoring a 2 out of each even factor, Q = 2^k x k!. Step 3 — compare the two products factor by factor. Quantity A is the product of 1, 3, 5, ..., 2k-1 and Quantity B is the product of 1, 2, 3, ..., k. Pair the i-th factors: 2i - 1 against i. For i = 1 they tie (1 = 1), and for every i >= 2, 2i - 1 > i since 2i - 1 - i = i - 1 > 0. Step 4 — k > 1, so at least one pair (i = 2, giving 3 against 2) is a strict inequality and all factors are positive. The product in Quantity A is therefore strictly greater for every allowed k. Check k = 2: P = 4! = 24, Q = 2 x 4 = 8, P/Q = 3, and 2! = 2, so 3 > 2. Check k = 3: P = 720, Q = 2 x 4 x 6 = 48, P/Q = 15, and 3! = 6, so 15 > 6. Q29 · SEC-AO-034 — Answer: 0

Factor the expression: k^3 - k = k(k^2 - 1) = (k - 1)k(k + 1). That is a product of three consecutive integers. Among any three consecutive integers at least one is even, so the product is divisible by 2, and exactly one is a multiple of 3, so the product is divisible by 3. Since 2 and 3 share no factor, the product is divisible by 2 x 3 = 6 and the remainder is 0. Spot checks: k = 2 gives 6, k = 3 gives 24, k = 5 gives 120, all multiples of 6.

Trap. Students test only k = 2, get 6, and worry that the result is a coincidence, or they test an odd k such as k = 3, see 24, and conclude the answer depends on the parity of k. The three-consecutive-integers factorization is what makes the divisibility automatic for every k.

Q30 · NUM-CON-048 — Answer: 4032

Step 1 — let the run start at a. Then sum = n x a + n(n-1)/2 = 2016, so n(2a + n - 1) = 4032. Note that a may be negative or zero; nothing restricts the integers to be positive. Step 2 — to make n huge, make the other factor tiny. The smallest usable value of |2a + n - 1| is 1, which needs n = 4032 and 2a + 4031 = 1, so a = -2015. Step 3 — check that run: the integers from -2015 to 2016. Every pair k and -k cancels for k = 1 to 2015, and 0 contributes nothing, so the sum is just 2016. Step 4 — its count is 2016 - (-2015) + 1 = 4032, and n cannot exceed 4032: any larger n would force |2a + n - 1| to be less than 1, that is 0, making the product 0 rather than 4032. Check: 4032 x (2(-2015) + 4032 - 1) = 4032 x 1 = 4032 = 2 x 2016. Correct. Answer: 4032.

Trap. Assuming the integers must be positive caps the answer at 63, because 1 + 2 + ... + 63 = 2016 is the longest run of consecutive positive integers with that sum. The stem says integers, and a run that reaches back below zero cancels itself out almost entirely, leaving only the top term - that is the whole trick. Answering 2016 or 2017 comes from taking the run as -2015 to 2016 but then counting it with 2016 - 2015 or forgetting to count 0.
