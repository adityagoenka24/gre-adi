Part C — Worked Solutions

Q1 · NUM-CON-001 — Answer: D

Step 1 — the count of integers from a to b inclusive is b - a + 1, not b - a. The +1 puts the starting integer back in, and the formula works unchanged when a is negative. Step 2 — apply it: 12 - (-8) + 1 = 12 + 8 + 1 = 21. Subtracting a negative adds, so the run is longer than it looks, not shorter. Step 3 — sanity-check by splitting at zero: -8 to -1 is 8 integers, 0 is 1 integer, 1 to 12 is 12 integers, and 8 + 1 + 12 = 21. Check by shifting the whole run up by 8: -8 to 12 has the same length as 0 to 20, and 0 to 20 holds 21 integers. Correct. Answer: D, 21.

Trap. Subtracting only, 12 - (-8) = 20, is the off-by-one that produces choice C. Choice B, 19, comes from counting the integers strictly between the endpoints (-7 through 11), choice E, 22, from over-correcting and adding the +1 twice, 12 - (-8) + 2. Choice A, 4, comes from mishandling the negative sign and adding the endpoints instead of subtracting them, -8 + 12 = 4 - the single most common slip once one endpoint is negative.

Q2 · NUM-CON-002 — Answer: B

Step 1 — work back from the term you were given. If the LARGEST is 17, the three integers are 15, 16, 17. Step 2 — for a run with an odd number of terms the sum is count x middle, so the sum is 3 x 16 = 48. Note the middle is 16, not 17: the given term sits at the top of the run, not at its centre. Check by adding directly: 15 + 16 + 17 = 48. Correct. Answer: B, 48.

Trap. Choice C, 51, is 3 x 17: it applies sum = count x middle but treats the given endpoint as the middle. Choice D, 54, builds the run upward from 17 instead of downward, summing 17 + 18 + 19 and reading 'largest' as 'smallest'. Choice A, 45, uses a spacing of 2 (13, 15, 17), the habit carried over from consecutive even or odd integers. Choice E, 57, is that same spacing-of-2 slip applied upward, 17 + 19 + 21.

Q3 · NUM-CON-007 — Answer: 34

Step 1 — the run is symmetric up to a point: -6 through 6 pairs off as (-6 + 6) + (-5 + 5) + ... + (-1 + 1) + 0 = 0. Step 2 — what is left is 7 + 8 + 9 + 10 = 34. Check with sum = count x average. Count = 10 - (-6) + 1 = 17. Average of an evenly spaced set = (first + last)/2 = (-6 + 10)/2 = 2. Sum = 17 x 2 = 34. Correct. Answer: 34.

Trap. Ignoring the negative half and answering 55, the sum of 1 through 10. The other slip is cancelling -6 through 6 but then counting the leftovers as 6 + 7 + 8 + 9 + 10 = 40, which double-counts 6 - it was already cancelled against -6.

Q4 · NUM-CON-008 — Answer: C

Step 1 — the count is odd, so the sum is 5 times the middle integer: middle = 85/5 = 17. Step 2 — consecutive ODD integers are spaced 2 apart, so the five are 13, 15, 17, 19, 21. Step 3 — the largest is 17 + 2 + 2 = 21. Check: 13 + 15 + 17 + 19 + 21 = 85. Correct. Answer: C, 21.

Trap. Answering 17, the middle integer, is choice A. Choice B, 19, comes from spacing the integers 1 apart (15, 16, 17, 18, 19) instead of 2, the single most common error in this topic. Choice D, 23, comes from stepping up three times from the middle instead of twice, 17 + 6, as if the middle sat second from the top. Choice E, 25, comes from treating 17 as the SMALLEST of the five and adding four steps of 2.

Q5 · NUM-CON-009 — Answer: C

Step 1 — add Column A term by term: n + (n+1) + (n+2) = 3n + 3. Step 2 — that is exactly Column B, for every n. The quantities are identical expressions, so no value of n can separate them. Check with n = 4: Column A = 4 + 5 + 6 = 15; Column B = 3(4) + 3 = 15. With n = 100: Column A = 100 + 101 + 102 = 303; Column B = 303. Answer: C.

Trap. Adding the offsets as 1 + 2 = 3 but then also carrying a spare unit gives 3n + 6 and the wrong pick of A. The other failure is choosing D on the grounds that n is unknown: the comparison is between two expressions that simplify to the same thing, so the unknown is irrelevant.

Q6 · NUM-CON-014 — Answer: E

Step 2 — for any evenly spaced set, average = (first + last)/2. So (first + last)/2 = 21.5 and first + last = 43. Step 3 — that is the answer without ever finding the integers. Check by listing them: 19, 20, 21, 22, 23, 24 sum to 129, and 19 + 24 = 43. Correct. Answer: E, 43.

Trap. Believing the average of consecutive integers must be a member of the set, so rounding 21.5 down to 21 and doubling, gives 42 (choice D). Choices A and B are the smallest and largest themselves, reported instead of their sum.

Q7 · NUM-CON-017 — Answer: A

Correct pair. Step 3 — their sum is 12 + 13 = 25. Check: the two consecutive integers around 12.5 are 12 and 13, their product is 156 and their sum is 25. Correct. Answer: A, 25.

Choice D, 32, comes from grabbing the factor pair 6 x 26, which is a factorisation of 156 but not a consecutive one. Q8 · NUM-CON-018 — Answer: A, C, D

Write the five as n, n+1, n+2, n+3, n+4. Their sum is 5n + 10 = 5(n + 2), i.e. 5 times the middle term. A - 5(n + 2) is a multiple of 5 for every integer n. This is the odd-count rule: the sum of an odd number of consecutive integers is the count times the middle term. TRUE. B - divisibility by 10 would also need the sum to be even, and 5(n + 2) is odd whenever n is odd. Take 1, 2, 3, 4, 5: the sum is 15. FALSE. C - the product of k consecutive integers is divisible by k!, and 5! = 120. TRUE. (Test 1 x 2 x 3 x 4 x 5 = 120 and 2 x 3 x 4 x 5 x 6 = 720 = 120 x 6.) D - multiples of 5 occur every fifth integer, so a window of exactly five consecutive integers catches exactly one - never zero, never two. TRUE. E - a multiple of 6 needs a multiple of 3 that happens to be even. Five consecutive integers must contain a multiple of 3, but not necessarily an even one: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 contains 3 only. FALSE. (Six consecutive integers would be needed to force a multiple of 6.) Check B and E on the same run 1, 2, 3, 4, 5: sum 15 is not a multiple of 10, and no term is a multiple of 6. Answer: A, C, D.

Trap. Selecting B by assuming that a sum divisible by the count is automatically divisible by twice the count; the sum is 5 x (middle term), and that middle term is odd half the time, so 1 + 2 + 3 + 4 + 5 = 15 kills it. Selecting E by stretching the guarantee 'among any 3 consecutive integers one is divisible by 3' into a guarantee for 6 - the window must be six long to force a multiple of 6, and 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 is the counterexample. The reverse slip is rejecting D on the fear that a run like 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10 holds two multiples of 5; that run is six integers long, not five.

Q9 · NUM-CON-023 — Answer: D

Step 1 — consecutive with p < q means q = p + 1. Step 2 — Column A = p^2 + p + 1. Column B = (p+1)^2 + p = p^2 + 3p + 1. Step 3 — subtract: Column B - Column A = 2p. The comparison is decided entirely by the sign of p, which the stem never fixes. Step 4 — test the three cases. p = 3, q = 4: A = 13, B = 19, Column B is greater. p = 0, q = 1: A = 1, B = 1, equal. p = -3, q = -2: A = 7, B = 1, Column A is greater. Since all three outcomes occur, the relationship cannot be determined. Check the gap formula with p = 5, q = 6: Column A = 25 + 6 = 31, Column B = 36 + 5 = 41, and 41 - 31 = 10 = 2 x 5. Correct. Answer: D.

Trap. Testing only small positive values such as p = 1, 2, 3 - every one of them makes Column B larger - and settling on B. Nothing in the stem says the integers are positive, and p = 0 gives equality while any negative p flips the comparison. Squaring a negative is exactly where this question is won.

Q18 · NUM-CON-035 — Answer: D

Step 1 — count the factors in Column A. The run goes from n up to 2n-1, so it holds (2n-1) - n + 1 = n consecutive integers. Step 2 — the k! rule guarantees that a product of k consecutive integers is divisible by k!, so this product is certainly divisible by n!. The divisor here is (n+1)! = n! x (n+1), one factor of (n+1) more than the rule delivers. That extra factor is NOT guaranteed, so the remainder is 0 only sometimes. Step 3 — write the quotient: n(n+1)...(2n-1) / n! = (2n-1)! / [(n-1)! n!], which is the integer C(2n-1, n). The remainder is 0 exactly when (n+1) divides C(2n-1, n). Step 4 — test n = 2: the product is 2 x 3 = 6 and (n+1)! = 3! = 6, so 6 divides exactly and the remainder is 0. Column A = Column B. Step 5 — test n = 3: the product is 3 x 4 x 5 = 60 and (n+1)! = 4! = 24. 60 = 24 x 2 + 12, so the remainder is 12. Column A > Column B. (Here C(5,3) = 10, which is not a multiple of 4.) Step 6 — two different outcomes occur among the allowed values of n, so the relationship is not determined. It is not a lone edge case either: n = 7 gives remainder 20160, while n = 4, 5, 6 and 8 all give 0. Answer: D.

Trap. Choosing C by reaching for the k! rule without checking which factorial is in the denominator: the product holds n consecutive integers, so n! is guaranteed, but (n+1)! demands one more factor of n + 1 that the run n to 2n-1 supplies only for some n. Choosing A comes from the opposite over-correction - assuming the extra factor is never there, when n = 2, 4, 5, 6 and 8 all leave remainder 0. A remainder is never negative, so B is impossible against a column of 0 and should be eliminated on sight.

Q24 · NUM-CON-044 — Answer: B
