Part C — Worked Solutions

Q1 · NUM-REM-004 — Answer: C

Step 1 — translate the remainder. A remainder of 0 means 6 divides n exactly, so n = 6k for some integer k. Step 2 — test I. n = 6k = 2(3k), so n is a multiple of 2. TRUE. Step 3 — test II. n = 6k = 3(2k), so n is a multiple of 3. TRUE. Step 4 — test III. Try the smallest case, n = 6. Then 6 divided by 12 gives quotient 0 and remainder 6, so 6 is not divisible by 12. One counterexample kills the statement. FALSE. Only I and II must hold, so the answer is C.

Trap. Choosing E by assuming that a multiple of 6 must also be a multiple of 12, the next multiple of 6. Divisibility passes DOWN to the factors of the divisor (2 and 3), never UP to its multiples: n = 6 is the counterexample.

Q2 · NUM-REM-005 — Answer: 5

Step 1 — write the division algorithm: 53 = 8q + r with 0 <= r < 8. Step 2 — find the largest multiple of 8 that does not exceed 53: 8 x 6 = 48, while 8 x 7 = 56 is too big. So q = 6. Step 3 — r = 53 - 48 = 5. Check: 5 is between 0 and 7, so it is a legal remainder, and 8 x 6 + 5 = 53. Correct. Answer: 5.

Trap. Overshooting to 8 x 7 = 56 and reporting 56 - 53 = 3. The quotient must be the largest multiple that stays BELOW the number; going past it turns the remainder into a negative one (-3), which then gets reported as 3.

Q3 · NUM-REM-006 — Answer: B

Column A: 2n is even for every integer n, and an even number divided by 2 leaves nothing over. Column A = 0. Column B: 2n + 1 is odd for every integer n, and an odd number divided by 2 leaves 1. Column B = 1. Check with n = 3: 6 divided by 2 is 3 remainder 0; 7 divided by 2 is 3 remainder 1. And with n = 10: 20 leaves 0, 21 leaves 1. 1 > 0, so Column B is greater.

Trap. Treating Column A as 'no remainder' and therefore as blank or undefined, and choosing D. A remainder of 0 is a perfectly ordinary value - it is what divisibility looks like - so Column A is the number 0 and the comparison resolves.

Q4 · NUM-REM-007 — Answer: D

Step 1 — the units digit of a product depends only on the units digits of the factors, because every other digit contributes a multiple of 10. Step 2 — units digit of 7 is 7; units digit of 48 is 8. Step 3 — 7 x 8 = 56, which ends in 6. Check: 7 x 48 = 336, and 336 does end in 6. Correct. Answer: 6.

Trap. Multiplying 7 by the tens digit instead: 7 x 4 = 28, so answering 8, which is choice E - but the tens digit of 48 only ever feeds the tens column and higher, and the units digit of the answer is decided by 7 x 8 alone. Choice C, 5, is the units digit of 7 + 8 = 15, from adding the units digits instead of multiplying them, and choice B, 4, is the tens digit of 48 copied straight down. Choice A, 3, is the tens digit of the product 336, read off in place of the units digit.

Q5 · NUM-REM-011 — Answer: C

Step 1 — write n in its remainder form: n = 7q + 4. Step 2 — add 7: n + 7 = 7q + 4 + 7 = 7(q + 1) + 4. Step 3 — the expression is again (a multiple of 7) + 4, and 4 is less than 7, so the remainder is 4. Check with the smallest legal n, n = 4: 4 + 7 = 11, and 11 = 7 x 1 + 4, remainder 4. Try n = 18: 18 + 7 = 25 = 7 x 3 + 4, remainder 4 again. Answer: 4.

Trap. Adding 7 to the remainder as well, giving 4 + 7 = 11, which is choice E; adding the divisor itself pushes the quotient up by 1 and leaves the remainder untouched. Choice D, 7, is the same instinct in a milder form - treating the added 7 as a fresh leftover chunk - and both 7 and 11 are impossible on their face, since a remainder on division by 7 must be 0 through 6. Choice B, 3, is the complement error 7 - 4 = 3, reporting what is left of the divisor instead of what is left of n, and choice A, 0, comes from believing that adding 7 completes the division and makes n + 7 divisible.

Q6 · NUM-REM-014 — Answer: C

Step 1 — use the product rule: the remainder of a product is the remainder of the product of the remainders, reduced again. Step 2 — rem(4n) = rem(4 x 5) = rem(20). Step 3 — reduce: 20 = 8 x 2 + 4, so the remainder is 4. The algebra behind it: n = 8k + 5, so 4n = 32k + 20 = 32k + 16 + 4 = 8(4k + 2) + 4. Check with n = 5: 4n = 20, and 20 = 8 x 2 + 4. Check with n = 13: 4n = 52 = 8 x 6 + 4. Same remainder both times. Answer: 4.

Trap. Stopping at 4 x 5 = 20 and choosing E. A remainder on division by 8 must be less than 8, so 20 is not finished - subtract 8 twice to reach 4. Choice D is the other live error: assuming that multiplying n leaves its remainder alone, the way ADDING a multiple of 8 would.

Q7 · NUM-REM-015 — Answer: D

Step 2 — locate the exponent: 10 = 4 x 2 + 2, so 10 mod 4 = 2. Step 3 — take the 2nd entry of the cycle: 4. Check: 2^10 = 1024 = 5 x 204 + 4. Correct. Answer: 4.

Trap. Using a cycle length of 5 because there are 5 possible remainders, computing 10 mod 5 = 0, and landing on 1 (choice A). The length of the cycle is however many powers it takes to return to the start - here 4, not 5. Choice B is position 1 of the cycle and choice C is position 3, the two off-by-one slips on either side of the correct position 2; choice E is the belief that a power of 2 must eventually be divisible by 5.

Q12 · NUM-REM-030 — Answer: A, B, C

Q13 · NUM-REM-031 — Answer: 3

Q16 · NUM-REM-036 — Answer: C

Handle each power separately, then add and reduce. Step 1 — powers of 3 mod 5: 3, then 9 leaves 4, then 12 leaves 2, then 6 leaves 1. The cycle is (3, 4, 2, 1), length 4. 100 mod 4 = 0, and when the exponent lands on 0 you take the LAST entry of the cycle, not a nonexistent 0th one. So 3^100 leaves 1. Step 2 — powers of 4 mod 5: 4, then 16 leaves 1. The cycle is (4, 1), length 2. 100 mod 2 = 0, so again the last entry: 4^100 leaves 1. Faster for 4: 4 is one less than 5, so 4 behaves like -1. Then 4^100 = (-1)^100 = 1. Even exponent, remainder 1; odd exponent, remainder 4. Step 3 — add the remainders: 1 + 1 = 2, and 2 < 5, so the remainder is 2. Answer: 2.

Trap. The off-by-one on the cycle. With 100 mod 4 = 0, a cycle position of 0 always means the END of the cycle - the 4th entry, 1 - but students read it as a nonexistent 0th position. Reading it as the remainder 0 for both terms gives choice A; reading position 1 for 3^100 (that is, 3) while handling 4^100 correctly gives 3 + 1 = 4, choice E; and reading position 1 for 3^100 while calling 4^100 zero gives choice D. Q21 · NUM-REM-041 — Answer: A

Q28 · NUM-REM-048 — Answer: D

Column A is fixed. Factor: n^3 - n = n(n^2 - 1) = (n - 1)n(n + 1), the product of three consecutive integers. Among any three consecutive integers at least one is even and exactly one is a multiple of 3, so the product is divisible by 2 x 3 = 6. Column A = 0 for every n. Column B moves. Powers of 2 mod 3 cycle (2, 1) with length 2, because 2 is one less than 3 and so behaves like -1: n odd -> 2^n leaves 2 -> 2^n + 1 leaves 2 + 1 = 3, which reduces to 0. n even -> 2^n leaves 1 -> 2^n + 1 leaves 1 + 1 = 2. Now compare: n = 1: Column A = 0 (0 divided by 6) and Column B = 0 (3 divided by 3). The columns are EQUAL. n = 2: Column A = 0 (n^3 - n = 6, and 6 divided by 6 leaves 0). Column B: 2^2 + 1 = 5, and 5 = 3 x 1 + 2, so Column B = 2. Column B is GREATER. n = 3: Column A = 0 (24 divided by 6) and Column B = 0 (9 divided by 3). Equal again. Two different relationships occur, so the relationship cannot be determined.

Trap. Testing only n = 1, where both columns happen to be 0, and choosing C. Column A is genuinely invariant, but Column B swings between 0 and 2 with the parity of n - and 'leaves remainder 3, so 0' at odd n is exactly the coincidence that makes n = 1 look conclusive. Whenever a column depends on n, test at least an odd and an even value before answering.

Q30 · NUM-REM-050 — Answer: B, D

A - the product rule requires a second reduction. Take a = 5, d = 7, so r = 5. Then 2a = 10, which leaves 3, not 10. The rule is rem(2a) = rem(2r) reduced again, and 2r can exceed d. FALSE. B - write a = dq1 + r and b = dq2 + r. Subtracting, a - b = d(q1 - q2), a multiple of d. This is the workhorse fact behind every 'same remainder' problem. TRUE. C - the remainder survives the switch to a factor only after being reduced again. Take a = 11, d = 15, so r = 11; dividing 11 by the factor 3 leaves 2, not 11 - and 11 is not even a legal remainder mod 3. FALSE. D - rem(a^2) = rem(r x r) reduced, and r is exactly the remainder of a, so nothing else about a enters. Example: every a leaving 3 mod 8 has a^2 leaving 1, whether a is 3, 11 or 19. TRUE. E - divisibility travels down to factors, not up to multiples. Take a = 6, d = 6: 6 is divisible by 6 but not by the multiple 12. FALSE. Answer: B, D.

Trap. A and C are the same habit wearing two hats: carrying a remainder through an operation without reducing it a second time, which lets an 'answer' like 10 (mod 7) or 11 (mod 3) stand even though it exceeds its divisor. E inverts the direction in which divisibility travels: a remainder of 0 passes down to every FACTOR of d, never up to its multiples, and a = d = 6 kills it in one line. D is the one that gets skipped rather than mis-answered - it is just the product rule rem(a^2) = rem(r x r) stated in words.
