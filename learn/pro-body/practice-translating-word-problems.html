Q1 · ALG-WPM-005 — Answer: A

Let k be the number of employees at Store X. Store Y's 11 is a known constant. Store X has 4 more than Store Y, so k = 11 + 4. Check the direction: Store X has more, so the 4 is added to Store Y's 11. Choice A gives k = 15, which is 4 more than 11. Answer: A.

Trap. Choice B, k + 4 = 11, adds the 4 to Store X instead of Store Y; it gives k = 7, making Store X the smaller store. Choice C reverses the subtraction into 4 - 11 = -7. Choice D reads '4 more' as '4 times', giving 44, and choice E does the same and also reverses who has more, giving k = 11/4.

Q2 · ALG-WPM-012 — Answer: B

Step 1 — name the unknown. Let n = the smaller integer. Consecutive EVEN integers differ by 2, so the two integers are n and n + 2. Step 2 — translate 'the sum is 54'. n + (n + 2) = 54 2n + 2 = 54 Step 3 — solve. 2n = 52 n = 26 Step 4 — answer what was asked. The question wants the SMALLER integer, which is n = 26. Check: the two integers are 26 and 28, both even, consecutive even, and 26 + 28 = 54. Correct. Answer: B.

Trap. Writing the pair as n and n + 1 gives 2n + 1 = 54 and n = 26.5, and a student who rounds it reports 27, choice C - but 27 is not even. Choice D, 28, is the larger integer rather than the smaller one, and choice E, 52, is the value of 2n reported before the final division by 2. Choice A comes from taking the average 54/2 = 27 and then subtracting the whole gap of 2 instead of half of it.

Q3 · ALG-WPM-011 — Answer: 4

Step 1 — translate each purchase into a cost. The apples cost 2x dollars and the oranges cost 3(x + 1) dollars. Step 2 — translate 'spending $23 in total' as part + part = whole. 2x + 3(x + 1) = 23 Step 3 — expand and solve. The parentheses matter: the 3 multiplies the whole count x + 1. 2x + 3x + 3 = 23 5x + 3 = 23 5x = 20 x = 4 Check: 4 apples cost $8 and 5 oranges cost $15, and 8 + 15 = $23. Correct. Answer: 4.

Trap. Dropping the parentheses and writing 2x + 3x + 1 = 23 gives 5x = 22 and x = 4.4, a non-integer that should immediately signal the slip. The 3 is a price per orange, so it must multiply the entire orange count x + 1, not just the x.

Q4 · ALG-WPM-008 — Answer: C

Step 1 — translate 'is 30 years older than'. Father's age now = son's age now + 30 = 10 + 30 = 40. Step 2 — translate 'in 5 years'. Five years from now the father will be 40 + 5 = 45. Step 3 — compare. Quantity A = 45 and Quantity B = 45, so the two quantities are equal. Check: in 5 years the son is 15 and the father is 45, and 45 - 15 = 30, the stated gap. Correct. Answer: C.

Trap. Reading '30 years older' backwards gives a father of 10 - 30 = -20 now, or 30 - 10 = 20 now and 25 in five years, both pointing to choice B. A second route to B is adding the 5 years to the son instead of the father, getting a father of 40 against Quantity B's 45.

Q5 · WRD-MST-051 — Answer: A, C, E

A - '5 less than n' takes 5 away from n, so it is n - 5. The words come in the reverse of the algebra: the 5 is spoken first but subtracted second. True. B - 'n less than 5' takes n away from 5, so it is 5 - n, not n - 5. False. C - 'n reduced by 5' starts at n and decreases it by 5, so it is n - 5. True. D - 'the quotient of n and 5' is a division, n/5, not a subtraction. False. E - if n exceeds 5, the amount of the excess is n - 5. True. Check with n = 12: A gives 7, B gives -7, C gives 7, D gives 2.4, E gives 7. Exactly A, C and E return 12 - 5 = 7. Correct. Answer: A, C and E.

Trap. Selecting B because the words 'n' and '5' appear in that order. 'Less than' always reverses the order of the two quantities, so B is 5 - n. Choice D fails for a different reason entirely: 'quotient' is the division keyword, so nothing about D is a subtraction.

Q6 · ALG-WPM-015 — Answer: 63

Step 1 — name the SMALLER part as the variable, so that no fractions appear. Let x = the smaller part; then the larger part is 3x. Step 2 — translate 'the sum of the two parts is 84' as part + part = whole. x + 3x = 84 4x = 84 x = 21 Step 3 — answer what was asked. The question wants the LARGER part: 3x = 3 * 21 = 63. Check: 21 + 63 = 84, and 63 = 3 * 21. Correct. Answer: 63.

Trap. Reporting 21, the value of x. The variable was deliberately attached to the smaller part to keep the algebra clean, so x is not the answer here. Naming the larger part x instead gives x + x/3 = 84 and the same 63, but with a fraction to clear - which is why the capsule says to let x be the simplest quantity.

Q10 · ALG-WPM-024 — Answer: 480

Step 1 — name the unknowns. Let a = the larger integer and b = the smaller, so a > b. Step 2 — translate both sentences. a - b = 14 a + b = 46 Step 3 — add the two equations; the b terms cancel. 2a = 60, so a = 30 Then b = 46 - 30 = 16. Step 4 — answer what was asked: the PRODUCT, not either integer. 30 * 16 = 480 Check: 30 - 16 = 14 and 30 + 16 = 46, and 30 * 16 = 480. Correct. Answer: 480.

Trap. Reporting 30 or 16, the integers themselves, instead of their product; or writing b - a = 14, which gives a = 16 and b = 30 and contradicts 'a is the larger'. The sum-and-difference pair is fastest solved by adding the equations: the larger is always (sum + difference)/2 = (46 + 14)/2 = 30.

Q11 · ALG-WPM-023 — Answer: C

Step 1 — name the unknown around the MIDDLE term, which is the efficient choice here. Let the three integers be m - 1, m and m + 1. Step 2 — translate 'have a sum of 3k'. (m - 1) + m + (m + 1) = 3m = 3k Step 3 — solve. The -1 and +1 cancel, so 3m = 3k gives m = k exactly, for every allowed k. Quantity A = m = k = Quantity B, so the two quantities are equal. Check with k = 5: the sum is 15, and the three integers are 4, 5, 6, whose middle term is 5 = k. Try k = 100: the sum is 300 and the integers are 99, 100, 101, middle 100 = k. Correct. Answer: C.

Trap. Choosing D on the grounds that the actual integers are never named. They do not need to be: the sum of three consecutive integers is always 3 times the middle one, so the middle term is pinned to k no matter which k is used. Setting the integers up as n, n + 1, n + 2 instead gives 3n + 3 = 3k, so n = k - 1; a student who then compares n rather than the middle term n + 1 wrongly concludes that Quantity B is greater.

Q12 · WRD-MST-052 — Answer: D

Quantity A - Quantity B = (3m - 5) - (2m + 1) = m - 6 Step 3 — test the sign of m - 6 against the only condition given, m > 4. If m = 5: Quantity A = 10 and Quantity B = 11, so Quantity B is greater. If m = 7: Quantity A = 16 and Quantity B = 15, so Quantity A is greater. Step 4 — the comparison changes direction inside the allowed range, so it cannot be determined. Q15 · WRD-MST-040 — Answer: B

Q18 · ALG-WPM-050 — Answer: B
