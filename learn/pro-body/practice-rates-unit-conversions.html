Q1 · ARI-RAE-004 — Answer: D

Step 1 — write the conversion as a fraction equal to 1, with the unit you want on top: (1.6 km)/(1 mile). Step 2 — multiply and cancel units diagonally: 5 miles x (1.6 km)/(1 mile) = 5 x 1.6 km = 8 km. The word 'miles' cancels and 'km' survives, which is the check that the fraction was the right way up. Answer: 8 km.

Trap. Dividing instead of multiplying: 5/1.6 = 3.125, choice A. That is the conversion for going km -> miles, and you can catch it instantly by units - dividing by (1.6 km/mile) leaves miles^2/km, which is meaningless. Choice B, 6.6, comes from adding 5 + 1.6 instead of multiplying. Choice C, 7.5, is 5 x 1.5, the factor rounded down to 1.5 before multiplying. Choice E, 16, comes from clearing the decimal - rewriting 1 mile = 1.6 km as 10 miles = 16 km - and then reporting 16 without scaling back down from 10 miles to 5.

Q2 · ARI-RAE-011 — Answer: C

Step 1 — chain the two conversions as fractions and cancel: 72 km/h x (1000 m)/(1 km) x (1 h)/(3600 s) Step 2 — km cancels against km and h cancels against h, leaving m/s: 72 x 1000/3600 = 72000/3600 = 20 m/s. Step 3 — the shortcut worth memorising: 1000/3600 = 5/18, so km/h to m/s is x5/18. 72 x 5/18 = 4 x 5 = 20. Check: going back, 20 x 18/5 = 72 km/h. Correct. Answer: 20 m/s.

Trap. Using the factor upside down: 72 x 18/5 = 259.2, choice D. 18/5 is the m/s -> km/h direction, and 259 m/s is faster than sound, which is the sanity check. Choice E is converting km to m but forgetting hours to seconds, and choice A is dividing by 3600 without converting km to m. Choice B, 1.2, is 72/60 - converting the hour to 60 minutes instead of 3600 seconds, so the answer is really km per minute with the wrong unit attached.

Q3 · ARI-RAE-012 — Answer: 45

Step 1 — find the unit rate, which is always 'amount per one unit of time': 120 units / 8 hours = 15 units per hour. Step 2 — Rate x Time = Amount: 15 units/hour x 3 hours = 45 units. The word 'hour' cancels, leaving units. Check: 8 hours would give 15 x 8 = 120. Correct. Answer: 45.

Trap. Building the rate upside down as 8/120 = 1/15 and answering 0.2, which is hours per unit rather than units per hour. Name the units as you divide: 120 units / 8 hours can only be units per hour, so the units go on top.

Q4 · ARI-RAE-013 — Answer: A, B, D

C - metres to kilometres: 30 m = 30/1000 km = 0.03 km, so the speed is 0.03 km/s, not 0.3. FALSE. D - metres to centimetres: 30 m x 100 cm/m = 3,000 cm/s. TRUE. E - 1,800 is the m/min figure with the wrong units pasted on; the true km/h value is 108. FALSE. Answer: A, B, D.

Trap. C is a one-decimal-place slip: dividing by 100 instead of 1000 when going metres -> kilometres. E is the more revealing error - a student computes 30 x 60 = 1,800 correctly but then labels it km/h instead of m/min. Always carry the units through the multiplication rather than attaching them at the end.

Q7 · ARI-RAE-042 — Answer: 12

Step 1 — notice that pace and speed are reciprocals: pace is min/km, speed is km/h. Flipping the fraction flips the units. Step 2 — invert the pace: 1 km per 5 minutes. Step 3 — scale the time up to an hour: 60 minutes / 5 minutes per km = 12 km. As a unit chain: (1 km)/(5 min) x (60 min)/(1 h) = 60/5 = 12 km/h. Check: at 12 km/h each kilometre takes 60/12 = 5 minutes. Correct. Answer: 12.

Trap. Answering 5, or 0.083, by treating pace as if it were already a speed. Minutes per kilometre and kilometres per hour are upside down from each other - a SMALLER pace means a FASTER runner - so the first move must be to invert, and only then to convert the time unit.

Q9 · ARI-RAE-025 — Answer: B

The useful compound unit here is the worker-day: one worker working for one day. Step 1 — size the job: 8 workers x 15 days = 120 worker-days. Step 2 — the wall is the same wall, so it still needs 120 worker-days. Step 3 — share them out: 120 worker-days / 12 workers = 10 days. 'Workers' cancels and 'days' survives. Check: 12 x 10 = 120 worker-days, the same job. Correct. Answer: 10 days.

Trap. Choice D, 22.5, scales in DIRECT proportion (15 x 12/8), as though more workers meant more days. Workers and days are inversely proportional at a fixed job size. Choice A halves the time because the crew grew by 50 percent - but a 50 percent bigger crew cuts the time by a factor of 1.5, not 2, so 15/1.5 = 10. Choice C subtracts the 4 extra workers as 4 days, and choice E multiplies 12 x 15 without ever dividing by the original 8.

Q10 · ARI-RAE-022 — Answer: B

Average speed is ALWAYS total distance divided by total time. Never average the speeds. Step 1 — time for the first leg: 100/50 = 2 hours. Step 2 — time for the second leg: 100/100 = 1 hour. Step 3 — total time = 3 hours, total distance = 200 km. Step 4 — average speed = 200/3 = 66.67 km/h. 66.67 < 75, so Column B is greater. The harmonic-mean shortcut for two EQUAL distances: 2ab/(a+b) = 2(50)(100)/150 = 10000/150 = 66.67. Same answer.

Trap. Averaging the two speeds - (50 + 100)/2 = 75 - and choosing C. The legs cover the same distance but NOT the same time: the car spends 2 hours crawling and only 1 hour flying, so the slow speed gets double weight and the average must be pulled below the midpoint. For equal distances the answer is always strictly less than the plain average of the speeds.

Q11 · ARI-RAE-029 — Answer: A, C, D

Let each half of the journey last t hours, so the total time is T = 2t. Total distance = 80t + 60t = 140t. Average speed = 140t/(2t) = 70 km/h. The t cancels, so this holds for any t. A - the average speed is exactly 70 km/h. TRUE. B - 2(80)(60)/140 = 9600/140 = 68.57 km/h. That is the harmonic mean, which applies when the two DISTANCES are equal, not the two times. FALSE. C - equal times at unequal speeds means the faster leg covers more ground: 80t > 60t. TRUE. D - total distance = 140t = 70 x (2t) = 70T. TRUE. E - the average is exactly 70, not less; the harmonic mean is the wrong tool here. FALSE. Answer: A, C, D.

Trap. Reaching for 2ab/(a+b) the moment two speeds appear, which gives 68.57 and makes B and E look right. The formula is only for equal DISTANCES. When the TIMES are equal, the plain average (a+b)/2 is correct - the one case in which averaging the speeds is legitimate.

Q12 · ARI-RAE-023 — Answer: D

Step 1 — turn each time into a rate in tanks per hour. A fills at 1/6 tank per hour. B drains at 1/9 tank per hour. Step 2 — the pipes oppose each other, so SUBTRACT: net rate = 1/6 - 1/9 = 3/18 - 2/18 = 1/18 tank per hour. Step 3 — invert the rate to get the time for one whole tank: 1 / (1/18) = 18 hours. Check: in 18 hours A puts in 18/6 = 3 tanks and B takes out 18/9 = 2 tanks; net 1 tank. Correct. Answer: 18 hours.

Trap. Adding the rates instead of subtracting: 1/6 + 1/9 = 5/18, giving 3.6 hours, choice A. Rates add only when the agents push the same way; a drain works against the fill and its rate carries a minus sign. Choice C adds the two TIMES (6 + 9), which is never a legal move, choice B averages them, and choice E multiplies them (6 x 9 = 54) on the mistaken analogy with the ab/(a + b) formula, whose denominator has been dropped.

Q13 · ARI-RAE-028 — Answer: 60

Step 2 — outflow: 2 L/min, which opposes the fill, so subtract. Net rate = 8 - 2 = 6 L/min. Step 3 — time = amount / rate = 360 L / 6 L/min = 60 minutes. Litres cancel, minutes survive. Q14 · ARI-RAE-038 — Answer: 30

Step 3 — collect: 40v - 24v = 480, so 16v = 480 and v = 30. Check with a convenient distance. Let each leg be 60 km. Out: 60/20 = 3 hours. Back: 60/30 = 2 hours. Total 120 km in 5 hours = 24 km/h. Trap. Treating 24 as the plain average of the two speeds and answering 28, because (20 + 28)/2 = 24. Test it: at 20 and 28 the true average would be 2(20)(28)/48 = 23.33, not 24. Q15 · ARI-RAE-046 — Answer: E

So the outbound leg takes 3 parts and the return leg 1 part, 4 parts in all. Step 3 — fraction of the total time spent going out = 3/4. Check with numbers: let v = 30 and d = 90. Trap. Reading the speed ratio 1 : 3 straight off as the time ratio and choosing A (1/4) - the leg driven three times faster takes one third of the time, so time is the INVERSE of speed, not proportional to it. Choice B, 1/3, is the same ratio read even more directly: the 1 : 3 in the speeds is copied over as the fraction of time, ignoring that the outbound leg is the SLOW one. Choice C comes from assuming equal distances mean equal times; choice D comes from misreading 3v as merely 'twice as fast', which gives times in the ratio 2 : 1 and a fraction of 2/3.

Q17 · ARI-RAE-047 — Answer: 19.2

Work in rates (jobs per day), because rates are what add. Step 1 — write what is given: a + b = 1/8 b + c = 1/12 a + c = 1/16 Step 2 — the insight: add all three. Every letter appears exactly twice. 2(a + b + c) = 1/8 + 1/12 + 1/16 Step 3 — common denominator 48: 6/48 + 4/48 + 3/48 = 13/48. So a + b + c = 13/96. Step 4 — isolate a by subtracting the pair that does NOT contain a: a = (a + b + c) - (b + c) = 13/96 - 1/12 = 13/96 - 8/96 = 5/96 job per day. Step 5 — invert the rate: A alone takes 96/5 = 19.2 days. Check: b = 13/96 - 1/16 = 7/96 and c = 13/96 - 1/8 = 1/96; then a + b = 12/96 = 1/8. Correct. Answer: 19.2.

Trap. Trying to average the three times - (8 + 12 + 16)/3 = 12 - or solving the three equations one substitution at a time and losing a sign. Times never combine arithmetically; only rates do. The clean route is to add all three equations at once, which gives 2(a+b+c) in one line, then subtract the pair that omits the worker you want.

Q20 · ARI-RAE-074 — Answer: A, B, C

Rate = 1,200 units / 120 machine-hours = 10 units per machine-hour. A - 10 units per machine-hour is exactly 10 units per hour for one machine. TRUE. B - 4 x 5 x 6 = 120 machine-hours, which is the 120 the job needs. TRUE. C - the job is fixed at 1,200 units and the rate is fixed at 10 units per machine-hour, so 1,200/10 = 120 machine-hours every time, however the machines and hours are arranged. TRUE. Q21 · ARI-RAE-056 — Answer: B

Step 3 — distance multiplier: 1.20 x 0.80 = 0.96. Step 4 — 0.96 is a 4% decrease. Check with numbers: 100 km/h for 10 hours = 1,000 km. Now 120 km/h for 8 hours = 960 km, and 960 is 4% below 1,000. Q22 · ARI-RAE-057 — Answer: A, B, D, E

Trap. The commonest error is computing the time for a FULL pool, 1/(1/8) = 8 hours, and rejecting B - the pool starts half full, so only 1/2 of the work remains. Q23 · ARI-RAE-049 — Answer: B

Step 1 — add the rates: 1/a + 1/b = (a + b)/(ab) jobs per day. Step 2 — invert to get the joint time: T = ab/(a + b). Step 3 — compare T with b/2 symbolically rather than by testing numbers: ab/(a + b) vs b/2 Divide both sides by b (positive): a/(a + b) vs 1/2 Cross-multiply (a + b > 0): 2a vs a + b, i.e. a vs b. Step 4 — the stem gives a < b, so the left side is the smaller one: T < b/2 for every allowed pair. Column B is greater. Numerical check: a = 4, b = 12 gives T = 48/16 = 3 against b/2 = 6. Squeeze the two times together: a = 9, b = 10 gives T = 90/19 = 4.74 against b/2 = 5, still below. Correct. Column B is greater.

Trap. Choosing C by taking a = b, where T = a/2 = b/2 exactly - but the stem makes the inequality strict, and a = b is the only case that ties. Choosing D because a and b are unspecified is the second error: the comparison collapses to 'a versus b', which the stem settles, so it resolves for every allowed pair. Choosing A comes from swapping the roles of a and b and comparing T with half of the FASTER worker's time; T is indeed greater than a/2, but b is the slower time and T never reaches half of it.

Q24 · ARI-RAE-058 — Answer: B

Route 1 - equal distances, so use the harmonic mean: 2(60)(40)/(60 + 40) = 4800/100 = 48 km/h. Route 2 - equal times, so the plain average is correct: (60 + 40)/2 = 50 km/h. 48 < 50, so Column B is greater. Why Route 1 loses: splitting the DISTANCE evenly forces the car to spend more TIME at 40 km/h than at 60 km/h, so the slow speed carries more weight. Splitting the time evenly weights the two speeds equally. Neither value depends on D, so D cancels and the comparison is fixed.

Trap. Choosing C because both routes 'use half and half at 60 and 40'. Half the distance and half the time are different halves. The harmonic mean is always less than or equal to the arithmetic mean of the same two numbers, so the equal-distance split can never beat the equal-time split - Column A is smaller for any two unequal speeds.

Q25 · ARI-RAE-062 — Answer: C

Trap. Copying the rate ratio straight across and answering 3/2, which makes Column A look greater. Choosing D because k is unknown is the other error - k cancels out of the ratio.

Q26 · ARI-RAE-064 — Answer: C

Q27 · ARI-RAE-065 — Answer: C

The job has a fixed size measured in worker-days, and that is the invariant to lean on. Step 1 — the job is n x d worker-days. Step 2 — the larger crew does the same job: (n + k)(d - x) = nd. Step 3 — solve for k: n + k = nd/(d - x) k = nd/(d - x) - n k = [nd - n(d - x)]/(d - x) k = [nd - nd + nx]/(d - x) k = nx/(d - x) That is exactly Column B, for every allowed n, d and x. The two quantities are equal. Check with numbers: n = 20, d = 10, x = 2. Then k = 20(2)/8 = 5, and 25 workers x 8 days = 200 = 20 x 10 worker-days. Correct.

Trap. Choosing D because four letters are unspecified. They are not free - the equation (n + k)(d - x) = nd pins k down exactly, so nothing is left to vary. The competing algebraic slip is writing k = nx/d, dividing by the ORIGINAL number of days instead of the new one; with n = 20, d = 10, x = 2 that gives 4 workers, and 24 x 8 = 192 worker-days, short of the 200 the job needs.

Q28 · ARI-RAE-068 — Answer: B, C, D

Add the rates: 1/a + 1/b = (a + b)/(ab), so T = ab/(a + b). A - times never add. Two pipes together are faster than either alone, yet a + b is larger than both. FALSE. B - T = a x [b/(a + b)], and since a > 0 the bracket b/(a + b) is strictly less than 1, so T < a. Help never makes a job slower. TRUE. C - the upper half is B. For the lower half: T >= a/2 is equivalent to ab/(a + b) >= a/2, i.e. 2b >= a + b, i.e. b >= a, which is given. So a/2 <= T < a. TRUE. (Equality T = a/2 occurs exactly when a = b.) D - that is the formula derived above. TRUE. E - the average of the two times. Test a = 2, b = 2: the true T is 1, but (a + b)/2 = 2. FALSE. Answer: B, C, D.

Trap. E is the most tempting wrong answer, because averaging times feels like a compromise between the two pipes; in fact it lands on a value that is slower than the faster pipe working alone. C is the statement most often rejected: students accept T < a but doubt the lower bound, when in fact the slower pipe can never do more than match the faster one, so the joint time can never drop below half of a - a bound worth remembering as a sanity check on any two-agent answer.

Average speed = total distance / total time, and the percentages describe DISTANCE, so they must be converted into times. Step 1 — let the route be 100 km, so the legs are 50 km, 30 km and 20 km. Step 2 — time for each leg: 50/20 = 2.5 hours 30/40 = 0.75 hour 20/60 = 1/3 hour, about 0.333 Step 3 — total time = 2.5 + 0.75 + 1/3 = 30/12 + 9/12 + 4/12 = 43/12 hours, about 3.583 hours. Step 4 — average speed = 100/(43/12) = 1200/43 = 27.9 km/h. 27.9 < 30, so Column B is greater. The 100 km was a free choice and cancels, so the result holds for any route length. Estimate first: over half the distance is driven at 20 km/h, and that leg alone eats 2.5 of the 3.58 hours - about 70% of the trip time - so the average must sit close to 20, nowhere near the middle of the speed range.

Trap. Weighting the SPEEDS by the distance shares: 0.5(20) + 0.3(40) + 0.2(60) = 10 + 12 + 12 = 34, which is above 30 and points at Column A.
