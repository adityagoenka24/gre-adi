Q1 · NUM-DPV-001 — Answer: E

Step 1 — name the places from the right: 8 is units, 2 is tens, 7 is hundreds, 4 is thousands. Step 2 — the digit in the hundreds place is 7. Step 3 — the question asks for the VALUE of that digit, which is the digit times its place: 7 x 100 = 700. Check: 4,728 = 4,000 + 700 + 20 + 8, and the four parts add back to 4,728. Correct. Answer: E.

Trap. Answering 7, choice C - the digit itself rather than its value. Choice B, 4, comes from counting places from the left and landing on the thousands digit. Choice D, 72, comes from reading off the two digits '7' and '2' as a block instead of using place value.

Q2 · NUM-DPV-003 — Answer: 18

Step 1 — the digits are 3, 4, 5 and 6. Step 2 — add them: 3 + 4 = 7, 7 + 5 = 12, 12 + 6 = 18. Check: the digit sum 18 is a multiple of 9, so 3,456 should be divisible by 9, and 3,456/9 = 384. Consistent. Answer: 18.

Q3 · NUM-DPV-004 — Answer: B

Step 1 — the two-digit integers run from 10 to 99. Step 2 — count an inclusive range as (last - first) + 1: 99 - 10 + 1 = 90. Step 3 — slot check, which is the method that generalises: the tens slot has 9 choices (1 through 9, since a leading digit cannot be 0) and the units slot has 10 choices (0 through 9). 9 x 10 = 90. Check: the integers from 1 to 99 number 99, and 9 of them are one-digit, leaving 99 - 9 = 90. Answer: B.

Trap. Subtracting without adding 1: 99 - 10 = 89, choice A. Choice C, 91, comes from counting the 100 integers from 1 to 100 and removing only the nine one-digit ones, forgetting that 100 is not two-digit either. Choice D, 99, comes from counting every integer from 1 to 99, and choice E from treating 100 as a two-digit number.

Q4 · NUM-DPV-006 — Answer: A

Step 3 — we are told a > b, so a - b is at least 1 and 9(a - b) is positive. Step 4 — a positive difference means Quantity A is larger, for every allowed a and b. Check with two cases: a = 5, b = 2 gives 52 vs 25, difference 27 = 9 x 3. a = 9, b = 0 gives 90 vs 09 = 9, difference 81 = 9 x 9. Quantity A wins both times. Answer: A.

Trap. Keying D on the grounds that a and b are not pinned down. They do not need to be: the difference is 9(a - b), and the single condition a > b fixes its sign. The other live error is treating 'the number formed by reversing' when b = 0 as impossible and calling the comparison undefined; 90 reversed is simply 9.

Q6 · NUM-DPV-011 — Answer: D

Q8 · NUM-DPV-017 — Answer: 5535

Step 1 — an integer is divisible by 9 exactly when the sum of its digits is divisible by 9. Use the digit sum; do not divide. Step 2 — by place value the integer is 5,000 + 100d + 30 + d, and its digit sum is 5 + d + 3 + d = 8 + 2d. Step 3 — 8 + 2d is even for every digit d, so it can never equal the odd multiples 9 or 27. With d from 0 to 9 the sum runs from 8 to 26, so the only possible value is 18. Step 4 — 8 + 2d = 18 gives 2d = 10, so d = 5. Step 5 — rebuild the integer: 5,000 + 100(5) + 30 + 5 = 5,535. Check: 5 + 5 + 3 + 5 = 18, a multiple of 9, and 5,535/9 = 615 exactly. Correct. Answer: 5535.

Trap. Answering 5, the digit d, instead of the integer the stem asks for. The second live error is testing divisibility by 3 rather than 9: 8 + 2d = 12 gives d = 2 and the integer 5,232, whose digit sum 12 makes it a multiple of 3 but not of 9. Setting 8 + 2d = 9 and getting the non-integer d = 0.5 is the third: the digit sum here is always even, so 9 itself is unreachable.

Q16 · NUM-DPV-028 — Answer: 67

Step 1 — let the tens digit be a (1 through 9) and the units digit be b (0 through 9). By place value the integer is 10a + b, NOT a x b; the product of its digits is the separate quantity a x b. Rewrite the numerator as 10(a - 1) - 2, which gives b = 10 - 2/(a - 1). For b to be a whole number, a - 1 must divide 2, so a - 1 = 1 or a - 1 = 2, that is a = 2 or a = 3.
