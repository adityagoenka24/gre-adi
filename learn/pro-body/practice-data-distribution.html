Part C — Worked Solutions

Q1 · STA-DDI-001 — Answer: C

Step 1 - a normal curve is perfectly symmetric about its centre and has a single peak. Step 2 - symmetry puts the balance point (the mean) at the centre, and it puts half the area on each side of the centre, so the median is at the centre too. Step 3 - the single peak sits at the centre as well, so the mode is there. All three measures land on the same value, so C is the only statement forced by normality. Check: a normal distribution is fully described by its mean and standard deviation, and the mean is exactly where the curve is highest. Correct.

Trap. A, B, D and E are all statements about SKEWED data smuggled into a normal-curve question. A and B describe right skew (mean above median, mode lowest), E describes left skew. A normal distribution has no skew at all, so any statement putting one of the three measures strictly above another is false.

Q2 · STA-DDI-006 — Answer: 95

Step 1 - measure the endpoints in standard deviations. 130 - 100 = 30, and 30/15 = 2, so 130 is 2 SDs above the mean. 100 - 70 = 30, and 30/15 = 2, so 70 is 2 SDs below the mean. Step 2 - the interval is exactly mean +/- 2 SDs. Step 3 - apply the 68-95-99.7 rule: about 68% lies within 1 SD, about 95% within 2 SDs, about 99.7% within 3 SDs. Within 2 SDs is 95%. Answer: 95.

Trap. Seeing the standard deviation 15 and the endpoints 30 away, then matching '3' or '30' to the 99.7% band and answering 99.7. Convert the distance to a NUMBER OF STANDARD DEVIATIONS first: 30/15 = 2, so the band is the 95% band. Answering 68 comes from using +/- 1 SD (85 to 115) instead.

Q3 · STA-DDI-007 — Answer: B

Step 1 - skew is named for the direction the TAIL points, not for where the bulk of the data sits. Step 2 - the tail here stretches toward the low values, on the left, so the distribution is left-skewed (negatively skewed). Step 3 - the consequence: those few very low values drag the mean down, so mean < median for this shape. Answer: left-skewed.

Trap. Reading 'most values on the right' as right-skewed and choosing A. The bulk of the data always sits OPPOSITE the tail, so the crowded side is never the side that names the skew. Remember the hook: the tail pulls the mean, and the tail is what gets named.

Q4 · STA-DDI-008 — Answer: C

Step 1 - a normal curve is symmetric about its mean m. Step 2 - symmetry means the area to the right of m equals the area to the left of m, and the two areas make up the whole curve. Step 3 - so each area is 0.50: Column A = 0.50 and Column B = 0.50. The two quantities are equal. Note that s never enters the calculation - a wide curve and a narrow curve both split 50/50 at the mean.

Trap. Choosing D because s is unknown. The size of s stretches the curve sideways but cannot move the axis of symmetry off the mean, so the split at m is always 50/50. This 'half the area is below the mean' fact is what makes the 68-95-99.7 bands split evenly on each side.

Q5 · STA-DDI-009 — Answer: A

Step 1 - the original six values: sum = 3 + 5 + 7 + 9 + 11 + 13 = 48, so mean = 48/6 = 8. With six values the median is the average of the 3rd and 4th: (7 + 9)/2 = 8. Step 2 - with 100 appended: sum = 48 + 100 = 148, so mean = 148/7 = 21.14 (about). With seven values the median is the 4th value, which is 9. Step 3 - compare. The mean jumped from 8 to about 21.1, a rise of more than 13. The median moved from 8 to 9, a rise of 1. Answer: the mean rises sharply, the median barely moves.

Trap. Choosing C on the assumption that an outlier drags both measures equally. The mean uses the actual SIZE of every value, so one huge value moves it a long way; the median only cares about POSITION in the ordered list, so an extreme value shifts it by at most one place. That resistance is exactly why the median is reported for skewed data such as incomes.

Q6 · STA-DDI-011 — Answer: C

Step 1 - locate the peak. The mode, 55, is the highest point of a single-peaked histogram, so the bulk of the data sits near 55. Step 2 - locate the mean. The mean, 40, sits 15 BELOW the peak. The mean is the balance point, so something far out on the low side must be pulling it down. Step 3 - that something is a long tail of low values. A tail pointing toward the low end is left skew (negative skew). Step 4 - the ordering confirms it: mean 40 < median 50 < mode 55, which is the standard signature of left skew. Answer: left-skewed with the tail toward lower values.

Trap. Choosing B by pairing the words 'mode is highest' with 'skewed right'. The direction of skew is set by where the TAIL is, and the tail is on the side the mean is dragged toward - here the low side. Choices A and E are also live: they both require mean = median, which 40 and 50 rule out immediately.

Q7 · STA-DDI-013 — Answer: 105

Step 1 - the original sum: 12 + 15 + 18 + 21 + 24 + 27 + 30 = 147, and 147/7 = 21, confirming the given mean. Step 2 - the new mean: 21 + 10.5 = 31.5. Step 3 - the new sum: 8 values with mean 31.5 gives 8 x 31.5 = 252. Step 4 - the added value: 252 - 147 = 105. Check: 147 + 105 = 252, and 252/8 = 31.5, which is 10.5 above 21. Correct. Answer: 105.

Trap. Answering 31.5 (the new mean) or 10.5 (the shift in the mean) instead of the value that was added. The single new value has to carry its own share of the mean PLUS the extra 10.5 needed by all eight entries: 31.5 + 7 x 10.5 = 105 is the same computation seen a second way.

Q8 · STA-DDI-017 — Answer: C

Step 1 - write down the midpoints: 65, 75, 85, 95. Step 2 - the mean of a frequency distribution is a WEIGHTED average: multiply each value by its frequency, add, then divide by the total frequency. 5 x 65 = 325 10 x 75 = 750 20 x 85 = 1700 15 x 95 = 1425 Step 3 - total of the products: 325 + 750 + 1700 + 1425 = 4200. Step 4 - total frequency: 5 + 10 + 20 + 15 = 50. Step 5 - mean = 4200/50 = 84. Check: 84 lies between the two heaviest intervals (80-90 with 20 scores and 90-100 with 15), pulled slightly below 85 by the lighter low intervals. Correct. Answer: 84.

Trap. Choice B, 80, is the average of the four midpoints (65 + 75 + 85 + 95)/4 - it ignores the frequencies entirely and so treats 5 scores as though they counted as much as 20. Choice D, 85, is the midpoint of the modal interval, the interval with the most scores. Choice A, 79, comes from using the LOWER endpoint of each interval instead of the midpoint, and choice E, 89, from using the upper endpoint.

Q9 · STA-DDI-019 — Answer: A, B, C

A - 'skewed to the right' means precisely that the long tail runs toward the high values, away from the peak. TRUE. B - by the definition of the median, at least half of the values are at or below it, and the median is 44. TRUE. C - the largest value is always at least the mean, and it is strictly greater unless every value is identical - which is ruled out here, since identical values would force mean = median. Removing a value that sits above the mean must pull the mean down: the nine-value list 10, 10, 10, 10, 10, 10, 10, 10, 370 has mean 50, and dropping the 370 leaves a mean of 10. TRUE. D - mode < median < mean is a rule of thumb for skewed data, not a theorem, and nothing given pins the mode below 44. FALSE. E - the mean is a balance point, not an entry in the list. The two values 44 and 56 have mean 50 even though neither of them is 50. FALSE. Answer: A, B, C.

Trap. D is the tempting one: the chain mode < median < mean describes typical skewed data, so it can never be asserted as something that MUST be true. E catches the belief that the mean has to be an actual data value - it is only the balance point of the list, and it frequently sits between the values rather than on one.

Q10 · STA-DDI-022 — Answer: 400

Step 1 - the 68-95-99.7 rule: about 68% of the data lies within 1 SD of the mean, so about 100% - 68% = 32% lies outside that band. Step 2 - the curve is symmetric, so halve the 32%: about 16% lies below mean - 1 SD, and about 16% above mean + 1 SD. Step 3 - so mean - 1 SD is the point with about 16% of the data below it, which is the 16th percentile to the accuracy of the rule. Step 4 - mean - 1 SD = 500 - 100 = 400. Check: the marks read 300, 400, 500, 600, 700 with cumulative percentages 2.5, 16, 50, 84, 97.5. The 16th percentile is 400. Correct. Answer: 400.

Trap. Forgetting to halve the 32% and treating the 32nd percentile as the 1-SD mark, or taking 16% of 500 and answering 80. The percentile scale on a normal curve is built from the halved tails: 2.5 / 16 / 50 / 84 / 97.5 at -2, -1, 0, +1 and +2 SDs.

Q11 · STA-DDI-023 — Answer: A

Step 1 - the standard deviation measures typical distance from the mean, so it describes SPREAD, not centre. Step 2 - Class A has SD 5, Class B has SD 15. Class A's scores sit on average three times closer to the mean, so they are more tightly clustered. A is true. Step 3 - check the others. C is false because 5 is not 15. D is false because the two means are both 70. E sounds plausible but is not forced: the standard deviation is computed from every value, while the highest score is a single value, and a larger SD does not guarantee a larger maximum in any particular pair of data sets. Answer: A.

Trap. Reading a bigger standard deviation as a bigger or better score and choosing D or E. Standard deviation says nothing about the centre and nothing about any single value - it reports only how far from the mean a typical value lies. Note also that no normal-curve percentages may be used here: the question never says the scores are normally distributed.

Q12 · STA-DDI-024 — Answer: D

Step 1 - with 11 values sorted, the median is the 6th value, so the 6th value is 45. Step 2 - 200 is larger than 45, so it is inserted somewhere after position 6. The first six values are therefore unchanged and the new 6th value is still 45, while the new 7th value is whichever is smaller, the old 7th value or 200. Step 3 - with 12 values the median is the average of the 6th and 7th: (45 + w)/2, where w is the smaller of the old 7th value and 200, so w >= 45. Step 4 - test two legal data sets. If the data set is 41, 42, 43, 44, 44, 45, 45, 45, 45, 46, 47, then the old 7th value is 45, so w = 45 and the new median is (45 + 45)/2 = 45, equal to Column B. If the data set is 41, 42, 43, 44, 44, 45, 55, 56, 57, 58, 59, then the old 7th value is 55, so w = 55 and the new median is (45 + 55)/2 = 50, greater than Column B. Two legal data sets give two different comparisons, so the relationship cannot be determined.

Trap. Choosing C on the slogan 'the median ignores outliers'. The median is resistant to an outlier's SIZE, but adding a twelfth value still changes which positions are averaged, so the median can move - here it can only move up, but whether it actually moves depends on the 7th value. Choosing A is the mirror error: assuming a large addition must raise the median.

Q13 · STA-DDI-025 — Answer: A, E

Step 1 - IQR = Q3 - Q1 = 60 - 40 = 20. Step 2 - 1.5 x IQR = 30. Step 3 - lower fence = Q1 - 30 = 40 - 30 = 10. Upper fence = Q3 + 30 = 60 + 30 = 90. Step 4 - test each value against the interval from 10 to 90. A - 8 < 10, below the lower fence. OUTLIER. B - 35 is between 10 and 90. Not an outlier. C - 55 is between 10 and 90 (it is inside the box itself). Not an outlier. D - 85 is between 10 and 90, so it is unusual but not an outlier by this rule. Not an outlier. E - 92 > 90, above the upper fence. OUTLIER. Answer: A and E.

Trap. Choice D, 85, is the bait: it lies outside the box (above Q3 = 60) and looks extreme, but the fence is 90, not 60. The other common slip is applying 1.5 x IQR to the wrong ends - subtracting 30 from Q3 and adding 30 to Q1 gives the reversed interval 30 to 70, which flags 85 as well and produces the wrong answer A, D and E.

Q14 · STA-DDI-027 — Answer: C

The interval is lopsided - 2 SDs on the left but only 1 SD on the right - so split it at the mean and use the 68-95-99.7 rule on each half. Step 1 - left piece, from m - 2s to m. 95% lies within 2 SDs, and symmetry puts half of that on each side, so this piece is 95/2 = 47.5%. Step 2 - right piece, from m to m + s. 68% lies within 1 SD, and half of that is on the right, so this piece is 68/2 = 34%. Step 3 - add: 47.5% + 34% = 81.5%, so p = 0.815. Column A = 0.815 = Column B. The two quantities are equal. Note that m and s never appear in the arithmetic - the percentages depend only on how many SDs away the endpoints are.

Trap. Choosing D because m and s are unknown; the band percentages are the same for every normal curve. The arithmetic trap is averaging 68 and 95 to get 81.5 by luck, or taking (95 + 68)/2 as a rule - it happens to give the right number here only because both pieces are exact halves. Always split at the mean and halve each band separately.

Q15 · STA-DDI-028 — Answer: A

Step 1 - recover the total. Mean 60 over 100 values means the sum is 100 x 60 = 6000. Step 2 - the replacement changes the sum by 500 - 5 = 495, so the new sum is 6000 + 495 = 6495. Step 3 - the new mean is 6495/100 = 64.95. The rise is 495/100 = 4.95, the change in the SUM spread over all 100 values. Step 4 - the median. Removing the smallest value and inserting a value at the top end moves every value at least as far up the sorted list as it was before, so the 50th and 51st values can only stay the same or increase. The new median is therefore 60 or greater. Step 5 - it need not increase: if the 50th, 51st and 52nd values are all 60, the median stays exactly 60. So 'is 60 or greater' is the strongest claim available. Answer: A.

Trap. Choice B applies the whole 495 to the mean, forgetting to divide by the 100 values that share it. Choice C assumes the median tracks the mean; the median moves at most one position when a single value is relocated, so it cannot jump 4.95. Q16 · STA-DDI-029 — Answer: 540

Step 1 - convert the endpoints to SDs. 600 = 500 + 100 = mean + 1 SD. 700 = 500 + 200 = mean + 2 SDs. Step 2 - the band between +1 SD and +2 SDs. Within 2 SDs is about 95%; within 1 SD is about 68%. Check with the standard band figures 34 / 13.5 / 2.35: from the mean upward they read 34% (0 to 1 SD), 13.5% (1 to 2 SDs), 2.35% (2 to 3 SDs), and 34 + 13.5 + 2.35 + 0.15 = 50%, the whole upper half. Q17 · STA-DDI-030 — Answer: A, B, D

A - a normal distribution is symmetric, so it forces mean = median. Here the mean is 80 and the median is 70, so the data cannot be normal. TRUE. B - the largest value is always at least the mean, and it is strictly greater unless every value is identical; identical values would force mean = median, which fails here. So some value exceeds 80. TRUE. C - the interval 60 to 100 is mean +/- 1 SD, and the 68% figure comes from the 68-95-99.7 rule, which holds only for a NORMAL distribution. Statement A has just established that this data is not normal, so no percentage can be claimed. FALSE. D - mean = sum/n, so sum = mean x n = 80n. This is the definition of the mean and holds for every data set. TRUE. E - mean > median is the usual signature of a high tail, but it does not prove one, and a counterexample has to honour all three given statistics. Take the nine values 40, 70, 70, 70, 70, 100, 100, 100, 100. Sum = 720, so the mean is 720/9 = 80. The middle value is the 5th, which is 70, so the median is 70. The variance is [(-40)^2 + 4(-10)^2 + 4(20)^2]/9 = (1600 + 400 + 1600)/9 = 3600/9 = 400, so the SD is 20. Every number in the stem is matched, yet the minimum sits 40 below the mean while the maximum sits only 20 above it, and the lone stray value 40 is the thin end: the long tail is on the LOW side. FALSE. Answer: A, B, D.

Trap. C is the headline trap named in the capsule: applying 68-95-99.7 to data that was never said to be normal - and here the numbers actually prove it is not normal. E is the subtler one: 'mean above median therefore right-skewed' is a reliable description of real data but not a logical implication, so it fails a 'must be true' test.

Q19 · STA-DDI-032 — Answer: A

Step 1 - the new value equals the old mean, so the sum rises by 50 and the count rises by 1: the mean of data set B is still 50. Step 2 - work with the total of the squared deviations. For data set A, variance = SD^2 = 10^2 = 100, and variance = (total squared deviation)/n, so the total squared deviation is 100 x 10 = 1000. Step 3 - the added value is 50, which is exactly the mean, so it contributes (50 - 50)^2 = 0. The total squared deviation for data set B is still 1000, but it is now divided among 11 values. Step 4 - variance of B = 1000/11 = 90.9, so SD of B = sqrt(90.9) = 9.54. Step 5 - 10 > 9.54, so Column A is greater. The principle: adding a value AT the mean adds no new deviation but does enlarge n, so it dilutes the spread. The standard deviation must fall.

Trap. Choosing C because 'a value at the mean changes nothing'. It changes nothing about the MEAN, but the standard deviation is an average squared distance, and averaging the same total distance over 11 values instead of 10 makes it smaller. Choosing D is the other error - the answer does not depend on which 10 values data set A contains, since only the totals matter.

Q20 · STA-DDI-033 — Answer: 50000

Step 1 - convert every mean into a total, because totals add and means do not. Total payroll = 200 x 75,000 = 15,000,000. Step 2 - total for the top 50 = 50 x 150,000 = 7,500,000. Q26 · STA-DDI-037 — Answer: D

Compute each region separately, halving every band because the curve is symmetric. Step 1 - Region I, below m - 2s. About 95% lies within 2 SDs, so 5% lies outside, and half of that is in the lower tail: 2.5%. Step 2 - Region II, between m + s and m + 2s. About 99.7% lies within 3 SDs, so 0.3% lies outside, and half of that is in the upper tail: 0.15%.
