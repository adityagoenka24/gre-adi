Bank code: WRD-WRK 30 questions

Bank code: WRD-WRK · Section: Word Problems · 30 questions — Easy 5 · Medium 9 · Hard 16

Rates add, times don't - set the job to the LCM of the individual times and every fraction disappears.

Every question below is live in the question bank under the ID shown — the sheet and the portal are the same questions. Attempt a level with the clock running, then check Part C.

Part C — Worked Solutions

Q1 · WRD-WRK-001 — Answer: B

Step 1 — use least common multiple units so no fractions appear. Let the job = the least common multiple of 6 and 4 = 12 units. Step 2 — A does 12/6 = 2 units per hour. B does 12/4 = 3 units per hour. Step 3 — rates add: together they do 2 + 3 = 5 units per hour. Step 4 — time = 12/5 = 2.4 hours = 2 hours 24 minutes. Shortcut: for two workers, time = ab/(a + b) = (6 x 4)/(6 + 4) = 24/10 = 2.4 hours. Check against the story: in 2.4 hours A does 2.4/6 = 0.4 of the job and B does 2.4/4 = 0.6 of the job, and 0.4 + 0.6 = 1 whole job. Correct. Answer: B.

Trap. Adding the two times, 6 + 4 = 10, gives choice E. Times never add; only rates do. Averaging the times, (6 + 4)/2 = 5, gives choice D. Both fail the bound test: two workers must finish faster than the faster one alone, so the answer has to be under 4 hours. Choice C, 4 hours, is simply B's solo time, the answer to 'how fast is the faster machine' - help can only make the job quicker than 4 hours, never exactly 4. Choice A, 25 minutes, comes from inverting the shortcut the wrong way as (a + b)/ab = 10/24 hour.

Q2 · WRD-WRK-005 — Answer: C

Step 1 — let the cistern = the least common multiple of 4 and 12 = 12 units. Step 2 — the pipe adds 12/4 = 3 units per hour. The drain removes 12/12 = 1 unit per hour, so its rate is -1. Step 3 — a drain opposes the fill, so subtract: net rate = 3 - 1 = 2 units per hour. Step 4 — time = 12/2 = 6 hours. Check against the story: in 6 hours the pipe pours in 6/4 = 1.5 cisterns' worth and the drain removes 6/12 = 0.5 of a cistern, leaving exactly 1 cistern full. Correct. Answer: C.

Trap. Treating the drain as a second filler and adding the rates, 1/4 + 1/12 = 1/3, gives 3 hours, choice A. The drain's rate is negative and must be subtracted. Choice B, 4 hours, ignores the drain altogether and reports the pipe's own filling time; a drain can only make the fill take longer. Averaging the times, (4 + 12)/2 = 8, gives choice D, and adding them, 4 + 12 = 16, gives choice E.

Q3 · WRD-WRK-003 — Answer: 4

Step 1 — let the tank = the least common multiple of 12 and 6 = 12 units. Step 2 — A delivers 12/12 = 1 unit per hour; B delivers 12/6 = 2 units per hour. Step 3 — rates add: 1 + 2 = 3 units per hour. Step 4 — time = 12/3 = 4 hours. Check against the story: in 4 hours A fills 4/12 = 1/3 of the tank and B fills 4/6 = 2/3, and 1/3 + 2/3 = 1 full tank. Correct. Answer: 4.

Trap. Adding the times, 12 + 6 = 18, or averaging them to get 9. Neither can be right: together must beat B's own 6 hours, so any answer of 6 or more is impossible on sight.

Q4 · WRD-WRK-014 — Answer: 6

Step 1 — combined rate = 1/4 of the report per hour. A's rate = 1/12 per hour. Step 2 — rates add, so B's rate = combined rate - A's rate = 1/4 - 1/12. Step 3 — 1/4 = 3/12, so 3/12 - 1/12 = 2/12 = 1/6 per hour. Step 4 — B alone takes 1/(1/6) = 6 hours. Check against the story: in 4 hours A prints 4/12 = 1/3 of the report and B prints 4/6 = 2/3, and 1/3 + 2/3 = 1 whole report. Correct. Answer: 6.

Trap. Subtracting the times, 12 - 4 = 8, and answering 8. Times do not subtract any more than they add; the subtraction happens at the level of rates. Testing 8 exposes it: 1/12 + 1/8 = 5/24, which is a combined time of 24/5 = 4.8 hours, not 4.

Q5 · WRD-WRK-007 — Answer: C

Step 1 — let the job = the least common multiple of 10 and 15 = 30 units. Step 2 — A does 30/10 = 3 units per hour; B does 30/15 = 2 units per hour. Step 3 — together they do 3 + 2 = 5 units per hour, so the time is 30/5 = 6 hours. Step 4 — Quantity A = 6 and Quantity B = 6, so the two quantities are equal. Check against the story: in 6 hours A finishes 6/10 = 3/5 of the job and B finishes 6/15 = 2/5, and 3/5 + 2/5 = 1 whole job. Correct. Answer: C.

Trap. Averaging the times gives (10 + 15)/2 = 12.5 hours, which makes Quantity A look larger and points to choice A. That answer is impossible before any arithmetic: two workers together must beat A's own 10 hours, so Quantity A must be below 10.

Q6 · WRD-WRK-016 — Answer: B

Step 1 — let the project = the least common multiple of 9 and 6 = 18 units. A does 2 units per hour; B does 3 units per hour. Step 2 — A alone for 3 hours does 3 x 2 = 6 units. Step 3 — remaining work = 18 - 6 = 12 units. Step 4 — once B joins the pair does 2 + 3 = 5 units per hour. Step 5 — time after B joins = 12/5 = 2.4 hours = 2 hours 24 minutes. Check against the story: A works 3 + 2.4 = 5.4 hours at 2 units per hour = 10.8 units; B works 2.4 hours at 3 units per hour = 7.2 units; 10.8 + 7.2 = 18 units, the whole project. Correct. Answer: B.

Trap. Forgetting to subtract the head start and dividing the whole job by the combined rate, 18/5 = 3.6 hours, which is choice D. The question asks only for the time after B joins, and 3 hours of work is already banked. Choice E, 6 hours, comes from taking the remaining 12 units at A's own rate of 2 units per hour, 12/2 = 6, as if B never arrived. Choice A, 2 hours, divides the remaining 12 units by 6, B's solo TIME, instead of by the combined 5 units per hour. Choice C, 3 hours, subtracts the 3 elapsed hours from the 18 units to get 15 and divides that by 5; hours and units cannot be subtracted from each other.

Q7 · WRD-WRK-026 — Answer: A

Step 1 — B takes 40 percent longer than 10 hours: 10 x 1.4 = 14 hours. Step 2 — A's rate = 1/10 per hour; B's rate = 1/14 per hour. Step 3 — combined rate = 1/10 + 1/14. Using denominator 70: 7/70 + 5/70 = 12/70 = 6/35 per hour. Step 4 — time = 35/6 hours = 5 5/6 hours = 5 hours 50 minutes. Shortcut: ab/(a + b) = (10 x 14)/24 = 140/24 = 35/6. Check against the story: in 35/6 hours A writes (35/6)/10 = 7/12 of the report and B writes (35/6)/14 = 5/12, and 7/12 + 5/12 = 1 whole report. Correct. Answer: A.

Trap. Reading '40 percent longer' as '40 percent as fast' and setting B's rate at 0.4 x (1/10) = 1/25, which gives (10 x 25)/35 = 50/7 hours, or 7 hours 9 minutes to the nearest minute, choice C. A separate error takes B's time as 14 and then averages the times, (10 + 14)/2 = 12, choice E. Choice B, 6 hours, reads the numerator of the combined rate 6/35 per hour as a count of hours, and choice D, 8 hours 24 minutes, is 60 percent of 14 hours - a percentage applied to B's time rather than to A's.

Q8 · WRD-WRK-034 — Answer: C

Step 1 — measure the job in worker-days: 10 workers x 20 days = 200 worker-days. Step 2 — the first 5 days use 10 x 5 = 50 worker-days. Step 3 — remaining work = 200 - 50 = 150 worker-days. Step 4 — 6 workers remain, so the days needed = 150/6 = 25. Check against the story: 50 worker-days done plus 6 x 25 = 150 worker-days gives 200, the full job, and the project runs 5 + 25 = 30 days in total instead of 20. Correct. Answer: C.

Trap. Answering 30, choice D, which is the TOTAL elapsed time from day zero rather than the additional days asked for. Choice A, 15 days, divides the remaining 150 worker-days by the original 10 workers instead of the 6 who stay. Choice B, 20 days, is the original schedule copied back, as if the departures cost nothing. Choice E, 35 days, finds the correct 30-day total and then adds the 5 days already worked a second time.

Q9 · WRD-WRK-023 — Answer: 18

Step 1 — combined rate of all three = 1/3 of the house per hour. Step 2 — first friend's rate = 1/6; second friend's rate = 1/9. Step 3 — third friend's rate = 1/3 - 1/6 - 1/9. Using denominator 18: 6/18 - 3/18 - 2/18 = 1/18 per hour. Step 4 — third friend alone takes 1/(1/18) = 18 hours. Check against the story: in 3 hours the three do 3/6 + 3/9 + 3/18 = 1/2 + 1/3 + 1/6 = 1 whole house. Correct. Answer: 18.

Trap. Subtracting times instead of rates: 6 + 9 = 15, then 15 - 3 = 12, or 9 - 6 - 3 = 0. Only rates are additive. A third friend at 12 hours would give 1/6 + 1/9 + 1/12 = 13/36 per hour, a combined time of 36/13 = 2.77 hours, not 3.

Q10 · WRD-WRK-025 — Answer: C

Step 1 — rates add: 1/n + 1/(2n) = 2/(2n) + 1/(2n) = 3/(2n) jobs per hour. Step 2 — time = 1/(combined rate) = 2n/3 hours. Step 3 — Quantity A = 2n/3, which is exactly Quantity B, for every n > 0. Check against the story with n = 6: A takes 6 hours, B takes 12 hours, together (6 x 12)/18 = 4 hours, and 2n/3 = 12/3 = 4. Correct. Answer: C.

Trap. Choosing D because n is unknown. The n cancels: the answer is the same algebraic expression for every positive n, so the relationship is fully determined. The other live error inverts once too often, reporting the combined RATE 3/(2n) as if it were the time, which makes Quantity B look larger and points to B.

Q11 · WRD-WRK-031 — Answer: C

Step 1 — let the total time be T hours. B works all T hours; A works T - 2 hours. Step 2 — the two contributions must sum to one whole job: (T - 2)/8 + T/12 = 1. Step 3 — multiply through by 24: 3(T - 2) + 2T = 24. Step 4 — 3T - 6 + 2T = 24, so 5T = 30 and T = 6. Step 5 — Quantity A = 6 hours = Quantity B. Check against the story: A works 4 hours and does 4/8 = 1/2 the job; B works 6 hours and does 6/12 = 1/2 the job; the halves sum to one job and A did indeed stop 2 hours early. Correct. Answer: C.

Trap. Computing the together-time first, (8 x 12)/20 = 4.8 hours, and then adding the 2 solo hours to get 6.8, which makes Quantity A look larger and points to choice A. The 2 hours are not extra time bolted on; they are the tail of the job during which only B is working, and the whole schedule must be solved as one equation.

Q12 · WRD-WRK-021 — Answer: B, C, D

Combined rate = 1/12 + 1/8 = 2/24 + 3/24 = 5/24 of the task per hour, so together they take 24/5 = 4.8 hours. A. False. 4.8 hours is not less than 4 hours. The bound is that the pair beats B's 8 hours, not that they beat half of it. B. True. The rate is the 5/24 computed above and 0.8 hour = 48 minutes, so 4.8 hours is 4 hours 48 minutes. C. True. A alone for 3 hours does 3/12 = 1/4 of the task, leaving 3/4. At the combined rate that takes (3/4)/(5/24) = (3/4)(24/5) = 18/5 = 3.6 hours = 3 hours 36 minutes, so the finish is at 3 + 3.6 = 6.6 hours = 6 hours 36 minutes. D. True. B's rate 1/8 exceeds A's rate 1/12 and both work the same 4.8 hours, so B does 4.8/8 = 3/5 of the task against A's 4.8/12 = 2/5. E. False. A's rate is 1/12 = 2/24 per hour, not 3/24; 3/24 = 1/8 is B's rate. Check against the story: in 4.8 hours A does 2/5 and B does 3/5, and 2/5 + 3/5 = 1 whole task; in the option C scenario A works 6.6 hours for 6.6/12 = 11/20 and B works 3.6 hours for 3.6/8 = 9/20, and 11/20 + 9/20 = 1. Correct. Answer: B, C and D.

Trap. Option A is the 'halve the faster time' error: students remember that the pair beats 8 hours and guess the answer is under 4. The true bound is between 4 and 8. Option C tempts the opposite slip - reporting 3 hours 36 minutes, the length of the shared stretch, rather than the 6 hours 36 minutes measured from A's start - and also catches anyone who divides the WHOLE task by the combined rate after the head start. Option E swaps the two rates, assigning B's 1/8 to A; the tell is that the slower worker cannot have the larger rate.

Q13 · WRD-WRK-028 — Answer: A, B, D

Net rate = 1/9 - 1/18. Using denominator 18: 2/18 - 1/18 = 1/18 of the tank per hour. A. True. That is the computation above; the drain is subtracted from the inlet, not added to it. B. True. Time = 1/(1/18) = 18 hours. C. False. After 6 hours the tank holds 6 x 1/18 = 1/3, not 2/3. D. True. Two stages: A alone for 3 hours puts in 3 x 1/9 = 1/3 of a tank, and the remaining 2/3 at the net rate 1/18 takes (2/3)(18) = 12 hours, so the tank is full at 3 + 12 = 15 hours. That is 18 - 15 = 3 hours sooner. E. False. Then the net rate would be 1/9 - 1/6 = 2/18 - 3/18 = -1/18, a net emptying. The tank would never fill at all. Check against the story: in 18 hours pipe A pours in 18/9 = 2 tanks' worth while pipe B removes 18/18 = 1 tank's worth, leaving exactly 1 tank. Correct. Answer: A, B and D.

Trap. Option C catches students who read 1/3 filled and report the 2/3 that is still empty. Option E catches the student who subtracts the times, 9 and 6, or who assumes any drain slower than the tank's total volume still lets it fill; once the drain rate exceeds the fill rate the level never rises. Option D catches the student who prices the head start at the whole difference between the two fill times, 18 - 9 = 9 hours, as if B stayed shut all the way; B is closed for 3 hours only, and the saving is 3 hours.

Q14 · WRD-WRK-055 — Answer: 5
