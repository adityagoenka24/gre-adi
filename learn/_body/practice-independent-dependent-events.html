Q1 · STA-IDE-001 — Answer: A

Step 1 — decide independent or dependent. Flipping a coin does nothing to the die, so the pool of die outcomes is unchanged. The events are independent. Step 2 — for independent events, P(A and B) = P(A) x P(B). P(heads) = 1/2. P(rolling a 4) = 1/6. Step 3 — multiply: (1/2) x (1/6) = 1/12. Check by listing: there are 2 x 6 = 12 equally likely coin-die pairs, and exactly one of them is (heads, 4). 1/12. Correct.

Trap. Adding instead of multiplying: 1/2 + 1/6 = 2/3, which is choice E. 'And' calls for multiplication; addition answers a different question. Choice D, 7/12, is P(heads or a 4) = 1/2 + 1/6 - 1/12, and choice B is the die probability alone.

Q2 · STA-IDE-006 — Answer: 1/25

Step 1 — independence means day 2 is unaffected by day 1: whatever happened yesterday, P(late) is still 1/5 today. So no number needs updating between the stages. Step 2 — AND across independent stages multiplies: (1/5) x (1/5) = 1/25. Check by listing the four combinations: late-late (1/5)(1/5) = 1/25, late-on time (1/5)(4/5) = 4/25, on time-late (4/5)(1/5) = 4/25, on time-on time (4/5)(4/5) = 16/25. Those add to (1 + 4 + 4 + 16)/25 = 1. Correct. Answer: 1/25.

Trap. Adding the two probabilities: 1/5 + 1/5 = 2/5. Addition belongs to 'late on at least one of the two days', and even there the overlap must come out: 2/5 - 1/25 = 9/25. The other error is treating the two days as mutually exclusive - as if being late twice were impossible - and answering 0; independent events with positive probabilities can and do both happen.

Q3 · STA-IDE-007 — Answer: C

Step 1 — apply the diagnostic: after the first event happens, has the pool changed? A - one card is gone, so the second draw comes from 51 cards. The pool changed. Dependent. B - the marble is kept, so the bag is one marble smaller. Dependent. C - a die and a coin are separate physical objects. Rolling the die removes nothing from the coin's two faces, so P(heads) is 1/2 either way. Independent. D - the first student cannot be selected again, so the class is one person smaller. Dependent. E - the card is set aside, so the deck has shrunk. Dependent. Answer: C.

Trap. Choosing E because the second card comes from 'the same deck', which sounds like nothing has changed. Setting the first card aside is exactly what removes it: same source, smaller pool, therefore dependent. Every option except C contains the phrase 'without replacement' in some disguise.

Q4 · STA-IDE-009 — Answer: C

Step 1 — read the mechanism. The chip is returned, so the jar is back to 5 red and 5 blue before the second draw. The draws are independent. Step 2 — Column A: P(first red) = 5/10 = 1/2. Step 3 — Column B: the conditioning event is 'the first chip was red', but that chip went back in. The jar the second draw sees is identical, so P(second red | first red) = 5/10 = 1/2. Both columns equal 1/2, so the quantities are equal. This is the formal test for independence in action: P(B | A) = P(B).

Trap. Assuming a conditional probability must differ from the plain probability, and answering B or A on the grounds that 'a red one is already gone'. Nothing is gone - the stem says the chip is returned. The vertical bar signals a condition, not automatically a change.

Q10 · STA-IDE-020 — Answer: A

Step 1 — independence means that learning whether A happened tells you nothing new about B. Here B has probability 0.4 whether A happened or not, so knowing A carries no information. That is independence. Step 2 — confirm with the formal test by first recovering P(B): P(B) = P(B | A) x P(A) + P(B | not A) x P(not A) = 0.4 x 0.5 + 0.4 x 0.5 = 0.20 + 0.20 = 0.40. Step 3 — the test is P(B | A) = P(B): 0.4 = 0.4. Independent. Equivalently P(A and B) = P(B | A) x P(A) = 0.4 x 0.5 = 0.20 = P(A) x P(B) = 0.5 x 0.4. Answer: A.

Trap. Choosing E on the grounds that P(B) was never stated. It does not need to be stated - P(B | A) = P(B | not A) forces P(B) to equal that common value, since P(B) is the weighted average of the two conditionals. Choice C compares P(B | A) with P(A), which is not the test; the test compares P(B | A) with P(B). Choice D is the mutually-exclusive confusion: independence does not require a zero overlap, it requires P(A and B) = P(A) x P(B), and 0.20 is exactly 0.5 x 0.4.

Q12 · STA-IDE-022 — Answer: A

A conditional probability restricts the universe to the condition, so the two columns count within different universes. Column A - restrict to the 13 hearts. Every heart is red, so 13 of 13 qualify: P(red | heart) = 13/13 = 1. Column B - restrict to the 26 red cards. Of these, 13 are hearts and 13 are diamonds: P(heart | red) = 13/26 = 1/2. 1 > 1/2, so Column A is greater. Q13 · STA-IDE-025 — Answer: A, C, D

Here the left side is 0, but P(A) > 0 and P(B) > 0 make the right side strictly positive. The equation fails, so the events are NOT independent. FALSE. C - P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B) - P(A and B) = P(A) + P(B) - 0. TRUE. D - P(A | B) = P(A and B)/P(B) = 0/P(B) = 0. TRUE. E - P(B | A) = P(A and B)/P(A) = 0, while P(B) > 0. Trap. Selecting B because 'mutually exclusive' sounds like 'unrelated'. It is the opposite: mutually exclusive events with nonzero probability are always dependent, because learning that A occurred drops B's probability all the way to 0. Selecting E is the same mistake stated numerically.
