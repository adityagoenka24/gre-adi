2. Must-Know Rules

Term Definition
Chord segment joining two points on the circle
Diameter the longest chord, passing through the centre
Tangent a line touching the circle at exactly one point
Secant a line cutting the circle at two points
Inscribed angle an angle with its vertex on the circle
Central angle an angle with its vertex at the centre

The key theorems:

Theorem Statement
Tangent-radius The radius to the point of tangency is perpendicular to the tangent
Perpendicular from centre A perpendicular from the centre to a chord bisects it
Equal chords Equal chords are equidistant from the centre
Inscribed angle An inscribed angle = half the central angle on the same arc
Angle in a semicircle An angle inscribed in a semicircle is a right angle
Same arc Inscribed angles on the same arc are equal
Two tangents from a point Tangent segments from an external point are equal in length

Chord distance formula. For a chord of length c in a circle of radius r, the perpendicular distance d from the centre satisfies:

r^2 = d^2 + (c/2)^2

Angle in a semicircle is the most-tested of these: if a triangle is inscribed in a circle with one side as the diameter, the opposite angle is 90 degrees.

3. Worked Examples

Example 1 — Chord distance

A circle has radius 13. A chord is 5 units from the centre. How long is the chord?

Drop a perpendicular from the centre to the chord; it bisects the chord and creates a right triangle with hypotenuse r = 13 and one leg d = 5.

r^2 = d^2 + (c/2)^2

169 = 25 + (c/2)^2 -> (c/2)^2 = 144 -> c/2 = 12

Chord = 24. (The 5-12-13 triple again.)

Example 2 — Tangent length

A point P is 17 units from the centre of a circle of radius 8. How long is the tangent segment from P to the circle?

The radius to the point of tangency is perpendicular, so we have a right triangle with hypotenuse 17 and one leg 8.

Tangent = sqrt(289 - 64) = sqrt(225) = 15. (8-15-17.)

Example 3 — Inscribed angle

A central angle subtends an arc of 80 degrees. What is the inscribed angle on the same arc?

Inscribed = half of central = 40 degrees.

4. GRE Traps

  • Assuming a line is tangent because it looks tangent. It must be stated or marked.
  • Inscribed angle equal to the central angle. It is half.
  • Central angle equal to the inscribed angle. It is double. Know which direction you are going.
  • Forgetting the semicircle right angle. If a triangle's side is a diameter, the opposite angle is 90 — this is often the unstated key to the problem.
  • Chord bisected by any line from the centre. Only the perpendicular one bisects it.
  • Confusing arc measure (in degrees) with arc length (in units).
  • Tangent segments from an external point assumed unequal. They are equal.

5. Speed Tricks

  • Draw the radius to the point of tangency immediately — it hands you a right angle for free.
  • Drop a perpendicular from the centre to any chord — it hands you a right triangle with legs d and c/2.
  • Look for a diameter in the figure. If one exists, hunt for the inscribed right angle.
  • Inscribed = central / 2. Say it once out loud per question to avoid inverting it.
  • Pythagorean triples appear constantly in chord and tangent problems: 5-12-13, 8-15-17, 3-4-5.

6. Self-Check

Q1. A chord of length 16 lies in a circle of radius 10. How far is it from the centre?

Q2. A triangle is inscribed in a circle with one side as the diameter. What is the angle opposite that side?

Q3. A central angle measures 110 degrees. What is the inscribed angle on the same arc?

Answers

A1. 100 = d^2 + 64 -> d^2 = 36 -> d = 6.

A2. 90 degrees (angle in a semicircle).

A3. 110/2 = 55 degrees.

7. One-Line Summary for the Board

Radius meets tangent at 90. Perpendicular from the centre bisects the chord. Inscribed angle is half the central angle.
