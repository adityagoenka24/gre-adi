2. Must-Know Rules

Circle inside a square (inscribed circle):

  • The circle touches all four sides.
  • Diameter = side of the square.
  • Area ratio circle : square = pi r^2 : (2r)^2 = pi/4, about 78.5%.

Square inside a circle (inscribed square):

  • All four corners touch the circle.
  • Diagonal of the square = diameter of the circle.
  • So side = d/sqrt(2), and the square's area = d^2/2.

Circle inside an equilateral triangle (incircle), side s:

  • radius r = s/(2 sqrt(3)) = s sqrt(3)/6

Circle around an equilateral triangle (circumcircle), side s:

  • radius R = s/sqrt(3) = s sqrt(3)/3
  • Note R = 2r — the circumradius is twice the inradius.

Right triangle inscribed in a circle:

  • The hypotenuse is the diameter. Therefore R = hypotenuse/2.

Rectangle inscribed in a circle:

  • The diagonal is the diameter.

Circle inscribed in a right triangle with legs a, b and hypotenuse c: r = (a + b - c)/2.

3. Worked Examples

Example 1 — Circle in a square

A circle is inscribed in a square of side 12. What is the area of the region inside the square but outside the circle?

Diameter = 12, so r = 6.

Square area = 144. Circle area = 36 pi.

Shaded = 144 - 36 pi (about 30.9).

Example 2 — Square in a circle

A square is inscribed in a circle of radius 5. Find the square's area.

Diameter = 10 = the square's diagonal.

Square area = d^2/2 = 100/2 = 50.

Example 3 — Right triangle in a circle

A right triangle with legs 9 and 12 is inscribed in a circle. Find the circle's area.

Hypotenuse = 15 (a 9-12-15 triple), and that is the diameter.

r = 7.5, so area = pi(56.25) = 56.25 pi.

4. GRE Traps

  • Circle inscribed in a square: diameter equals the diagonal. No — it equals the side. The diagonal relationship belongs to the square inscribed in a circle.
  • Getting the direction backwards. Always identify which figure is inside.
  • Using the radius where the diameter is needed (or vice versa).
  • Assuming a figure touches when the problem doesn't say so. "Inscribed" means touching; a figure merely "inside" another gives no relationship.
  • Assuming the inscribed triangle is equilateral without justification.
  • Forgetting the semicircle right angle when a triangle is inscribed with the diameter as a side.

5. Speed Tricks

  • Write the linking equation first, in words: "circle's diameter = square's side". Then substitute.
  • Two facts cover 80% of these questions: inscribed circle -> diameter = side; inscribed square -> diagonal = diameter.
  • Shaded-region questions are always outer minus inner.
  • A right triangle inscribed in a circle hands you the diameter for free.
  • For area ratios, use r = 1. Circle in a square: pi/4. Square in a circle: 2/pi. Fast and reusable.

6. Self-Check

Q1. A circle is inscribed in a square of side 8. Find the circle's area.

Q2. A square is inscribed in a circle of diameter 12. Find the square's side.

Q3. A right triangle with legs 5 and 12 is inscribed in a circle. Find the radius.

Answers

A1. r = 4, area = 16 pi.

A2. Diagonal = 12, so side = 12/sqrt(2) = 6 sqrt(2) (about 8.49).

A3. Hypotenuse 13 is the diameter, so r = 6.5.

7. One-Line Summary for the Board

Circle in a square: diameter = side. Square in a circle: diagonal = diameter. Write the link before you compute.
