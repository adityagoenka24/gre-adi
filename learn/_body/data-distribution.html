2. Must-Know Rules

Shape and centre:

Shape Relationship Picture
Symmetric mean = median balanced bell
Skewed right (positive) mean > median long tail to the right
Skewed left (negative) mean < median long tail to the left

Memory hook: the tail pulls the mean. A long right tail drags the mean above the median.

The normal distribution:

  • Perfectly symmetric and bell-shaped
  • Mean = median = mode, all at the centre
  • Described completely by its mean and standard deviation

The 68-95-99.7 rule:

Within Percent of data
1 SD of the mean about 68%
2 SDs about 95%
3 SDs about 99.7%

Because the curve is symmetric, half of each band lies on each side. So about 34% lies between the mean and +1 SD, about 13.5% between +1 and +2 SDs, and about 2.35% between +2 and +3 SDs.

Frequency distributions. When data is given as a table of values and frequencies, the mean is a weighted average: multiply each value by its frequency, sum, and divide by the total frequency.

3. Worked Examples

Example 1 — Reading the normal curve

Test scores are normally distributed with mean 70 and SD 6. What percent of scores are above 82?

82 is 2 SDs above the mean (70 + 12).

95% lies within 2 SDs, so 5% lies outside — split evenly, 2.5% above and 2.5% below.

About 2.5%.

Example 2 — Skew

A town's household incomes have mean $85,000 and median $62,000. What does this tell you?

Mean > median, so the distribution is skewed right — a small number of very high incomes are pulling the mean up. The median is the better description of a typical household.

Example 3 — Frequency table

Find the mean of: value 2 (frequency 5), value 3 (frequency 8), value 7 (frequency 2).

Sum = 2(5) + 3(8) + 7(2) = 10 + 24 + 14 = 48.

Total frequency = 15.

Mean = 48/15 = 3.2.

4. GRE Traps

  • Skew direction reversed. Skewed right means the tail points right and the mean exceeds the median — even though most of the data sits on the left.
  • Applying 68-95-99.7 to non-normal data. The problem must say "normally distributed".
  • Forgetting to halve. "5% outside 2 SDs" means 2.5% in each tail.
  • Assuming mean = median always. Only for symmetric distributions.
  • Reading a frequency as a value. In a frequency table, multiply, don't add the frequencies to the values.
  • Confusing the normal curve's height with probability. It is the area under the curve that gives the proportion.

5. Speed Tricks

  • Sketch the bell curve and write the mean plus the +/-1, 2, 3 SD marks. Every normal question then reads off the sketch.
  • Remember 34 / 13.5 / 2.35 for the bands between consecutive SD marks — they cover almost every GRE normal question.
  • "Tail pulls the mean" settles every skew question in three seconds.
  • For frequency tables, build a running total of value x frequency.
  • Symmetry halves the work: the area below the mean is always 50%.

6. Self-Check

Q1. In a normal distribution with mean 100 and SD 15, what percent lies between 85 and 115?

Q2. A distribution has mean 40 and median 46. Is it skewed left or right?

Q3. In a normal distribution, approximately what percent lies more than 3 SDs from the mean?

Answers

A1. That is 1 SD either side: about 68%.

A2. Mean < median, so skewed left.

A3. 100 - 99.7 = about 0.3%.

7. One-Line Summary for the Board

The tail pulls the mean. Normal curve: 68 - 95 - 99.7, split symmetrically about the mean.
