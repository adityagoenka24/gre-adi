Q1 · ARI-NLO-001 — Answer: B

Step 1 — place each number. Everything negative sits left of 0; everything positive sits right of 0. Step 2 — order the negatives. Among negatives, the one further from zero is further LEFT, so it is smaller: -7 < -3. Step 3 — assemble left to right: -7, -3, 0, 1, 4. Check: each number is greater than the one before it. -7 < -3 < 0 < 1 < 4. Correct. Answer: B.

Trap. Writing -3 before -7 because 3 < 7 - that ranks the absolute values, not the numbers, and produces choice A. Choice C is the same list read greatest to least, and choice D orders the negatives correctly but then puts 1 before 0.

Q2 · ARI-NLO-005 — Answer: 8

Step 1 — distance between a and b is |a - b|, and the order does not matter. Step 2 — |-6 - 2| = |-8| = 8. Check by counting: -6 up to 0 is 6 units, 0 up to 2 is 2 more units, total 6 + 2 = 8. Correct. Answer: 8.

Trap. Reporting -8, the raw value of -6 - 2, and forgetting that distance is an absolute value and can never be negative. The other slip is 2 - 6 = -4, which drops the minus sign inside -(-6) and loses the 4 units left of zero.

Q3 · ARI-NLO-006 — Answer: D

Step 1 — convert the two endpoints to decimals: -1/2 = -0.50 and 1/4 = 0.25. We need a value x with -0.50 < x < 0.25. Step 2 — test each choice. A. -3/4 = -0.75, which is left of -0.50. Out. B. -1 = -1.00, further left still. Out. C. 1/2 = 0.50, which is right of 0.25. Out. D. -1/4 = -0.25. Is -0.50 < -0.25 < 0.25? Yes. In. E. 3/4 = 0.75, right of 0.25. Out. Answer: D.

Trap. Rejecting -1/4 because -1/4 'looks bigger' than -1/2 in the wrong direction, and taking -3/4 instead. Comparing 1/4 with 1/2 gives 1/4 < 1/2, but attaching the minus sign flips it: -1/4 > -1/2. Among negatives, the fraction closer to zero is the greater one.

Q4 · ARI-NLO-007 — Answer: C

Step 1 — Column A: |-5 - 3| = |-8| = 8. Step 2 — Column B: |2 - (-6)| = |2 + 6| = 8. Step 3 — 8 = 8, so the two quantities are equal. Check by counting Column B: from -6 to 0 is 6 units, from 0 to 2 is 2 units, total 8. Correct. Answer: C.

Trap. In Column B, reading 2 - (-6) as 2 - 6 = -4 and taking the distance as 4, which makes Column A look greater and points at choice A. Subtracting a negative adds; the two points are on opposite sides of zero, so their distance is the sum of the two gaps, not the difference.

Q5 · ARI-NLO-010 — Answer: -2

Step 1 — the midpoint is the AVERAGE of the two coordinates: (A + B)/2. Step 2 — keep the signs: (-8 + 4)/2 = (-4)/2 = -2. Check: distance from -8 to -2 is |-8 - (-2)| = 6, and distance from -2 to 4 is |4 - (-2)| = 6. Equal, so -2 really is the midpoint. Correct. Answer: -2.

Trap. Averaging the absolute values, (8 + 4)/2 = 6, or subtracting instead of adding, (-8 - 4)/2 = -6. The midpoint is an average, never a difference, and the signed coordinates must go in exactly as they stand.

Q6 · ARI-NLO-011 — Answer: A

Step 3 — order the three negatives. The most negative is the smallest: -0.800 < -0.750 < -0.625. Step 4 — assemble: -0.8 < -3/4 < -5/8 < 0.7 < sqrt(0.5). Trap. Choice E is the big one: 5/8 = 0.625 is less than 3/4 = 0.750, so students carry that over and write -5/8 before -3/4. Negating reverses the order, so -5/8 is the GREATER of the two. Q7 · ARI-NLO-012 — Answer: B

Step 1 — locate each column relative to zero. x is negative, so Column A < 0. A square of a non-zero number is positive, so Column B > 0. Step 2 — anything negative is left of anything positive on the number line, so x < x^2 for EVERY negative x. Step 3 — sample all of the negative territory to be sure: x = -5: Column A = -5, Column B = 25. B greater. x = -1: Column A = -1, Column B = 1. B greater. x = -0.5: Column A = -0.5, Column B = 0.25. B greater. x = -0.001: Column A = -0.001, Column B = 0.000001. B greater. Column B is greater in every case. Answer: B.

Trap. Choosing D because squaring behaves differently on x < -1 (it enlarges the magnitude) than on -1 < x < 0 (it shrinks it). That difference is real but irrelevant here: whatever the magnitude does, the sign flips to positive, and a positive always beats a negative. Never key D just because the magnitudes vary - check whether the ORDER varies.

Q8 · ARI-NLO-019 — Answer: A

Step 1 — read |n - 3| as a distance: n is less than 4 units away from 3 on the number line. Step 2 — that is the open interval centred at 3 with radius 4: 3 - 4 < n < 3 + 4, i.e. -1 < n < 7. Step 3 — the inequality is strict, so the endpoints -1 and 7 are excluded. The integers strictly inside are 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6 - seven of them. Check the boundary cases: n = -1 gives |-1 - 3| = 4, not less than 4, so -1 is out; n = 7 gives |7 - 3| = 4, also out; n = 6 gives |6 - 3| = 3 < 4, in. Correct. Answer: A.

Trap. Choice D keeps both endpoints, treating '<' as '<='. Choice E is the mirror mistake made only on the left: including -1 but stopping at 5. Choice B includes -1 while correctly excluding 7. Translate the absolute value into a centre and a radius, then decide the endpoints once from the strictness of the sign.

Q10 · ARI-NLO-021 — Answer: D

Q11 · ARI-NLO-022 — Answer: 7

Step 1 — x - y grows when x grows and when y SHRINKS, because y enters with a minus sign. Step 2 — take x at its right endpoint, x = 5, and y at its LEFT endpoint, y = -2. Step 3 — x - y = 5 - (-2) = 5 + 2 = 7. Check the other three corners: 5 - 4 = 1, -3 - (-2) = -1, -3 - 4 = -7. The largest is 7. Correct. Answer: 7.

Trap. Maximising both variables and answering 5 - 4 = 1. Subtracting a number is the same as adding its opposite, so the most negative allowed y contributes the most. Only the four endpoint combinations can ever be extreme, so if you are unsure, test all four.

Q12 · ARI-NLO-023 — Answer: A

Step 1 — write Column B in terms of Column A: x^3 = x^2 times x. Step 2 — on the interval 0 < x < 1, multiplying a positive number by x SHRINKS it, because the multiplier is between 0 and 1. So x^3 < x^2. Step 3 — test across the whole interval, including the ends: x = 0.9: Column A = 0.81, Column B = 0.729. A greater. x = 0.5: Column A = 0.25, Column B = 0.125. A greater. x = 0.1: Column A = 0.01, Column B = 0.001. A greater. Column A is greater everywhere in the interval. Answer: A.

Trap. Applying 'a higher power is a bigger number' and choosing B. That rule holds only for x > 1. For 0 < x < 1 each extra factor of x drags the value further LEFT toward zero, so the powers run x > x^2 > x^3 > ... The stem pins x inside (0, 1), so the relationship never flips and D is wrong.

Q14 · ARI-NLO-027 — Answer: A

Step 1 — translate the condition. x < y < 0 puts both numbers left of zero with x further left, so |x| > |y|. Step 2 — squaring depends only on magnitude: x^2 = |x|^2 and y^2 = |y|^2. Since |x| > |y| > 0, squaring preserves that: x^2 > y^2. Step 3 — therefore x^2 - y^2 > 0 = Column B. Factored view: x^2 - y^2 = (x - y)(x + y). Here x - y < 0 (x is left of y) and x + y < 0 (both negative), and a negative times a negative is positive. Same conclusion. Check two very different pairs. x = -4, y = -2: 16 - 4 = 12 > 0. x = -0.5, y = -0.1: 0.25 - 0.01 = 0.24 > 0. Both give A. Answer: A.

Trap. Carrying x < y straight through the squaring to conclude x^2 < y^2 and choosing B. Squaring only preserves order for non-negative numbers; on the negatives it REVERSES order, so the smaller number has the larger square. The stem pins both variables below zero, so the direction never changes and D is wrong.

Q15 · ARI-NLO-028 — Answer: E

Step 1 — unpack |a| < b. Since a is negative, |a| = -a, so the condition says -a < b, i.e. b is further from zero than a is. I - add a to both sides of -a < b: 0 < a + b. So a + b > 0. TRUE. II - a is negative and b is positive, and neither is zero, so their product is negative. TRUE. III - start from -a < b and rearrange to a > -b. Divide both sides by b, which is POSITIVE so the inequality direction is unchanged: a/b > -1. TRUE. All three hold, so the answer is E. Check with a = -2, b = 5: a + b = 3 > 0, ab = -10 < 0, a/b = -0.4 > -1. All three confirmed. Answer: E.

Trap. Stopping at choice C after verifying the two easy statements and treating III as unknowable. III is exactly statement I divided by the positive number b, so it carries no new risk. The reverse error is worrying that a/b might equal -1: that needs |a| = b, and the stem makes the inequality strict.

Q16 · ARI-NLO-029 — Answer: -2

Step 1 — total length: AB = 7 - (-5) = 12. Step 2 — the ratio 1 : 3 splits AB into 1 + 3 = 4 equal parts, so AC is 1/4 of the whole, not 1/3. AC = (1/4)(12) = 3. Step 3 — walk 3 units to the RIGHT from A, since B is to the right: C = -5 + 3 = -2. Check: CB = 7 - (-2) = 9, and AC : CB = 3 : 9 = 1 : 3. Correct. Answer: -2.

Trap. Reading '1 : 3' as 'one third of the way' and taking AC = 12/3 = 4, which lands on -1. A ratio of m : n divides the segment into m + n parts, so the first piece is m/(m + n) of the total. The other slip is reversing the ratio and putting C three quarters of the way along, at -5 + 9 = 4.

Q17 · ARI-NLO-030 — Answer: C

Q20 · ARI-NLO-033 — Answer: -2

Step 1 — the midpoint condition: (p + q)/2 = 3, so p + q = 6. Step 2 — the distance condition with p < q: q - p = 10, so q = p + 10. Step 3 — substitute: p + (p + 10) = 6, giving 2p = -4 and p = -2. Q21 · ARI-NLO-034 — Answer: B

C - both factors lie strictly between 0 and 1, so the product is smaller than either of them and certainly less than 1. FALSE. Q22 · ARI-NLO-035 — Answer: D

Column A is the midpoint of the two OUTER numbers. Column B is the middle number, which the stem only requires to lie somewhere between a and c - anywhere at all. Case 1 — b sits exactly halfway: a = 1, b = 2, c = 3. Column A = (1 + 3)/2 = 2 = Column B. Equal. Case 2 — b hugs the left end: a = 1, b = 2, c = 10. Column A = 5.5, Column B = 2. Column A greater. Q26 · ARI-NLO-037 — Answer: A

Step 1 — sign each of the seven values. a: negative (given). b: positive (given). a - b: negative minus positive, so negative. ab: negative times positive, so negative. a/b: negative over positive, so negative. b/a: positive over negative, so negative. a + b: sign unknown - it depends on which of |a| and b is larger. So five values are certainly negative, one is certainly positive, and one is unknown. Step 2 — statement I. a is negative and b is positive, so both signs are present no matter what. TRUE. Step 3 — statement II. With seven values the median is the 4th smallest. At least five of them are negative, so positions 1 through 5 in sorted order are all negative, and the 4th is among them. The median is therefore negative, never positive. FALSE. Step 4 — statement III. a/b is negative and b/a is negative - a quotient and its reciprocal always share a sign. They are both negative, so they do NOT have opposite signs. FALSE. Only I must be true. Check with a = -1, b = 4: the values are -1, 4, 3, -5, -4, -0.25, -4. Sorted: -5, -4, -4, -1, -0.25, 3, 4. Median = -1, negative, and both quotients are negative. Consistent. Answer: A.

Trap. Statement III is the bait: a and b have opposite signs, so students transfer that to their quotients. Reciprocals never flip sign - if a/b is negative, so is b/a. Statement II fails for a counting reason people skip: you must notice that FIVE of the seven values are forced negative, which pins the 4th smallest below zero regardless of where a + b lands.
