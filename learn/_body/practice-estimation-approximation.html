Q1 · ARI-EST-003 — Answer: 0.0039

Step 1 — find the first significant figure. Leading zeros never count, so counting starts at the first non-zero digit: 3. Step 2 — the second significant figure is the next digit, 8. So we are rounding at the 8. Step 3 — look at the digit after it: 6. Since 6 >= 5, round the 8 up to 9. Result: 0.0039. Check: 0.0039 and 0.003862 differ by 0.000038, about 1% - the right size of error for 2 significant figures. Answer: 0.0039.

Trap. Counting the two leading zeros as significant figures, which makes 0 and 0 the first two digits and gives 0.0038 (or even 0.00). Significant figures start at the first non-zero digit, so the leading zeros are placeholders only.

Q2 · ARI-EST-006 — Answer: C

Step 1 — notice the direction of each rounding. 197 rounds UP to 200 and 48 rounds UP to 50, so 200 x 50 = 10,000 inflates the product twice over and must be an OVERestimate. Step 2 — let the two roundings cancel instead. Round 197 DOWN to 190 and 48 UP to 50, so the errors point in opposite directions: 190 x 50 = 9,500. Step 3 — check how good that is. 200 x 48 = 9,600, and correcting for the 3 added to 197 gives 9,600 - 3 x 48 = 9,600 - 144 = 9,456. Step 4 — match to the choices: 9,456 is 44 from 9,500 and 544 from 10,000. 9,500 is closest by a wide margin. Check: 197 x 48 = 9,456. Correct. Answer: 9,500.

Trap. Rounding both factors up to 200 x 50 = 10,000 and picking D: both errors push the same way, nothing cancels, and the estimate lands 544 high. Choice B, 9,000, is 200 x 45, rounding 48 down instead of up. Choice A, 7,880, is 197 x 40, truncating 48 to 40 rather than rounding. Choice E, 11,820, is 197 x 60, rounding 48 up to the wrong ten. The habit worth keeping: round one factor up and the other down so the two errors cancel.
