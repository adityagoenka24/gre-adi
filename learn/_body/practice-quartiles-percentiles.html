Q1 · STA-IQR-001 — Answer: B

Step 1 — the list is already sorted and has 7 values, so the median is the 4th value: Q2 = 12. Step 2 — Q1 is the median of the values BELOW the median. Lower half = {3, 7, 9}, so Q1 = 7. Step 3 — Q3 is the median of the values ABOVE the median. Upper half = {15, 18, 21}, so Q3 = 18. Step 4 — IQR = Q3 - Q1 = 18 - 7 = 11. Check: the middle 50% of the seven values runs from 7 up to 18, a span of 11. Correct. Answer: 11.

Trap. Choice D, 18, is the full range (21 - 3), not the interquartile range. Choice E, 25, is Q3 + Q1 instead of Q3 - Q1. Choice A, 8.5, comes from the inclusive convention, which keeps the median 12 in BOTH halves: lower {3, 7, 9, 12} gives Q1 = 8, upper {12, 15, 18, 21} gives Q3 = 16.5, and 16.5 - 8 = 8.5. With an odd count the median belongs to neither half.

Q2 · STA-IQR-002 — Answer: B

Step 1 — there are 5 values, so the median is the 3rd value: Q2 = 8. Step 2 — the lower half is everything strictly below the median: {2, 5}. The median 8 is not included. Step 3 — Q1 is the median of that half: (2 + 5)/2 = 3.5. Check: Q3 by the same rule is the median of {11, 14} = 12.5, so the middle 50% runs 3.5 to 12.5. Correct. Answer: 3.5.

Trap. Choice C, 5, is the second-lowest value of the whole list, picked as if Q1 were simply 'the value a quarter of the way in'; it is also what the inclusive convention gives, since that method uses the lower half {2, 5, 8} and takes 5. Choice E, 12.5, is Q3, not Q1. Choice A, 2, is the minimum.

Q3 · STA-IQR-003 — Answer: 12

Step 1 — there are 6 values, an even count, so there is no single middle value to set aside. Split the list straight down the middle into two halves of 3. Step 2 — lower half = {4, 8, 12}, so Q1 = 8. Step 3 — upper half = {16, 20, 24}, so Q3 = 20. Step 4 — IQR = 20 - 8 = 12. Check: the median is (12 + 16)/2 = 14, and 8 < 14 < 20 as required. Correct. Answer: 12.

Trap. Putting the two middle values 12 and 16 into BOTH halves - the rule 'the median belongs to neither half' misremembered as 'the median belongs to both' - builds {4, 8, 12, 16} and {12, 16, 20, 24}, giving Q1 = 10, Q3 = 18 and IQR = 8. With an even count there is no middle data value to place anywhere: the halves are simply the first three and the last three values.

Q4 · STA-IQR-009 — Answer: C

Step 2 — check the others. A - a percentile is a RANK among test takers, not a share of questions answered correctly. FALSE. B - the 40% sit at or below 60, so the students above 60 are the other 60%, not 40%. FALSE. D - 60% is the share ABOVE, not the share at or below. FALSE. Q5 · STA-IQR-010 — Answer: B

Q6 · STA-IQR-012 — Answer: 22.5

Step 1 — sort first. Quartiles are positions, and positions mean nothing until the list is ordered. Sorted: 3, 5, 7, 9, 14, 16, 19, 22, 28, 33, 37, 41. Step 2 — 12 values, an even count, so split into two halves of 6. Lower half: 3, 5, 7, 9, 14, 16. Upper half: 19, 22, 28, 33, 37, 41. Step 3 — Q1 = median of the lower half = (7 + 9)/2 = 8. Step 4 — Q3 = median of the upper half = (28 + 33)/2 = 30.5. Step 5 — IQR = 30.5 - 8 = 22.5. Check: the overall median is (16 + 19)/2 = 17.5, and 8 < 17.5 < 30.5. Correct. Answer: 22.5.

Trap. Reading quartiles straight off the unsorted list. Taking positions 3 and 4 of the printed order gives (9 + 37)/2 = 23 for 'Q1' and positions 9 and 10 give (3 + 19)/2 = 11 for 'Q3', producing a negative IQR - a sure sign the sort was skipped.

Q7 · STA-IQR-013 — Answer: C

Step 1 — Data set X. Median = 30. Lower half {10, 20} gives Q1 = 15; upper half {40, 50} gives Q3 = 45. IQR = 45 - 15 = 30. Step 2 — Data set Y = 110, 120, 130, 140, 150. Median = 130. Lower half {110, 120} gives Q1 = 115; upper half {140, 150} gives Q3 = 145. IQR = 145 - 115 = 30. Step 3 — the two are equal. The one-line reason: adding a constant c moves every value, so Q1 becomes Q1 + c and Q3 becomes Q3 + c. In Q3 - Q1 the c cancels: (Q3 + c) - (Q1 + c) = Q3 - Q1. Answer: the two quantities are equal.

Trap. Choosing A because the numbers in Y are all much bigger, so the data 'must' be more spread out. Bigger values are not wider spread. A shift changes every measure of LOCATION (mean, median, quartiles) and no measure of SPREAD (range, IQR, standard deviation).

Q8 · STA-IQR-015 — Answer: A, D, E

Step 3 — anything strictly outside [-30, 90] is an outlier. Test each option. A - -35 < -30. Outlier. TRUE. B - -10 lies inside [-30, 90]. Not an outlier. FALSE. C - 0 lies inside [-30, 90]. Trap. Flagging B (-10) and C (0) because they look small or negative next to Q1 = 15. Size has nothing to do with it; only the fences matter, and the lower fence sits at -30, well below both. The other slip is measuring 1.5 x IQR from the median instead of from Q1 and Q3.

Q9 · STA-IQR-017 — Answer: D

Step 1 — original set, 7 values. Median = 4th value = 8. Lower half {2, 4, 6} gives Q1 = 4; upper half {10, 12, 14} gives Q3 = 12. IQR = 12 - 4 = 8. Step 2 — after deleting 14 the set is 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, an even count of 6. Split into halves of 3. Lower half {2, 4, 6} gives Q1 = 4; upper half {8, 10, 12} gives Q3 = 10. IQR = 10 - 4 = 6. Step 3 — the change is 6 - 8 = -2, a decrease of 2. Answer: it decreases by 2.

Trap. Choosing C on the reasoning that Q3 is not the maximum, so deleting the maximum cannot touch it. Q3 is the median of the upper half, and deleting a value re-cuts the halves: the upper half slides from {10, 12, 14} to {8, 10, 12}, dragging Q3 from 12 down to 10. Choice E, a decrease of 4, comes from putting both middle values of the new 6-value set into both halves - {2, 4, 6, 8} and {6, 8, 10, 12} give Q1 = 5, Q3 = 9 and IQR = 4, so the change looks like 4 - 8 = -4. Choices A and B are those same two magnitudes with the direction reversed, from subtracting old minus new (8 - 6 = 2, or 8 - 4 = 4) and reporting the result as an increase.

Q10 · STA-IQR-021 — Answer: 88

Step 1 — 8 values, an even count, so split into two halves of 4. Lower half: 55, 62, 68, 74. Upper half: 79, 85, 91, 97. Step 2 — Q3 is the median of the upper half: (85 + 91)/2 = 176/2 = 88. Q11 · STA-IQR-022 — Answer: A, C, D

First compute the two spreads: range = 90 - 8 = 82, and IQR = Q3 - Q1 = 62 - 26 = 36. A - the range is 82. TRUE. B - the IQR is 36, not 41. (41 is half the range, 82/2.) FALSE. Trap. Choice B rests on the belief that the IQR is half the range - true only for special symmetric data, and here it gives 41 instead of 36. Choice E is the capsule's warning that the 50th percentile is the median, not the mean; a right-skewed set with maximum 90 would have a mean well above 40.

Q12 · STA-IQR-024 — Answer: C

Step 1 — Q1 is the 25th percentile and Q3 is the 75th percentile. Step 2 — the share of data between them is 75% - 25% = 50%. Step 3 — so the box always holds about half the data values: 1/2. This is true for every box plot, whatever the numbers on the axis: the lower whisker covers the bottom 25%, the box the middle 50%, the upper whisker the top 25%. Answer: 1/2.

Trap. Choice B comes from measuring the picture instead of counting the data: (54 - 30)/(70 - 5) = 24/65, about 3/8. That is the fraction of the numeric RANGE the box covers, not the fraction of data POINTS inside it. Quartiles cut the data into four equal counts, not four equal numeric widths - which is exactly why a narrow box next to a long whisker signals skew, and why E is wrong.

Q13 · STA-IQR-026 — Answer: A, B, C, E

A - Q3 is at or above Q1 in every sorted data set, so IQR = Q3 - Q1 >= 0. TRUE. B - Q1 is at or above the minimum and Q3 is at or below the maximum, so Q3 - Q1 <= max - min. TRUE. C - if every value equals k then Q1 = Q3 = k and IQR = 0. TRUE. D - a counterexample: 1, 1, 3, 9, 9. Median = 3, lower half {1, 1} gives Q1 = 1 = minimum, upper half {9, 9} gives Q3 = 9 = maximum. IQR = 8 = range, yet the values are not all equal. FALSE. E - multiplying by the positive constant 2 preserves the sorted order, so Q1 becomes 2 x Q1 and Q3 becomes 2 x Q3, giving new IQR = 2Q3 - 2Q1 = 2 x IQR. TRUE. Answer: A, B, C, E.

Trap. Choice D is the one that catches people: IQR = range looks like it should force a single repeated value, but all it forces is that the bottom quarter and the top quarter be flat, as in 1, 1, 3, 9, 9, where the middle value is free. The other slip is rejecting E on the grounds that quartiles are positions rather than values: multiplying by 2 leaves the sorted positions alone but doubles the values sitting at them, so the IQR doubles. ADDING a constant is the operation that would leave the IQR untouched.

Q14 · STA-IQR-027 — Answer: B

Write the sorted values as v1 <= v2 <= ... <= v9. Step 1 — locate the quartiles. With 9 values the median is v5. The lower half is v1..v4, so Q1 = (v2 + v3)/2 = 20. The upper half is v6..v9, so Q3 = (v7 + v8)/2 = 50. Step 2 — to make the range v9 - v1 small, push v1 up and pull v9 down. Since v1 <= v2 <= v3 and v2 + v3 = 40, the largest v1 can be is 20 (take v2 = v3 = 20). Since v7 <= v8 <= v9 and v7 + v8 = 100, the smallest v9 can be is 50 (take v7 = v8 = 50). Step 3 — so the range is at least 50 - 20 = 30. Step 4 — 30 is achievable: 20, 20, 20, 20, 20, 50, 50, 50, 50 has Q1 = (20 + 20)/2 = 20, Q3 = (50 + 50)/2 = 50, and range 50 - 20 = 30. Answer: 30.

Trap. Choice C, 31, comes from assuming the minimum must sit strictly below Q1 and the maximum strictly above Q3, so the range has to beat the IQR by at least 1. Repeated values are allowed, and once ties are permitted the extremes can sit exactly on the quartiles. Choice D, 49, keeps the maximum at 50 but drops the minimum to 1, the smallest positive integer, forgetting that v1 is free to rise all the way to 20. Choice A, 0, is the reflex that the least possible range is always 0 because all values could be equal; Q1 = 20 and Q3 = 50 already rule that out. Choice E, 60, is range = 2 x IQR, a rule that does not exist.

Q15 · STA-IQR-028 — Answer: 33

Q16 · STA-IQR-029 — Answer: C

Write A sorted as v1 <= v2 <= ... <= v9. Step 1 — with 9 values the median is v5, the lower half is v1..v4 and the upper half is v6..v9. So Q1 = (v2 + v3)/2 and Q3 = (v7 + v8)/2. Step 3 — the new Q1 is still (v2 + v3)/2 and the new Q3 is still (v7 + v8)/2, because v9 does not appear in either formula. Step 4 — so IQR(B) = IQR(A). The two quantities are equal. Example: A = 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9 has Q1 = 2.5, Q3 = 7.5, IQR = 5; B = 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 109 has the same Q1 and Q3, so IQR = 5.

The IQR is built only from the values sitting at the quartile positions, and with 9 values Q3 = (v7 + v8)/2 never involves the maximum at all. Q22 · STA-IQR-035 — Answer: 13.2
